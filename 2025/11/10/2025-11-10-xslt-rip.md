# XSLT RIP

- Score: 635 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45873434) | Link: https://xslt.rip/

### TL;DR

This deliberately hyperbolic protest page opposes browser vendors’ reported plan to remove native XSLT support by 2027. It demonstrates the stakes by serving itself as XML transformed with XSLT, falling back to a failure message when that capability is absent. The page argues that client-side transformations help make RSS, Atom, and public XML documents human-readable, but its accusations about vendor motives are rhetoric rather than substantiated reporting. The real dispute is whether low usage and maintenance risk justify removing an open-web capability.

### Comment pulse

- Even sympathetic readers criticized the page’s tone while acknowledging that some sites and feeds still depend on browser-side XSLT.
- Alternatives included server-side transformation or a safer replacement implementation, each shifting costs or control away from the browser feature.

### LLM perspective

- View: Low usage alone is an incomplete metric when a feature preserves decentralized publishing and document readability.
- Impact: Removal transfers transformation responsibility to publishers, servers, or extensions and may strand neglected XML resources.
- Watch next: Whether vendors support a maintained replacement or publish migration evidence for affected sites and feeds.
