# Bitcoin's big secret: How cryptocurrency became law enforcement's secret weapon

- Score: 148 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45910305) | Link: https://bitwarden.com/blog/how-cryptocurrency-became-law-enforcements-secret-weapon/

### TL;DR

A summary of Andy Greenberg’s talk argues that Bitcoin’s public ledger turned a supposedly anonymous payment system into a powerful investigative record. It recounts cases involving Silk Road agents, Mt. Gox, AlphaBay, Welcome to Video, and major government seizures, crediting blockchain tracing alongside conventional investigation. Commenters stressed that this transparency was never truly secret: Bitcoin is pseudonymous, not anonymous. They also noted that tracing identities and securing accountability differ, especially across jurisdictions or when conversion points remain inaccessible.

### Comment pulse

- Several commenters said the headline overstates novelty because Bitcoin’s public transaction history has always been fundamental.
- Privacy coins and Lightning prompted debate, with regulated conversion to fiat described as a recurring identification point.

### LLM perspective

- View: An immutable public ledger can preserve investigative leads long after users mistakenly believe their activity is anonymous.
- Impact: Criminal utility depends less on traceability alone than on identity linkage, jurisdiction, and enforceable access to assets.
- Watch next: Whether privacy tools meaningfully break transaction linkage or merely move exposure to exchanges and cash-out points.
