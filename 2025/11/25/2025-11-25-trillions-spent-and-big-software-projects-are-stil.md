# Trillions spent and big software projects are still failing

- Score: 276 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46045085) | Link: https://spectrum.ieee.org/it-management-software-failures

### TL;DR

IEEE Spectrum argues that rising IT spending has not markedly improved large-project outcomes because organizations repeat familiar management failures: unrealistic goals, weak testing, ignored risk, scope creep, politics, and absent accountability. It cites Phoenix payroll, Horizon, failed legacy replacements, and harmful automated-benefits systems, while warning that AI cannot repair irrational governance. Commenters favor starting small and scaling through production evidence, but debate whether that always applies; many instead blame incentives, leadership, lost institutional memory, and vendor contracting.

### Comment pulse

- Pilot before national rollout → small deployments expose operational failures safely — counterpoint: some organizations launch reliable large systems immediately.
- Retrospectives often protect management → developers inherit corrective actions while requirements, staffing, and deadlines remain politically insulated.
- Software history is underused → recurring abstractions and workforce churn force teams to relearn documented lessons.
