# Orion 1.0

- Score: 342 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46047350) | Link: https://blog.kagi.com/orion

### TL;DR

Kagi’s native WebKit browser has left beta on macOS after six years, joining its iPhone and iPad versions. It promises zero telemetry, built-in blocking, extensive customization, extension support, and no embedded AI agent, with external AI integrations kept separate. The free product is sustained by tips and optional paid perks; Linux remains alpha and Windows targets late 2026. Despite one million downloads and a six-developer team, commenters questioned closed-source privacy claims, online installation, vague speed marketing, and serious password-manager lag.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy is the product thesis → zero telemetry and direct user funding remove advertising incentives from browser design.
- WebKit diversifies engines → a polished alternative weakens Chromium monoculture — counterpoint: closed source limits independent verification of privacy claims.
- Daily compatibility remains decisive → password-manager lag and web-app breakage can outweigh novel features or benchmark speed.
