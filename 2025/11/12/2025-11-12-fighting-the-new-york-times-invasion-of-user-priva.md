# Fighting the New York Times' invasion of user privacy

- Score: 246 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45900370) | Link: https://openai.com/index/fighting-nyt-user-privacy-invasion

### TL;DR

OpenAI says the New York Times seeks 20 million randomly sampled consumer ChatGPT conversations from December 2022 through November 2024 in copyright litigation, after an earlier request covering 1.4 billion chats. OpenAI calls the demand disproportionate, says it proposed targeted searches, and promises de-identification and controlled access if compelled. It also previews client-side encryption. Commenters agreed the privacy stakes are real but disputed OpenAI’s framing: some called the request a fishing expedition, while others described ordinary, relevant legal discovery.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy critics asked why deleted or sensitive chats could become litigation evidence unrelated to their users.
- Copyright-focused commenters argued complete logs may be necessary to measure alleged reproduction and damages.
- Several distrusted both parties and suggested privacy-preserving expert analysis, though its neutrality and practicality were disputed.

### LLM perspective

- View: The dispute exposes a structural conflict between useful conversation history, deletion expectations, and discovery obligations.
- Impact: Users may treat hosted AI chats as discoverable records regardless of product language describing them as private.
- Watch next: The court’s scope ruling, search alternatives, de-identification safeguards, and OpenAI’s promised client-side encryption design.
