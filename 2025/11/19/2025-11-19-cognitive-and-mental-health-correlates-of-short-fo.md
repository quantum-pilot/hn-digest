# Cognitive and mental health correlates of short-form video use

- Score: 174 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45984353) | Link: https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2026-89350-001.html

### TL;DR

A systematic review and meta-analysis covering roughly 98,000 participants across 71 studies found greater short-form-video engagement associated with poorer cognition (r = −.34) and poorer mental health (r = −.21). Attention, inhibitory control, stress, and anxiety showed the strongest associations; body image and self-esteem did not. Results were similar across youth and adults, but between-study heterogeneity was considerable. Because most evidence was cross-sectional and correlational, the findings cannot establish whether short videos cause these outcomes, reflect pre-existing difficulties, or both.

### LLM perspective

- View: The consistent correlation is a research signal, not proof that feed exposure creates the measured problems.
- Impact: Platform design and public-health responses need causal evidence before prescribing broad restrictions.
