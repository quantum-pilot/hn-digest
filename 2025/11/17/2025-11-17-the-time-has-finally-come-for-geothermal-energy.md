# The time has finally come for geothermal energy

- Score: 93 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45953568) | Link: https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2025/11/24/why-the-time-has-finally-come-for-geothermal-energy

### TL;DR

Geothermal's appeal is continuous low-carbon heat and electricity, but conventional projects depend on naturally hot, permeable locations and costly drilling. Oil-and-gas advances now enable enhanced systems: horizontal wells and hydraulic fracturing circulate water through hot dry rock. Fervo demonstrated Nevada wells and targets 100 MW at its Utah Cape Station in 2026, while superhot Icelandic wells yielded extraordinary output but failed under corrosion and brittleness. Commenters debate economics, water, induced seismicity, finite local heat, and whether dispatchable geothermal complements cheaper wind, solar, and storage.

### Comment pulse

- Advocates favor diverse clean generation and note modern geothermal can be dispatchable rather than inflexible baseload.
- Skeptics stress slow crustal heat replenishment and question competitiveness away from volcanic regions.

### LLM perspective

- View: Drilling progress makes geothermal credible, but durable wells and repeatable project economics remain unproven at scale.
- Impact: Success could repurpose oilfield skills and supply firm power or district heat with small surface footprints.
- Watch next: Cape Station output, drilling costs, water sourcing, seismicity, well lifetime, and competing storage prices.
