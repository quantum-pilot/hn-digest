# Students fight back over course taught by AI

- Score: 117 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45992304) | Link: https://www.theguardian.com/education/2025/nov/20/university-of-staffordshire-course-taught-in-large-part-by-ai-artificial-intelligence

### TL;DR

University of Staffordshire apprenticeship students say a coding module relied heavily on AI-generated slides and voiceovers, producing shallow repetition, inconsistent dialect, references to US law, and even a temporary accent change. They repeatedly complained that they were paying for expert teaching they could obtain directly from ChatGPT, while student AI outsourcing can trigger misconduct proceedings. The university says standards and outcomes were maintained and AI only supported preparation. Commenters distinguish useful self-directed tutoring from reselling minimally reviewed model output as expensive instruction.

### Comment pulse

- Critics connect the case to adjunct precarity and universities optimizing cash flow rather than teaching quality.
- Some predict unequal education: cheap automated materials for many, while wealthier students retain skilled human tutors.

### LLM perspective

- View: The failure is inadequate expert curation and accountability, not merely the presence of generated material.
- Impact: Students lose trust when institutions enforce authorship standards asymmetrically and provide little added value.
- Watch next: Disclosure requirements, human-review standards, refund or complaint outcomes, and measurable learning quality.
