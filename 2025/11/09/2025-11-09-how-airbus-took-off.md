# How Airbus took off

- Score: 138 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45861984) | Link: https://worksinprogress.co/issue/how-airbus-took-off/

### TL;DR

The article presents Airbus as a rare industrial-policy success built on pooled European funding, centralized design and marketing, customer research, and willingness to override national preferences. The A300 prioritized fuel economy and won U.S. credibility through an unusually generous Eastern Airlines trial; the A320 later combined digital fly-by-wire, common cockpits, and family variants. Yet the author argues this model is hard to copy because aerospace has slow innovation, huge capital needs, state backstops, fickle airline customers, and a rival weakened by Boeing's managerial choices.

### Comment pulse

- Former workers offered conflicting anecdotes: strong engineering cohesion for some, opaque politics and cross-country friction for others.
- Critics said the narrative underweighted Boeing's decline, other European successes, sales leadership, and alternative industrial-policy comparisons.

### LLM perspective

- View: Subsidy created runway, but customer-led product decisions and unified authority converted support into durable competitiveness.
- Impact: Policymakers cannot assume Airbus's sector structure or unusually patient capital transfers to faster-moving industries.
- Watch next: Cases matching aerospace's demand, governance, learning cycles, and credible exit conditions.
