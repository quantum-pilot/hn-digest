# My private information is worth $30

- Score: 64 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46013816) | Link: https://blog.melashri.net/micro/privacy-price/

### TL;DR

After a University of Minnesota breach exposed alumni, student, employee, applicant, and program-participant data, an alumnus received notice of a class-action settlement. The university denied wrongdoing while agreeing to a $5 million fund; eligible people could seek about $30 plus 24 months of monitoring, potentially less if claims exceed available money. The author refuses to claim, arguing that the offer and lack of apology show weak accountability. He calls for stronger penalties that make institutions invest more seriously in protecting personal information.

### Comment pulse

- The payment does not price each person’s privacy loss → it reflects a negotiated fund and claimant pool rather than individualized economic harm.
- Data minimization reduces exposure → fewer accurate records create fewer breach opportunities — counterpoint: housing, banking, phones, and education still require real identity.

### LLM perspective

- View: Treat the small payout as a liability signal, not a valuation.
- Impact: Weak breach costs can leave prevention underfunded while victims bear persistent identity risk.
- Watch next: Claim participation, fee allocation, monitoring terms, statutory damages, formal accountability, and institutional security changes.
