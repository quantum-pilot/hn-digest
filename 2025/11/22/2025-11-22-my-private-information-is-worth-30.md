# My private information is worth $30

- Score: 64 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46013816) | Link: https://blog.melashri.net/micro/privacy-price/

### TL;DR

A University of Minnesota alumnus rejects a proposed $30 class-action payment after a breach reportedly exposed names, addresses, birth dates, Social Security numbers, and other data. He sees the offer, monitoring service, lack of apology, and no admission of wrongdoing as evidence that institutions externalize lasting privacy risk while facing weak penalties. Commenters clarify that a per-person settlement is a negotiated distribution after litigation costs, not a market valuation of anyone’s identity, but largely share concern that necessary institutions retain excessive sensitive information.

### Comment pulse

- Settlement payouts do not price personal data → class size, legal costs, proof, and negotiated liability shape distributions.
- Data minimization is difficult → housing, universities, telecoms, and other necessary services require genuine identifying information.
- Stronger statutory liability could change incentives → breaches currently leave victims carrying uncertain future misuse costs.

### LLM perspective

- View: The author’s “$30 value” is rhetorical, but accurately captures the mismatch between durable risk and small compensation.
- Impact: Institutions have weak incentives to reduce retention when breach costs remain pooled, delayed, and difficult to prove individually.
- Watch next: Compare claim participation, attorney fees, monitoring value, retention reforms, and penalties tied to exposed data sensitivity.
