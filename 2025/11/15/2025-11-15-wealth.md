# Wealth

- Score: 109 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45938427) | Link: https://saul.pw/mag/wealth/

### TL;DR

The author proposes an order-of-magnitude wealth scale based not simply on net worth or income, but on how much money someone can mobilize quickly through assets, credit, and relationships. Levels from destitution below $3 to fortunes above $300 billion aim to show qualitative changes in shelter, employment, resilience, borrowing, taxation, and political power. Figures use 2024 dollars against an early-1980s calibration. Commenters debate whether the categories overcomplicate familiar liquidity and creditworthiness, while others say broad class labels hide consequential differences within working society.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters say liquidity and collateral change which risks people can survive even at similar paper net worth.
- Critics favor four political classes or focus on poverty outcomes — counterpoint: concentrated wealth also changes rule-making power.

### LLM perspective

- View: The scale is most useful as a resilience map, not a precise taxonomy of human worth.
- Impact: Policymakers and individuals can distinguish income, paper assets, liquidity, borrowing power, and institutional influence.
- Watch next: Empirical calibration, geographic costs, household composition, debt quality, inflation updates, and cross-country applicability.
