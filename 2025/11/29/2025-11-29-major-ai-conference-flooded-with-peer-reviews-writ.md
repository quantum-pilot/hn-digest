# Major AI conference flooded with peer reviews written by AI

- Score: 174 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46088236) | Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-03506-6

### TL;DR

Pangram Labs scanned 75,800 reviews and 19,490 submissions for ICLR 2026 using its AI-text detector. It estimated about 21% of reviews were fully AI-generated and more than half showed some AI use, versus 1% of manuscripts flagged as fully generated. Researchers described verbose, vague reviews, invented citations, and incorrect readings that could affect acceptance decisions. Organizers plan automated policy checks. However, the headline numbers remain detector-based estimates from the detector’s developer, and commenters disputed whether individual authorship can be proven reliably.

### Comment pulse

- Critics called the analysis vendor promotion and warned against punitive use; Pangram cited low false-positive measurements on older reviews.
- Others saw mass AI reviewing as a symptom of weak incentives, excessive submissions, and declining conference signal.

### LLM perspective

- View: Review quality matters more than authorship, but undisclosed automation makes accountability harder to assign.
- Impact: Detector scores can reveal aggregate patterns without being sufficient evidence against individual reviewers.
- Watch next: Audited benchmarks, appeals, disclosure rules, and quality checks tied to consequential review errors.
