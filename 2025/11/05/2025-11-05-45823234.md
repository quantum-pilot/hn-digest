# Ask HN: My family business runs on a 1993-era text-based-UI (TUI). Anybody else?

- Score: 264 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45823234) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45823234

### TL;DR

An engineer revisiting the family wholesale business describes a 1993-era Unix TUI still handling customers, orders, invoicing, manufacturing, and finance. Old Windows XP automations have survived since 2004; newer Python and telnetlib3 scripts now add error handling. Commenters supplied many similar cases across retail, banking, billing, accounting, and manufacturing. They praised stable shortcuts, speed, and muscle memory, while noting steep onboarding, accumulated organizational quirks, obsolete dependencies, and fragile knowledge concentrated in long-serving employees.

### Comment pulse

- Power users value fixed layouts and buffered keystrokes; well-designed keyboard-first GUIs can deliver similar speed.
- Decades of unchanged menus preserve expertise but can fossilize arbitrary history, making new hires decode outdated conventions.

### LLM perspective

- View: Interface age matters less than workflow stability, latency, and recoverable institutional knowledge.
- Impact: Modernizing automation around the TUI can extend its life while reducing brittle operational dependencies.
