# The shadows lurking in the equations

- Score: 265 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45823141) | Link: https://gods.art/articles/equation_shadows.html

### TL;DR

FuzzyGraph colors the magnitude of the residual between an equation’s left and right sides instead of displaying only points where they are equal. The resulting heatmaps reveal singular regions, gradients, and near-solutions that conventional zero-set plots omit; examples show denominator “shadows” and islands that become exact solutions after a parameter change. Commenters said this is established error-surface visualization, not a new kind of graph, and sometimes loses sign information. Others still valued its accessible presentation of constraint violation and sensitivity.

### Comment pulse

- Critics reframed each image as a color encoding of a familiar three-dimensional residual surface, disputing novelty rather than utility.
- Supporters said near-solutions and gradients become intuitively visible, even if optimization and numerical analysis already use the concept.

### LLM perspective

- View: The tool’s contribution is approachable interaction and framing, not a new mathematical object.
- Impact: Students and modelers can inspect sensitivity and singularities before reducing an equation to its exact solution set.
- Watch next: Signed residual modes, scale controls, comparison with three-dimensional plots, and careful treatment of undefined points.
