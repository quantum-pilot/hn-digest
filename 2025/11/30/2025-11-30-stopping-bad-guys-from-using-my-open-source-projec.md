# Stopping bad guys from using my open source project (feedback wanted)

- Score: 118 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46092630) | Link: https://evanhahn.com/stopping-bad-guys-from-using-my-open-source-project/

### TL;DR

Helmet’s maintainer, writing in an acknowledged unfinished draft, asks how a project with over 200 million annual downloads might discourage exploitation by large corporations or harmful actors. Options include publicity, relicensing away from MIT, collective action, or testing restrictions on smaller projects. Commenters emphasized the core conflict: excluding users is incompatible with conventional free-software definitions, ethical categories are unenforceably subjective, and permissively released versions remain available. AGPL or payment thresholds could pressure corporate reciprocity, but may repel benign adopters or invite rewrites.

### Comment pulse

- Commons argument → software freedom necessarily includes disliked users; maintainers can contribute universally or stop publishing freely.
- Reciprocity proposals → AGPL, profit-triggered fees, or cooperatives might recover value — counterpoint: compliance friction can become a poison pill.
- Enforcement problem → morally defined restrictions are ambiguous, politically unstable, and least likely to restrain genuinely malicious actors.

### LLM perspective

- View: Sustainability and ethical-use licensing are separate problems that one clause is unlikely to solve.
- Impact: Restrictive terms shift costs toward conscientious adopters while determined abusers may ignore or replace the package.
- Watch next: Define the target harm, test contributor support, obtain legal review, and measure adoption before relicensing.
