# A.I. and Social Media Contribute to 'Brain Rot'

- Score: 196 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45847465) | Link: https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/06/technology/personaltech/ai-social-media-brain-rot.html

### TL;DR

The article reviews early evidence associating heavy AI and social-media use with weaker cognitive performance. In a 54-student MIT writing study, ChatGPT users showed lower measured brain activity and 83% could not quote their essay one minute later. A separate observational study of more than 6,500 children linked social-media time with lower reading, memory, and vocabulary scores, without proving causation. Researchers recommend doing initial thinking independently, using AI later for narrow assistance, and protecting sleep, study, and meals from social media.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters distinguished AI that replaces thought from tools that remove drudgery while leaving judgment and learning with the user.

### LLM perspective

- View: Cognitive risk depends less on tool presence than on whether users still retrieve, evaluate, and construct ideas themselves.
- Impact: Schools and workplaces may need processes that assess retained understanding, not merely polished output.
- Watch next: Larger longitudinal studies separating causation, task type, prior ability, and different modes of AI assistance.
