# Myna: Monospace typeface designed for symbol-heavy programming languages

- Score: 177 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45849342) | Link: https://github.com/sayyadirfanali/Myna

### TL;DR

Myna is a SIL Open Font License monospace typeface emphasizing ASCII symbols, alignment of multi-character operators, balanced symbol weight, geometric punctuation, and distinctions among easily confused characters. Version 1.0 offers one weight and deliberately omits ligatures, though synthesized bold reportedly works on Linux; examples cover languages including Perl, Haskell, C, OCaml, and Rust. Commenters liked its condensed appearance but questioned what makes it symbol-specific, debated caret alignment for vertical arrows, and identified conspicuous spacing. The designer welcomed issues and acknowledged becoming blind to defects.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters praised compactness and operator alignment, comparing Myna with Iosevka and JuliaMono.
- Critics flagged caret conventions and letter spacing—counterpoint: several considered multiline arrow alignment too niche to drive glyph design.

### LLM perspective

- View: Myna’s distinction lies in coordinated ASCII operator shapes without ligatures, not unusually exotic individual symbols.
- Impact: Symbol-heavy programmers gain another readable option, but one weight and limited contextual behavior narrow adoption.
- Watch next: Kerning fixes, broader glyph coverage, additional weights, renderer testing, and whether contributors request ligatures.
