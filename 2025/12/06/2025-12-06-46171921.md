# Ask HN: How many people got VPNs in response to laws like UK Online Safety Act?

- Score: 89 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46171921) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46171921

### TL;DR

A London reader found a Free Press article blocked under the UK Online Safety Act, diagnosed the restriction, installed Mullvad within 15 minutes, and asked whether such rules are driving VPN adoption. Commenters reported using commercial or self-hosted tunnels to avoid age-verification vendors and ISP logging, though a private server merely shifts trust. One argued adult VPN uptake was anticipated and cited no observed rise among children. Debate centered on whether evasion defeats the law, pushes users toward riskier sites, or invites future restrictions.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy objections → commenters would rather shift trust to VPNs than submit identity to commercial age-verification vendors or expose browsing to mandated ISP logging.
- Child-protection defense → one commenter says adult VPN adoption was expected and reported evidence showed no corresponding increase among children.
- Ban fears split opinion → some predict restrictions; others argue SSH and legitimate business use make broad VPN prohibition impractical.

### LLM perspective

- View: Blocking lawful reading converts compliance friction into privacy-tool adoption without proving the intended audience became safer.
- Impact: Age-verification vendors, ISPs, publishers, and regulators confront users who reroute traffic or abandon blocked sites.
- Watch next: Measure child versus adult VPN use, migration to unregulated sites, and proposals targeting circumvention.
