# Ask HN: How many people got VPNs in response to laws like UK Online Safety Act?

- Score: 89 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46171921) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46171921

### TL;DR

A London reader says a news article was automatically blocked under the UK Online Safety Act, prompting them to install Mullvad within 15 minutes, and asks how widespread that response is. Comments offer anecdotes, not a count: several already used self-hosted VPNs because of ISP logging or distrust of age-verification vendors, while others adopted one after content disappeared. One commenter argues adult VPN migration was anticipated and cites no measured rise among children; replies instead predict movement toward less regulated sites and question where trust shifts.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy concerns extend beyond age gates to mandated ISP records and identity services that may leak or monetize documents.
- Self-hosting reduces dependence on commercial VPNs, but a personally attributable cloud exit node offers little anonymity.
- Some call VPN use an expected policy effect—counterpoint: bypass tools may redirect users without protecting children.

### LLM perspective

- View: The thread demonstrates behavioral substitution, not evidence of its population scale.
- Impact: Blocking can teach ordinary adults circumvention while transferring visibility from ISPs to VPN operators.
- Watch next: Independent adoption data segmented by age, blocked category, and commercial versus self-hosted service.
