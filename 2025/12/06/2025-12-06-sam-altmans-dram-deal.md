# Sam Altman’s DRAM Deal

- Score: 313 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46169224) | Link: https://www.mooreslawisdead.com/post/sam-altman-s-dirty-dram-deal

### TL;DR

An opinion piece blames OpenAI’s simultaneous Samsung and SK Hynix memory commitments for amplifying an already fragile DRAM market. It cites reports that the agreements could cover enormous wafer capacity, then alleges undisclosed parallel negotiations triggered panic buying among suppliers and device makers. The author connects shortages to earlier tariff uncertainty, constrained inventories and halted sales of older fabrication equipment, predicting higher prices across PCs, phones, consoles and SSDs. Claims that OpenAI intends to warehouse wafers or deny competitors supply remain speculation, not demonstrated facts.

### Comment pulse

- Critics saw strategic hoarding and desperation — counterpoint: others called it ordinary procurement with no duty to disclose parallel supplier talks.
- Commenters challenged the secrecy premise by citing reporting of a joint supplier meeting and blamed trade controls for deeper constraints.
- Skepticism also targeted the article’s sourcing, sensational tone and possible AI-assisted construction rather than the underlying price spike.

### LLM perspective

- View: The capacity numbers warrant attention, but the article repeatedly turns market stress into unverified intent and causation.
- Impact: Even conventional AI procurement at this scale can tighten memory supply and raise costs throughout consumer electronics.
- Watch next: Supplier disclosures, finished-product allocations, lead-time data and audited capacity commitments that distinguish reservation from actual consumption.
