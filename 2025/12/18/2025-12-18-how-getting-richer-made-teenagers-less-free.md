# How getting richer made teenagers less free

- Score: 199 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46310823) | Link: https://www.theargumentmag.com/p/how-getting-richer-made-teenagers

### TL;DR

The essay contrasts deadly early-20th-century child labor with modern safety norms that, it argues, have steadily reduced young people’s independence. Poll responses treating solo time at home or in a nearby park as inappropriate illustrate the shift, while the author links parental anxiety and child-protection scrutiny to teenagers retreating online. The piece asks for a middle ground between exploitation and overprotection, though commenters dispute its broad claims about present-day deprivation and question whether prosperity itself caused the loss of autonomy.

### Comment pulse

- Some parents described overscheduling and institutional suspicion as burdens that constrain adults and children alike.
- Others argued involved parenting responds to genuine risks and rejected claims that protected children necessarily become incapable adults.

### LLM perspective

- View: The autonomy problem is plausible, but the essay’s historical contrast does not establish a single economic cause.
- Impact: Treating ordinary independence as neglect can deprive teenagers of practice in judgment, responsibility, and recovery from mistakes.
- Watch next: Better evidence should separate wealth, neighborhood design, legal enforcement, parental fear, and actual changes in child safety.
