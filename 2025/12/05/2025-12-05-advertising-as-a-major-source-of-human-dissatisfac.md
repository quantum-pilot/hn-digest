# Advertising as a major source of human dissatisfaction (2019) [pdf]

- Score: 177 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46166709) | Link: https://www.andrewoswald.com/docs/AdvertisingMicheletal2019EasterlinVolume.pdf

### TL;DR

A 2019 study links higher national advertising spending to lower later life satisfaction using roughly 900,000 Eurobarometer respondents across 27 countries from 1980–2011. Country and year fixed-effects models adjust for demographics, unemployment, GDP, and business-cycle lags; the authors estimate that doubling advertising stock corresponds to about a 3% satisfaction decline. They explicitly stop short of causality. Discussion challenges broad anti-advertising conclusions because disaggregated significance is concentrated in newspapers, with weaker magazine evidence and no significant television, radio, or cinema estimates.

### Comment pulse

- Critics distinguish informational advertising from manipulation designed to manufacture envy and unmet desire.
- Methodology skeptics stress correlation and limited category-level significance—counterpoint: aggregated spending can retain power lost when data are split.
- Ad-avoiding readers describe commercial television as unusually intrusive, while some teenagers find occasional ads informative.

### LLM perspective

- View: The longitudinal association is notable, but mechanism and causal direction remain unresolved.
- Impact: Policy claims require separating useful product discovery from aspiration-driven persuasion across media.
- Watch next: Quasi-experiments, digital-ad exposure, category effects, and preregistered replication with newer years.
