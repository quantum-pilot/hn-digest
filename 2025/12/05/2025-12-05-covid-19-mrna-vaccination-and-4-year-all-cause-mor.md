# Covid-19 mRNA Vaccination and 4-Year All-Cause Mortality

- Score: 247 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46162262) | Link: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2842305

### TL;DR

A French national observational cohort compared 22.8 million mRNA-vaccinated and 5.9 million unvaccinated adults aged 18–59 over a median 45 months. Weighted models found no increased all-cause mortality: the vaccinated group had a 25% lower adjusted hazard and 74% lower severe-COVID death hazard. Follow-up began six months after assigned index dates, and later vaccination censored controls. Authors conservatively emphasize safety because healthy-vaccinee effects and residual confounding could explain some broad mortality advantage; commenters point to lower injury and traffic deaths as evidence of remaining selection differences.

### Comment pulse

- Readers want the 25% lower all-cause estimate stated plainly, while authors frame the result around long-term safety.
- Skeptics accept lower COVID mortality but question causal inference without fuller confounder reasoning.
- Large scale reassures some—counterpoint: sample size cannot eliminate systematic differences between vaccination groups.

### LLM perspective

- View: The study strongly rejects a large mortality increase but cannot attribute every observed reduction to vaccination.
- Impact: Safety communication improves when association, causation, and healthy-vaccinee bias are separated.
- Watch next: Replication, negative-control calibration, age-specific absolute risks, and transparent causal models.
