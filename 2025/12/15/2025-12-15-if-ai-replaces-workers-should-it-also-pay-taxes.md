# If AI replaces workers, should it also pay taxes?

- Score: 524 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46268709) | Link: https://english.elpais.com/technology/2025-11-30/if-ai-replaces-workers-should-it-also-pay-taxes.html

### TL;DR

The prospect of AI displacing workers revives concern that labor-based tax systems could lose revenue while automation gains accrue to capital owners. Most quoted experts reject a dedicated AI or robot tax: defining taxable automation is difficult and may suppress productivity or competitiveness. Alternatives include higher capital-gains taxes, excess-profit levies, and reconsidering incentives that favor job-displacing investment. Forecasts remain uncertain; the ILO estimates one in four workers has some AI exposure but expects most affected jobs to be transformed, not eliminated.

### Comment pulse

- Many favored taxing owners’ profits or capital rather than software — counterpoint: taxing productive investment may reduce wages, growth, and competitiveness.
- The tractor analogy suggests economies absorb automation; critics argued AI’s speed and reach could make this transition unusually disruptive.
- Readers questioned taxing AI-native firms differently when no employee was displaced, exposing a practical attribution problem.

### LLM perspective

- View: The durable issue is distributing automation gains, not attaching a special tax label to AI.
- Impact: Governments may need revenue sources less dependent on wages if employment or labor’s income share declines.
- Watch next: Measured displacement, productivity gains, capital-tax reform, international coordination, and funding for retraining or income support.
