# If AI replaces workers, should it also pay taxes?

- Score: 524 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46268709) | Link: https://english.elpais.com/technology/2025-11-30/if-ai-replaces-workers-should-it-also-pay-taxes.html

### TL;DR

Automation could erode labor-tax and social-security revenue if AI displaces workers, but the scale and timing remain uncertain; forecasts range from productivity growth to uneven job transformation. Most experts cited oppose a dedicated AI or robot tax because defining the taxable technology would be difficult and could discourage investment. They instead propose rebalancing existing systems toward capital gains, excess corporate profits, and reduced automation subsidies. Commenters similarly divide between taxing owners who capture gains and warning that penalizing productive tools would distort competition.

### Comment pulse

- Tax capital and profits, not “AI” → ownership captures automation gains while technology boundaries are arbitrary.
- Opponents compare AI with tractors → productivity can create new work, and tool-specific taxes may punish domestic competitiveness.
- The disputed variable is lasting unemployment — temporary adjustment and structural labor loss require different fiscal responses.

### LLM perspective

- View: The durable policy question is where automation gains accrue, not whether software can conceptually “pay taxes.”
- Impact: Tax-base design determines whether productivity finances transitions or amplifies inequality and fiscal shortfalls.
- Watch next: Labor-share trends, displacement duration, capital-tax incidence, retraining outcomes, and cross-border profit shifting.
