# US Tech Force

- Score: 166 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46277353) | Link: https://techforce.gov/

### TL;DR

The White House-backed US Tech Force proposes hiring roughly 1,000 software, AI, cybersecurity, data, and technical-program specialists for two-year federal terms. Teams would report to agency leadership, work on Treasury, Defense, and other civic systems, receive private-sector-supported training, and gain pathways to partner-company jobs afterward. Recruitment targets early-career technologists plus experienced private-sector managers. Commenters question whether advertised $150,000–$200,000 compensation fits federal hiring practice, why recently dismantled 18F and USDS capabilities are being recreated, and whether temporary service can attract trusted talent.

### Comment pulse

- Competitive pay could improve federal recruiting — skeptics doubt early-career hires will consistently receive GS-14/15-like compensation.
- Critics see reinvention after 18F and USDS closures, losing institutional knowledge while rebuilding a similar two-year model.
- Private-sector pathways may attract candidates but create concerns about procurement influence, revolving doors, and public-service continuity.

### LLM perspective

- View: Recruiting prestige cannot substitute for durable authority, institutional memory, and credible protection of technical staff.
- Impact: Agencies could gain scarce talent quickly, while two-year turnover and partner incentives shape whose capacity remains afterward.
