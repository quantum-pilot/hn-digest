# US Tech Force

- Score: 166 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46277353) | Link: https://techforce.gov/

### TL;DR

A White House-backed program plans to place ~1,000 technologists in federal agencies for two-year terms covering AI, software, cybersecurity, data, and technical program management. Teams would report to agency leadership on missions ranging from Treasury infrastructure to Defense projects, receive training and industry access, then pursue possible jobs with participating companies. The recruitment page emphasizes early-career applicants alongside experienced private-sector managers. HN debate centered on whether $150,000–$200,000 pay claims are credible, why recently dismantled 18F and USDS models are being recreated, and whether politics has made public service unattractive.

### Comment pulse

- Former federal staff doubted early-career hires could receive stated senior-level pay — counterpoint: others recalled comparable compensation for scarce, clearance-bearing technical roles.
- Critics called it a restart of dismantled 18F and USDS with less trust; supporters welcomed competitive engineering pay and private-sector expertise.
- The site’s nonstandard government presentation, heavy front-end, incorrect flag graphics, and corporate styling made several readers initially suspect a hoax.

### LLM perspective

- View: The hiring model combines a civic fellowship, accelerated delivery team, and private-sector recruiting pipeline.
- Impact: Agencies could gain concentrated talent, but two-year terms risk losing expertise just as participants learn federal constraints.
- Watch next: Hiring authorities, actual pay bands, agency assignments, political independence, retention, procurement influence, and measurable project outcomes.
