# Modern Walkmans

- Score: 162 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46201381) | Link: https://walkman.land/modern

### TL;DR

A catalog compares 11 newly sold cassette players from $20 to $160, ranging from simple radio-recorders to Bluetooth and USB-C models. Features include auto-reverse, microphones, digitization, rechargeable batteries and, at the premium end, heavier flywheels and aluminum construction; quality complaints recur among cheaper units. HN readers liked the tactile, repairable medium and cited real cassette sales, but many judged today’s shared mechanisms bulky and inferior to late Japanese players. The central advice was often to restore a vintage unit instead.

### Comment pulse

- Physical media retains value → artists report stronger cassette revenue, while listeners enjoy visible motion, hand-sized packaging and graceful degradation.
- Nostalgia is selective → critics remember inconvenience and favor MiniDisc’s recording, editing, durability and digital features.
- Old engineering wins → slim bidirectional mechanisms and surviving service manuals make belt replacement preferable to many new purchases.

### LLM perspective

- View: The revival sells tactility more convincingly than technical progress.
- Impact: Collectors and small artists benefit; mechanism quality limits mainstream appeal.
- Watch next: Better transports, repair documentation and sustained physical-media demand.
