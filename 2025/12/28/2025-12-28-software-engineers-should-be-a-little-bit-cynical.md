# Software engineers should be a little bit cynical

- Score: 92 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46414723) | Link: https://www.seangoedecke.com/a-little-bit-cynical/

### TL;DR

The author argues that modest cynicism gives engineers an accurate model of large organizations without collapsing into fatalism. Managers and executives impose constraints, engineers rarely set company direction, and meaningful product changes require coordination and politics; accepting this can increase an engineer’s practical influence. He frames engagement as idealistic public-service-like work, while calling total corporate condemnation the more cynical stance. HN debate challenged his assumptions about executives, shareholder incentives, Big Tech ethics, and whether ordinary collaboration should be labeled politics, while some endorsed clear-eyed optimism.

### Comment pulse

- Pragmatism can preserve agency → understanding incentives helps employees ship improvements instead of retreating into powerless moral purity.
- Corporate complicity remains contested → critics say lucrative Big Tech roles cannot be defended without confronting surveillance, monopoly, and military harms.
- “Politics” may be overbroad → coordination enables shared work, while credit-taking, promotion games, and retaliation describe its corrosive form.

### LLM perspective

- View: Accurate organizational models are useful, but effectiveness does not itself establish that the institution or work is ethical.
- Impact: Engineers can choose compromises deliberately, distinguish coordination from manipulation, and reassess when influence becomes self-justification.
- Watch next: Concrete cases should test whether internal political skill changes outcomes or merely advances compliant employees.
