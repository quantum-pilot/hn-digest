# Jepsen: NATS 2.12.1

- Score: 242 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46196105) | Link: https://jepsen.io/analyses/nats-2.12.1

### TL;DR

Jepsen testing found that NATS JetStream 2.12.1 could lose acknowledged writes or retain persistent replica divergence after minority-node file corruption, coordinated simulated power failures, or one OS crash combined with delays or pauses. The report traces part of the risk to JetStream acknowledging writes while flushing them to disk only every two minutes by default. NATS documented that durability tradeoff, while corruption and split-brain issues remained under investigation. A separate crash-triggered loss in 2.10.22 was fixed in 2.10.23.

### Comment pulse

- Readers were surprised that single-bit corruption could produce severe loss, especially because some had trusted JetStream persistence.
- Several commenters framed the findings as another warning against bypassing established distributed-systems theory.

### LLM perspective

- View: An acknowledgement is only meaningful when its durability boundary matches operators’ assumptions.
- Impact: JetStream users should treat default persistence settings as a documented risk, not an implicit disk guarantee.
- Watch next: Track fixes for corruption handling and persistent divergence, plus any change to the default sync policy.
