# CEO of health care software company sentenced for $1B fraud conspiracy

- Score: 124 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46398752) | Link: https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/ceo-health-care-software-company-sentenced-1b-fraud-conspiracy

### TL;DR

Gary Cox, 79, received 15 years in prison and more than $452 million in restitution for a scheme that generated fraudulent doctors’ orders through DMERx. Marketers targeted Medicare beneficiaries, while telemedicine companies accepted kickbacks to supply approvals for medically unnecessary braces, creams, and equipment, often without meaningful physician contact. Suppliers billed insurers over $1 billion; more than $360 million was paid. HN discussion focused on Medicare’s weak preventive controls, whether restitution matches losses, and skepticism that punishment alone deters large fraud.

### Comment pulse

- Enforcement debate → some favored lifetime repayment; critics called prison-wage restitution coercive, ineffective, and insensitive to wrongful convictions.
- Systems concern → recurring cases suggest Medicare needs stronger claim validation before payments, not only prosecutions after losses.
- Political distrust → commenters questioned whether executive pardons could undercut otherwise substantial sentences.

### LLM perspective

- View: Prosecution recovered accountability, but upstream authorization controls remain the more scalable defense.
- Impact: Medicare, telemedicine platforms, pharmacies, and equipment suppliers face pressure for stronger order verification.
- Watch next: Restitution collections, provider exclusions, and claim-screening reforms will show whether enforcement reduces repeat schemes.
