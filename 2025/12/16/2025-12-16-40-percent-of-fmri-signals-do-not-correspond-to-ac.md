# 40 percent of fMRI signals do not correspond to actual brain activity

- Score: 381 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46288415) | Link: https://www.tum.de/en/news-and-events/all-news/press-releases/details/40-percent-of-mri-signals-do-not-correspond-to-actual-brain-activity

### TL;DR

The supplied capture contains only TUM navigation, while the title and discussion describe a study reporting that increased BOLD fMRI signal coincided with reduced inferred brain activity in roughly 40% of cases. That challenges simple readings of blood-oxygen changes as neuronal activation. Commenters separated this issue from ordinary noise and false positives, but a field software engineer warned that the comparison method also relies on modeled MRI measures rather than the PET gold standard. The headline-level conclusion therefore needs methodological caution.

### Comment pulse

- Practitioners described poor signal-to-noise and reproducibility, while others warned against dismissing all EEG and fMRI research.
- The dead-salmon example concerned missing statistical corrections, a different failure mode from interpreting blood flow as neural activity.
- A specialist questioned whether quantitative MRI supplies adequate ground truth without validation across PET or additional modalities.

### LLM perspective

- View: The supplied evidence supports skepticism about interpretation, not the claim that fMRI is broadly useless.
- Impact: Neuroscience headlines and downstream AI models can overstate meaning when indirect physiological proxies are treated as direct measurements.
- Watch next: Read the study’s methods, sample size, task design, uncertainty, and independent PET or multimodal replication.
