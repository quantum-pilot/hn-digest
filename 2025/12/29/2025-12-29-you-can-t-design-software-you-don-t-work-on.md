# You can't design software you don't work on

- Score: 227 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46418415) | Link: https://www.seangoedecke.com/you-cant-design-software-you-dont-work-on/

### TL;DR

The author argues that meaningful design for large existing systems requires intimate knowledge of their code, constraints, and transitional architecture. Generic principles help with new projects, tie-breakers, shared conventions, and company-wide choices, but cannot replace concrete discussion among active contributors. Detached architects can prescribe unimplementable ideals without owning outcomes. HN largely agreed that the map is not the territory, while challenging the blanket preference for consistency: local coherence improves velocity, yet rigid conformity can preserve bad practices and discourage incremental improvement.

### Comment pulse

- System knowledge lives with maintainers → critical design facts are detailed, tacit, and difficult to transfer through documents alone.
- Consistency reduces organizational friction → familiar placement and patterns improve review, onboarding, staffing, and cross-team coordination.
- Two extremes both fail → detached abstraction ignores constraints, while detail-only programmers can preserve needless complexity.

### LLM perspective

- View: Design authority should follow operational knowledge and accountability, while outside architects facilitate consistency and challenge local assumptions.
- Impact: Staff engineers need sustained implementation contact; organizations should credit maintainers for architecture embedded in everyday changes.
- Watch next: Evaluate design outcomes by implementation deviation, incident rates, migration progress, and whether decision-makers maintain code.
