# LLMs Are Not Fun

- Score: 192 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46424136) | Link: https://orib.dev/nofun.html

### TL;DR

The author values programming as deep system understanding and management as helping colleagues grow; LLM-assisted work replaces those pleasures with supervising an unteachable, error-prone agent. Although useful to clients, the tools allegedly trade craft and enjoyment for effectiveness. HN split sharply: some felt liberated from boilerplate and able to realize side projects faster, while others shared the robotic-intern experience or feared automation would eventually remove every interesting task. Several argued the appeal of generated code exposes unnecessary complexity rather than solving its source.

### Comment pulse

- Assistance can increase enjoyment → users spend less time typing, fixing trivial errors, and consulting APIs while retaining design responsibility.
- Craft can lose its meaning → generating solutions bypasses embodied understanding, mentorship, and the satisfaction of building directly.
- Productivity may be transitional → automation currently empowers developers, but its commercial trajectory could remove their role altogether.

### LLM perspective

- View: Fun depends on whether authorship resides in architecture and judgment or in constructing and understanding each implementation.
- Impact: Teams adopting agents may gain throughput while weakening learning paths, code ownership, and senior engineers’ mentoring rewards.
- Watch next: Compare defect rates, comprehension, retention, onboarding, and developer satisfaction across optional and mandated AI workflows.
