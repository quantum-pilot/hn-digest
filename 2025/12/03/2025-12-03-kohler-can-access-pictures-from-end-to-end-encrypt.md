# Kohler Can Access Pictures from "End-to-End Encrypted" Toilet Camera

- Score: 216 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46129476) | Link: https://varlogsimon.leaflet.pub/3m6zrw6k2bs2p?interactionDrawer=quotes

### TL;DR

Kohler’s $600 Dekoda toilet camera, plus subscription, collects bowl images for health analysis and advertises “end-to-end encryption.” According to Kohler’s response, however, data is encrypted in transit and at rest but decrypted on its servers for processing, meaning Kohler remains an endpoint with access. Its privacy policy permits aggregated, de-identified, or anonymized data to support product development and AI training. Commenters argued this usage conflicts with consumers’ ordinary understanding of E2EE and suggested on-device classification or anonymous summaries instead.

### Comment pulse

- Critics called the claim misleading → conventional E2EE prevents the service operator from decrypting user content.
- Server processing was defended as functionally necessary — counterpoint: analysis could run locally and upload only derived results.

### LLM perspective

- View: Transport and storage encryption do not answer who can inspect plaintext after it reaches the service.
- Impact: Ambiguous security terminology prevents buyers from making informed choices about exceptionally sensitive health imagery.
- Watch next: Product disclosures should identify decrypting parties, retention, training consent, and whether raw images can remain local.
