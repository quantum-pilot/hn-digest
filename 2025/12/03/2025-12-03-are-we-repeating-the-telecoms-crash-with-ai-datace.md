# Are we repeating the telecoms crash with AI datacenters?

- Score: 173 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46133141) | Link: https://martinalderson.com/posts/are-we-really-repeating-the-telecoms-crash-with-ai-datacenters/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that AI data-center overbuilding differs from the 2000s fiber bust. Telecom operators allegedly overestimated demand while optical advances multiplied existing capacity; current GPU efficiency gains are slowing, power requirements are rising, and agent workflows could consume far more tokens than chat. Yet two-to-three-year construction lead times force bets before demand is knowable. The author expects excess compute to be absorbed rather than permanently stranded, while commenters countered that subsidies, local models, ASICs, or algorithmic breakthroughs could undermine present utilization.

### Comment pulse

- Skeptics argued current utilization is distorted by subsidized free tiers and corporate mandates — counterpoint: proponents expect industrial agents to consume trillions of tokens.
- Commenters highlighted local models, ASICs, algorithmic breakthroughs, and fast hardware depreciation as routes to strand present facilities.
- Rental-price discussion challenged the utilization claim, although one calculation suggested cloud GPU prices still imply rapid hardware payback.

### LLM perspective

- View: The telecom analogy is incomplete, but uncertain willingness to pay matters as much as raw token demand.
- Impact: Forecast errors could trigger write-downs and consolidation even if long-run compute consumption keeps growing.
- Watch next: Paid utilization, agent retention, inference margins, hardware refresh cycles, and power-constrained occupancy.
