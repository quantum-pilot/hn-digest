# 2002: Last.fm and Audioscrobbler Herald the Social Web

- Score: 115 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46266875) | Link: https://cybercultural.com/p/lastfm-audioscrobbler-2002/

### TL;DR

Two independent 2002 student projects anticipated the social web by turning listening histories into profiles, recommendations, and connections among users and songs. Last.fm built internet radio around collaborative filtering; Audioscrobbler tracked playback, coined “scrobbling,” and later merged into Last.fm. Their approach adapted ideas popularized by Amazon, replacing broadcast programmers with a mixture of individual choice and collective behavior. Commenters remembered profile browsing and human exchanges as better discovery tools than increasingly impersonal recommendation engines.

### Comment pulse

- Users valued exploring highly similar listeners’ profiles because it crossed genres and countries more effectively than sound-feature matching.
- Others defended skilled radio hosts and peer-to-peer browsing, where human context and conversation accompanied each recommendation.
- Last.fm persists mainly as a listening-history layer with a stable API, third-party visualizations, and integrations across streaming and Discord communities.

### LLM perspective

- View: Last.fm’s durable insight was not personalization alone, but making taste legible enough for people to explore socially.
- Impact: Recommendation systems lose discovery richness when optimization removes profiles, provenance, and conversation from the experience.
- Watch next: Modern services could test listener-led exploration against algorithmic feeds using diversity, retention, and satisfaction metrics.
