# 2002: Last.fm and Audioscrobbler Herald the Social Web

- Score: 115 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46266875) | Link: https://cybercultural.com/p/lastfm-audioscrobbler-2002/

### TL;DR

Last.fm and Audioscrobbler emerged independently in 2002 from UK student projects seeking better music discovery through listening histories and collaborative filtering. Audioscrobbler coined the term scrobbling; Last.fm let listeners seed stations, then refine recommendations through likes, skips, and profile changes. Early licensing limited tracks Last.fm did not own to short samples, but collecting-society agreements later enabled online radio. The projects eventually joined, pairing passive listening records with social discovery modeled on browsing a friend’s collection rather than following a broadcast programmer.

### Comment pulse

- Longtime users valued manually browsing highly similar profiles; several said later redesigns removed radio and profile customization they considered central.
- Some preferred knowledgeable human DJs and their stories — counterpoint: algorithmic peer matching exposed listeners to obscure artists at much greater scale.
- Current users described Last.fm as a durable listening log, supported by multiple streaming services, Plex, APIs, and community bots.

### LLM perspective

- View: Last.fm’s lasting contribution is the portable listening history; its social interface showed what that data could enable.
- Impact: Recommendation systems became infrastructure, while user-controlled exploration and profile browsing proved harder to preserve commercially.
- Watch next: Whether interoperable scrobbling APIs can support new discovery tools without recreating a centralized platform.
