# AI and the ironies of automation – Part 2

- Score: 198 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46262816) | Link: https://www.ufried.com/blog/ironies_of_ai_2/

### TL;DR

Applying Lisanne Bainbridge’s 1983 automation analysis to AI agents, the essay argues that human supervision becomes hardest as systems become faster and usually correct. Verbose, confident plans conceal rare failures; experts lose manual skills when they stop practicing; simulators cannot cover unknown faults; and workers must learn to direct agent fleets like managers. Better automation therefore demands more investment in interfaces, general problem-solving, recurrent practice, and leadership training—not simply fewer humans. Commenters found parallels in aviation, reporting systems, and software work.

### Comment pulse

- Rare failures create monitoring fatigue: long stretches of success reduce vigilance exactly when exceptional intervention requires sharp expertise.
- Aviation preserves manual competence through recurrent scenarios — counterpoint: software organizations lack comparable regulation and incentives for costly training.
- Practitioners said agents still require specification and direction, so current work resembles programming a machine more than passively monitoring one.
