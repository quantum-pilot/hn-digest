# A Cozy Mk IV light aircraft crashed after 3D-printed part was weakened by heat

- Score: 207 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46152941) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c1w932vqye0o

### TL;DR

An experimental, homebuilt Cozy Mk IV lost all engine power on final approach after a 3D-printed air-induction elbow softened and collapsed. The pilot avoided a road and bushes but struck airport equipment; the aircraft was destroyed and occupants suffered minor injuries. The part had been bought during a fuel-system modification and used unsuitable plastic. The accident prompted plans to alert inspectors, while discussion stressed that the failure concerned unverified material performance in a safety-critical installation, not additive manufacturing in general.

### LLM perspective

- View: A part matching the required shape is not evidence that its material survives the operating environment.
- Impact: Safety-critical fabrication needs traceability, temperature testing, and installation-specific validation regardless of manufacturing method.
- Watch next: Inspector guidance should distinguish qualified printed components from undocumented parts with unknown thermal properties.
