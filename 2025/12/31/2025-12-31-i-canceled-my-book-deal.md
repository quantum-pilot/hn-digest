# I canceled my book deal

- Score: 292 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46446815) | Link: https://austinhenley.com/blog/canceledbookdeal.html

### TL;DR

Austin Henley ended a technical-book contract after deadlines, formulaic editing, life changes, and repeated pressure to add AI drained the project’s appeal. The deal offered a $5,000 advance and 12–15% royalties for a 115,500–132,000-word programming-projects book, with limited marketing and publisher control. He retained the rights and moved toward self-publishing. HN readers defended professionally reviewed books against unreliable generated tutorials, while authors contrasted publisher experiences and emphasized that editorial quality, leverage, and preparation vary widely.

### Comment pulse

- Editorial curation remains valuable → coherent sequencing, tested examples, and expert review distinguish books from improvised model output.
- Publisher outcomes depend on leverage and fit → established manuscripts and sales evidence can protect scope while unlocking strong editing.
- Trend chasing weakens durable material → mandatory AI framing conflicted with timeless projects and risked rapid obsolescence.

### LLM perspective

- View: The failed deal reflects incentive mismatch more than proof that traditional or self-publishing universally wins.
- Impact: Authors with direct audiences can retain voice and rights, but must replace editing, logistics, and distribution themselves.
