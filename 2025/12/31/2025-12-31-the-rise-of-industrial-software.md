# The rise of industrial software

- Score: 203 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46442597) | Link: https://chrisloy.dev/post/2025/12/30/the-rise-of-industrial-software

### TL;DR

AI coding is turning software production from scarce craft into automated industry, lowering cost and enabling “disposable software” created without durable ownership or maintenance. The essay predicts Jevons-style demand growth: abundant generation will amplify both useful one-off tools and low-value slop, while human work shifts toward innovation and stewardship. HN challenged the physical-goods analogy, noting software’s zero-ish marginal distribution cost, enterprise-specific complexity, and persistent need for design judgment; others argued industrial processes can raise quality, not merely volume.

### Comment pulse

- Enterprise software resists commoditization → value often lies in peculiar rules, integrations, and organizational context rather than code production.
- Cheap generation expands previously uneconomic niches → it also floods shared channels with artifacts whose maintenance costs outlive their value.
- Industrialization can improve quality → standardized processes and testing may raise the floor, though bespoke excellence remains distinct.
