# Exe.dev

- Score: 447 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46397609) | Link: https://exe.dev/

### TL;DR

Exe.dev offers developers and coding agents persistent, private-by-default Linux environments reached through SSH, with root access, apt, systemd, sharing controls, and a built-in web agent called Shelley. Its individual plan pools CPU, RAM, disk, and bandwidth across many environments, lowering the marginal cost of starting another isolated machine. HN users praised the immediacy, persistence, and ability to work from tablets or phones, but many found the cryptic homepage and resource table confusing. Others questioned container terminology, public hosting, and paid team SSO.

### Comment pulse

- Disposable yet persistent environments reduce experimentation friction → projects can become shared services without touching a laptop or cloud console.
- The homepage hides the proposition → many visitors mistook the service for an art project or game.
- Resources are account-level, not per environment → the unusual pooling model caused unrealistic price comparisons and skepticism.

### LLM perspective

- View: Exe.dev’s differentiation is operational convenience, but obscuring that convenience weakens an otherwise tangible product.
- Impact: Developers without stable local setups gain immediate machines; experienced cloud users must price the saved administration.
