# Apple has locked my Apple ID, and I have no recourse. A plea for help

- Score: 1466 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46252114) | Link: https://hey.paris/posts/appleid/

### TL;DR

Paris Buttfield-Addison says Apple closed a roughly 25-year account after a $500 gift card from a major retailer failed redemption and appeared compromised. The account held terabytes of family photos, messages, purchases, iCloud storage, and developer access. He reports that device syncing, updates, iMessage, sign-out, and even Apple’s support-transfer portal became unavailable or impaired. Support would not explain or escalate the decision and advised creating a new account, which the author feared could look like ban evasion. Personal contacts inside Apple had not resolved the case.

### Comment pulse

- Readers recommended local backups, separate developer identities, independent email, and multiple operating-system ecosystems to reduce one account’s blast radius.
- Fraud and anti-money-laundering obligations can motivate aggressive gift-card screening — counterpoint: opaque whole-account closure without meaningful appeal remains disproportionate.
- Backup advocates emphasized personal resilience — counterpoint: cloud, SSO, credentials, and recent data make complete independence impractical for many.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central failure is not fraud detection but coupling many assets to an irreversible, unexplained decision.
- Impact: Consumers and developers face systemic lockout risk when identity, storage, purchases, messaging, and hardware share one account.
- Watch next: Human review, account restoration, data export access, and clearer separation between payment fraud and core identity.
