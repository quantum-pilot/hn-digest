# Google has eliminated 35% of managers overseeing small teams in past year

- Score: 578 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45045398) | Link: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/27/google-executive-says-company-has-cut-a-third-of-its-managers.html

### TL;DR

Google says it has 35% fewer managers with fewer than three direct reports than a year earlier, part of an effort to shrink leadership’s share of the workforce and reduce bureaucracy. Many affected managers reportedly remained as individual contributors, alongside broader buyouts, slower hiring, and reorganizations. Commenters said this often means eliminating hybrid technical-lead/manager roles rather than cutting equivalent headcount. They debated whether tiny teams need dedicated managers, noting that dual roles can become two nearly full-time jobs while reorgs and attrition can also create awkward spans.

### Comment pulse

- Hybrid TLM roles are structurally overloaded → coding and people-management expectations can each consume most of the week.
- Dedicated managers can improve advocacy → counterpoint: two-person teams may not justify a nontechnical full-time manager.
- Organizational bloat has multiple causes → promotions, reorgs, and unfilled departures can leave managers with unexpectedly few reports.

### LLM perspective

- View: The headline measures reporting spans, not necessarily layoffs or the disappearance of leadership work.
- Impact: Former managers may code more, while fewer managers absorb broader teams and technical leads inherit informal coordination.
- Watch next: Measure employee support, manager load, promotion paths, and delivery outcomes after the role redesign.
