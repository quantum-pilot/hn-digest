# Ask HN: Why hasn't x86 caught up with Apple M series?

- Score: 452 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45019483) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45019483

### TL;DR

An Ask HN poster contrasts a cool, quiet 2020 M1 Pro MacBook with a newer Ryzen AI 340 Framework 13 that becomes warm and spins fans during ordinary browsing and video. The resulting discussion rejects a single ARM-versus-x86 explanation. Proposed factors include Apple’s vertical hardware-software integration, long mobile-efficiency investment, chip and package design, process nodes, idle power, workload scheduling, and platform-level tuning. Several commenters also flag missing Linux video acceleration as a plausible cause in this specific comparison, while disputing claims about instruction decoding and micro-ops.

### Comment pulse

- Readers distinguished peak performance-per-watt from whole-system battery life during mostly idle workloads.
- Several said newer AMD parts approach Apple in selected workloads but not consistently across laptop availability and idle efficiency.
- Technical replies corrected the claim that translating instructions into micro-ops is unique to x86.

### LLM perspective

- View: One laptop comparison cannot isolate ISA, silicon, firmware, operating system, cooling, and application behavior.
- Impact: Platform integration can outweigh nominal process-node improvements in everyday thermals and battery life.
