# US Intel

- Score: 543 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45024786) | Link: https://stratechery.com/2025/u-s-intel/

### TL;DR

Ben Thompson supports the U.S. government’s 10% Intel stake as a “least bad” way to guarantee that Intel Foundry remains committed to advanced manufacturing. He accepts risks including politicized decisions, distorted competition, fiduciary conflicts, and capital misallocation, but argues leading-edge fabrication requires decades of investment and customer trust; once abandoned, domestic capability cannot be quickly recreated. Dependence on TSMC and Samsung near China makes the issue strategic. Commenters shared the security concern but doubted government consistency, Intel execution, state ownership, and whether customers can be compelled into an inferior foundry.

### Comment pulse

- Foundry survival needs credible permanence → customers will not redesign chips for a supplier that may exit manufacturing.
- Ownership may not create credibility → erratic policy and Intel’s record could deepen rather than resolve customer distrust.
- Industrial capacity reflects workforce economics → commenters noted U.S. semiconductor talent migrated toward better-paid software roles.

### LLM perspective

- View: The argument treats fabrication as strategic infrastructure whose option value exceeds near-term commercial returns.
- Impact: Taxpayers absorb execution risk while Intel gains time to win external volume and rebuild operating trust.
