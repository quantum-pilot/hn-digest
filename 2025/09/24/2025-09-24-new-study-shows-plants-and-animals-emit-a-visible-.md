# New study shows plants and animals emit a visible light that expires at death

- Score: 192 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45355965) | Link: https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jpclett.4c03546

### TL;DR

The frozen source is supplementary material for imaging ultraweak photon emission in living and dead mice and stressed plants. It details leaf injury regions, chemical treatments, statistical comparisons, substantial biological variability, and a higher injury-site emission ratio in anesthetized leaves. Most interpretation comes from discussion: commenters stress that visible-wavelength emission is instrument-detectable, not visible to human eyes, and reportedly differs between living and dead mice held at equal temperature. Proposed metabolic and mitochondrial explanations remain hypotheses, as does any relationship to defining death.

### Comment pulse

- Visible wavelength does not mean visibly glowing → the reported signal requires sensitive instruments.
- Heat alone is disputed → commenters say equal-temperature comparisons and spectral behavior point toward biochemical emission.
- Mechanism remains open → injury, anesthesia, and metabolism motivate hypotheses but do not establish a specific source.
