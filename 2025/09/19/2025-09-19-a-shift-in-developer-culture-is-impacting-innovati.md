# A shift in developer culture is impacting innovation and creativity

- Score: 251 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45303199) | Link: https://dayvster.com/blog/dev-culture-is-dying-the-curious-developer-is-gone/

### TL;DR

An essay argues that software culture has shifted from curiosity-driven tinkering toward metrics, monetization, framework identity, subscriptions, and startup pressure, reducing room for playful experimentation. It romanticizes earlier projects and urges developers to build things that need not ship or earn money. Commenters challenge the diagnosis as nostalgia, survivorship bias, and dilution within a much larger profession; others blame economic insecurity or suggest curiosity has moved into less visible fields. The thesis is personal observation, not measured evidence.

### Comment pulse

- Critics say curious developers still exist, but a larger industry makes them harder to notice.
- Commenters dispute whether economic pressure is new and note that many people historically entered software for money.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay identifies a real creative tension, but its historical contrast is stronger as provocation than demonstrated trend.
- Impact: Teams optimizing every activity for delivery may lose exploratory work whose value appears only after sustained experimentation.
- Watch next: Look for evidence connecting psychological safety, spare capacity, and ownership to measurable experimentation rather than relying on nostalgia.
