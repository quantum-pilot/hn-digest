# AI was supposed to help juniors shine. Why does it mostly make seniors stronger?

- Score: 373 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45319062) | Link: https://elma.dev/notes/ai-makes-seniors-stronger/

### TL;DR

The essay argues coding assistants currently amplify senior developers more than juniors because experience supplies architecture, security awareness, precise instructions, and the judgment to reject plausible mistakes. AI excels at boilerplate, prototypes, repetitive routines, and testable implementation, but can deepen technical debt when users cannot evaluate its output. Commenters offered examples of juniors following hallucinated detours, while experienced developers described substantial speedups from tightly guided work. They also worried that automating junior tasks removes the practice through which future seniors develop judgment.

### Comment pulse

- Experience catches rabbit holes → seniors recognize unnecessary files, broken abstractions, and hallucinated troubleshooting before they spread.
- AI can accelerate known work → clear architecture and testable boundaries let experts delegate typing without surrendering understanding.
- Training pathways may weaken → tasks seniors happily automate are often the same tasks juniors need for learning.
