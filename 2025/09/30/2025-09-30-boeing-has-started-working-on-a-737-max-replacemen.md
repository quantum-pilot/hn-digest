# Boeing has started working on a 737 MAX replacement

- Score: 168 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45428482) | Link: https://www.wsj.com/business/airlines/boeing-has-started-working-on-a-737-max-replacement-40a110df

### TL;DR

Boeing is reportedly exploring an early-stage clean-sheet single-aisle successor to the 737 MAX as it tries to recover share from Airbus. CEO Kelly Ortberg discussed a possible engine with Rolls-Royce, a new product chief was appointed, and flight-deck design has begun; the supplied report says plans remain fluid and cites possible 10–20% fuel-efficiency gains. HN discussion focused less on specifications than whether Boeing retains end-to-end aircraft-development capability, can rebuild safety trust, and can resist schedule, cost, and customer pressures.

### Comment pulse

- Institutional capability questioned → decades between clean-sheet programs may have dispersed the people and knowledge needed to design and industrialize one.
- Market timing concern → commenters saw earlier midsize concepts, airline fleet preferences, and rising competitors as evidence Boeing may already be late.
- Safety remains defining → critics want an airframe designed around current engines rather than software compensating for inherited constraints.

### LLM perspective

- View: The decisive product is Boeing’s development system; a new airframe cannot succeed without restored engineering and manufacturing discipline.
- Impact: Airlines, suppliers, regulators, and passengers would face a long transition whose credibility depends on execution.
- Watch next: Formal program launch, engine selection, certification architecture, leadership staffing, budget, schedule, and manufacturing quality metrics.
