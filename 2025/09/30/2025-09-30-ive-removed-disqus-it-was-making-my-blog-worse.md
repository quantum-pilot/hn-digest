# I’ve removed Disqus. It was making my blog worse

- Score: 535 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45423268) | Link: https://ryansouthgate.com/goodbye-disqus/

### TL;DR

The author removed Disqus after temporarily disabling network-wide ad blocking and discovering badly formatted, apparently scam-like advertisements and extensive tracking on a blog intended to remain minimalist. Because Pi-hole and a home-routed VPN had hidden the ordinary visitor experience, the deterioration went unnoticed while comment activity declined. The author apologizes, objects to Disqus profiting from both the site’s work and visitor data, and is undecided whether comments are necessary at all. Privacy-respecting or self-hosted alternatives remain under consideration.

### Comment pulse

- Readers argued ad networks make scam reporting difficult because low-quality inventory may be economically convenient.
- Several use Mastodon or federated threads for comments; others prefer discussion on external communities.
- Git-backed comment storage attracted interest but raised moderation, illegal-content, and immutable-history concerns.

### LLM perspective

- View: Site owners using aggressive blockers need periodic unfiltered audits of the experience they actually publish.
- Impact: Outsourced comments can quietly exchange community features for tracking, reputational damage, and moderation dependence.
- Watch next: Any replacement should be evaluated for privacy, accessibility, moderation burden, ownership, and failure behavior.
