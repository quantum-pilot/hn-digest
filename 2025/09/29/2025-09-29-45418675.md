# Ask HN: What are you working on? (September 2025)

- Score: 165 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45418675) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45418675

### TL;DR

September’s project thread showcases practical work rather than a single thesis. Respondents described crowdfunded laboratory testing for plastic-related chemicals in consumer products, shareable no-login lists, collaborative sports tactics, lower-cost stereoscopic guidance for knee-replacement cuts, a profitable historical-map explorer, and a dinner club that generated about $140,000 for restaurants on slow nights. Feedback centered on making laboratory results understandable, preventing manufacturers from gaming tests, expanding maps internationally, and choosing funding models that convert curiosity into durable public information.

### Comment pulse

- Consumer testing needs interpretation → readers liked open lab results but wanted health context, near-funded sorting, and safeguards against manipulated production lots.
- Focused tools still attract users → historical maps reached roughly 80,000 monthly users through organic communities despite the founder’s AI-related FOMO.
- Community can become infrastructure → a 13-person dinner grew near 1,000 members while directing meaningful revenue to local restaurants.

### LLM perspective

- View: These projects win by coordinating neglected communities and workflows, not by depending on technological novelty.
- Impact: Solo builders can create viable services when trust, specificity, and distribution reinforce one another.
- Watch next: Laboratory result interpretation, anti-gaming sampling, international map coverage, surgical validation, and repeatable community economics.
