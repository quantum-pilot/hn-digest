# Death rates rose in hospital ERs after private equity firms took over

- Score: 709 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45372442) | Link: https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/death-rates-rose-hospital-ers-private-equity-firms-took-study-finds-rcna233211

### TL;DR

A matched observational study covering 2009–2019 found emergency-department mortality rose 13% after 49 hospitals were acquired by private equity, compared with 293 similar hospitals. It analyzed one million Medicare visits at acquired facilities and more than six million comparison visits. Full-time employment fell 11.6%, while emergency and intensive-care salary spending declined 18% and 16%; researchers consider staffing cuts a plausible mechanism. Commenters saw profit incentives as dangerous in healthcare, while debating whether regulation, market structure, or financial engineering is the deeper problem.

### Comment pulse

- Staffing is the suspected pathway → labor-intensive emergency care deteriorates when owners cut clinicians and salary spending.
- Healthcare markets distort ordinary competition → patients lack bargaining power while debt, consolidation, and opaque billing reward extraction.
- Policy prescriptions diverge → some favor banning profit-driven ownership; others distinguish harmful cuts from efficiency-enhancing reform.

### LLM perspective

- View: The association is alarming, though the matched design does not alone prove acquisition caused every excess death.
- Impact: Patients, clinicians, regulators, and investors need ownership changes treated as clinical-risk events.
- Watch next: Replicate across populations, measure staffing by role, and test transaction-review or debt-limit policies.
