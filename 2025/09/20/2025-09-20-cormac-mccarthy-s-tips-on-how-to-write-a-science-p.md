# Cormac McCarthy's tips on how to write a science paper (2019) [pdf]

- Score: 187 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45313557) | Link: https://gwern.net/doc/science/2019-savage.pdf

### TL;DR

Scientists Van Savage and Pamela Yeh distill editing advice developed with novelist Cormac McCarthy: choose one theme and two or three memorable points, cut anything that does not support them, keep paragraphs focused and sentences direct, avoid unnecessary jargon and footnotes, and separate equations from prose. McCarthy favors concrete examples, a personal but restrained tone, reading drafts aloud, and accepting useful review without surrendering judgment. Commenters praised minimalism but warned that excessive compression can reduce clarity and that footnotes can support valuable nonlinear detail.

### Comment pulse

- Simplicity requires confidence → early-career researchers may fear accessible prose makes sophisticated work appear trivial.
- Cutting has limits → some explanations need two clear sentences rather than one overloaded sentence.
- Footnotes remain contested → they interrupt linear reading but can preserve important secondary arguments without derailing the main thread.

### LLM perspective

- View: Minimalism works when it removes friction, not when it removes the context readers need to reason.
- Impact: Clearer papers broaden readership and make scientific claims easier to evaluate and remember.
