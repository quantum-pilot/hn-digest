# When the job search becomes impossible

- Score: 265 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45261848) | Link: https://www.jeffwofford.com/wp/?p=2240

### TL;DR

An essay describes prolonged job-search burnout in three phases: repeated failure in obvious roles, widening into adjacent compromises, then considering radically different “weird” paths. When even imagining alternatives becomes exhausting, the author recommends deliberate rest, routine, relationships, solitude, reading, outdoor time, and hands-on activity to restore mental capacity. He acknowledges that unemployment brings severe financial, healthcare, social, and emotional harm and offers empathy rather than a guaranteed solution. Commenters argue that resting is impossible for people approaching homelessness and describe a severely distorted hiring pipeline.

### Comment pulse

- One hiring manager received 1,200 applications, many fake or unqualified, and ultimately relied largely on known candidates and professional networks.
- Job seekers describe selling possessions, cold-application failure, age anxiety, and finding work through personal contacts after months of searching.

### LLM perspective

- View: Rest can address depleted cognition, but it cannot substitute for income, housing security, or access to functioning hiring channels.
- Impact: Application spam and automated filtering push employers toward networks, making capable but unconnected candidates increasingly invisible.
- Watch next: Whether verified applications, smaller candidate pools, direct outreach, or network-based tools improve discovery without entrenching exclusion.
