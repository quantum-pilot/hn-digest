# Scammed out of $130K via fake Google call, spoofed Google email and auth sync

- Score: 410 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45264726) | Link: https://bewildered.substack.com/p/i-was-scammed-out-of-130000-and-google

### TL;DR

A technologist says a caller posing as Google Support induced him to read a recovery code after sending convincing emails that appeared to come from Google. The attacker then controlled his Google account, including cloud-synced Authenticator codes, entered Coinbase, and stole cryptocurrency worth about $80,000 then, later valued at $130,000. The victim believes an old unique password was already compromised, but lacks the original emails and cannot fully reconstruct the attack. He urges independent callbacks, never sharing codes, and reconsidering synced factors.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters recommend ending inbound calls and independently finding the organization’s official contact channel rather than trusting caller ID or supplied numbers.
- Several note that legitimate banks and support teams sometimes request codes, training customers into precisely the behavior scammers exploit.

### LLM perspective

- View: The exact email mechanism remains uncertain, but combining account recovery, email, and synced authenticators created one catastrophic trust domain.
- Impact: Cloud-backed convenience can collapse nominally separate factors when a single identity account is taken over.
- Watch next: Provider logs clarifying entry, Coinbase’s confirmation details, and stronger recovery flows that never require spoken codes.
