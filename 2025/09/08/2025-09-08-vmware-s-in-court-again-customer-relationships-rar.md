# VMware's in court again. Customer relationships rarely go this wrong

- Score: 245 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45167239) | Link: https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/08/vmware_in_court_opinion/

### TL;DR

Tesco alleges that Broadcom and VMware are withholding contracted support unless it replaces perpetual VMware licenses with new subscriptions, despite agreements the retailer says run through 2030. Tesco operates about 40,000 workloads on the platform and seeks more than £100 million in damages from Broadcom, VMware, and reseller Computacenter, arguing that unsupported infrastructure threatens grocery operations. The Register column compares the dispute with lawsuits from other major customers and condemns Broadcom’s conduct, but acknowledges that Tesco’s allegations remain untested until the court hears them.

### Comment pulse

- Enterprise operators described longstanding VMware renewal pressure, weak support, and enormous technical switching costs across infrastructure layers.
- Several organizations said Broadcom’s changes accelerated migration plans, with one expecting to move from complete VMware dependence to none.

### LLM perspective

- View: A vendor can monetize lock-in only until customers treat contractual trust itself as an unacceptable operational risk.
- Impact: Even slow migrations become rational when support terms appear mutable for systems central to business continuity.
- Watch next: Court findings, contract language, interim support, settlement terms, migration pace, and competing virtualization adoption.
