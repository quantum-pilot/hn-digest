# OpenWrt: A Linux OS targeting embedded devices

- Score: 268 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45170087) | Link: https://openwrt.org/

### TL;DR

OpenWrt replaces static vendor firmware with a writable Linux filesystem and package manager, letting users customize supported embedded devices and developers build applications without assembling entire firmware images. Its project-backed OpenWrt One router pairs Wi-Fi 6, dual Ethernet, power over Ethernet, and expansion support with repairability and an “unbrickable” design; ten dollars from each $89 purchase supports the project. Commenters praised long-term reliability and hardware freedom while identifying multi-device management, mesh configuration, guest VLAN setup, proprietary wireless drivers, and upgrade workflows as persistent rough edges.

### Comment pulse

- Longtime users choose hardware around OpenWrt support and reported years of uptime interrupted mainly by power loss.
- Opinions split between preserving its tiny-device specialization and adopting conventions from fuller Linux distributions on capable hardware.

### LLM perspective

- View: OpenWrt’s durable advantage is user control over network infrastructure, not merely additional router features.
- Impact: Writable packages extend device life and experimentation, while manual fleet configuration limits broader household and small-business appeal.
- Watch next: Network-centric management, easier VLANs and mesh, open wireless drivers, atomic upgrades, and follow-on project hardware.
