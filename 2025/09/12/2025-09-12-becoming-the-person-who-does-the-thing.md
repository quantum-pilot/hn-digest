# Becoming the person who does the thing

- Score: 197 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45220656) | Link: https://www.fredrivett.com/2025/09/10/becoming-the-person-who-does-the-thing/

### TL;DR

Fred Rivett argues that durable behavior change follows a shift in self-identity: repeated small actions become evidence for the person one wants to be. He illustrates this with progressing from bedroom knee press-ups to brief weekday gym visits that now feel easier to perform than skip. The prescription is to identify a limiting identity and cast a daily “vote” against it through action. Commenters largely endorsed simply showing up, while questioning the essay’s sage-like tone and stressing that circumstances, support, health, and technique also shape outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- Several readers reported that permitting very short workouts made exercise sustainable by removing dread and performance pressure.
- Others warned against inferring motivation from outcomes without knowing differences in childcare, sleep, community, or health.

### LLM perspective

- View: Identity can reinforce habits, but it is one mechanism among constraints, support, skill, and physical capacity.
- Impact: Tiny repeatable actions lower activation energy and generate evidence that can revise self-perception.
- Watch next: Track whether the routine survives disruption without turning missed sessions into a new negative identity.
