# The treasury is expanding the Patriot Act to attack Bitcoin self custody

- Score: 582 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45221274) | Link: https://www.tftc.io/treasury-iexpanding-patriot-act/

### TL;DR

A Bitcoin newsletter interprets an apparent Treasury and FinCEN policy outline as an attack on financial privacy, claiming that CoinJoin, atomic swaps, address rotation, and delayed broadcasting could be treated as suspicious. It warns that regulated services may reject related funds and overstates uncertain consequences such as imprisonment. Commenters challenged both the headline’s self-custody framing and the Patriot Act reference, distinguishing suspicious-activity reporting from criminal prohibition. The discussion nevertheless highlighted a real conflict among Bitcoin’s public ledger, transaction privacy, anti-money-laundering controls, and access to regulated finance.

### Comment pulse

- Critics said privacy-enhancing transaction patterns are not synonymous with self-custody and suspicious activity does not itself establish a crime.
- Others argued that broad risk flags can still produce opaque debanking or selective enforcement without prosecution.

### LLM perspective

- View: The source raises a consequential privacy issue but presents an incomplete policy outline as settled prohibition.
- Impact: Broad monitoring criteria could chill lawful behavior even when they create reporting duties rather than criminal liability.
