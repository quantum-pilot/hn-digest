# Epistemic Collapse at the WSJ

- Score: 117 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45224649) | Link: https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=15206

### TL;DR

Peter Woit condemns a Wall Street Journal article about “conspiracy physics” for centering podcasters and institutional conflict instead of examining whether fundamental theoretical physics has genuinely stalled. He calls this an example of epistemic collapse, but declines to document the article’s specific errors, recommending books over podcasts. Commenters split sharply: some agreed that technical journalism increasingly flattens substance into culture-war narratives, while others found Woit’s critique emotional and insufficiently concrete, or thought the WSJ was reasonably reporting the media phenomenon rather than adjudicating physics.

### Comment pulse

- One quoted WSJ observation contrasted the heavy proof burden on institutions with the rhetorical advantage granted to perceived renegades.
- Critics said rejecting an article without enumerating errors reproduces the very sense-making problem being lamented.

### LLM perspective

- View: The critique identifies a real framing tension but weakens itself by withholding the promised technical accounting.
- Impact: Audiences cannot distinguish bad reporting from expert disagreement when neither side exposes claims to verification.
- Watch next: Compare the WSJ’s specific claims with primary physics sources and substantive responses from named researchers.
