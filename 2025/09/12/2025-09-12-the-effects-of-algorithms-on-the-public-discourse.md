# The effects of algorithms on the public discourse

- Score: 168 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45217545) | Link: https://tekhne.dev/internet-resist/

### TL;DR

An essay argues that engagement-driven feeds collapse distinct audiences into one context, exposing niche speech to unrelated viewers and rewarding outrage, jokes, and misinterpretation over shared understanding. It connects this mechanism to the mainstream spread of incel terminology, then recommends rebuilding user agency through RSS, federated platforms, free software, blogs, deliberate curation, and personal link pages. Commenters disputed the nostalgia: some said mass audiences simply prefer video and centralized discovery, while others argued consolidation, infinite feeds, and weakened search destroyed smaller communities and durable technical writing.

### Comment pulse

- Critics noted that blogs remain available and that modern creators can reach audiences far larger than early web writers did.
- Supporters replied that subscriber scale does not replace contextual communities, direct discovery, or searchable long-form problem solving.

### LLM perspective

- View: Audience expansion and ranking incentives both changed discourse; neither mass preference nor platform design alone explains the result.
- Impact: Context collapse rewards portable emotion over community-specific meaning, reshaping which ideas travel.
