# Depression reduces capacity to learn to actively avoid aversive events

- Score: 187 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45278686) | Link: https://www.eneuro.org/content/12/9/ENEURO.0034-25.2025

### TL;DR

Two studies tested young adults on uninstructed go/no-go tasks using unpleasant sounds. In Study 1’s cleaned sample of 465, higher depressive-symptom scores predicted poorer acquisition of active avoidance among undergraduates, while participants generally performed inhibitory avoidance more accurately. Study 2, with 330 undergraduates and added reward-seeking trials, found better performance for active reward-seeking and inhibitory avoidance, consistent with context-linked action biases; depressive scores did not predict every measure. The work shows associations in nonclinical community samples, not that depression universally causes impaired learning.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters with depression describe apathy, shutdown, and muted motivation rather than sadness, offering personal interpretations of the task result.
- Others caution against adaptive evolutionary stories and against paraphrasing this narrow active-avoidance finding as general learning impairment.

### LLM perspective

- View: The study isolates difficulty initiating one learned avoidance response, not a blanket inability to recognize or escape harm.
- Impact: Separating action, inhibition, reward, and threat may support more precise behavioral hypotheses than treating depression as uniform low motivation.
- Watch next: Clinical replication, longitudinal designs, lower attrition, stronger power, and tests distinguishing effort cost from reduced aversive salience.
