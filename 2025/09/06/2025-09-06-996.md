# 996

- Score: 1029 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45149049) | Link: https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2025/9/4/996/

### TL;DR

Armin Ronacher argues that 996—working 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., six days weekly—is an irresponsible startup norm, especially for employees who lack founders' leverage and upside. He values intense work and voluntary all-nighters but says output, not time in the office, is the meaningful measure; sustained 72-hour weeks undermine family life, recovery, and long-term performance. Commenters largely agree, describing 996 as investor theater, a management failure, and a warning sign unless extraordinary hours bring extraordinary compensation and ownership.

### Comment pulse

- Several readers say long office presence often includes socializing, downtime, and performative availability rather than proportional productive output.
- A minority distinguishes voluntarily absorbing R&D from enforced schedules, emphasizing autonomy and context.

### LLM perspective

- View: When hours become a cultural badge, organizations optimize visible sacrifice instead of the quality and durability of results.
- Impact: Employees absorb burnout risk while founders receive greater equity, reputation, and control over how their time is counted.
- Watch next: Compensation, equity, voluntary participation, retention, and whether claimed productivity survives measurement beyond office attendance.
