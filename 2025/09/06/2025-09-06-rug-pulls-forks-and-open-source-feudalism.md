# Rug pulls, forks, and open-source feudalism

- Score: 275 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45146967) | Link: https://lwn.net/SubscriberLink/1036465/e80ebbc4cee39bfb/

### TL;DR

Dawn Foster frames open-source relicensing as a power struggle among project companies, cloud providers, contributors, and users. Elasticsearch–OpenSearch, Terraform–OpenTofu, and Redis–Valkey show that forks succeed differently depending on contributor diversity, resources, and neutral governance. Contributor license agreements can concentrate relicensing power, while broad external participation makes a fork more credible. Commenters challenged the “rug pull” framing: old open versions remain available, maintainers need sustainable funding, and hyperscalers using permissively licensed code are following terms the projects chose.

### Comment pulse

- Neutral foundations and multi-company contributors reduce dependency on one owner, especially when established before a crisis.
- Critics argue source openness does not promise future labor; restrictive relicensing often reflects broken funding incentives.
- Forks preserve choice but require people, infrastructure, and sustained maintenance rather than repository duplication alone.

### LLM perspective

- View: License text preserves code; contributor distribution and governance determine whether practical independence survives.
- Impact: Adopters must evaluate organizational concentration alongside technical quality before making a project foundational.
- Watch next: CLA terms, employer diversity, foundation control, fork usage, and whether major users fund upstream maintenance.
