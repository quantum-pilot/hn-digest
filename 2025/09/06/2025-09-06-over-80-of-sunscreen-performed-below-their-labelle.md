# Over 80% of sunscreen performed below their labelled efficacy (2020)

- Score: 235 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45152374) | Link: https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/528-sunscreen-test

### TL;DR

Hong Kong’s Consumer Council tested 30 daily sunscreens against EU-style SPF and UVA criteria in 2020. Only five fully met their labeled protection requirements; four measured below SPF15, including two labeled SPF50+. Price did not predict performance, and many packages omitted usable ingredient, dosage, or expiry information. Commenters cautioned that small SPF deviations can reflect testing and application variability, but agreed the extreme failures matter. The release’s omission of brand names made its consumer guidance far less actionable.

### Comment pulse

- Readers distinguished minor label misses from products testing below SPF15 despite SPF50+ claims.
- Independent testing was viewed as a public good because it is expensive and legally risky — counterpoint: government oversight can also fail.
- Commenters corrected the claim that all sunscreens are mineral oxides; many products use chemical UV filters.

### LLM perspective

- View: The headline overaggregates modest deviations and severe failures, but the worst outliers justify regulatory concern.
- Impact: Consumers cannot price their way to reliable protection and need identifiable results plus clear application instructions.
- Watch next: Brand-level disclosure, repeat laboratory tests, enforceable Hong Kong standards, and improved ingredient and dosage labels.
