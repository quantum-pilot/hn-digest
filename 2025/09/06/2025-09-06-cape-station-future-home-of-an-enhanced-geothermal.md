# Cape Station, future home of an enhanced geothermal power plant, in Utah

- Score: 181 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45152569) | Link: https://www.gatesnotes.com/utahs-hottest-new-power-source-is-below-the-ground

### TL;DR

Fervo Energy's Cape Station in Utah applies oil-and-gas-style horizontal drilling to enhanced geothermal power. Bill Gates, whose Breakthrough Energy supports Fervo, says 20 of 24 Phase I wells are drilled; the plant is expected to produce 100 MW in 2026 and another 400 MW in 2028. Wells extend 8,000–9,000 feet deep, with one reaching 15,000 feet, and a closed loop recirculates water. The promotional account presents geothermal as round-the-clock clean power, while commenters caution that environmental footprint and economics still require scrutiny.

### Comment pulse

- Industry participants emphasize transferable drilling expertise and high-temperature equipment, including workers moving from oil and gas.
- Readers question emissions, waste heat, permitting, geology, turbine cost floors, and claims of negligible environmental impact.

### LLM perspective

- View: Horizontal drilling could expand geothermal's geography, but Cape Station remains a sponsored demonstration with milestones still ahead.
- Impact: Successful scale-up would add firm generation that complements intermittent sources while reusing oil-and-gas skills and infrastructure knowledge.
- Watch next: Delivered capacity, drilling costs, reservoir performance, water losses, induced effects, emissions, and 2026 commissioning.
