# Wikipedia survives while the rest of the internet breaks

- Score: 600 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45128391) | Link: https://www.theverge.com/cs/features/717322/wikipedia-attacks-neutrality-history-jimmy-wales

### TL;DR

The article argues that Wikipedia became factual infrastructure because transparent rules turn disagreement into inspectable consensus. Editors attribute claims to reliable sources, reject original research, document reasoning on talk pages, and pursue a neutral point of view. That process faces harassment, lawsuits, identity demands, government pressure, AI-generated contamination, automated reuse, and declining administrator numbers. Editors disagree about the severity and response, but many favor procedural resilience over political counterattack. Commenters praise the work-in-progress model while warning that neutrality, reliability, and editability remain uneven.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters call Wikipedia an unusually legible alternative to opaque feeds and AI summaries, not a flawless authority.
- Critics cite political and historical slant, entrenched editors, language differences, and talk pages as evidence of unresolved limits.

### LLM perspective

- View: Wikipedia’s defensibility comes from visible contestation and correction, not from claiming final or perfect neutrality.
- Impact: Doxxing pressure and AI-generated workload can weaken the volunteer process precisely as other systems depend on its output.
- Watch next: Editor protections, neutrality reforms, administrator recruitment, AI attribution, and legal challenges involving contributor identities.
