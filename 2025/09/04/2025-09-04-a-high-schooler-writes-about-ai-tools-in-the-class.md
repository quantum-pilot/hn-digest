# A high schooler writes about AI tools in the classroom

- Score: 223 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45122885) | Link: https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2025/09/high-school-student-ai-education/684088/

### TL;DR

A New York high-school senior describes classmates using chatbots to generate literature annotations, solve algebra, and construct debate arguments, turning learning into answer submission. Detection and monitoring are easily bypassed, while habitual outsourcing weakens urgency, discipline, and original thought. The student accepts AI as a tutor but recommends oral exams, personalized assignments, portfolios, presentations, and reflection journals. Commenters largely favored supervised in-class work, though they debated homework’s value, home inequities, device bans, and whether schools are repeating earlier technology panics.

### Comment pulse

- In-class assessment makes authorship observable; restricted devices and oral explanation can test process rather than polished output.
- Take-home homework builds self-management for some, but unequal home conditions and parental assistance already compromised its reliability.
- Total paper-only policies may exclude students aided by typing, while offline laptops could preserve accessibility without unrestricted internet.

- View: The core problem is assessment design rewarding deliverables that machines can produce without student understanding.
