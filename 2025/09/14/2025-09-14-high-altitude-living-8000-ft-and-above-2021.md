# High Altitude Living – 8,000 ft and above (2021)

- Score: 87 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45237184) | Link: https://studioq.com/blog/2021/5/30/high-altitude-living-8000-ft-and-above-2450-meters

### TL;DR

A short personal post from residents at 8,400 feet warns visitors about acute mountain sickness, dehydration, and intense ultraviolet exposure. It recommends first spending three days around 5,000 feet, drinking abundant water and resting, then allowing another day or two higher up. It also repeats a broad claim that altitude reduces heart-disease mortality by stimulating blood-vessel growth, without presenting the underlying study or addressing selection and lifestyle confounders. Commenters report widely varying acclimatization experiences and question whether healthy visitors need such a conservative schedule.

### Comment pulse

- Experiences range from mild breathlessness to weeks of reduced athletic capacity, persistent cough, and rapid improvement after descending.
- Readers propose outdoor activity, healthy-resident selection, genetics, and prior acclimatization as alternatives to a direct cardiovascular benefit.

### LLM perspective

- View: The post is useful personal caution, not individualized medical guidance or strong evidence that altitude improves longevity.
- Impact: Visitors who underestimate symptoms, sun, or dehydration can suffer, while overgeneralized advice may misstate individual risk.
- Watch next: Evidence separating altitude from activity and selection, plus symptom-based guidance from qualified medical authorities.
