# If my kids excel, will they move away?

- Score: 229 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45236411) | Link: https://jeffreybigham.com/blog/2025/where-will-my-kids-go.html

### TL;DR

A Carnegie Mellon professor connects US immigration and university policy to a personal fear: if international students stop coming and research excellence migrates abroad, his own children may eventually need to leave the country to pursue top opportunities. He contrasts that possibility with his move from rural Ohio to nearby Pittsburgh, which preserved family ties while reaching a leading computer-science center. The essay argues that fear around visas, detention, protest, and political pressure on universities can trigger self-reinforcing talent shifts whose effects reach American families.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters report talented students choosing European universities and other countries offering incentives for diaspora researchers to return.
- Others caution that political administrations are temporary, elite institutions durable, and excellence-driven migration already separates many international families.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay reframes talent attraction as local family infrastructure, though its forecast of institutional migration remains speculative.
- Impact: Small changes in perceived safety and predictability can compound because researchers choose places with strong existing peers.
- Watch next: International applications, visa issuance, faculty moves, graduate retention, research funding, and competing countries’ recruitment programs.
