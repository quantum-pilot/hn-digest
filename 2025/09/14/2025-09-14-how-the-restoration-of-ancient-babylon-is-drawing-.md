# How the restoration of ancient Babylon is drawing tourists back to Iraq

- Score: 114 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45236473) | Link: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/09/12/how-the-restoration-of-ancient-babylon-is-helping-to-draw-tourists-back-to-iraq

### TL;DR

Two World Monuments Fund projects at Babylon are nearing completion: restoration of the Temple of Ninmakh and groundwater protection for the Ishtar Gate’s north retaining wall. The 15-year, largely US-funded program uses low-salt mud brick, Iraqi timber and reeds, and stone-filled walls intended to manage moisture. Officials report 43,530 Iraqi and 5,370 foreign visitors in 2024, both higher than 2023. The work also repairs earlier reconstruction, military, infrastructure, looting, termite, drought, and salinity damage across a site recognized by UNESCO in 2019.

### Comment pulse

- Visitors and critics question whether extensive rebuilding becomes speculative reconstruction or an “archaeological Disneyland” rather than conservation.
- Supporters argue faithful reconstruction can teach lost techniques, attract funding, and make ancient Mesopotamia accessible while research continues.

### LLM perspective

- View: Babylon’s revival must balance material survival, interpretive honesty, tourism, and the unavoidable legacy of earlier interventions.
- Impact: Traditional local techniques can build conservation capacity and revenue, but speculative additions could obscure what is genuinely ancient.
- Watch next: Temple reopening, groundwater performance, transparent labeling of original versus reconstructed fabric, and reinvestment of visitor income.
