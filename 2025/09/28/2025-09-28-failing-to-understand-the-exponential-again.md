# Failing to Understand the Exponential, Again

- Score: 115 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45403803) | Link: https://www.julian.ac/blog/2025/09/27/failing-to-understand-the-exponential-again/

### TL;DR

The author argues that skepticism about rapid AI progress repeats early pandemic failures to extrapolate exponential trends. Citing METR's software-task horizon and OpenAI's multi-occupation GDPval evaluation, he says newer models remain on or above trend and predicts day-long autonomous work by mid-2026, broad expert parity before 2027, and frequent expert outperformance by 2027's end. Commenters strongly challenge straight-line extrapolation: technologies follow S-curves, benchmarks may not represent deployment, and data, cost, architecture, or context limitations could slow gains.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters note repeated premature plateau claims and accelerating laboratory investment—counterpoint: incentives and visibility make lab signals imperfect.
- Critics argue every physical exponential eventually saturates, while the inflection point is unknowable until slowing appears.
