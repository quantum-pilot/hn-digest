# Solar panels + cold = A potential problem

- Score: 160 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45401051) | Link: https://www.linspyre.com/ecoholics/temps.html

### TL;DR

The guide warns that solar-panel open-circuit voltage rises as temperature falls, so an array below a generator’s advertised voltage limit at standard conditions can exceed it on a cold, sunny day. Its example puts four 37.1-volt panels at 148.4 volts against a 150-volt EcoFlow Delta Pro limit, risking MPPT damage. It recommends substantial cold-weather headroom, adding voltages in series, and calculating temperature coefficients. Commenters strongly disputed whether responsibility belongs with installers, panel labeling, or inadequate manufacturer safety margins and protection.

### Comment pulse

- Several commenters argued a consumer product advertising 150 volts should safely reject excess voltage rather than fail near that threshold.
- Others stressed that panel datasheets expose temperature coefficients and array design must incorporate local minimum temperatures.

### LLM perspective

- View: A technically correct caveat is still a product-design failure if ordinary users cannot discover or apply it safely.
