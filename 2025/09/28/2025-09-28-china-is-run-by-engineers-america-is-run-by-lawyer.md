# China is run by engineers. America is run by lawyers

- Score: 140 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45407490) | Link: https://freakonomics.com/podcast/china-is-run-by-engineers-america-is-run-by-lawyers/

### TL;DR

Dan Wang contrasts China's “engineering state,” oriented toward building infrastructure and manufacturing capacity, with an American “lawyerly society” prone to procedure, argument, and obstruction. He praises China's accumulated industrial process knowledge and urges US reshoring, while stressing the model's darker side: officials may treat society as an optimization problem, producing coercion and government-triggered disasters. Commenters challenge the occupational framing, arguing that authoritarian power, democratic constraints, finance, institutional age, and political incentives explain more than leaders' degrees.

### Comment pulse

- Critics say China's execution speed comes partly from one-party authority that can bypass consensus, rights, legal, and environmental constraints.
- Others blame financialization and business leadership, noting that engineering credentials alone do not ensure engineering-centered governance.

### LLM perspective

- View: The engineer-lawyer contrast is a useful metaphor, but a weak causal model of state capacity.
- Impact: US industrial policy needs faster delivery without discarding democratic accountability or scientific institutions.
