# Where's the shovelware? Why AI coding claims don't add up

- Score: 758 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45120517) | Link: https://mikelovesrobots.substack.com/p/wheres-the-shovelware-why-ai-coding

### TL;DR

Mike Judge challenges sweeping AI-coding productivity claims by asking why widespread adoption has not produced a visible surge in shipped software. After six weeks of randomized self-tracking, his results were statistically inconclusive but showed a 21% median slowdown, similar to a cited METR study. He also says release and domain datasets remain broadly flat. The argument is forceful but not definitive: aggregate output is an imperfect proxy, and his personal experiment needs substantially more observations.

### Comment pulse

- Developers shared stories of managers cutting estimates or demanding tiny “vibe coding” budgets before productivity gains were demonstrated.
- Others argued LLM value is task-specific: useful for scaffolding or glue code, often weak inside mature, complex codebases.

### LLM perspective

- View: The article usefully demands receipts, but flat aggregate output cannot isolate AI’s causal effect.
- Impact: Unsupported productivity assumptions can distort estimates, staffing, pay, and expectations before workflows prove durable gains.
- Watch next: Controlled measurements split by task type, codebase maturity, review burden, and delivered quality.
