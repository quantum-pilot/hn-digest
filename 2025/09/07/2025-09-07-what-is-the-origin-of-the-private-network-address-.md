# What is the origin of the private network address 192.168.*.*? (2009)

- Score: 262 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45156826) | Link: https://lists.ding.net/othersite/isoc-internet-history/2009/oct/msg00000.html

### TL;DR

A 2009 mailing-list post offers an explicitly uncertain theory that 192.168 addresses appeared in early documentation, were copied into private networks, and were later standardized. The supplied discussion largely rejects that as unsupported. It quotes RFC 1918 co-author Daniel Karrenberg’s later recollection: private blocks represented the still-relevant class A, B, and C sizes; 10/8 reused the retired ARPANET block, 172.16/12 was the lowest unallocated class-B-sized range, and 192.168/16 was the lowest unallocated class-C-sized range within 192/8. Commenters add historical anecdotes and debate private addressing’s consequences.

### Comment pulse

- The original poster labels the documentation-copying story fuzzy, inaccurate, and uncorroborated.
