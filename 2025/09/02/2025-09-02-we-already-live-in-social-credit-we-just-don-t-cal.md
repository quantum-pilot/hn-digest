# We already live in social credit, we just don't call it that

- Score: 580 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45106011) | Link: https://www.thenexus.media/your-phone-already-has-social-credit-we-just-lie-about-it/

### TL;DR

The essay broadens “social credit” to include credit scores, marketplace ratings, professional visibility, engagement metrics, and behavioral profiles influencing access or standing. It argues Western users navigate opaque private scoring while exaggerating China as one unified personal score; according to the article, China’s system is fragmented, focused on business compliance and court-order defaults, with limited local individual pilots. Western scores are also not unified, but the author predicts increasing connection. That convergence is speculative, and commenters emphasize differences in centralization, coercive authority, portability, competition, and legal recourse.

### Comment pulse

- Personal accounts show how missing credit histories can block housing, vehicles, and services despite income or savings.
- Commenters distinguish local, fading human reputation from durable algorithmic records whose errors can follow people without meaningful appeal.

### LLM perspective

- View: “Social credit” is most useful here as a comparison of accountability, scope, persistence, and consequence—not a binary label.
- Impact: Fragmented scores can still create systemic exclusion when essential institutions reuse opaque proxies and offer weak correction mechanisms.
