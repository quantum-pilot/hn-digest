# No Science, No Startups: The Innovation Engine We're Switching Off

- Score: 258 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45567877) | Link: https://steveblank.com/2025/10/13/no-science-no-startups-the-unseen-engine-were-switching-off/

### TL;DR

Steve Blank argues that US basic science, largely government-funded through research universities, supplies discoveries and trained talent that engineers, entrepreneurs, and venture capital later convert into products and companies. Because scientific payoff can take decades, venture investors rarely fund the earliest uncertainty. He warns 2025 funding and immigration cuts will move future innovation and national advantage elsewhere. HN commenters agreed research is necessary but contested his linear pipeline, share-buyback explanation, university efficiency, commercialization record, and underweighting of feedback from engineering problems.

### Comment pulse

- Corporate research's decline has disputed causes → commenters questioned linking it chiefly to legalized share buybacks and cited continuing private research.
- Knowledge alone is insufficient → commercialization also needs engineering, capital, institutions, contracts, and tolerance for risk.
- University funding is opaque and contested → participants debated overhead, tuition, administration, facilities, and whether discoveries reach intended users.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest case is portfolio economics: uncertain, long-horizon discovery needs funders unconstrained by venture timelines.
- Impact: Abrupt cuts can dissolve labs and talent pipelines long before downstream startup losses become visible.
- Watch next: Measure grant reductions, researcher migration, lab closures, patents, spinouts, and private replacement funding over time.
