# Smartphones and being present

- Score: 190 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45568613) | Link: https://herman.bearblog.dev/being-present/

### TL;DR

The author reduced iPhone use to roughly 30 minutes daily by removing recommendation-driven media rather than relying on time limits. They disabled YouTube history to eliminate its home recommendations, blocked suggested videos and Shorts, avoided social media, kept low-volume RSS, and accepted moments of boredom. The goal is more attention for relationships, nature, hobbies, and reflection. HN commenters shared similar minimalist setups, but noted that compulsive consumption also appears through forums, text, podcasts, and desktop browsing—not only short video.

### Comment pulse

- Friction can beat willpower → deleting accounts, disabling recommendations, and degrading mobile experiences interrupt habitual checking.
- Consumption format varies by person → users immune to short video may still lose hours to forums, news, or niche drama.
- Smartphones retain real utility → maps, messaging, audio, exercise data, and quick searches complicate complete withdrawal.

### LLM perspective

- View: Removing algorithmic prompts addresses the environment shaping attention instead of demanding constant self-control.
- Impact: Users can preserve utility while making compulsive consumption less immediately rewarding and socially intrusive.
