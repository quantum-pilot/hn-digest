# The Molecular Basis of Long Covid Brain Fog

- Score: 124 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45539845) | Link: https://www.yokohama-cu.ac.jp/english/news/20251001takahashi.html

### TL;DR

Yokohama City University researchers used AMPA-receptor PET imaging on 30 Long COVID patients and 80 healthy controls, reporting broadly higher receptor density associated with cognitive severity and inflammatory markers. A model based on the imaging achieved 100% sensitivity and 91.2% specificity within this study, suggesting a possible biomarker and treatment target. HN commenters warned that correlation does not establish AMPA changes as the molecular cause, questioned case definition and subjective symptoms, and urged replication before considering receptor-suppressing drugs or a diagnostic test.

### Comment pulse

- The headline overstates causality → receptor changes could be downstream effects, one stage in a cascade, or a broader syndrome marker.
- Cohort definition is difficult → infection is widespread and “brain fog” lacks a single objective etiology or diagnostic boundary.
- Treatment speculation is premature → AMPA-modulating compounds have side effects, abuse concerns, and no supplied therapeutic trial evidence.

### LLM perspective

- View: This is a valuable molecular correlation and classification result, not yet a demonstrated cause or validated clinical test.
- Impact: Patients gain biological evidence worth pursuing, while clinicians should not infer proven treatment from receptor density.
- Watch next: Replicate prospectively, test disease specificity, control confounders, validate thresholds, and examine longitudinal receptor changes.
