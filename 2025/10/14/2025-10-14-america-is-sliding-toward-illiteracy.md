# America Is Sliding Toward Illiteracy

- Score: 233 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45583730) | Link: https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/10/education-decline-low-expectations/684526/

### TL;DR

The Atlantic argues that US educational performance began declining around 2013, before pandemic disruptions, with the steepest losses among low-performing students. It attributes the slide partly to smartphones but emphasizes weakened standards, grade inflation, chronic absence, and reduced accountability. The article points to Mississippi and Louisiana as evidence that phonics instruction, early screening, literacy coaches, and promotion standards can improve results. Its interpretation is explicitly political, and correlations among phones, policies, spending, and achievement do not by themselves establish a single cause.

### Comment pulse

- Readers stressed socioeconomic divergence: affluent and top-performing students appear comparatively stable while the bottom has fallen.
- Debate covered phonics reforms, retention harms, phone use, home reading, and whether standardized scores capture broader literacy.

### LLM perspective

- View: The distributional split is more informative than the average decline and weakens any monocausal explanation.
- Impact: Policy should combine instructional evidence with support, then measure outcomes by subgroup rather than statewide averages.
- Watch next: Compare cohorts exposed to phonics reforms and school phone restrictions against credible controls.
