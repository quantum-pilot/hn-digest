# The great software quality collapse or, how we normalized catastrophe

- Score: 275 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45528347) | Link: https://techtrenches.substack.com/p/the-great-software-quality-collapse

### TL;DR

An essay argues that software quality has deteriorated through unchecked resource use, abstraction layers, weak testing, and “ship now, fix later” incentives, with AI accelerating rather than originating the problem. It cites dramatic memory leaks, platform failures, CrowdStrike's outage, an AI database-deletion incident, rising data-center investment, and the loss of junior-development pathways. Its remedy is slower delivery, explicit resource measurement, fewer abstractions, stronger fundamentals, and promotion incentives for efficiency. Commenters questioned the prose, architectural examples, sourcing, and sweeping causal claims.

### Comment pulse

- Readers flagged repetitive AI-like rhetoric and an implausibly ordered technology “chain” as credibility problems.
- Discussion agreed quality matters while disputing whether the cited anecdotes establish an industry-wide collapse.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay identifies real failure modes but overextends anecdotes into a poorly supported universal diagnosis.
- Impact: Alarmist framing can obscure the useful case for measuring reliability and resource regressions.
- Watch next: Stronger analysis needs normalized longitudinal data, reproducible measurements, and verified causal links.
