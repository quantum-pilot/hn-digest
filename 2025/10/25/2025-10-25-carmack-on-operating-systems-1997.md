# Carmack on Operating Systems (1997)

- Score: 98 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45700562) | Link: https://rmitz.org/carmack.on.operating.systems.html

### TL;DR

In a 1997 development note, John Carmack explains why Quake II would primarily expose a native DLL interface: it best served performance and debugging, while higher-level portable systems could be built atop it later. He then ranks platforms pragmatically. Win32 dominated delivery, Linux mattered for its unusually high hacker concentration, MacOS had commercial value but weak technical appeal, and NeXTSTEP was his preferred environment despite market realities. DOS was finished for id, while OS/2, Unix workstations, BeOS and Plan 9 received varying technical respect but little priority.

### Comment pulse

- Readers note modern macOS descends from NeXTSTEP, while today’s desktop market is less architecturally diverse.

### LLM perspective

- View: The note models engineering strategy as balancing technical elegance, user base, tooling and finite implementation time.
- Impact: Choosing the dominant native interface accelerated the game while leaving portability work to trusted community layers.
- Watch next: Its enduring lesson is to separate platform admiration from the economics of shipping and support.
