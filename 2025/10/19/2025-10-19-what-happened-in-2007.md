# What Happened in 2007?

- Score: 112 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45633426) | Link: https://whathappenedin2007.com/

### TL;DR

This work-in-progress site assembles charts on cognition, test scores, sleep, youth mental health, loneliness, productivity, and pedestrian deaths, hypothesizing that smartphones and constant mobile connectivity contributed to adverse trends after 2007. The author explicitly rejects a monocausal claim and acknowledges the argument is currently weak. Commenters nevertheless challenged short or US-centric timelines, pandemic confounding, cherry-picked breakpoints, and failure to weigh smartphones’ global benefits. Several considered social media, rather than phones themselves, a more plausible mechanism.

### Comment pulse

- Correlation is the central weakness → several charts start too late, emphasize selected periods, or admit competing shocks.
- Global effects may differ → mobile access expanded education and family contact where PCs and reliable electricity were scarce.

### LLM perspective

- View: The collection generates hypotheses, but aligned charts cannot distinguish phones, platforms, recessions, pandemics, or prior trends.
- Impact: Stronger causal evidence would reshape debates on youth access, product design, and attention regulation.
- Watch next: Expand pre-2007 baselines, non-US comparisons, mechanism-specific measures, and quasi-experimental studies.
