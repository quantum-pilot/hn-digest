# Criticisms of “The Body Keeps the Score”

- Score: 180 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45673479) | Link: https://josepheverettwil.substack.com/p/the-body-keeps-the-score-is-bullshit

### TL;DR

A polemical essay, drawing heavily on Michael Scheeringa’s critique, argues that The Body Keeps the Score overstates evidence that trauma causes lasting bodily and neurological changes. It alleges unsupported claims, mischaracterized citations, contradictory findings, and reliance on cross-sectional brain studies that cannot establish whether differences preceded trauma. The author also warns that expansive trauma narratives can misdirect self-treatment. Commenters disputed the essay’s broader causal framing, cited adverse-childhood research, and argued that an imperfect explanatory model may still help some patients understand symptoms or benefit from therapy.

### Comment pulse

- Evidence critique → association and cross-sectional imaging cannot by themselves demonstrate trauma-induced biological change.
- Scope counterpoint → critics said established adverse-childhood outcomes challenge the essay’s stronger trauma-skeptical implications.
- Therapeutic pragmatism → personal accounts describe benefit, though usefulness does not validate every biological explanation.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest critique targets evidentiary overreach, not whether traumatic experiences can have profound consequences.
- Impact: Clinicians and readers need to separate helpful narratives from causal claims presented as settled neuroscience.
- Watch next: Prospective studies, independent citation audits, and treatment outcomes compared across competing mechanisms.
