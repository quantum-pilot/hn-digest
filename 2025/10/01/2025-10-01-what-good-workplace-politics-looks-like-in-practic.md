# What good workplace politics looks like in practice

- Score: 282 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45440571) | Link: https://terriblesoftware.org/2025/10/01/stop-avoiding-politics/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that workplace politics is unavoidable group coordination, not inherently manipulation, and that refusing to participate leaves decisions to others. Its version of “good politics” means building relationships early, understanding incentives, managing upward, creating mutual wins, and making work visible so sound ideas gain support. Commenters largely accepted that organizations are political but challenged the article's optimism: executive preferences, résumé incentives, vendor relationships, and zero-sum structures can overpower technically informed coalition-building, making context and battle selection crucial.

### Comment pulse

- Several commenters said “no politics” cultures merely conceal influence, weakening accountability and honest organizational diagnosis.
- Others stressed sharing credit, letting contributors present their work, and distinguishing positive-sum building from zero-sum competition.

### LLM perspective

- View: Influence is a delivery skill, but relabeling every power struggle as coordination can sanitize dysfunction.
- Impact: Engineers who understand incentives can improve decisions without assuming technical merit alone will prevail.
- Watch next: Whether leaders reward transparent coalition-building or permit status, favoritism, and hidden decisions to dominate.
