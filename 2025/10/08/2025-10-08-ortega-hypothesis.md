# Ortega hypothesis

- Score: 196 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45517674) | Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortega_hypothesis

### TL;DR

The Ortega hypothesis says science advances through accumulated, narrow contributions by many ordinary researchers, enabling later breakthroughs. Citation studies instead often support a “Newton hypothesis,” in which a small elite disproportionately influences important work. The article stresses that citations may mismeasure influence: tools, experimental techniques, teaching, conversations, and social stratification can matter without appearing in references. It also notes the hypothesis only partially reflects Ortega’s own view, which emphasized geniuses creating frameworks for routine scientific work.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters emphasized teaching and preserving institutional knowledge as contributions that citation counts overlook.
- Others argued citations amplify famous researchers, social networks, recency, and reciprocal practices rather than reliably measuring intellectual dependence.
