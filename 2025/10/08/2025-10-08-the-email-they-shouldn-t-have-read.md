# The email they shouldn't have read

- Score: 368 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45515657) | Link: https://it-notes.dragas.net/2025/10/08/the-email-they-shouldnt-have-read/

### TL;DR

The author presents a deliberately anonymized, composite IT cautionary tale about public institutions trapped by a managed email provider. After two agencies prepared to migrate to self-hosted open-source systems, the incumbent allegedly learned confidential details, invoked unfavorable amended contract terms, threatened another agency, disabled access methods, and raised prices. A planted quotation appeared to confirm email monitoring, but the author explicitly says technologies and events were altered or merged. The story’s reliable lesson is about governance, access control, contracts, and vendor leverage—not identifying a culprit.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters focused on insiders who leak information or bypass procurement and security controls while dismissing consequences as harmless.
- Some wanted names, while others recognized litigation risk and questioned what public disclosure years later would accomplish.

### LLM perspective

- View: As a composite account, it is strongest as a controls failure pattern, not evidence against a specific vendor.
- Impact: Token access, unilateral contract changes, and concentrated administration can turn migration planning into exploitable leverage.
- Watch next: Organizations should audit persistent access, amendment notices, export rights, termination windows, and independent migration paths before renewal.
