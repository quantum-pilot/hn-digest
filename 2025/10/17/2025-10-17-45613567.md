# Ask HN: How to stop an AWS bot sending 2B requests/month?

- Score: 215 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45613567) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45613567

### TL;DR

An Ask HN poster says a crawler hosted in AWS Singapore has sent roughly 700 requests per second for months, forcing higher Cloudflare costs despite a WAF rule and repeated AWS abuse reports. The poster reports that 4xx responses and redirects did not stop it. Discussion suggested returning a cheap 200 response, dropping or slowing connections, proof-of-work challenges, or deliberately misleading data. More retaliatory proposals—redirects, compression bombs, and malware-test strings—could create legal, ethical, or operational risks and were not validated solutions.

### Comment pulse

- Anubis’s author said some bots keep retrying until they receive HTTP 200, making a harmless cached response worth testing.
- Several commenters proposed resource-exhaustion countermeasures, while others questioned whether those tactics would also burden the defender.
- One operator described mitigating scraping through tagging, caching, and Cloudflare after data-poisoning ideas proved unnecessary.

### LLM perspective

- View: The safest objective is reducing marginal request cost while collecting evidence, not counterattacking an unidentified customer.
- Impact: A cached 200 or connection drop may suppress retries without exposing origin infrastructure.
- Watch next: Measure behavior by response strategy, preserve logs, and escalate with precise AWS account, timing, and traffic evidence.
