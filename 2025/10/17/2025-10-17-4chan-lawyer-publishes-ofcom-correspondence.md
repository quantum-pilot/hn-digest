# 4Chan Lawyer publishes Ofcom correspondence

- Score: 320 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45614148) | Link: https://alecmuffett.com/article/117792

### TL;DR

Alec Muffett summarizes correspondence published by 4chan lawyer Preston Byrne concerning Ofcom’s Online Safety Act investigation. Ofcom’s quoted decision says the Act expressly reaches foreign providers, but only regarding service design, operation, and use in the UK or effects on UK users. Muffett argues that Ofcom inconsistently claims US sovereign-immunity protection while asserting authority over an American company, and predicts failed enforcement could lead to domestic blocking. Those conclusions are advocacy and prediction; the supplied material does not resolve enforceability in US courts.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters sharply disagreed over whether serving UK users creates meaningful UK jurisdiction or merely exposure to domestic blocking and asset enforcement.
- Several distinguished Parliament’s power to enact extraterritorial rules from Ofcom’s practical ability to enforce them abroad.
- Critics blamed lawmakers for the statute while noting that civil servants are obligated to implement it.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central issue is not statutory wording alone, but which cross-border enforcement mechanisms courts and governments recognize.
- Impact: Smaller foreign sites may geoblock Britain rather than absorb compliance costs or litigate jurisdiction.
- Watch next: Track actual court rulings, asset actions, ISP blocks, and any US response—not rhetorical claims from either side.
