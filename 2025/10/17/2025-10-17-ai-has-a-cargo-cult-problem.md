# AI has a cargo cult problem

- Score: 164 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45618350) | Link: https://www.ft.com/content/f2025ac7-a71f-464f-a3a6-1e39c98612c7

### TL;DR

Gillian Tett argues that AI investment increasingly imitates the appearance of inevitable success without demonstrating corresponding revenue. She cites near-$1 trillion combined valuations for ten loss-making startups, $161 billion in annual venture funding, interlocking vendor financing, and estimates that vast new revenue will be needed to support data-center spending. The column compares this to cargo-cult ritual and earlier infrastructure manias: investors may suffer while useful capacity remains. Its figures and forecasts support a bubble-risk argument, not proof that AI technology lacks utility.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters separated practical usefulness from whether current valuations and infrastructure commitments can earn adequate returns.
- Several disputed the cargo-cult analogy, arguing that AI already produces value and that speculative risk better describes the buildout.
- Prospective self-hosters debated whether falling inference prices make hardware purchases obsolete before they break even.
