# A change of address led to our Wise accounts being shut down

- Score: 112 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45766253) | Link: https://shaun.nz/why-were-never-using-wise-again-a-cautionary-tale-from-a-business-burned/

### TL;DR

A New Zealand business owner says Wise rejected a telecommunications tax invoice submitted after an office move, then restricted both business and personal accounts. According to the account, support offered contradictory guidance, the nominal appeal only collected corporate and refund details, and Wise later alleged improper business payments through the personal account without examples. A refund also failed over the disputed verification documents, leaving funds inaccessible while the owner escalated to a local dispute-resolution service. This is one customer's unresolved account.

### Comment pulse

- Readers viewed fintech automation as cheap on the happy path but brittle when compliance cases require contextual judgment and escalation.
- Others noted traditional banks can also close accounts under opaque anti-money-laundering policies, so the risk is not fintech-exclusive.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central failure alleged here is procedural opacity, not merely a strict verification decision.
- Impact: Businesses using one payment intermediary can lose operational liquidity when an automated compliance process dead-ends.
- Watch next: Follow the complaint outcome, refund completion, Wise's response, and whether human review explains the alleged policy breach.
