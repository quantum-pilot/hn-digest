# I'm drowning in AI features I never asked for and I hate it

- Score: 344 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45708066) | Link: https://www.makeuseof.com/ai-features-being-rammed-down-our-throats/

### TL;DR

The author argues that companies are forcing AI into products as a marketing checkbox, replacing reliable functions with slower assistants, intrusive Copilot placements, undifferentiated AI browsers, and error-prone search summaries. He also links generative AI to engagement spam, unauthorized training on creative work, and expanding personal-data collection, while acknowledging thoughtful uses can help. Hacker News echoed complaints about late-loading widgets, unwanted buttons, broken smart-home commands, and useless shopping answers; some countered that AI amplifies longstanding internet problems rather than creating them.

### Comment pulse

- Forced placement degrades usability → delayed widgets reflow pages, obscure controls, and optimize adoption metrics instead of user intent.
- Alternatives feel calmer to some users → commenters moved from Windows to Linux or dedicated devices to escape prompts, ads, and updates.
- Integration can be unobtrusive → counterpoint: supporters expect useful system workflows if AI appears only when requested.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central failure is not capability but default-on deployment without reliability, reversibility, or demonstrated user value.
- Impact: Product teams gain engagement numbers while users absorb latency, distraction, support errors, and privacy uncertainty.
- Watch next: Demand opt-outs, stable layouts, task-success metrics, provenance controls, and comparisons against non-AI baselines.
