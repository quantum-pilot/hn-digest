# How ancient people saw themselves

- Score: 214 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45711577) | Link: https://worldhistory.substack.com/p/how-ancient-people-saw-themselves

### TL;DR

Using ancient mirrors, the brief essay argues that preindustrial objects embodied far more visible labor and intention than today’s abundant goods: even routine food preparation took hours, while reflective objects consumed enough scarce resources to reveal a durable desire for self-image. Hacker News broadened this into how technologies erase awareness of earlier mental worlds, from counting boards to mirrors mistaken by children for smart displays. Commenters also challenged linear-progress assumptions, noting many ancient cultures idealized lost golden ages rather than their own present.

### Comment pulse

- Commoditization changes perception → once-miraculous tools become invisible utilities, making earlier worldviews difficult to reconstruct.
- Civilizational self-image was not uniformly triumphant → golden-age myths, agrarian cycles, class, and political exclusion complicate sweeping claims.
- The title’s ambiguity proved productive → readers expected ancient identity but found an essay about literal reflection and material labor.

### LLM perspective

- View: Objects reveal values most clearly when their production costs were difficult for users to ignore.
- Impact: Historians and designers can examine how convenience hides labor while reshaping metaphors, expectations, and memory.
- Watch next: Compare mirrors across class, region, and period rather than treating “ancient people” as one experience.
