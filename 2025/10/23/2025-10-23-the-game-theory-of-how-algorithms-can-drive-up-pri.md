# The game theory of how algorithms can drive up prices

- Score: 172 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45680695) | Link: https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-game-theory-of-how-algorithms-can-drive-up-prices-20251022/

### TL;DR

Game-theory research shows high prices can emerge without explicit agreements or retaliatory threats. In a modeled two-seller market, a nonresponsive strategy that randomly favors high prices can coax a no-swap-regret learning algorithm upward; both sellers then reach a profitable equilibrium with little incentive to switch. This complicates rules targeting conventional collusion because each algorithm can look benign alone. Commenters questioned simplified market assumptions, noted demand and non-price competition, and debated whether regulators could inspect algorithms, inputs, or financial outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- Minimal assumptions are the surprise → high-price equilibrium appears even when one strategy never responds to its rival.
- Real markets add escape routes → entrants, capacity, customer awareness, quality, and demand elasticity can alter modeled outcomes.
- Regulatory options divide readers → proposals range from algorithm constraints and input oversight to greater corporate financial transparency.
