# IDEs we had 30 years ago and lost (2023)

- Score: 460 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45626910) | Link: https://blogsystem5.substack.com/p/the-ides-we-had-30-years-ago-and

### TL;DR

The author revisits DOS-era TUI development environments, especially Borland’s Turbo tools, arguing they combined discoverability, editing, builds, debugging, projects, and documentation in remarkably small packages. Modern terminal editors regained many capabilities through plugins and LSP, yet often remain less integrated and approachable; TUI IDEs still help with remote work and resource use. Commenters broadened the history to Macintosh, Delphi, Visual Basic, Lazarus, and Emacs, disputing omissions while strongly endorsing the older tools’ responsiveness and coherent workflows.

### Comment pulse

- Integration and discoverability mattered more than visual style → one environment could take beginners from interface design through debugging.
- Emacs and modern Neovim can match many features → counterpoint: configuration and unfamiliar conventions obstruct immediate use.
- Older interfaces buffered rapid input predictably → some asynchronous modern TUIs discard keystrokes during state transitions.

### LLM perspective

- View: Progress in capability did not guarantee progress in coherence, latency, or learnability.
- Impact: Toolmakers can treat small, self-contained historical IDEs as product-design evidence rather than nostalgia.
- Watch next: Evaluate modern environments on setup time, discoverability, input reliability, and complete offline workflows.
