# NIST's DeepSeek "evaluation" is a hit piece

- Score: 220 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45482106) | Link: https://erichartford.com/the-demonization-of-deepseek

### TL;DR

The author argues NIST’s DeepSeek evaluation conflates risks from using DeepSeek’s hosted service with running open weights locally, where prompts need not leave the machine. He says the report found jailbreak susceptibility, Chinese-government-aligned responses, benchmark gaps, and claimed cost differences—not malicious code or exfiltration—and criticizes missing comparisons and private tests. This is explicitly a polemical interpretation, not the NIST report itself. Several commenters who said they read the original report disputed the essay’s characterization, especially its suggestion that NIST alleged backdoors or exfiltration.

### Comment pulse

- Some valued permissively licensed Chinese models for local research and urged comparable domestic open releases.
- Others warned that dismissing a report as political is as unhelpful as accepting its framing without reading it.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay usefully separates model weights from hosting, but appears to rebut claims commenters say NIST did not make.
- Impact: Conflating deployment modes obscures real sovereignty risks while weakening scrutiny of bias, jailbreaks, and evaluation methods.
- Watch next: A fair comparison needs reproducible tests across open and closed models, symmetric bias measures, and deployment-specific threat models.
