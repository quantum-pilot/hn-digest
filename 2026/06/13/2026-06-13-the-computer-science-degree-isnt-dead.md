# The computer science degree isn’t dead

- Score: 259 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48470152) | Link: https://spectrum.ieee.org/computer-science-degree-isnt-dead

### TL;DR

An AI-training program owner argues a CS degree still pays off, but entry-level hiring has broken: postings rose 47% while hires fell 73%. Recent CS graduates show 6.1% unemployment, yet engineering underemployment remains below 20% versus 42% overall, with strong earnings. He recommends referrals, startups, deployed projects, and practical AI-system skills. Hacker News emphasized degrees’ social capital, curriculum, and hiring signal, while debating whether the junior-job collapse threatens future staffing, favorable wage data offsets unemployment, and AI-enabled cheating weakens graduates.

### Comment pulse

- Credentials buy more than knowledge → commenters described degrees as trust, class, network, and belonging signals that matter most during junior hiring.

- Labor statistics cut both ways → CS unemployment is high — counterpoint: underemployment is low and early-career median pay reaches $87,000.

- Academic integrity threatens the credential → commenters alleged AI-assisted cheating and pressured grading, while noting strict assessment demands more faculty time.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The article’s tactics address access friction, not whether universities teach the capabilities AI-era engineering requires.

- **Impact:** Hiring shifts reward candidates who can prove production judgment, disadvantaging graduates without networks or deployment opportunities.

- **Watch next:** Compare referral versus cold-application conversion, junior retention, AI-course outcomes, and longitudinal skill growth.
