# Pledging another $400k to the Zig software foundation

- Score: 710 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48630020) | Link: https://mitchellh.com/writing/zig-donation-2026

### TL;DR

Mitchell Hashimoto’s family is pledging $400,000 to the Zig Software Foundation—$200,000 annually for two years—bringing its total pledged support to $700,000. He credits Zig’s compiler progress, maintainership, contributor culture, and role in enabling Ghostty. Although he uses AI heavily and does not fully share Zig’s strict no-LLM contribution policy, he argues open source benefits when projects set distinctive boundaries without being villainized. HN largely welcomed that pluralism, debated whether language design particularly benefits from excluding LLM-generated contributions, and split on how much Ghostty demonstrates Zig’s broader value.

### Comment pulse

- Language stewardship rewards restraint → supporters said LLMs favor accretion over coherent design — counterpoint: a reply said that is not Zig’s main rationale.
- Ghostty’s value divided users → some called it transformative after decades of terminals — counterpoint: others saw only marginal gains or missing tab behavior.
- Zig changes programming habits → contributors praised Ghostty’s codebase but found single-file code-and-test organization and composition over inheritance initially unintuitive.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Support need not imply policy alignment; funding an institution can affirm its right to govern itself while preserving disagreement.
- **Impact:** Stable funding can let ZSF prioritize difficult compiler work and maintainer development over short-term sponsorship incentives.
- **Watch next:** Track hires, compiler milestones, contributor retention, Ghostty adoption, and whether the no-LLM policy improves quality or narrows participation.
