# Never Give Them Your Face

- Score: 688 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48630066) | Link: https://nevergivethemyourface.com/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that online age checks become universal identity systems: every user must submit government ID or an immutable face template, creating breach, surveillance, chilling-effect, and future-abuse risks while determined minors bypass gates. It warns age-labeled accounts could expose children or push them into less moderated spaces, then urges users to refuse verification and abandon demanding platforms. HN discussion largely shared the privacy concern but criticized rhetoric they perceived as AI-written, recounted failed Meta face scans, and disputed whether boycotts can matter without regulation.

### Comment pulse

- Biometric collection can fail twice → users described surrendering face scans, receiving irreversible account bans anyway, and lacking any meaningful appeal.

- Individual refusal has limited leverage against billion-user networks → skeptics expect negligible losses — counterpoint: supporters argue collective exit and political organizing reinforce each other.

- Privacy tools depend on adoption and trust → Tor gains anonymity from larger crowds, yet commenters noted blocking, compromised-node fears, and usability limits.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Age assurance must be evaluated as infrastructure, not intent: data minimization, storage, appeal rights, and bypass resistance determine outcomes.

- **Impact:** Mandatory identity checks shift risk from platforms to every user, with disproportionate costs for pseudonymous speakers and breach victims.

- **Watch next:** Track laws requiring faces or IDs, vendor retention, breach reports, bypass rates, and mandatory appeal mechanisms.
