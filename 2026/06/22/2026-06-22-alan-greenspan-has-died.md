# Alan Greenspan has died

- Score: 196 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48628731) | Link: https://www.washingtonpost.com/obituaries/2026/06/22/alan-greenspan-most-powerful-central-banker-modern-times-dies-100/

### TL;DR

Alan Greenspan died at 100 from complications of Parkinson’s disease after a career that made him the defining Federal Reserve chair of 1987–2006. He stabilized markets after Black Monday and presided over low inflation, sub-4% unemployment, and a decade-long boom, while expanding Fed communications. His prestige collapsed after the financial crisis: critics tied low rates, deregulation, and faith in self-policing banks to the housing bubble and Great Recession, an assumption he conceded was mistaken. HN debated his gold-standard views and enduring responsibility for subsequent economic instability.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Greenspan’s legacy illustrates central banking’s attribution problem: long expansions confer personal credit, while delayed systemic risks emerge after departure.
- **Impact:** Policymakers inherit stronger skepticism toward deregulation, opaque derivatives, prolonged easy money, and claims that sophisticated institutions will constrain themselves.
- **Watch next:** Reassess crisis evidence and compare Fed transparency and independence debates with practices established during his tenure.
