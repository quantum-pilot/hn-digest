# The Doom Justifies the Valuation

- Score: 141 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48624195) | Link: https://geohot.github.io//blog/jekyll/update/2026/06/21/the-doom-justifies-the-valuation.html

### TL;DR

The author contrasts GLM-5.2’s technical account of incremental progress with Anthropic’s warnings about exponential advancement and recursive self-improvement. He argues AI-doom messaging is a valuation strategy: hypothetical catastrophe makes current products seem powerful, sustains investment, and captures media attention despite weak economics. He calls for accountability and analysis of how a bubble might unwind. HN split sharply over motive: some said companies sincerely expect destabilizing AGI and accept costly controls; others saw free publicity, regulatory capture, and investor incentives. Commenters also blamed engagement systems for favoring catastrophe over sober sustainability.

### Comment pulse

- Warnings may be sincere → believers argue AGI would be destabilizing and export controls can hurt profits — counterpoint: skeptics say danger claims advertise capability.
- Doom can shape regulation → critics suspect safety rhetoric positions incumbent vendors to influence benchmarks, restrict Chinese or open models, and protect subscriptions.
- Attention economics favors panic → readers said apocalyptic content earns clicks while mundane plans for sustainable infrastructure, water, or software improvement struggle.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Risk communication and promotion can coexist; sincere concern does not remove financial incentives to dramatize uncertainty.
- **Impact:** Policymakers and investors may overweight speculative tail risks while underfunding measurable, incremental applications and public-interest technology.
- **Watch next:** Compare capability forecasts with realized benchmarks, revenue, safety incidents, lobbying outcomes, capital flows, and corrections to prior predictions.
