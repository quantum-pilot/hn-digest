# The best response to AI slop and online noise is from Robin Williams

- Score: 365 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48703452) | Link: https://jayacunzo.com/blog/your-move-chief

### TL;DR

Using the Good Will Hunting bench monologue, the essay argues that fluent knowledge differs from lived experience: AI can absorb descriptions of love, grief, work, and art but cannot inhabit them. Human creators therefore retain an advantage by turning personal memories, vulnerability, and perspective into meaning rather than producing generic information. HN split over the example itself. Supporters said experience supplies empathy and embodied understanding unavailable from text; critics replied that young screenwriters and an actor created resonant fictional experiences they had not lived, demonstrating imagination’s reach.

### Comment pulse

- Lived experience does not guarantee wisdom → it can deepen judgment or breed overconfidence — counterpoint: acknowledging its limits is central to the monologue.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The advantage is situated accountability: creators risk revealing themselves and must live with what their work means to others.
- **Impact:** Audiences may increasingly value provenance, specificity, witnessed detail, and evidence of genuine stakes over polished fluency alone.
