# Michigan bill would bar employers from requiring after-hours coms with workers

- Score: 229 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48707769) | Link: https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/workplace-boundaries-act-employees-after-hours/

### TL;DR

Michigan Senate Bill 948 would prohibit employers from requiring workers to access or answer job-related email, texts, social messages, or scheduling requests outside assigned hours. Exceptions include contractually compensated on-call duty, employee-defined availability windows, and state or federal emergencies affecting operations. Complaints could bring employer fines or overtime payments; the bill is in the Labor Committee. HN split over regulation versus voluntary compensation: supporters cited unpaid interruptions, caregiver burdens, and chaotic shift scheduling, while critics warned about slower handoffs, coverage costs, and reduced flexibility for workers who accept irregular hours.

### Comment pulse

- Unpaid availability is hidden labor → evening pings and scheduling churn transfer operating costs into workers’ family time, especially in lower-paid roles.
- Contract clarity preserves choice → workers may accept irregular hours for higher pay — counterpoint: bargaining power often makes nominal consent meaningless.
- Disconnection also needs product support → scheduled per-account notifications, work profiles, and separate devices help employees enforce boundaries without losing optional access.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The bill prices availability as labor instead of treating constant reachability as an implicit property of employment.
- **Impact:** Employers must formalize coverage and compensation; employees gain a defensible boundary, while customers may see slower off-hours responses.
- **Watch next:** Examine definitions of assigned hours, salaried-worker coverage, retaliation protections, interstate remote work, complaint evidence, remedies, and emergency scope.
