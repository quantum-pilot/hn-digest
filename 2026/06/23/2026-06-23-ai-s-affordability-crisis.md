# AI's Affordability Crisis

- Score: 241 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48646276) | Link: https://blog.dshr.org/2026/06/ais-affordability-crisis.html

### TL;DR

The essay argues AI demand was manufactured through deeply subsidized subscriptions, citing tests where $200 plans allowed $8,000 of Anthropic or $14,000 of OpenAI token usage. As vendors move heavy users toward metered billing, companies are imposing approvals, cheaper-model rules, and spending limits because productivity gains often fail ROI tests. It links this price shock to OpenAI’s reported 2025 losses and enormous industry capital needs. HN broadly observed elastic enterprise demand but disputed the subsidy math, enterprise-plan applicability, inference margins, and whether falling model costs can avert a crash.

### Comment pulse

- Enterprises rapidly rationed tokens → teams moved from unrestricted experimentation to approvals, model quotas, waitlists, and suspended tools once metered costs appeared.
- Productivity is not profit → faster code can accelerate bad ideas or preserve review burdens — counterpoint: offloading grunt work still creates value.
- Subsidy estimates are contested → critics said individual $200 plans do not represent enterprise pricing and the assumed 75% gross margin lacks evidence.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The decisive variable is demand elasticity at fully loaded cost, not benchmark quality or raw token-price decline.
- **Impact:** Finance teams will turn model selection, context length, agent loops, and approvals into ordinary cloud-cost governance.
