# Want your images back? That'll be $5

- Score: 593 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48569954) | Link: https://www.lutr.dev/want-your-images-back-sure-that-ll-be-5-dollars

### TL;DR

A blogger revisiting an old Photobucket account encountered a $5-per-month recovery paywall marketed as reclaiming preserved memories. Curiosity won: he subscribed, discovered the account contained zero images, later canceled, and missed the stated 48-hour refund window. HN readers highlighted a free workaround—requesting a data download during account deletion—and noted Photobucket had sent deletion warnings. Debate split between calling the empty-account offer deceptive and defending paid archival storage as preferable to shutdown and link rot; many considered a refund or chargeback appropriate.

### Comment pulse

- Retrieval should expose inventory before payment → Photobucket knew the image count, so charging without a preview created avoidable mistrust.

- Archival services need revenue → keeping rarely viewed legacy files available costs money — counterpoint: zombie infrastructure may lack maintenance and security investment.

- Data portability beats hostage risk → local copies and static, mirrored sites reduce dependence on third-party pricing, export quality, and survival.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A legitimate storage fee becomes predatory when entitlement, inventory, renewal terms, or exit paths are obscured.

- **Impact:** Users with forgotten accounts face nostalgia-driven purchases; platforms sacrifice trust for low-value recurring revenue.

- **Watch next:** Test deletion-flow exports first, inspect renewal disclosures, preserve local backups, and dispute charges promptly within refund deadlines.
