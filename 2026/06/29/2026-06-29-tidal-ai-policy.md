# Tidal AI Policy

- Score: 290 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48718840) | Link: https://tidal.com/ai-policy

### TL;DR

TIDAL will continue accepting AI-generated music but will tag content identified as fully generated, require distributors to disclose it, and later extend labels to substantially generated tracks as detection improves. Knowingly wholly generated music earns no royalties; from mid-July, fraudulent, deceptive, impersonating, high-volume, or suspiciously streamed AI uploads may be blocked or removed. Upload artists face the same rules. HN welcomed demonetization as an anti-spam incentive but questioned hybrid-work boundaries, detection errors, and TIDAL keeping revenue from popular nonpaying tracks. Subscribers wanted a complete AI-content filter, not merely an icon.

### Comment pulse

- Demonetization attacks the flood’s incentive → removing royalties discourages automated spam — counterpoint: TIDAL could still profit from popular tracks while paying creators nothing.
- Mixed authorship defeats binary labels → AI basslines, vocals, lyrics, or stems blur substantial generation, while imperfect detectors create appeals and false classifications.
- Labels need user control → subscribers want an opt-out comparable to explicit-content filters, especially to avoid artist impersonation in recommendation feeds.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The policy correctly targets economic incentives, but provenance is a spectrum that content detection alone cannot govern reliably.
- **Impact:** Human artists retain a protected royalty pool; AI-assisted musicians and distributors inherit disclosure, classification, and appeal burdens.
- **Watch next:** Publish detector precision, appeal outcomes, distributor compliance, royalty allocation, hybrid-content thresholds, removal rates, impersonation incidents, and opt-out adoption.
