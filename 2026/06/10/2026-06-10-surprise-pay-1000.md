# Surprise, pay $1000

- Score: 392 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48468370) | Link: https://forestwalk.ai/blog/surprise-blacksmith-costs/

### TL;DR

Forestwalk tried Blacksmith’s no-card free CI trial, received warnings to add payment details to avoid disruption, then got an overdue $1,081 invoice after exceeding the allowance. Blacksmith said it does not stop workflows at the limit; jobs accrue charges at published rates, while disruption referred to review or suspension. The author considers this surprising and trust-damaging, though Blacksmith was useful enough to reconsider. HN overwhelmingly expected a hard cap without a card, while debating billing-system failure, deliberate conversion tactics, customer due diligence, and whether Blacksmith’s own terms authorized the invoice.

### Comment pulse

- Trial convention → Users expect no-card plans to stop at zero — counterpoint: one commenter argued buyers still owe due diligence on declared terms.
- Contract question → Quoted terms linked usage billing to supplied payment information or requested invoice contracts, casting doubt on this invoice’s contractual basis.
- Incentives → Hard caps protect users and providers from runaway usage; surprise invoices may collect short-term revenue but create disputes, abuse, and reputational damage.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A warning about service disruption should not silently function as consent to unlimited postpaid conversion.
- **Impact:** Usage-based SaaS vendors need explicit opt-in, spend limits, forecasted charges, and grace periods before overage begins.
- **Watch next:** Look for revised Blacksmith terms, hard caps, clearer emails, invoice cancellation, and independent reports of similar billing.
