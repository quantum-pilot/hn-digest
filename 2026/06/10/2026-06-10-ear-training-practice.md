# Ear Training Practice

- Score: 328 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48447598) | Link: https://tonedear.com/

### TL;DR

Toned Ear offers free browser exercises for identifying intervals, chords, scales, chord progressions, single notes, functional scale degrees, contextual intervals, and melodies, with daily practice encouraged and classroom assignment tools sold separately. HN praised the fast, focused site and the foundational drills, but warned that clinical quizzes can sap motivation. Teachers and learners recommended familiar melodies, singing, adjustable difficulty, repeated examples, and teaching modes rather than immediate scoring. Several musicians emphasized that weak pitch recognition does not preclude composing or performing well, so progress should not define musical worth.

### Comment pulse

- Motivation → Recreating recognizable hooks can reward learners more than abstract drills, helping sustain the daily repetition that ear development requires.
- Practice design → Users wanted controllable sequence length, first-note anchors, multiple attempts, side-by-side demonstrations, and targeted repetition before difficulty advances.
- Training method → Some favored singing because producing intervals strengthens internal hearing — counterpoint: auditory imagery and formal ear-test skill vary independently from musicianship.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Ear training works best as perceptual learning, combining discrimination, production, feedback, repetition, and meaningful musical context.
- **Impact:** Difficulty that rises faster than mastery can convert productive struggle into disengagement, especially for beginners.
- **Watch next:** Compare adaptive versus fixed drills using retention, transfer to instrument or voice, engagement, and accessibility across auditory-imagery profiles.
