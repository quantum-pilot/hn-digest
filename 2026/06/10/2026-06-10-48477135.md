# I'm Eric Ries, author of "The Lean Startup" and new book "Incorruptible" – AMA

- Score: 798 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48477135) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48477135

### TL;DR

Eric Ries introduces Incorruptible, his attempt to explain financial gravity: incentive structures that pull organizations away from their founding missions. Drawing on Costco, Patagonia, Novo Nordisk, the Long-Term Stock Exchange, and governance work, he argues durable resistance requires organizational design. The AMA focused on whether structure can outlast founders, whether leadership remains indispensable, and whether damaged companies can be transformed. Ries says governance, management, leadership, and business models must align with mission; commenters pressed him on succession, Wall Street incentives, historical corruption research, and the limits of reform.

### Comment pulse

- Structure versus leadership → Costco’s hot-dog story prompted claims that principled executives matter most — counterpoint: Ries says structural defenses repeatedly blocked Wall Street pressure.
- Succession → Commenters called mission drift inevitable after founders leave; Ries attributes its frequency to financial-system incentives, not organizational destiny.
- Alignment → Revenue models may overpower culture; Ries proposes mission drive as maintained alignment among business model, management system, leadership, and structure.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Durable mission requires incentives that make desired conduct economically normal, not permanent reliance on exceptional founders.
- **Impact:** Boards, investors, executives, and employees must evaluate ownership, succession, revenue, compensation, and control as one system.
- **Watch next:** Compare cited companies’ charters, voting rights, compensation, succession outcomes, and crisis decisions against conventional peers.
