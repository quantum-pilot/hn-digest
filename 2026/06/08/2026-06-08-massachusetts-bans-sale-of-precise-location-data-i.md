# Massachusetts bans sale of precise location data in new privacy rights bill

- Score: 236 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48448012) | Link: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/massachusetts-votes-to-pass-new-privacy-rights-bill-that-bans-sale-of-precise-location-data/

### TL;DR

Massachusetts’s House voted 146–0 for a Consumer Data Privacy Act, following a 40–0 Senate vote; reconciled text still needs final passage and the governor’s signature. It would give residents access and deletion rights, require explicit consent before covered companies share or sell sensitive data, and bar precise-location sales for residents and visitors statewide. Businesses processing more than 100,000 consumers would be covered. HN welcomed the measure but questioned whether sale, anonymization, and vehicle-data definitions leave loopholes, arguing that collection itself—not merely resale—creates surveillance risk.

### Comment pulse

- Sale may be too narrow → exchange or transfer could prevent non-cash workarounds, while anonymous identifiers remain vulnerable to movement-pattern reidentification.
- Data minimization addresses upstream harm → precise histories can enable surveillance even without resale, so collection and retention should carry business liability.
- Vehicle telemetry is a key test → the bill’s coverage seemed unclear — counterpoint: one connected-car user reported no apparent harm.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A transfer restriction is durable only when paired with purpose limits, retention deadlines, deletion duties, and reidentification prohibitions.
- **Impact:** Location brokers, ad networks, app developers, and automakers may need consent flows plus auditable data lineage.
- **Watch next:** Follow Senate reconciliation, final definitions, governor action, effective date, enforcement authority, and exemptions for processors or government access.
