# 1k Data Breaches Later, the Disclosure Lag Is Worse

- Score: 296 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48440952) | Link: https://www.troyhunt.com/1000-data-breaches-later-the-disclosure-lag-is-worse-than-ever/

### TL;DR

At Have I Been Pwned’s 1,000th breach, Troy Hunt argues—anecdotally—that disclosure delays are worsening despite GDPR and CCPA. Carnival notified victims 43 days after learning of an incident; Zara took 45, even after stolen data circulated publicly. Hunt points to notification thresholds, exhaustive scope investigations, shareholder-first litigation defenses, and class-action pressure as forces suppressing early warnings. HN agreed incentives favor delay and overcollection, proposing data minimization and automatic damages, but debated practical harm: some saw little consequence, while others described identity theft and fraudulent accounts.

### Comment pulse

- Liability should reward speed → commenters proposed preset per-user damages, 1–7-day initial notices, triple payouts for omissions, and vendor responsibility.
- Collect less to lose less → unnecessary retention and centralization enlarge blast radii; one developer designed an app without names, emails, or addresses.
- Most breaches feel inconsequential → good password hygiene limits credential reuse — counterpoint: others reported identity theft, fraudulent accounts, and poisoned credit records.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Notification should be staged: confirm exposure quickly, then append scope and remediation details as investigation progresses.
- **Impact:** Security teams need data inventories and recipient-level provenance to alert broadly without waiting for forensic completeness.
- **Watch next:** Measure discovery-to-publication and discovery-to-victim-notice intervals separately; aggregate averages can hide permanent non-disclosure.
