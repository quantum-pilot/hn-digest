# Failing grades soar with AI usage, dwindling math skills in Berkeley CS classes

- Score: 721 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48392004) | Link: https://www.dailycal.org/news/campus/academics/failing-grades-soar-as-professors-see-greater-ai-usage-dwindling-math-skills-in-uc-berkeley/article_16fad0bf-02cb-4b8c-8d88-888ffd9f8608.html

### TL;DR

UC Berkeley’s spring 2026 F rates reached 35.3% in CS 10, 10.6% in CS 61A, and 16.8% in EECS 127, with the first two averaging C-plus under fixed thresholds. Instructors attributed the jump to multiple factors: LLM-enabled cheating and overreliance, weak prerequisite math, reduced staffing, removed coursework, and unusually low office-hour attendance. Nearly 30 CS 10 students were caught cheating on take-home exams. HN split over AI, standardized-test removal, and course design, while debating how students should demonstrate independent understanding when tools are allowed.

### Comment pulse

- Cognitive load → Commenters reported reduced patience and retention when AI acts first — counterpoint: targeted worksheets and tutoring can increase deliberate practice.
- Causation → Some blamed removal of standardized tests for weak preparation; others noted a 2021 policy change cannot alone explain 2026’s abrupt spike.
- Assessment → Oral questioning exposed copied architectures; students who used LLMs yet explained choices appeared to retain ownership — counterpoint: some rejected any generated code.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The article reports instructor observations and grade trends, not a controlled estimate of AI’s causal effect.
- **Impact:** Departments need assessment designs that reward tool-assisted productivity without letting homework conceal missing foundations.
- **Watch next:** Cohort-adjusted grade data, exam-homework gaps, misconduct counts, prerequisite diagnostics, staffing restoration, and outcomes under explicit AI policies.
