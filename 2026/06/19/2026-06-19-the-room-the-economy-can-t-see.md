# The room the economy can't see

- Score: 222 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48596911) | Link: https://wilsoniumite.com/2026/06/19/the-room-the-economy-cant-see/

### TL;DR

A Stockholm gaming clubhouse gives lonely teenagers a free third place only because Sweden subsidizes it. The essay argues markets cannot price its positive externalities, while wage dependence pushes people toward paid work and away from childcare, friendships, clubs, and neighbors. Targeted grants can build known goods but miss unanticipated local needs; a universal income floor could create financial slack for bottom-up care without replacing grants. HN agreed unpaid social value is obscured but debated whether income, real-estate costs, consumer preference, or weak trust networks are the binding constraint.

### Comment pulse

- Markets may be functioning as designed → profit optimization excludes unbillable welfare, so commenters favored public or civic institutions with different objectives.
- Scarce space may be policy-made → planning restrictions inflate rents and old community halls survive where land remains affordable — counterpoint: demand is uncertain.
- Third places also emerge through trust → families share churches, libraries, parks, carpools, and play dates, though access is uneven and increasingly commercialized.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Allocation is the tradeoff: grants target visible needs; universal floors empower local knowledge but cannot guarantee collective goods.
- **Impact:** More discretionary time could benefit caregivers and volunteers, while scarce premises still require land, organization, and durable funding.
- **Watch next:** Pilot income floors alongside grants; measure volunteer hours, club formation, loneliness, participation equity, rent sensitivity, and program durability.
