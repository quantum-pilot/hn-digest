# A Call to Action: Stop the FCC's KYC Regime

- Score: 330 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48504697) | Link: https://blog.lopp.net/call-to-action-stop-the-fcc-kyc-regime/

### TL;DR

The FCC is considering stronger identity verification for voice service, potentially requiring names, addresses, government IDs, alternate numbers, prepaid-user checks, law-enforcement screening, and four-year record retention. Jameson Lopp argues this would turn basic communications into a surveillance chokepoint while determined criminals use stolen identities; survivors, journalists, whistleblowers, protesters, and low-income users would lose pseudonymous access. He urges comments before June 25 and prefers targeting spoofing infrastructure, bulk callers, negligent carriers, and SIM boxes. HN largely distrusted telecom data custody, though some wanted callers traceable for accountability.

### Comment pulse

- Fix origination before identifying everyone → enforce caller-ID authentication and cut off carriers forwarding spoofed spam rather than collecting universal customer dossiers.
- Anonymity and accountability conflict → traceable callers aid abuse enforcement — counterpoint: hidden numbers protect medical patients, survivors, sources, and vulnerable speakers.
- Carrier KYC concentrates breach risk → telcos already retain location and behavioral data, making added identity documents attractive for theft, sale, or subpoena.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Network abuse should be controlled at behavior and origination layers, not by abolishing lawful pseudonymity.
- **Impact:** Prepaid users and high-risk communities bear the greatest access costs, while carriers inherit larger compliance and breach liabilities.
- **Watch next:** Review the final rule for scope, data minimization, retention, appeals, watchlist due process, prepaid exemptions, and enforcement proportionality.
