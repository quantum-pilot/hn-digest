# "Don't You Just Upload It to ChatGPT?"

- Score: 470 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48507278) | Link: https://correresmidestino.com/dont-you-just-upload-it-to-chatgpt/

### TL;DR

A freelance translator rejects the idea that documents can simply be uploaded for instant professional output. Translation requires intent, tone, localization, terminology research, consistency, formatting, and verification; models invent acronyms, omit sentences, disregard glossaries, and miss meaning. She still uses AI to extract terminology, check 500-page style guides, or suggest edits, treating it as supervised tooling rather than a reason to cut fees. HN centered on expertise blindness: people spot AI’s failures in their own fields yet trust it elsewhere, while debating whether improving models will shift translators toward auditing.

### Comment pulse

- Expertise changes perceived quality → nonexperts miss errors, while practitioners immediately see domain failures — counterpoint: some users say models already outperform their own skills.

- Translation quality is creative and contextual → different human versions can transform novels through names, rhythm, tone, and cultural choices beyond literal correctness.

- Famous-book tests are contaminated → model outputs may recall published translations from training, so evaluation needs unseen texts and fluent bilingual reviewers.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The scarce skill is accountable judgment: knowing when phrasing is wrong, why it fails, and which alternative serves readers.

- **Impact:** Translators may spend less time drafting and more on source analysis, terminology control, cultural adaptation, verification, and liability-bearing signoff.

- **Watch next:** Benchmark unseen documents by domain and language pair, measure omissions and terminology drift, and price expert review explicitly.
