# Ultra-processed foods in the global food system: The role of tobacco companies

- Score: 210 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48411190) | Link: https://ajph.aphapublications.org/doi/10.2105/AJPH.2026.308501

### TL;DR

A review of 113 internal and public industry documents argues that Philip Morris and RJ Reynolds built multibillion-dollar food businesses from the 1980s to mid-2000s by reusing tobacco playbooks: acquisitions, joint ventures, shared distribution, political relationships, consumption-focused sizing, and high-flavor products marketed as lighter. Their food operations reached 76 countries; both companies later divested. The authors recommend monitoring hyper-palatable foods and considering tobacco-style regulation, while acknowledging one incomplete archive and uneven country detail. HN disputed whether these practices were uniquely manipulative or ordinary packaged-food logistics and marketing.

### Comment pulse

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The study documents strategic transfer and reach, not direct causal attribution for present-day disease or every current brand formulation.
- **Impact:** Regulators gain historical hypotheses for surveillance; food producers face scrutiny of formulation, promotion, distribution, and health-positioning practices.
- **Watch next:** Brand-level longitudinal sales and nutrition data, independent archives, exposure-health links, policy trials, and modern ownership tracing.
