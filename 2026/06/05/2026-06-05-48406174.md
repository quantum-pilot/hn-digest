# Ask HN: What was your "oh shit" moment with GenAI?

- Score: 341 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48406174) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48406174

### TL;DR

Respondents’ turning points involved concrete agency: running an imperfect local model on a desktop, weaponizing a real exploit from a failed proof of concept, reverse-engineering obsolete synthesizer and piano firmware, diagnosing household equipment from photos and video, and synthesizing towing or property-inspection plans. The shared realization was that natural-language systems could compress specialist investigation from days into hours and extend novice capability into physical tasks. HN’s excitement came with a hard warning: confident errors in exact specifications can make these tools dangerous without source checking and hands-on judgment.

### Comment pulse

- Local inference changed the frame → a 7 GB model answering English on an ordinary CPU made centralized intelligence feel distributable.
- Cybersecurity capability cuts both ways → an agent converted a nonworking exploit into a default-configuration crash, prompting responsible-disclosure resolve and sleepless concern.
- Physical troubleshooting feels like a superpower → photo-guided furnace, capacitor, fuse, and firmware repairs saved time — counterpoint: bad specifications can cause real harm.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The threshold is closed-loop agency: inspect evidence, propose actions, observe results, and revise—not merely produce fluent answers.
- **Impact:** Motivated novices gain temporary specialist reach, while experts face faster discovery, verification, and disclosure cycles.
- **Watch next:** Measure task success, unsafe advice, expert correction time, source traceability, and outcomes after users act.
