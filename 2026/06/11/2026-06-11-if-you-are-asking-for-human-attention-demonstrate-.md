# If you are asking for human attention, demonstrate human effort

- Score: 1723 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48497609) | Link: https://tombedor.dev/human-attention-and-human-effort/

### TL;DR

AI makes producing code, documents, and critiques cheap, but leaves teammates with the expensive work of reading and verification. The author proposes a workplace norm: anyone requesting attention should first demonstrate effort by reviewing AI output, labeling it, and adding personal judgment. HN commenters broadly endorsed the attention-cost framing, describing oversized, unchecked pull requests and automated replies that discourage review. Debate centered on whether visible effort is a fair proxy for value, and whether better automation can scale review without merely multiplying low-quality output.

### Comment pulse

- Reciprocity → Contributors said low-effort requests create asymmetric costs and train colleagues to disengage, especially when careful feedback receives another automated response.
- Review systems → Some argued prolific agents overwhelm pull-request workflows; stronger tests, linters, continuous deployment, and automated reviewers may supply necessary guardrails.
- Effort signals → Visible work can build trust — counterpoint: brevity may require effort, and rewarding appearances invites performative labor over actual value.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Treat AI output as a draft whose sponsor remains accountable for accuracy, relevance, and reviewability.
- **Impact:** Contribution metrics should reward validated outcomes and reduced reviewer load, not document, message, or commit volume.
- **Watch next:** Teams need explicit norms for disclosure, prompt provenance, PR size, response expectations, and rejection of unchecked output.
