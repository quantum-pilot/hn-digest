# H.R. 6028 would fundamentally change the U.S. Copyright Office

- Score: 279 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48484496) | Link: https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/congress-just-rushed-through-disastrous-copyright-office-overhaul

### TL;DR

The House passed H.R. 6028 by voice vote, removing Library of Congress supervision of the Copyright Office, transferring powers—including DMCA Section 1201 rulemaking—to the Register of Copyrights, and making that official a presidential appointee subject to Senate confirmation. EFF argues this would concentrate authority, weaken the Library’s public-access counterweight, and invite political and entertainment-industry influence after House passage without hearings. HN focused on the unrecorded vote’s weak accountability and the office’s unusual constitutional history, while a side debate challenged EFF’s stance on AI training and fair use.

### Comment pulse

- Voice voting obscures accountability → no roll call records individual positions, though members could have demanded a recorded vote.
- Library supervision provides institutional balance → its preservation-and-access mission counters a Copyright Office accused of favoring large rightsholders.
- AI training sharpened disagreement → critics called it uncompensated copying — counterpoint: others distinguished transformative training from infringing model outputs.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Institutional design matters because appointment, supervision, and rulemaking authority shape copyright outcomes without changing statutory rights.
- **Impact:** Researchers, repairers, libraries, educators, and accessibility users could face a more politicized DMCA exemption process.
- **Watch next:** Senate hearings, amendments, recorded votes, and preservation of Library oversight will reveal whether scrutiny changes the bill.
