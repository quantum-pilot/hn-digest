# International investment and local rules push prices up faster than supply

- Score: 128 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48693420) | Link: https://news.mccombs.utexas.edu/research/foreign-funds-help-make-housing-unaffordable/

### TL;DR

Research by Caitlin Gorback and Benjamin Keys uses foreign-buyer taxes introduced abroad after 2011 to estimate how global capital affected US housing. From 2011–2018, neighborhoods with greater foreign-born exposure saw prices average 6.7% higher but supply grow only 1%; nationally, each 1% price increase produced just 0.26% more housing. San Francisco’s response was 0.06%, while permissive markets added more units. HN debated whether the headline overweights foreigners, but agreed effects are intensely local and municipal zoning and permitting determine whether demand raises prices or construction.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Demand shocks reveal institutions: capital inflows become new homes in flexible cities and scarcity rents in constrained ones.
- **Impact:** Renters and first-time buyers in low-elasticity neighborhoods absorb price pressure; existing owners capture appreciation.
- **Watch next:** Compare permit times, zoning reforms, completions, vacancies, and ownership records before and after foreign-buyer policy shocks.
