# Larry Ellison: "Citizens will be on their best behavior because we’re recording"

- Score: 283 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48373391) | Link: https://www.techradar.com/pro/quote-of-the-day-by-oracle-co-founder-larry-ellison-citizens-will-be-on-their-best-behavior-because-were-constantly-recording-and-reporting-everything-that-is-going-on-a-dire-warning-on-the-erosion-of-privacy

### TL;DR

TechRadar revisits Larry Ellison’s 2024 prediction that AI could analyze live feeds from body cameras and ubiquitous cameras, automatically reporting incidents and changing behavior under constant observation. The article frames this as an Orwellian acceleration of government and workplace surveillance. HN focused on AI eliminating the labor bottleneck, making monitoring searchable, continuous, and scalable while power remains opaque. Debate was polarized: some cited fairer automated traffic enforcement, others warned of chilling effects, rule-gaming, and political abuse; one commenter noted the headline omitted Ellison’s police-accountability context.

### Comment pulse

- Scalable machine interpretation transforms surveillance → AI converts passive footage into searchable risk scores, alerts, and administrative decisions without proportional staffing.
- Automation can constrain selective enforcement → Bangladesh’s traffic cameras reportedly deter violations and bypass police deference — counterpoint: privileged actors may repurpose or evade systems.
- Panopticons optimize compliance, not judgment → people become risk-averse, game measurable rules, and suppress legitimate dissent when authorities define acceptable behavior.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Reciprocal visibility is the key safeguard: cameras should expose official conduct at least as reliably as civilian behavior.
- **Impact:** Cheap analysis shifts the constraint from technical capacity to governance, access controls, retention, and appeal rights.
- **Watch next:** Independent audits of error disparities, abuse logs, deletion compliance, officer override patterns, and whether footage reaches defendants.
