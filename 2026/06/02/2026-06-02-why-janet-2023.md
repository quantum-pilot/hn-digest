# Why Janet? (2023)

- Score: 422 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48367907) | Link: https://ianthehenry.com/posts/why-janet/

### TL;DR

Janet is a small imperative Lisp designed for approachable semantics and practical distribution: eight core instructions, a one-page standard library, and sub-megabyte self-contained binaries via embedded bytecode and a C runtime. Its standout tools include parsing-expression grammars, a shell DSL, easy C embedding, mutable and immutable collections, macros, and compile-time state snapshots that can preserve closures and generators. HN readers liked its portability, REPL, debugger, sandboxing, and creative applications, while flagging weak dependency versioning, a limited library ecosystem, and uncertain editor tooling.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Janet’s edge is coherence: a compact runtime makes language extension, embedding, and deployment feel like one workflow.
- **Impact:** Solo toolmakers gain unusual leverage; teams must weigh ecosystem gaps against simpler distribution and customization.
- **Watch next:** Versioned dependency tooling, LSP maturity, and sustained library maintenance would signal readiness beyond enthusiast projects.
