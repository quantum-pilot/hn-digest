# Federal judge blocks H1B visa $100K fee

- Score: 188 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48454210) | Link: https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/06/08/federal-judge-blocks-h1-b-visa-100k-fee/

### TL;DR

U.S. District Judge Leo Sorokin blocked the administration’s $100,000 fee for new H-1B visas, finding the unexplained policy violated the Administrative Procedure Act and Constitution and functioned as a tax. Alaska officials welcomed the decision: 341 of 573 international teachers use H-1Bs, and some rural districts depend on visa holders for 50–80% of staff. HN debated whether an appeal will classify the charge as a permissible user fee, whether visas fill remote shortages or suppress wages, and how to curb consulting-firm abuse without pricing schools, healthcare, and other employers out.

### Comment pulse

- Rural workforce → Districts already spend $6,000–$12,000 per sponsored teacher; commenters said remote Alaska also relies on foreign doctors, nurses, engineers, and construction workers.
- Legal dispute → One view says presidential entry restrictions permit this user fee — counterpoint: opponents call revenue far beyond processing cost an unconstitutional tax.
- Reform options → Proposals included direct employment, subcontracting bans, sponsor penalties after layoffs, labor-market tests, or auctions directing scarce visas toward highest-value uses.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A flat $100,000 barrier filters by employer wealth, not worker scarcity, local need, wage quality, or program integrity.
- **Impact:** Small rural institutions face greater exclusion than large corporations, even where recruitment difficulty is demonstrable.
- **Watch next:** Follow appeal outcomes, fee-versus-tax analysis, exemptions, congressional action, sponsorship data, wages, vacancies, subcontracting, and enforcement.
