# Harness engineering: Leveraging Codex in an agent-first world

- Score: 280 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48416264) | Link: https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/

### TL;DR

OpenAI describes an internal product whose 1 million lines, 1,500 pull requests, tests, CI, docs, and tooling were generated by Codex in five months, directed initially by three engineers. The claimed speedup came from engineering the harness: repository-local knowledge, isolated worktrees, browser and observability access, mechanical architecture rules, agent review loops, and recurring cleanup. HN practitioners recognized these patterns, but challenged lines and PRs as productivity evidence, noted the product is undisclosed, and warned that high throughput without bounded risk or independently inspectable outcomes may amplify defects and maintenance costs.

### Comment pulse

- Harness consensus → Practitioners endorsed self-verification, high-fidelity environments, in-repo plans, observability, strict boundaries, and automated cleanup as the enabling stack.
- Metric skepticism → One million lines and 1,500 PRs demonstrate output, not value — counterpoint: hundreds of internal users provide some product signal.
- Review risk → Automated correction works only when change impact is bounded and tests are trustworthy; one unsampled defect can remain catastrophic.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Agent productivity is primarily an environment-design achievement; model capability compounds only when feedback is executable and local.
- **Impact:** Engineers shift from implementation toward specification, controls, evaluation, and converting tacit judgment into enforceable artifacts.
- **Watch next:** Publish task outcomes, defect rates, incident severity, maintenance cost, token spend, rollback frequency, and year-over-year architecture drift.
