# An Ohio Valley 100k-watt FM signal is severed in broad daylight

- Score: 137 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48430860) | Link: https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/headlines/an-ohio-valley-100000-watt-fm-signal-is-severed-in-broad-daylight

### TL;DR

An alleged copper thief severed WDGG’s pressurized FM transmission line in Kentucky, apparently after disabling surveillance, taking its 100,000-watt country signal off air. The station returned at roughly 10 watts through backup equipment, but cannot simply splice the unusual line; repairs may cost $70,000–$100,000, and full power will take longer. Police found chopped cable near the tower and arrested a suspect after a pursuit. HN focused on the extreme risk and economic asymmetry: a few thousand dollars in scrap can cause six-figure damage, while transmitter foldback likely prevented worse consequences.

### Comment pulse

- Modern transmitters protect themselves → foldback can detect a short within roughly 100 ms and reduce output toward 1%, limiting reflection damage.
- Infrastructure theft has asymmetric economics → commenters estimated $1,360–$6,400 in nominal scrap versus $70,000–$100,000 repairs, often with much lower illicit proceeds.
- Physical danger was severe → exposed RF could heat tissue, energized conductors could electrocute, and failed protection could reflect power into equipment.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Resilience depends on repair logistics as much as backup transmitters; uncommon feedline can become a single point of failure.
- **Impact:** Broadcasters need protected sites, tested auxiliary paths, insurer coordination, and strategically stocked line sections or compatible adapters.
- **Watch next:** Track restoration to full power, antenna damage, prosecution, insurance coverage, and whether recurring theft prompts stronger site protections.
