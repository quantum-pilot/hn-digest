# LLMs are eroding my software engineering career and I don't know what to do

- Score: 768 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48434312) | Link: https://human-in-the-loop.bearblog.dev/llms-are-eroding-my-software-engineering-career-and-i-dont-know-what-to-do/

### TL;DR

A backend engineer with 10 years in finance argues that agents have successively commoditized his domain knowledge, design work, implementation, and distributed-systems debugging; he reports they now solve 90% of his bugs on the first attempt. His remaining advantage—architecture and code quality—also seems weaker as employers accept machine-maintainable C-grade code and hire generalists. He fears falling demand will erase specialist premiums. HN split sharply between forecasts of accelerating displacement and evidence that expert review, contextual judgment, and ownership remain indispensable.

### Comment pulse

- Domain expertise remains a safety system → agents confidently misstate regulations, while vibe-coded PRs shift effort into longer review and correction cycles.
- Capability trajectories divide the thread → rapid gains imply layoffs — counterpoint: disruption bursts often slow after easily captured improvements are exhausted.
- Execution is not ownership → fast generation cannot supply sustained maintenance, product care, or the work of earning users’ attention.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The central risk is fewer seats, flatter specialization premiums, and concentrated accountability—not zero engineers.
- **Impact:** Regulated teams may shift effort from producing code to verifying requirements, historical corrections, and responsibility.
- **Watch next:** Track end-to-end cycle time, escaped defects, incident remediation, hiring mix, and compensation—not first-draft speed alone.
