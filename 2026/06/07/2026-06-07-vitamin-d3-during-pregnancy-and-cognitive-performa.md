# Vitamin D3 During Pregnancy and Cognitive Performance at 10 Years

- Score: 178 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48435584) | Link: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849122

### TL;DR

In a post hoc analysis of a Danish pregnancy RCT, 498 ten-year-olds whose mothers received 2,800 versus 400 IU/day of vitamin D3 from week 24 to one week postpartum scored 0.17 SD higher on verbal memory and 0.24 SD higher on visual memory after adjustment and false-discovery correction. No significant differences appeared in eight other functions; a set-shifting signal lost significance after correction. HN viewed the selective findings as hypothesis-generating, stressing that cognition was not the original trial endpoint while debating multiplicity and generalizability from a 95.6%-White, largely vitamin-D-sufficient cohort.

### Comment pulse

- Multiplicity drove skepticism → 11 functions were tested; readers initially miscounted survivors, but the table shows both memory outcomes retained q=.02.
- Randomization does not rescue every inference → cognition was a post hoc secondary analysis of an asthma trial, increasing false-positive risk.
- The cohort narrows applicability → only 15% started below 20 ng/mL and 95.6% were White — counterpoint: baseline levels were balanced and adjusted.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Statistical survival is insufficient; clinical meaning depends on replication, magnitude, safety, and relevance to deficient populations.
- **Impact:** The paper should guide research priorities, not prenatal dosing decisions.
- **Watch next:** Run a prespecified cognition trial with longitudinal postnatal exposure, repeated assessments, safety outcomes, and parental cognitive covariates.
