# Making peace with your unlived dreams (2023)

- Score: 119 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48437290) | Link: https://nik.art/making-peace-with-your-unlived-dreams/

### TL;DR

Niklas Göke uses his medically foreclosed snowboarding ambition to argue that a finite life necessarily leaves most possible selves unlived. Instead of treating every dream as an obligation, he recommends enjoying some through imagination, books, videos, and other people’s journeys, then choosing deliberately where to spend limited time. HN broadened the idea: separate genuine desires from culturally implanted status goals, grieve futures erased by illness or caregiving, and distinguish impossible dreams from uncertain ones whose pursuit still creates growth. Several warned that acceptance can become premature surrender.

### Comment pulse

- Identity audit → Repeatedly asking why can expose dreams driven mainly by status or mass culture, freeing attention for personally meaningful boundaries.
- Uncertainty → Known impossibilities permit grieving and redirection; uncertain goals can remain worth pursuing because effort itself produces relationships, taste, and self-knowledge.
- Acceptance → Letting go protects finite time — counterpoint: declaring defeat too early can disguise fear, especially when adaptation or incremental action remains possible.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Peace is not indifference; it is allocating effort after separating constraints, uncertainty, and inherited expectations.
- **Impact:** Caregivers and people facing illness may need grief and revised meaning, not productivity advice.
- **Watch next:** Revisit abandoned goals periodically, test smaller versions, track motivation sources, and notice whether acceptance creates relief or regret.
