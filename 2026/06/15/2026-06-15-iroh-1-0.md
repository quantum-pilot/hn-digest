# Iroh 1.0

- Score: 887 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48542480) | Link: https://www.iroh.computer/blog/v1

### TL;DR

Iroh 1.0 is a stable, embeddable networking stack that lets applications address peers by cryptographic keys rather than changing IPs. It combines QUIC, NAT traversal, multipath route switching, encrypted direct connections, and fallback relays; local discovery works offline, while custom transports can support BLE, Tor, or future LoRa adapters. Wire compatibility is promised across v1 minor releases and Rust, Python, Node.js, Swift, and Kotlin APIs. HN’s analogy was Tailscale at the application layer, though readers wanted the landing page to explain keys, relays, and differences from DNS or WebRTC.

### Comment pulse

- Embedding changes deployment economics → apps gain secure peer connectivity without requiring user VPN accounts; teams can switch from public to self-hosted relays.
- NAT traversal is the concrete win → devices behind home and mobile NATs can hole-punch direct paths for lower latency and higher bandwidth.
- Key abstraction needs concrete explanation → readers inferred dual identity and encryption use plus WebRTC-like signaling; vague superiority claims invited mistaken DNS comparisons.
