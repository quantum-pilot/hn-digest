# Write for One Person

- Score: 268 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48507039) | Link: https://wizardzines.com/comics/write-for-one-person/

### TL;DR

The comic advises choosing one concrete reader—often yourself—instead of trying to satisfy an abstract mass audience. Specificity clarifies voice, assumptions, and what deserves explanation; paradoxically, work aimed narrowly can travel widely. HN stories supported this: an obscure math review led to a film-consulting role, and a criticized personal project later brought awards and work. Commenters also defended distinctive prose against LLM-driven blandness. Technical writers noted a limit: audiences vary in prerequisite knowledge, so tutorials and how-tos still need explicit baselines, links, and careful scope.

### Comment pulse

- A single imagined reader reduces performance anxiety → speaking as in office hours can make large audiences feel conversational and preserve natural explanation.

- Authenticity can outperform reception → harsh HN criticism did not stop projects from winning awards or generating work — counterpoint: publishing still exposes personal vulnerability.

- Technical specificity requires prerequisites → naming one audience helps choose assumed literacy, while linked foundational material serves readers below that baseline.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Writing for one person is a constraint that produces concrete choices, not a claim that only one reader matters.

- **Impact:** Authors gain a stronger voice and clearer scope; readers encounter material shaped around coherent needs rather than averaged preferences.

- **Watch next:** Define the reader’s goals and prior knowledge, test drafts with adjacent audiences, and preserve intentional personality during editing.
