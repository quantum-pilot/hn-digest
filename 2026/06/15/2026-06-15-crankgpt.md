# CrankGPT

- Score: 547 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48540854) | Link: https://crankgpt.com

### TL;DR

CrankGPT proposes human-powered local inference while satirizing AI’s energy use, privacy costs, and platform economics. Its mock lineup assigns a 20-watt hand crank to chat, a 150-watt pedal system to agentic coding and video, and 2,000-plus-watt gym partnerships to training and agent swarms. The pitch promises private, offline tokens that burn calories instead of fuel or subscription money. HN enjoyed the premise and explored exercise-generation limits, but spent much of the thread attacking the scroll-hijacked marketing page; commenters noted the linked technical material claims usable Raspberry Pi 5 models.

### Comment pulse

- The joke extends into interaction design → scroll hijacking makes visitors crank through slides — counterpoint: many still found the choppy, link-hostile page exhausting.
- Human output is limited → bicycle dynamos yield roughly 3 watts at normal speed, while 700-watt cycling illustrates elite, unsustainable effort.
- Energy accounting needs system boundaries → human food, farming, transport, and baseline metabolism prevent simple calorie-versus-datacenter comparisons.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The concept works best as embodied criticism: token generation becomes visibly physical, local, slow, and expensive.
- **Impact:** Tiny local models may fit off-grid experiments, but human generation cannot supply workstation-class inference continuously.
- **Watch next:** Verify sustained electrical output, battery buffering, Raspberry Pi model latency, thermal load, and full lifecycle emissions.
