# Claude Sonnet 5

- Score: 814 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48736605) | Link: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5

### TL;DR

Anthropic positions Sonnet 5 as a cheaper agentic workhorse approaching Opus 4.8 on coding, tool use, search, and computer control, with selectable effort levels and improved safety over Sonnet 4.6. It is now the Free and Pro default, priced temporarily at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens before rising to $3/$15; a new tokenizer may consume up to 1.35× more tokens. HN questioned the product niche: above medium effort, Opus often looks more cost-effective, while some users prefer lower-effort Sonnet for speed, long contexts, free access, or supervised coding.

### Comment pulse

- Cost curves suggest a switch point → use Sonnet through medium effort, then move to Opus — counterpoint: subscriptions and input-heavy workloads complicate that rule.
- Agentic optimization divides users → autonomous completion appeals to some, while supervised developers fear overreach, overcomplication, and weaker compliance with narrow instructions.
- Benchmarks remain provisional → provider speed varies, independent tests conflict, and commenters said project-specific trials matter more than aggregate model rankings.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Sonnet 5’s value is workload routing, not universal dominance: cheap autonomy at low effort, escalation when judgment becomes expensive.
- **Impact:** Teams need model-selection policies by task shape, context growth, latency, supervision level, and failure-recovery cost.
- **Watch next:** Measure verified task completion, total tokens, cleanup time, boundary adherence, and regressions after introductory pricing ends.
