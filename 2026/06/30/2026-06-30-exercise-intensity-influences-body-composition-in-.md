# Exercise intensity influences body composition in healthy older adults (2025)

- Score: 192 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48730694) | Link: https://www.maturitas.org/article/S0378-5122(25)00571-7/fulltext

### TL;DR

In a six-month randomized sub-study, 123 healthy adults averaging 72 completed three supervised treadmill sessions weekly: low intensity, continuous moderate intensity, or four 4-minute high-intensity intervals. HIIT and moderate training reduced fat similarly versus control and both reduced visceral fat over time; only moderate training lost fat-free mass, while HIIT maintained it and improved body-fat percentage. However, authors judged changes small and not clinically meaningful relative to measurement error. HN emphasized this was aerobic, not resistance, training and cautioned against extrapolating from healthy volunteers or treating all-out exercise as safe.

### Comment pulse

- Do not confuse HIIT with lifting → every arm was aerobic treadmill work; the key difference was muscle preservation, not greater fat loss.
- Protocol realism is limited → equal 45-minute sessions favor intensity comparisons, whereas real endurance programs use longer easy volume with carefully dosed intervals.
- Intensity needs progression → warm-ups, recovery, periodization, and medical suitability matter — counterpoint: six months of supervised HIIT achieved 96% adherence.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The finding favors HIIT for body-composition efficiency, but effect size and clinical relevance matter more than statistical significance.
- **Impact:** Healthy older adults may preserve lean tissue with supervised intervals; clinicians should not substitute this evidence for individualized clearance.
- **Watch next:** Replicate with intention-to-treat analysis, resistance-training arms, longer follow-up, real-world session durations, adverse events, and clinically meaningful endpoints.
