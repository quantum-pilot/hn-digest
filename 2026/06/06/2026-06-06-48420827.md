# Ask HN: Why is the HN crowd so anti-AI?

- Score: 369 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48420827) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48420827

### TL;DR

The post asks why HN criticizes AI coding when users care about working products and AI-assisted teams can supposedly ship, learn, and iterate up to 10 times faster than hand-coding. Commenters rejected the premise that HN is uniformly anti-AI: thread framing selects different factions, and many users simultaneously value rapid prototyping while distrusting large-scale agentic development. The debate separated output speed from verified outcomes, highlighting security, maintainability, team coordination, and review costs, alongside personal fears that prompting will replace enjoyable, paid programming and structural concerns about proprietary model dependence.

### Comment pulse

- HN is divided, not anti-AI → titles and initial comments attract different constituencies, reflecting society’s broader polarization rather than a stable consensus.
- Faster generation is not faster delivery → verification, security, and maintainability can erase gains — counterpoint: human teams already produce bugs and technical debt.
- Resistance can be personal and structural → developers fear losing paid flow-state work, while cloud models centralize knowledge, access, telemetry, and political leverage.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The useful question is which workflows improve end-to-end outcomes, not whether AI-written code is intrinsically acceptable.
- **Impact:** Teams need modular boundaries, explicit guardrails, human review, and outcome metrics before scaling agents beyond prototypes.
- **Watch next:** Compare cycle time, defect escape, security findings, maintenance effort, developer satisfaction, and incidents across assisted and unassisted teams.
