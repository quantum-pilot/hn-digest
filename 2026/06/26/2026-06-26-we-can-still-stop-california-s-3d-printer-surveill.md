# We can still stop California's 3D printer surveillance scheme

- Score: 144 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48692051) | Link: https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/we-can-still-stop-californias-3d-printer-surveillance-scheme

### TL;DR

California’s Assembly advanced AB 2047, which would require 3D-printer software to detect and block prohibited firearm designs before the bill moves to the Senate. EFF says amendments spare private resales but leave open-source tools lawful only when they implement compliant filtering, weaken anti-circumvention standards, and favor entertainment-industry users. It argues universal print monitoring will be ineffective, chill lawful creation, and expose designs. HN commenters chiefly feared manufacturer-controlled slicers and broader computing restrictions, while debating whether automated advocacy emails influence legislators.

### Comment pulse

- The slicer mandate could lock hardware to vendors → commenters saw authorized-software pathways as eliminating alternative tools and normal user control.
- Constituent pressure remains worthwhile → several urged contacting state senators — counterpoint: offices may discount automated campaign messages unless voters personalize them.
- The proposal resembles a wider restriction trend → commenters connected printer controls with operating-system age checks and longstanding attacks on general-purpose computing.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A mandate conceding circumvention while inspecting every job imposes certain surveillance costs for an explicitly limited enforcement gain.
- **Impact:** Compliance liability may shift decentralized maker experimentation toward vertically integrated vendors able to certify software and absorb legal risk.
- **Watch next:** Track Senate amendments on software authorization, standard setters, testing, data handling, and parity for studio and independent users.
