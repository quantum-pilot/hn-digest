# AI children's books, body horror edition

- Score: 203 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48681250) | Link: https://lcamtuf.substack.com/p/ai-childrens-books-body-horror-edition

### TL;DR

After cataloging roughly 220 similar AI-generated children’s books, the author bought an Amazon category bestseller to inspect quality. Its encyclopedia illustrations contain grotesque failures—distorted faces, fused animals and trees, and uncanny reflections—despite likely using a recent flagship model. Such products exploit gift buyers judging covers, nonfiction’s lower infringement risk, and category visibility. HN split between blaming low-effort publishing and showing that carefully directed AI can make stories children enjoy; critics argued industrialized generation shifts costly verification onto parents and could distort children’s baseline sense of reality.

### Comment pulse

- Effort, not tooling, determines quality → structured story arcs, art direction, and character sheets can produce bespoke books for $0.25–$0.50.
- Scale changes the harm → cheap generation floods marketplaces and forces consumers to perform factual, visual, and authorship checks formerly absorbed by publishers.
- Human craft remains the benchmark → commenters recommended richly detailed classics — counterpoint: some children found the malformed images delightfully absurd rather than damaging.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The failure is distribution without editorial accountability: model capability matters less when no responsible adult validates the finished artifact.
- **Impact:** Parents become unpaid quality assurance; careful authors face discoverability pressure; children consume plausible errors before developing robust visual priors.
