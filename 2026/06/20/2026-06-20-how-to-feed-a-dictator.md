# How to feed a dictator

- Score: 154 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48605301) | Link: https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/jun/09/how-to-feed-a-dictator-film

### TL;DR

Andrew Neel’s documentary, adapted from Witold Szabłowski’s 2020 book, interviews five chefs who served Pol Pot, Kim Jong-il, Idi Amin, Saddam Hussein, and Augusto Pinochet. Food becomes a lens on coercion, privilege, complicity, and survival: some cooks recall surveillance or death threats, one regrets trading peace for luxury, while others remain devoted despite genocide and torture. The film argues dictatorships are sustained by ordinary people rationalizing a good job. HN debated whether invoking Arendt’s banality of evil fits that claim and whether a late Trump comparison compromises the film’s credibility.

### Comment pulse

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Proximity humanizes power without absolving it; private kindness and public atrocity can coexist in the same remembered person.
- **Impact:** Testimony from service workers exposes how coercion, patronage, fear, and status bind ordinary labor to violent regimes.
- **Watch next:** Evaluate testimony challenges, survivor balance, archival corroboration, and whether spectacle overwhelms context.
