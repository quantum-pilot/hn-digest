# Plunging test scores are a slow-moving catastrophe

- Score: 206 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49857442) | Link: https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/09/10/plunging-test-scores-are-a-slow-moving-catastrophe

### TL;DR

An Economist editorial warns that 2026 PISA reading, mathematics, and science scores across wealthy countries are the lowest since testing began in 2000, with declines continuing after pandemic disruption. It argues that literacy and numeracy become more important, not less, as AI encourages cognitive outsourcing. The editorial advocates phone limits, rigorous exams, fact-rich curricula, attendance, and evidence-led teaching, while criticizing lowered expectations. HN commenters offer competing explanations including social media, digitized classrooms, pandemic effects, demographic composition, and reduced emphasis on testing; none alone establishes causation.

### Comment pulse

- Attention capture may predate generative AI → declines began before widespread AI use, implicating social media, screens, and reduced sustained practice.
- Analog classrooms could restore focus → commenters favor phone bans, textbooks, handwriting, and notebooks — counterpoint: online habits extend beyond school boundaries.
- Aggregate scores can obscure subgroup trends → demographic reweighting was proposed, though others requested longitudinal controls for wealth, language, and parental education.
