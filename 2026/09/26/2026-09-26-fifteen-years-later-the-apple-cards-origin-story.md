# Fifteen years later, the Apple Cards origin story

- Score: 436 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49854693) | Link: https://lexontech.org/fifteen-years-later-the-apple-cards-origin-story

### TL;DR

An anonymous former printing partner recounts Apple Cards as a Steve Jobs-inspired 2011 project that mailed custom letterpress cards from an iPhone. Apple demanded cotton stock, heavy debossing, invisible tracking barcodes, and a custom USPS stamp, forcing complex printing and manual handling across US and European operations. The source describes frantic preparation for hundreds of thousands of launch-day orders, only for demand to fit nearly in a glovebox; Apple discontinued Cards in 2013. Commenters split between admiring obsessive execution and condemning founder-driven overwork.

### Comment pulse

- Apple’s physical details required operational invention → cotton printing, hidden tracking, and custom postage complicated an apparently simple app.
- Founder-led ambition can externalize costs → workers reportedly slept in conference rooms for demand forecasts that proved wildly inflated.
- Cards still delivered a distinctive experience → some users valued effortless physical mail—counterpoint: a competing founder called the product limited and flawed.
