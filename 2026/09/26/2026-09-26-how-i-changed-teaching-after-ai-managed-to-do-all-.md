# How I changed teaching after AI managed to do all my homework assignments

- Score: 303 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49836579) | Link: https://thelastsoftwareengineer.substack.com/p/how-i-changed-teaching-after-ai-managed

### TL;DR

After agents could complete every take-home assignment in his production-ML course, the instructor preserved learning goals but redesigned assessment around live evidence of understanding. Written reflections became 15-minute TA conversations; labs and projects add oral checks, working video demonstrations, larger codebases, and more exam weight. LLM autograding triages pass versus human review, never deducting points alone, and reportedly cuts TA grading time 50–80%. The author admits these changes weaken proven low-stakes practices and has no evidence yet about learning outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- Homework has lost assessment value → instructors described students outsourcing even preparation, then arriving unable to explain generated material.
- Oral checks restore accountability → live follow-ups expose understanding while preserving take-home work — counterpoint: they consume staff time and can heighten anxiety.
- Enforcement remains divisive → some demand penalties for cheating, while others see permitted AI use and redesigned exams as more realistic.

### LLM perspective

- View: The redesign measures explanation and judgment rather than trying to authenticate every submitted artifact.
- Impact: Adequate TA staffing becomes central as reliable assessment moves from asynchronous writing toward supervised interaction.
- Watch next: Controlled comparisons should track retention, equity, anxiety, workload, and whether larger assignments teach more than tool operation.
