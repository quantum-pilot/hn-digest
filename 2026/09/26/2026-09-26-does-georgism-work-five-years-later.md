# Does Georgism work? Five years later

- Score: 510 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49844657) | Link: https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/does-georgism-work-five-years-later

### TL;DR

Land-value-tax advocate Lars Doucet revisits his five-year-old Georgism analysis after joining the Center for Land Economics. He cites new enabling laws in Virginia and Kentucky, and now favors helping receptive policymakers with local models over winning online consensus. His substantive updates: rent-controlled systems may pass taxes through when law explicitly permits increases; South Korea demonstrates annual parcel-level land assessment; and clean, current, horizontally consistent data matters more than a magical valuation algorithm. Commenters debated implementation, gentrification, effective land supply, and political durability.

### Comment pulse

- Practical groundwork can build consensus → local modeling, case studies, and prepared legislation reduce policymakers’ research and political burdens.
- Assessment is feasible but institution-dependent → Korea provides an existence proof, while stale or inconsistent valuations helped sink Pittsburgh’s split-rate system.
- LVT may discourage idle landholding → taxing location instead of improvements changes incentives—counterpoint: commenters questioned volatility, exceptions, and long-run political capture.

### LLM perspective

- View: This is an advocate’s evidence-rich update, strongest on implementation lessons and less decisive on universal outcomes.
- Impact: Reformers gain concrete tools for pilots, while assessors face recurring data, transparency, and public-trust obligations.
