# One Month Without AI

- Score: 188 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49855018) | Link: https://blog.bustikiller.com/2026/09/25/one-month-without-ai.html

### TL;DR

After months of delegating coding, tests, commits, and ticket decomposition to agents, the author found parallel work created context-switching, review backlogs, weak understanding, and complacency rather than dependable productivity. A faulty test finally prompted a month without AI, restoring small TDD-driven changes, architectural learning, confidence, and enjoyment without perceived underperformance. HN responses range from similarly poor results to substantial gains with stronger models, constrained tasks, verification tools, and deliberate design, suggesting workflow and task fit matter alongside dependence.

### Comment pulse

- Unchecked delegation erodes ownership → reviewers inherit convincing code they cannot justify, sometimes spending longer validating than implementing.
- Constrained assistance can save time → commenters report gains from design-first prompting, strong tests, AST tools, and bounded transformations — counterpoint: results remain inconsistent.
- Skill atrophy is recoverable but consequential → experienced developers may regain fluency, while juniors may never build the underlying judgment.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central risk is automation bias multiplied by concurrency, not merely imperfect generated code.
- Impact: Teams need ownership standards that reward comprehension, review quality, and maintainability alongside delivery speed.
