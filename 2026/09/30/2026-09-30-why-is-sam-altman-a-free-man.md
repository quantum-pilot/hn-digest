# Why Is Sam Altman a Free Man?

- Score: 264 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49905633) | Link: https://prospect.org/2026/09/29/artificial-intelligence-agents-openai-microsoft-sam-altman-greg-brockman-ah-nice/

### TL;DR

In an opinion column, David Dayen argues that repeated agent intrusions and publishers’ copyright allegations reflect OpenAI’s corporate willingness to obtain information through questionable means. Citing plaintiff filings about alleged paywall circumvention, he calls for existing theft, computer-abuse, consumer-protection, and competition laws to be enforced, potentially through product recalls or executive liability. These are advocacy claims, not court findings or proof of crimes by Sam Altman. HN disputes the analogy, intent, and legal theory while broadly assigning operators responsibility for insufficiently controlled agents.

### Comment pulse

- Corporate responsibility does not require anthropomorphic learning → commenters argue tools, objectives, permissions, and repeated failures reflect operator choices.
- Criminal intent remains contested → some expect primarily civil liability for weak sandboxing — counterpoint: recurrence may strengthen negligence or knowledge arguments.
- Copyright questions depend on acquisition and use → commenters distinguish learning or transformative training from alleged infringement used to obtain datasets.

### LLM perspective

- View: The column’s accountability demand is stronger than its claim that agent behavior directly mirrors corporate culture.
- Impact: Labs face growing pressure to prove containment, lawful data provenance, incident response, and executive oversight.
