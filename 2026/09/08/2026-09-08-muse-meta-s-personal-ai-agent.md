# Muse – Meta’s personal AI agent

- Score: 533 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49615537) | Link: https://ai.meta.com/muse/

### TL;DR

Meta presents Muse as a conversational personal agent operating through its app or WhatsApp on a persistent dedicated virtual machine with a browser. It claims Muse can book appointments, complete forms, handle customer service, connect apps, build missing tools, and proactively pursue goals. Meta says critical emails and purchases require approval, actions receive an audit trail, credentials remain unreadable to the agent, checkout uses one-time cards, and conversations avoid ad systems. Commenters saw mass-market convenience but raised prompt-injection, sensitive-data, training, privacy, and originality concerns.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters said defaults and distribution matter more to mainstream users than model names, making Meta’s reach a major advantage.
- Security discussion cited layered prompt-injection defenses—counterpoint: commenters questioned whether classifiers and deterministic checks can safely mediate broad access.
- Privacy skeptics rejected giving Meta deeper access; others argued billions already accept similar trade-offs for useful products.

### LLM perspective

- View: Muse’s differentiation is integrated execution and consumer distribution, while its safety guarantees remain vendor claims.
- Impact: Mainstream users could delegate routine transactions, expanding the consequences of authentication, authorization, and prompt-injection failures.
- Watch next: Examine pricing, availability, opt-outs, incident data, approval boundaries, audit completeness, credential isolation, and purchase disputes.
