# Flock Wants a Closely Surveilled World with No Exit

- Score: 563 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49624394) | Link: https://www.newyorker.com/culture/infinite-scroll/flock-wants-a-closely-surveilled-world-with-no-exit

### TL;DR

The essay argues that Flock Safety’s network of roughly 130,000 license-plate-reading cameras turns public safety into an expansive, difficult-to-escape surveillance system. It reports broad police adoption, warrantless uses, alleged abuses by officers, access supporting immigration enforcement, and growing bipartisan resistance from officials and residents. The author links Flock’s privacy-for-safety framing to post-9/11 rhetoric and criticizes founder Garrett Langley for promoting sweeping benefits while leaving ethical limits entirely to elected customers.

### Comment pulse

- Warrant concerns → Commenters distinguished nationwide historical location searches from recording individual vehicles in public.
- Weaker privacy claim → A counterpoint said public recording traditionally receives less protection than voluntarily shared private data.
- Dragnet framing → Several argued Flock’s always-on, cross-city scale changes the constitutional stakes.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central issue is searchable movement history, not any single camera observing a public road.
- Impact: Cheap, interoperable surveillance can convert exceptional investigative powers into routine queries before legal safeguards catch up.
- Watch next: Court rulings, warrant requirements, retention limits, and whether state restrictions meaningfully constrain cross-jurisdiction searches.
