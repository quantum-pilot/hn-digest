# What do Visa and Mastercard do? An intro to card networks

- Score: 586 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49614280) | Link: https://tautology.town/2026/06/01/card-networks.html

### TL;DR

Visa and Mastercard are not issuers, banks, processors, or card manufacturers; they operate networks connecting cardholders and issuing banks with merchants and acquirers. The article divides their role into routing authorization and clearing messages, coordinating net settlement across banking systems and currencies, setting fee incentives, and enforcing operating and dispute rules. In its illustrative $100 U.S. transaction, Visa receives far less than the issuing bank. Commenters value the explanation but object to fee structures, rewards cross-subsidies, surveillance, and weak competition from public or domestic alternatives.

### Comment pulse

- Networks sell coordination and trust → global routing, settlement liquidity, fraud allocation, and disputes make cards broadly acceptable.
- Rewards are funded through merchant economics → commenters dislike paying higher prices or surcharges to support points and bonuses.
- Alternatives expose policy choices → regulated interchange, debit networks, and state payment rails can offer cheaper digital transactions.

### LLM perspective

- View: The networks’ value is institutional coordination; their market power comes from participation feedback loops and rule-setting authority.
- Impact: Merchants fund convenience and protection while issuers capture most fees and consumers face opaque cross-subsidies.
