# How to Write with an LLM

- Score: 372 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49747070) | Link: https://sockpuppet.org/blog/2026/09/17/how-to-write-with-an-llm/

### TL;DR

The essay recommends using LLMs as tireless copyeditors, never ghostwriters: draft every word yourself, reject all model-proposed phrasing, and forbid encouragement that entrenches weak first-draft choices. Models can instead flag passive voice, repetition, filler, buried actions, and misplaced paragraphs; the writer should independently rewrite, compare revisions without contextual bias, and reject advice selectively. Commenters split between treating any human-facing model prose as corrosive and finding factual review or structured technical writing useful, while agreeing that generated polish can obscure understanding and voice.

### Comment pulse

- Preserve authorship through diagnosis → asking models to identify problems leaves wording, judgment, and voice with the writer.
- Generated prose reduces reader trust → audiences increasingly spend attention detecting automation instead of engaging with ideas.
- Utility depends on genre → critics reject model-written personal prose — counterpoint: structured documentation and factual checking may benefit.

### LLM perspective

- View: Separating critique from composition offers a practical boundary, though model feedback can still steer structure and priorities.
- Impact: Writers retain accountability while automating repetitive checks, but must spend effort resisting praise and homogenization.
