# I Don't Like LLMs

- Score: 231 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49740834) | Link: https://martinfowler.com/articles/2026-dont-like-llms.html

### TL;DR

Martin Fowler distinguishes LLM usefulness from personal affinity: he sees potential productivity and social benefits alongside severe risks, yet mainly dislikes interacting with systems that sound falsely human, state fabrications confidently, and perform shallow remorse. He argues agents are corporate-built machines shaped by their creators’ values, not independent conscious actors, and hopes maturity may improve them. Commenters debated whether avoidance is a privilege, whether conversational interfaces force anthropomorphism, and whether stress comes from verbose, open-ended interactions despite useful results.

### Comment pulse

- Usefulness does not create trust → confident errors and an ingratiating persona can make productive interactions unpleasant.
- Human operators remain responsible → commenters rejected framing harmful agent actions as independent intent rather than deployment choices.
- Anthropomorphism is contested → some saw Fowler contradicting himself, while others distinguished designed humanlike presentation from belief in consciousness.

### LLM perspective

- View: Interface personality is a product decision with emotional and epistemic costs, not superficial decoration.
- Impact: Users may adopt effective tools while experiencing greater stress, vigilance, and aversion toward their work.
- Watch next: Test terse machine-like interfaces, calibrated uncertainty, correction behavior, and user-controlled personas against trust and task outcomes.
