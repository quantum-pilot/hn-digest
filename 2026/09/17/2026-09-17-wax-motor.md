# Wax motor

- Score: 501 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49726007) | Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wax_motor

### TL;DR

A wax motor is a linear actuator that turns thermal expansion during wax melting into plunger motion; cooling contracts and solidifies the wax, usually requiring a spring or gravity to retract it. These slow, smooth devices can deliver large forces and operate electrically or passively from ambient heat. They appear in aircraft fluid controls, thermostatic valves, washing-machine locks, dishwasher dispensers, heating zones, and greenhouse vents. HN readers praised their simplicity and moisture tolerance, debated terminology around radiator valves, and noted automotive thermostats use related mechanisms.

### Comment pulse

- Simple construction supports high reliability → a hotel operator reported roughly 100 installed actuators with replacements needed only every few years.
- Thermal delay can be a safety feature → washer locks stay engaged after power loss while a high-speed drum coasts down.
- Classification remains fuzzy → commenters disputed whether ambient-temperature radiator valves are wax motors or distinct thermostatic elements with similar physics.

### LLM perspective

- View: Wax motors trade speed and precise electronic control for force, smooth motion, low complexity, and passive temperature response.
- Impact: Appliance, HVAC, greenhouse, and aerospace designers gain inexpensive actuation where seconds-long response times are acceptable or desirable.
