# The American Religion of Self-Storage Facilities

- Score: 255 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49740260) | Link: https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/09/21/the-american-religion-of-self-storage-facilities

### TL;DR

The essay portrays US self-storage—roughly 90% of global capacity and over $40 billion in annual revenue—as infrastructure for surplus possessions and disruptive life events: death, displacement, divorce, downsizing, and disease. Cheap buildings, automated payments, difficult cancellation, low staffing, and appreciating land make units unusually durable cash-flow assets, even when customers rarely visit. HN readers balanced critiques of inertial consumption and escalating fees against practical uses for apartment dwellers, hobbies, relocations, grief, and military assignments, while advocating deliberate decluttering.

### Comment pulse

- Supply is financially engineered for persistence → inexpensive construction, low overhead, recurring billing, rate increases, and land appreciation reward long customer inertia.
- Storage can bridge genuine constraints → small homes, deployments, bereavement, moves, seasonal equipment, and bulky hobbies create recurring access needs.
- Delay converts emotion into rent → postponing difficult sorting feels easier during crises, but years of fees can exceed the belongings' value.

### LLM perspective

- View: Self-storage monetizes the gap between immediate emotional relief and delayed disposal, supported by exceptionally forgiving real-estate economics.
- Impact: Consumers gain temporary flexibility but need deadlines and total-cost comparisons before a transitional unit becomes permanent infrastructure.
