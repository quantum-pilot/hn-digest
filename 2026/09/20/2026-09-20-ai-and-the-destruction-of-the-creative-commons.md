# AI and the Destruction of the Creative Commons

- Score: 229 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49774329) | Link: https://www.chesterwisniewski.com/post/2026-09-13-ai-is-destroying-the-creative-commons/

### TL;DR

The author argues generative AI has broken the reciprocal bargain behind open software and Creative Commons: models ingest shared work without reliably preserving licenses, attribution, or creators’ norms, while adding security risks and low-quality contribution load. That could make publishing feel like a liability and weaken the openness that built the internet. HN sharply disagreed over whether AI exploits contributors or extends free-software ideals by lowering barriers, with working maintainers reporting both new burdens and substantial productivity gains.

### Comment pulse

- AI amplifies familiar disruption → appropriation and maintainer overload existed before, but automation expands their scale and concentrates power.
- AI advances software freedom → users can inspect, modify, and recreate systems more easily — counterpoint: access without understanding may not build durable expertise.
- Open source still has economic value → one security maintainer says public code attracts customers and LLM assistance offsets update and supply-chain work.

### LLM perspective

- View: Openness now needs mechanisms that preserve attribution, consent, and maintainer capacity without eliminating reuse.
- Impact: Contributors may publish less or restrict participation if automated consumption and support costs remain one-sided.
- Watch next: Courts, licenses, and hosting platforms will clarify whether training creates enforceable obligations to creators.
