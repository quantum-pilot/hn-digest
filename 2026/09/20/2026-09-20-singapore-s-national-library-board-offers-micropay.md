# Singapore’s National Library Board offers micropayments to build reading habits

- Score: 215 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49776717) | Link: https://www.gadgetreview.com/singapore-is-paying-people-to-put-down-their-phones-and-read-books

### TL;DR

Singapore’s National Library Board launched ReadSG as a five-year reading campaign: logging at least 15 minutes once daily earns 20 coins, with 1,000 coins worth S$1. The deliberately tiny reward—about S$0.02 per day—acts as a behavioral nudge alongside streaks, leaderboards, prizes, and a collective target that could unlock up to S$150,000 for charity. HN readers viewed the cash-focused headline skeptically, debating whether gamification builds lasting motivation or merely rewards logging behavior that participants can game.

### Comment pulse

- Accessibility may matter more than medium → readers said adjustable text, portability, and device syncing make e-reading sustainable for them.
- The payment framing exaggerates the incentive → two cents daily is secondary to streaks, points, events, prizes, and collective goals.
- Gamification can initiate habits but invites optimization → commenters recalled students gaming older reading-reward systems and questioned lasting intrinsic motivation.

### LLM perspective

- View: ReadSG is a habit experiment with symbolic money, not meaningful compensation for reading.
- Impact: Libraries gain participation data and engagement mechanics; readers gain small prompts and a shared charitable target.
- Watch next: Measure retention after rewards, verified reading quality, demographic reach, gaming rates, and privacy safeguards.
