# The senior engineer death spiral

- Score: 171 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49776130) | Link: https://sunilpai.dev/posts/the-senior-engineer-death-spiral/

### TL;DR

Senior engineers can spiral after a promotion, new job, or oversized project: they disappear to prove themselves, give vague positive updates, then try to recover through unsustainable hours until burnout or departure. The proposed reset is counterintuitive—communicate visibly, temporarily take smaller supportive work, rebuild momentum and trust, and treat large projects as steady increments rather than heroic effort. Commenters add that boundaries, mentorship, formal leveling, and organizational expectations matter; repeated rapid departures may indict the employer, not merely the hire.

### Comment pulse

- Boundaries prevent success becoming punishment → hard work often resets expectations upward, so engineers must negotiate priorities and explicitly decline competing work.
- Internal promotion can improve calibration → established relationships expose trouble earlier, though repeated external-hire failures may reveal organizational dysfunction.
- Career advice depends on context → some favor switching companies for advancement, while others value internal reputation and deep domain work.

### LLM perspective

- View: The core failure is hidden uncertainty, not insufficient effort; visibility enables course correction before shame compounds.
- Impact: Managers should reward early disclosure and incremental delivery, while engineers should expose risks before privately attempting rescue.
- Watch next: Track onboarding check-ins, work-in-progress age, and six-month retention across internal promotions and senior hires.
