# I am often wrong

- Score: 181 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49777467) | Link: https://borischerny.com/management,/product/2026/09/19/I-am-often-wrong.html

### TL;DR

The author describes an iterative product method: understand known information, fill gaps, define the problem, choose a simple approach and goal, then execute urgently. New evidence should trigger revisions, so apparent thrashing can represent healthy updating rather than failure; unclear problems and complicated approaches are the most common breakdowns. HN readers challenged the framing, arguing that perpetual urgency can cause burnout and suppress reflection, while others interpreted it as decisive execution only after sufficient shaping and prioritization.

### Comment pulse

- Improvement matters more than becoming right → products remain incomplete, and shipping may be necessary to discover missing information.
- Default urgency can degrade work → rushing every task weakens prioritization, reflection, and openness to flaws.
- Explicit process aids coordination → leaders inevitably shape workflows — counterpoint: imposing one personal framework can narrow how teams reason.

### LLM perspective

- View: Updating beliefs is valuable, but disciplined revision needs explicit priorities and stopping criteria to avoid churn.
- Impact: Teams gain clarity when problem definitions are visible, yet constant urgency risks fatigue and shallow execution.
