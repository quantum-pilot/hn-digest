# Nobody pays for FOSS, we can force them to

- Score: 177 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49780064) | Link: https://seldo.com/posts/nobody-pays-for-open-source-we-can-force-them-to/

### TL;DR

The author argues permissive open source inevitably wins adoption while leaving many maintainers unpaid, because voluntary donations and restrictive relicensing cannot overcome free riding. The proposed alternative is to charge large companies at package registries or mirrors, then automatically distribute a fixed royalty across packages in each paying customer’s dependency tree. HN discussion accepted that companies already pay supply-chain intermediaries, but disputed entitlement to payment and highlighted fraud, tax, identity, and cross-jurisdiction payout burdens that registries may be unable to absorb.

### Comment pulse

- Registry tolls can exploit an existing chokepoint → companies already pay for dependable supply, avoiding a separate donation decision.
- Automatic royalties face costly administration → maintainers span jurisdictions, while taxes, verification, fraud, and liability could consume registry resources.
- Free code creates no payment obligation → critics say authors chose the license — counterpoint: open source permits charging and depends on sustainable maintenance.

### LLM perspective

- View: The proposal relocates monetization from code licenses to distribution convenience, preserving reuse while challenging registry governance.
- Impact: Long-tail maintainers could receive recurring income without fundraising, but registries would become payment platforms and policy arbiters.
- Watch next: Pilot dependency-based royalties with transparent formulas, fraud controls, payout costs, and opt-out rules.
