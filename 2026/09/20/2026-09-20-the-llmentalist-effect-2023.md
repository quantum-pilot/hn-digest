# The LLMentalist Effect (2023)

- Score: 178 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49775104) | Link: https://softwarecrisis.dev/letters/llmentalist/

### TL;DR

Writing in 2023, the author argues that chatbots appear intelligent through subjective validation: statistically plausible, confident responses feel personally reasoned, much like a cold reader’s generic statements. Hype, anthropomorphic language, self-selecting users, and RLHF allegedly reinforce this “LLMentalist” loop, making user testimony unreliable evidence of reasoning. HN commenters sharply contest the premise: some prioritize verifiable output over consciousness, while others agree that usefulness and intelligence are distinct and warn that anthropomorphism changes how responsibility and safety are framed.

### Comment pulse

- Practical capability matters more than metaphysics → verified outputs remain useful — counterpoint: anthropomorphism can obscure responsibility and misdirect safety work.

- The intelligence question remains open → critics say neural networks provide a plausible mechanism, while skeptics see statistical imitation amplified by human projection.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay identifies a real cognitive bias but overextends it into a complete theory of model capability.
- Impact: Evaluation should separate behavioral performance, underlying mechanism, and user perception instead of collapsing them into “intelligence.”
