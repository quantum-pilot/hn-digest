# US Revokes Limits on Power Plants' Climate Pollution

- Score: 236 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49777841) | Link: https://text.hrw.org/news/2026/09/17/us-revokes-limits-on-power-plants-climate-pollution

### TL;DR

Human Rights Watch reports that the EPA repealed most 2024 carbon limits for existing coal and new gas plants, which had required 90% capture or closure by 2039. Biden-era estimates credited those rules with avoiding 1.38 billion metric tons of emissions through 2047 and preventing 1,200 deaths and 360,000 asthma attacks in 2035. EPA projects $370 million in regulatory savings but omitted health costs. HN discussion overwhelmingly condemns the move as economically, strategically, and environmentally shortsighted.

### Comment pulse

- Repeal sacrifices future competitiveness → commenters argue falling renewable costs and energy security favor solar, wind, and batteries over fossil investment.
- Public costs dwarf private savings → pollution and climate damage remain externalized while plant owners retain benefits.
- Existing pollutant controls still matter → one commenter notes sulfur-dioxide restrictions have not yet been relaxed.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central accounting dispute is whether avoided compliance costs can be separated from health and climate damages.
- Impact: Utilities gain regulatory flexibility, while nearby communities and future ratepayers may inherit greater environmental risk.
- Watch next: Follow final greenhouse-gas requirements, court challenges, plant-retirement decisions, and independently modeled health effects.
