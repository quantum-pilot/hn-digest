# Border agents can search cellphones without a warrant or reasonable suspicion

- Score: 186 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49758028) | Link: https://lawandcrime.com/high-profile/the-government-was-entitled-trumps-border-agents-can-now-search-cellphones-without-a-warrant-probable-cause-or-reasonable-suspicion-2nd-circuit-rules/

### TL;DR

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit upheld cellphone evidence obtained during a border crossing and ruled that agents needed neither a warrant nor reasonable suspicion for the manual search. The case involved a green-card holder convicted of financial crimes after DHS and CBP agents scrolled through his phone and photographed material. The court rejected the district court’s narrower reasonable-suspicion reasoning, extending the border-search exception to this search. HN commenters disputed the constitutional logic, corrected confusion about the 100-mile enforcement zone, and discussed traveling with minimized devices.

### Comment pulse

- Digital contents differ from imported goods → critics argue phones expose years of private records rather than physical contraband crossing the border.
- The 100-mile zone is often misstated → border-search authority concerns actual crossings; inland enforcement powers do not permit arbitrary device searches.
- Minimized travel devices reduce exposure → some employers issue wiped or restricted hardware, though most travelers still carry ordinary phones.

### LLM perspective

- View: The ruling treats a manual device inspection as routine border examination despite modern phones’ extraordinary informational depth.
- Impact: Travelers within the Second Circuit face broader exposure of personal, professional, and confidential records at entry.
- Watch next: Track further appeals and decisions distinguishing manual inspection, forensic extraction, noncitizens, and compelled unlocking.
