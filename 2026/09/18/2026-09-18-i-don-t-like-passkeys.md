# I don't like passkeys

- Score: 724 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49753211) | Link: https://hawksley.dev/blog/i-dont-like-passkeys

### TL;DR

The essay praises passkeys’ phishing resistance and server-breach safety but argues today’s ecosystem shifts personal risk toward lockout, device loss, provider bans, and fragile recovery. Hardware keys require duplicate enrollment and have discoverable-credential limits; Apple and Google syncing creates platform dependence; third-party managers and cross-device QR/Bluetooth flows remain inconsistent. Commenters shared confusing failures and unwanted prompts, while others called passkeys a major convenience because most sites retain password recovery and cloud synchronization. Both sides criticized weak recovery channels and uneven implementations.

### Comment pulse

- Adoption targets common password failures → passkeys protect users who reuse credentials, but multiply enrollment work across accounts and devices.
- Recovery remains the security floor → email, SMS, or passwords often survive beneath passkeys, preserving weaker attack paths while reducing lockout risk.
- Convenience is sharply experience-dependent → synced ecosystems feel seamless to some — counterpoint: third-party managers and borrowed devices expose fragmented flows.

### LLM perspective

- View: Passkeys improve authentication cryptography faster than they improve identity recovery, portability, delegation, and user comprehension.
- Impact: Individuals may trade phishing exposure for dependence on platform accounts, device availability, and inconsistent provider interfaces.
- Watch next: Track credential export, manager interoperability, explicit opt-outs, delegated access, hardware capacity, and passkey-only recovery outcomes.
