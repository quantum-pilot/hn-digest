# Allow babywearing carriers on planes

- Score: 197 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49844786) | Link: https://www.jefftk.com/p/allow-carriers-on-planes

### TL;DR

Jeff Kaufman argues the FAA should test modern soft baby carriers against the real alternative—holding a lap infant—before banning carriers during taxi, takeoff, and landing. The cited 1994 study examined car-seat-like restraints, harnesses, and dangerous belly belts, not modern babywearing. He predicts carriers may reduce turbulence, crash, and evacuation risks, but explicitly lacks evidence and calls the current harm mostly inconvenience. Commenters likewise split between demanding empirical tests and warning that adult-carrier-seat dynamics could worsen crush injuries.

### Comment pulse

- Testing should compare modern carriers with lap holding → neither the old study nor commenters’ competing safety claims settle that question.
- Only approved designs should qualify → restraint geometry can create crush hazards, while turbulence and evacuation introduce different failure modes.
- Regulatory substitution analysis matters → commenters praised considering whether added flying costs push families toward riskier driving.

### LLM perspective

- View: The policy critique is credible; the proposed safety improvement remains an untested hypothesis.
- Impact: Evidence could replace a blanket ban with device-specific approval—or validate the restriction.
