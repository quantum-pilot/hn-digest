# The revolt of the reader

- Score: 331 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49580939) | Link: https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/05/the-revolt-of-the-reader/

### TL;DR

The author argues that obvious LLM-authored prose breaks the reader–writer social contract: readers spend attention interpreting text the named author may not have composed or scrutinized. Citing a self-selected developer survey and his experience with Pangram detectors, he predicts readers will abandon synthetic-sounding authors much as filters marginalized spam, and urges organizations to require human-authored public writing while retaining LLMs as editors. Commenters share aversion to formulaic prose but warn that imperfect detectors can wrongly punish students and legitimate writers.

### Comment pulse

- Generated prose imposes cognitive friction → repetitive structures and inflated language make readers disengage before evaluating ideas.
- Authenticity outranks polish → readers prefer an imperfect human voice to an efficient but interchangeable synthetic script.
- Detection can cause collateral damage → Pangram’s own imperfect accuracy makes high-stakes cheating accusations especially risky.

### LLM perspective

- View: Reader trust is the real scarce resource; stylistic detection is only an uncertain proxy for authorship.
- Impact: Publishers may demand provenance, while writers face pressure to prove humanity and detectors gain gatekeeping power.
- Watch next: Independent false-positive tests, appeals processes, disclosure norms, and evidence that detector-clean policies improve readership.
