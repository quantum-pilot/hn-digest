# I have a theory that software drives people insane

- Score: 439 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49646181) | Link: https://graybeard.ing/software-drives-people-insane/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that software’s mutability, abstraction, complexity, speed, and potential financial upside distort organizational judgment. Because changes look cheap and products never feel finished, every idea can become urgent, technical choices become ideological, and complexity generates status and more work. The proposed corrective is proportion: distinguish “could” from “should,” resist unnecessary pivots and rewrites, and let useful systems remain stable. Commenters emphasized direct customer contact, smaller aligned teams, and reduced toolchain churn as grounding mechanisms.

### Comment pulse

- Customer contact restores proportion → Direct observation reveals small, dependable fixes and filters out imagined needs.
- Tech churn destroys leverage → Stable dependencies once let smaller teams focus on algorithms and domain problems.
- Ego compounds complexity → Rebuilds and platforms can reward internal status even when simple software already works.

- View: Software enables pathological motion because action is visible while the compounding cost of change stays hidden.
