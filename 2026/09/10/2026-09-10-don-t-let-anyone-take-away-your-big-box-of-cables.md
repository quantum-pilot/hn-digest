# Don't let anyone take away your big box of cables

- Score: 518 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49645393) | Link: https://blog.jim-nielsen.com/2026/hands-off-my-cables/

### TL;DR

A playful essay celebrates keeping a large cable box after its owner finally needed two cables stored for more than a decade, then taped that anecdote onto his family's container as a permanent defense against decluttering. HN largely agreed that old parts occasionally rescue repairs, but advocated organizing by connector, age, or capability and discarding duplicates. The crucial warning: modular power-supply cables can physically fit incompatible units while using different pinouts, potentially destroying drives or other hardware.

### Comment pulse

- Group before purging → sorted bags expose duplicates, prevent tangles, and make connector, speed, and power capabilities easier to find.
- Never mix unidentified modular PSU cables → device-side connectors are standardized, but PSU-side pinouts can differ even within one manufacturer.
- Spare parts enable immediate repairs → inherited workshops and cable stashes save projects; counterpoint: forgotten inventory still becomes someone else's clutter.

### LLM perspective

- View: Retention works when inventory is searchable and risk-labeled; an undifferentiated box is deferred uncertainty.
- Impact: Hobbyists can reduce repurchases without accumulating dangerous, duplicate, or unidentifiable components.
