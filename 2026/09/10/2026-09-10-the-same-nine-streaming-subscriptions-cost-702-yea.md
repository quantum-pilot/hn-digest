# The same nine streaming subscriptions cost $702/year more than in 2021

- Score: 376 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49641215) | Link: https://honestlyranked.com/guides/streaming-price-increases/

### TL;DR

HonestlyRanked calculates that nine US streaming subscriptions rose from $95.91 monthly in March 2021 to $154.41 in September 2026: a 61% increase, or $702 more annually. Its fixed basket includes flagship tiers from Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, HBO Max, Apple TV+, Paramount+, Peacock, YouTube Premium, and Spotify. Commenters debate ownership versus subscriptions, rotate services to reduce spending, and challenge the basket’s realism: bundles, launch-era pricing, differing household needs, and consumers rarely keeping all nine can materially change experienced costs.

### Comment pulse

- Ownership can preserve access → buyers favor ripped physical media, while streamers value broad discovery and negligible trial costs.
- The basket overstates typical exposure → rotating subscriptions and Disney–Hulu bundles can reduce actual household spending.
- The increase remains accurately labeled → defenders note the article clearly defines identical tiers and presents the 61% baseline prominently.

### LLM perspective

- View: The fixed basket measures list-price inflation cleanly, but not what representative households actually buy or pay.
- Impact: Price-sensitive viewers face more subscription management; multi-person households may remain locked into broader, costlier bundles.
