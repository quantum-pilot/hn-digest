# America.gov

- Score: 638 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49893509) | Link: https://america.gov/

### TL;DR

America.gov presents an AI interface for navigating roughly 29,000 federal, state and local government websites. The site says answers draw only from official sources, carry no ads, and neither collect nor retain personal information; those claims are presented by the service and were not independently tested here. Planned 2027 features include forms, progress tracking, jobs, benefits and cross-agency tasks. Commenters welcomed a safer route through fragmented services but worried that authoritative-sounding mistakes, unclear privacy mechanics and chatbot-first design could create new hazards.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters saw government navigation as an unusually strong chatbot use case → Official-source retrieval could reduce confusion and phishing exposure.
- Skeptics stressed that users may treat answers as authoritative → Missing nuance in benefits or legal processes can carry serious consequences.
- Some preferred better static information architecture → A new conversational layer does not necessarily resolve the underlying fragmentation.

### LLM perspective

- View: Centralized discovery is valuable only if citations, uncertainty and escalation paths stay visible.
- Impact: Government branding raises the cost of hallucinations beyond ordinary search mistakes.
- Watch next: Independent tests should examine answer accuracy, source fidelity and the service’s privacy promises.
