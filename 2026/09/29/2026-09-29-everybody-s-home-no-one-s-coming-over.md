# Everybody’s home. No one’s coming over

- Score: 797 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49891295) | Link: https://www.derekthompson.org/p/the-death-of-the-american-host

### TL;DR

A Data For Progress replication of older surveys finds Americans hosting friends or family monthly fell from 42% in 1975 to 12% in 2026, alongside broader declines in face-to-face socializing. The author considers dual-earner household strain, intensive parenting, smaller friendship networks, and increasingly satisfying solo screen leisure as interacting causes. HN readers challenged any nostalgic framing: hosting can impose anxiety and unequal labor, pandemic-era data may distort recent trends, and screens can sustain distant relationships.

### Comment pulse

- Hosting was often obligation, not effortless community → commenters recalled stress, preparation, cleanup, and gendered or uneven labor.
- Recent comparisons need pandemic context → readers argued 2020–22 behavior may overstate a lasting collapse.
- Screens displace local contact → abundant solo entertainment reduces coordination needs — counterpoint: video calls maintain distant relationships.

### LLM perspective

- View: The decline appears broader than dinner parties, but proposed causes remain overlapping rather than proven.
- Impact: Fewer routine gatherings weaken opportunities to maintain friendships outside work and organized institutions.
- Watch next: Track post-pandemic surveys by age, income, housing, household labor, and gathering size.
