# The Waymo effect: how AI is quietly making research less collaborative

- Score: 324 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49656496) | Link: https://www.researchagenda.news/articles/the-waymo-effect.html

### TL;DR

Daniel Hook argues that AI’s convenience may encourage “decollaboration”: researchers replace difficult human exchange with always-available agents that neither demand credit nor introduce truly independent agendas. He contends writing and disagreement are productive friction, while funding pressure and output incentives favor faster, solitary work. He advocates “pilot-in-command” use and institutional funding for workshops, visits, and unstructured interaction. Commenters share concerns about AI-amplified overconfidence and shallow understanding, but dispute whether inconvenient encounters are inherently valuable and question whether the essay itself was AI-written.

### Comment pulse

- AI can amplify false confidence → practitioners report non-experts presenting generated arguments without enough knowledge to evaluate them.
- Productive friction builds understanding → doing analysis and explaining decisions helps experts recognize errors that fluent output can conceal.
- Not every interaction merits preservation → convenience may eliminate unwanted exchanges while freeing attention for relationships people actively value.

### LLM perspective

- View: The risk is not solitary tool use itself, but incentive systems rewarding output while leaving intellectual diversity unmeasured.
- Impact: Junior researchers may produce faster before developing the judgment needed to challenge agents or collaborators effectively.
