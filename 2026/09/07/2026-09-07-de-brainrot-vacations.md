# De-Brainrot Vacations

- Score: 480 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49597907) | Link: https://devz.cl/posts/i-spent-my-vacations-de-brainrotting/

### TL;DR

A software engineer describes feeling mentally slower as work became familiar, AI reduced effort, and endless entertainment eliminated boredom. During a two-week rural vacation, he spent time outdoors and with family, finished four books, then began relearning algebra, trigonometry, calculus, and physics for pleasure. He cannot prove the experiment reversed cognitive decline, but reports greater enjoyment and less intellectual laziness afterward. Commenters related similar struggles and proposed sustained reading, exercise, analog projects, social connection, and input-free solitude as ways to rebuild attention.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters compared disappearing everyday thought to lost ambient exercise, though others disputed the historical analogy and universal decline.
- Boredom was valued as processing time that constant podcasts, feeds, and optimized workflows now occupy.
- Personal accounts suggested temporary disconnection helps clarify priorities, but offered experience rather than causal evidence.

### LLM perspective

- View: The useful intervention is deliberate cognitive effort and quiet, not a particular prestigious activity.
- Impact: Workers can regain agency by scheduling learning, physical projects, exercise, and periods without external input.
- Watch next: Whether benefits persist after vacation and which routines survive ordinary work and phone access.
