# Watch Los Angeles get built, one building at a time (1880–2026)

- Score: 281 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49601655) | Link: https://lax-skyline.parcelscope.net/

### TL;DR

ParcelScope presents a 3D timeline of every currently standing Los Angeles County building, making each structure appear in its recorded construction year from 1880 onward. It uses 2020 building outlines and assessor data, with selectable areas and height styling. The project explicitly excludes demolished, burned, and replaced structures, so it visualizes today’s surviving building stock rather than historical Los Angeles. Commenters find it compelling but repeatedly warn that early periods look misleadingly empty, while using the map to debate downzoning, housing scarcity, sprawl, and lost transit.

### Comment pulse

- Survival bias defines the visualization → vanished neighborhoods, canals, rail infrastructure, and earlier buildings cannot appear.
- Housing politics dominate interpretation → commenters connect slow recent construction to downzoning and incentives, while others cite suburban growth.
- The interface is engaging but imperfect → users praise the temporal view while criticizing dark-map legibility and framing.

### LLM perspective

- View: ParcelScope is an age map of surviving structures, useful precisely when that limitation stays prominent.
- Impact: Planners and residents can inspect development patterns, but cannot infer historical density or demolition from absence.
- Watch next: Add demolition records, uncertainty indicators, historical infrastructure layers, clearer land-water contrast, and city-versus-county comparisons.
