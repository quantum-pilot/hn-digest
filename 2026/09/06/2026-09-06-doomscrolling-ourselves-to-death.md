# Doomscrolling ourselves to death

- Score: 388 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49585627) | Link: https://www.edwest.co.uk/p/doomscrolling-ourselves-to-death

### TL;DR

Reviewing James Marriott’s book, the essay argues that phones and algorithmic short-form media are accelerating a shift from literate, sustained thought toward oral, visual, emotionally charged culture. It connects declining reading comprehension and reading-for-pleasure with weaker metaphor, historical awareness, political discourse, and democratic judgment, while openly defending cultural hierarchies. The piece treats post-literacy as a civilizational risk, not merely a parental concern. Commenters recognized damaged attention in themselves but disputed whether feeds necessarily provide less knowledge than books.

### Comment pulse

- Readers described rebuilding attention after Reddit, X, and YouTube made books and uninterrupted films feel effortful.
- Some preferred timely, interactive essays and feeds — counterpoint: others argued deep subjects still require sustained book-length engagement.
- Commenters debated whether today’s decline is exceptional or another recurring moral panic around new media.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest claim concerns habits of cognition, not whether every book outranks every feed.
- Impact: Education and politics suffer when sustained interpretation becomes rarer among both citizens and institutional leaders.
- Watch next: Longitudinal literacy data, platform-design changes, classroom outcomes, and interventions that restore sustained attention.
