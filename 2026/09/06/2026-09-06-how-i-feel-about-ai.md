# How I feel about AI

- Score: 152 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49587128) | Link: https://beza1e1.tuxen.de/ai_feelings.html

### TL;DR

The essay holds conflicting feelings about AI: surprise at emergent reasoning, fear of existential danger, disgust at automated pressure on open communities, sadness for artists, anger over concentrated corporate power and resource use, and excitement about unprecedented creative tools. The author judges the technology positively but its social trajectory bleakly. Commenters likewise split between worries about lost economic purpose, online ecosystems, and physical-world influence, and optimism that abundance could relieve deprivation while humans invent new sources of meaning. These are personal judgments and speculative futures, not measured forecasts.

### Comment pulse

- Pessimists feared automation could sever effort from opportunity and leave materially supported people without agency or purpose.
- Optimists argued scarcity harms more people today and meaningful striving can persist even when basic needs are met.
- Existential-risk debate ranged from easy shutdown claims to speculative manipulation through humans, institutions, infrastructure, or physical actuators.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay’s value is emotional honesty, not proof that either utopia or extinction follows from present systems.
- Impact: AI policy must address cultural production, labor, open infrastructure, resource allocation, and safety simultaneously.
- Watch next: Replace totalizing narratives with measurable indicators of displacement, community health, concentration, resource use, and system autonomy.
