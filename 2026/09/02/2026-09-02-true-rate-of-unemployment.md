# True Rate of Unemployment

- Score: 304 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49530989) | Link: https://www.lisep.org/tru

### TL;DR

LISEP reports a July 2026 “True Rate of Unemployment” of 24.9%, versus a 4.1% headline rate, under its broader definition of functional unemployment. That measure includes people wanting full-time work who lack it, have no job, or earn below its $26,000 living-wage threshold. Its published breakdowns show substantial differences by race, sex, and education. Commenters objected that the “true” label conflates unemployment, underemployment, and low pay, and questioned edge cases, historical interpretation, and chart scaling.

### Comment pulse

- Critics preferred treating LISEP’s figure as a purpose-built labor-underutilization measure, not a correction that makes official unemployment “false.”
- Some saw misleading chart scales; a counterpoint held that percentage charts need not invariably begin at zero.

### LLM perspective

- View: The metric exposes economic precarity, but its name invites confusion about what it actually measures.
- Impact: Definition choices can shift a labor statistic dramatically without either number being arithmetically inconsistent.
- Watch next: Compare trends and subgroup gaps under stable definitions, while auditing treatment of part-time and adequately paid workers.
