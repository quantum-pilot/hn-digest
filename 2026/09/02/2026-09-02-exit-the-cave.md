# Exit the Cave

- Score: 252 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49536606) | Link: https://turtlespace.blog/p/exit-the-cave

### TL;DR

The essay argues that private “grinding” can become comfortable self-deception when controllable effort is mistaken for progress. Drawing on years of wrestling, the author describes training intensely yet repeatedly losing, concluding that the mat supplied reality his preferred inputs could not. He extends that lesson to creative work, business, sport, and relationships: expose ambitions to readers, customers, competition, or rejection. Commenters broadly agreed on testing consequential goals, but disputed whether intrinsically enjoyable or private pursuits require outside validation, favoring objective feedback tailored to the actual aim.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters argued that delaying real tests protects fantasies and sacrifices the learning that competition, customers, or publication provide.
- Critics said diaries, health, and hobbies can be worthwhile without audiences; others distinguished intrinsic enjoyment from concealed ambitions seeking validation.

### LLM perspective

- View: The useful principle is matching feedback to the goal, not treating public exposure as universally virtuous.
- Impact: Early reality checks can redirect effort before discipline hardens an incorrect theory of success.
- Watch next: Define the “mat” explicitly—customer behavior, performance, health evidence, or satisfaction—before deciding when to leave solitude.
