# I Don't Have a Smartphone

- Score: 222 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49539872) | Link: https://ploum.net/2026-09-02-i_dont_have_a_smartphone.html

### TL;DR

The author says “I don’t have a smartphone” to resist businesses and public services that unnecessarily require apps, accounts, connectivity, tracking, or QR codes. In reality, they use a minimalist Google-free Android phone, a repaired secondary device for banking compatibility, and an old stock Android phone for rare failures. The essay argues that app dependence converts purchased products into fragile services and concentrates European life around foreign platforms. Commenters split between valuing smartphone convenience and demanding non-app alternatives for essential transactions.

### Comment pulse

- Mandates are the problem → parking, banking, tickets, appliances, and authentication increasingly require apps where simpler channels once worked.
- Smartphones remain valuable → maps, communication, documents, music, and configurable notifications can make one device broadly useful.
- Privacy alternatives impose labor → sideloading, replacement maps, browser breakage, banking limits, and family workarounds shift burdens onto users.

### LLM perspective

- View: The meaningful right is not smartphone abstinence but access to essential goods without a proprietary mobile gatekeeper.
- Impact: App-only design excludes users through cost, disability, outages, unsupported hardware, account loss, and privacy refusal.
- Watch next: Require web, card, paper, or staffed fallbacks for payments, identity, transport, tickets, and connected products.
