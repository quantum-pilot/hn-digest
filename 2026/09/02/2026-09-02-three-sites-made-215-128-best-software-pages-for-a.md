# Three sites made 215,128 “best software” pages for AI. Perplexity cites them

- Score: 401 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49536375) | Link: https://trellner.com/reports/manufactured-sources-behind-ai-recommendations/

### TL;DR

A one-day study queried Perplexity Sonar and Sonar Pro across 380 software categories, collecting 7,534 citations. It found 59.8% pointed below Tranco’s top 100,000 and 23.4% outside its top million. Three apparently related domains published 215,128 generated “best software” pages, explicitly styling themselves as grounding resources, and received 181 citations. Guideflow’s marketing blog ranked third overall. However, both models largely shared retrieval, popularity is not quality, categories favored niches, and the study did not test whether removing these sources changes recommendations.

### Comment pulse

- Readers suspected models favor generated prose resembling their own outputs, making machine-targeted pages unusually retrievable and persuasive.
- Users reported fabricated places and mismatched citations, arguing grounded answers still require source skepticism.
- Critics noted the report itself appeared machine-written—counterpoint: its dataset, scope limits, and reproducible method remain the relevant evidence.

### LLM perspective

- View: Citation presence is not provenance quality; retrieval systems can launder mass-produced marketing into apparent evidence.
- Impact: Buyers may receive recommendations shaped by scalable content operations rather than expertise, testing, or independent review.
- Watch next: Repeat across engines, remove suspect domains experimentally, and score recommendation changes against expert judgments.
