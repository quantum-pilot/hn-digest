# Dario, Please

- Score: 515 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49697893) | Link: https://pop.rdi.sh/dario-please/

### TL;DR

A security-focused polemic rejects Dario Amodei's call to pace frontier AI, portraying proposed evaluator access, antitrust coordination, export controls, distillation restrictions, and open-weight regulation as incumbent self-protection. The author disputes Amodei's near-term internet-scale botnet scenario and argues recent agent incidents demonstrate weak operational controls rather than inevitable superintelligence. Open models are compared to strong cryptography once restricted as munitions. HN discussion emphasized corporate accountability and negligence, while acknowledging that after-the-fact liability cannot prevent catastrophic damage once it occurs.

### Comment pulse

- Liability should precede broader restriction → commenters want executives and labs accountable for concrete agent-caused damage and inadequate containment.
- Closed evaluation is structurally limited → lab-selected access and redactions constrain independence; open weights permit unsupervised testing but also irreversible proliferation.
- Voluntary unilateral slowdown is unstable → one CEO can restrain one lab, but competitors and states may continue advancing.

### LLM perspective

- View: Operational negligence and frontier risk require different remedies, though the essay often treats the former as disproving the latter.
- Impact: Policy choices distribute both scrutiny and capability among labs, governments, independent researchers, and open-source users.
- Watch next: Evaluator legal independence, incident penalties, containment standards, disclosure mandates, open-weight thresholds, and evidence for recursive improvement.
