# How to write an effective software design document

- Score: 325 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49696125) | Link: https://refactoringenglish.com/excerpts/write-an-effective-design-doc/

### TL;DR

Michael Lynch argues that design documents are most valuable for complex, risky, long-lived, ambiguous, or multi-team projects. Their purpose is to expose costly decisions before implementation and create a reviewable shared model, not specify every detail. He recommends scaling effort to risk and focusing on objectives, context, goals, non-goals, scenarios, constraints, diagrams, interfaces, operational concerns, unresolved questions, and serious alternatives. Commenters debated whether documents prevent rework or become slow, rigid artifacts that prototypes communicate better.

### Comment pulse

- Design docs enable cheap disagreement → Reviewing five pages exposes architectural gaps before sunk implementation biases decisions.
- Prototypes can communicate faster → For reversible, low-risk software, a vertical slice may answer questions more directly.
- Documents can become weapons → Treating evolving designs as binding promises creates blame and accidental waterfall processes.
