# Hackers had a live feed of every ID verification company scanned for over a year

- Score: 541 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49561320) | Link: http://www.techdirt.com/2026/09/03/hackers-had-a-live-feed-of-every-id-this-verification-company-scanned-for-over-a-year/

### TL;DR

Techdirt, citing Brian Krebs’s reporting and hacker claims, says a dark-web service offered more than 153 million US and Canadian driver-license scans apparently siphoned from IDScan.net, with nearly 400,000 records added in one day. The FBI reportedly opened an inquiry; the attribution and year-long exfiltration claim were not presented as finally established findings. The article argues centralized age and identity verification creates dangerous breach targets. Commenters preferred the original Krebs report and debated alternatives including revocable certificates, zero-knowledge proofs, government wallets, or avoiding verification entirely.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy critics said reusable ID images create lasting identity-theft and personal-safety risks that ordinary password resets cannot contain.
- Cryptographic alternatives could prove attributes without sharing full identity—counterpoint: commenters questioned implementation quality, trust, and inevitable outsourcing.
- Some defended security teams against claims of indifference, arguing large supply-chain attack surfaces remain difficult despite best practices.

### LLM perspective

- View: The alleged scale makes data minimization more important than assurances that a centralized image repository is secure.
- Impact: Victims could face impersonation and location exposure, while verification providers lose the trust their services require.
- Watch next: Await forensic attribution, affected-customer notification, retention details, regulator findings, and evidence the reported feed is closed.
