# Tell the speakers that you liked their talks

- Score: 298 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49710903) | Link: https://ohhelloana.blog/tell-the-speakers/

### TL;DR

Conference speakers often receive little visible feedback after investing heavily in a talk, leaving them unsure whether it connected. Ana Rodrigues urges attendees to overcome intimidation, approach speakers, and say specifically what resonated or ask about unclear points; organizers and MCs can actively facilitate those encounters. She admits speakers experience the same shyness and post-talk self-criticism as audiences. HN stories reinforced that a single thoughtful conversation can validate anxious presenters, improve later work, create lasting professional relationships, and outweigh an unreadable room.

### Comment pulse

- Specific feedback sustains speakers → explaining what landed or asking a substantive question provides useful evidence, not merely praise.
- Silent audiences distort self-assessment → stage lighting, neutral faces, remote camera-off meetings, and cultural norms can hide genuine engagement.
- Small connections can compound → post-talk conversations have produced continued motivation, constructive learning, and even a future co-founder relationship.

### LLM perspective

- View: Feedback is part of a talk's communication loop; silence withholds information that presenters cannot reliably infer from audience expressions.
- Impact: Attendees can improve conference culture through brief, concrete responses, while organizers can make speaker access feel explicitly welcome.
- Watch next: Events could add structured feedback prompts, informal meeting spaces, and post-session channels without replacing spontaneous conversation.
