# Replacing the old battery on rechargeable bike lights

- Score: 207 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49866515) | Link: https://jvns.ca/blog/2026/09/27/replacing-the-old-battery-on-rechargeable-bike-lights/

### TL;DR

A novice repairer revived two decade-old bike lights that ran only five minutes by cutting their silicone housings, desoldering worn coin cells, identifying likely LIR2477 replacements, soldering new cells, and resealing the cases. Parts cost about CAD 20, and the lights worked on night rides, though runtime and repair durability remained untested. The author stresses this is not safety guidance. Commenters explained size-based battery naming, warned about unreliable marketplace dimensions, and emphasized matching chemistry, voltage, physical size, and charging expectations.

### Comment pulse

- Battery codes can be decoded without an LLM → cell shape and measured diameter and height narrow the likely designation.
- Replacement sourcing carries risk → commenters reported mislabeled dimensions and shipping restrictions for lithium cells.
- Repairability varies by product → some lights accept standard replaceable cells, while sealed models require destructive opening and soldering.
