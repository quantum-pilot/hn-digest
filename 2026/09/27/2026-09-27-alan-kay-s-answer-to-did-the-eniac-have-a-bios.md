# Alan Kay's answer to “Did the ENIAC have a BIOS”?

- Score: 185 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49870070) | Link: https://www.quora.com/Did-the-ENIAC-have-a-BIOS/answer/Alan-Kay-11

### TL;DR

Alan Kay says ENIAC had neither a BIOS nor a meaningful analogue, and he does not consider its original design a stored-program computer. He separates BIOS’s firmware definition from its practical role: many later machines cold-started with switch-entered instructions that loaded code from paper tape. Kay cites 1975 CP/M as the first officially named BIOS and calls it ROM-based, a detail requiring caution. Commenters supplied earlier boot mechanisms and challenged his ENIAC classification, noting its postwar adaptation while disputing how fully that resembled later stored-program machines.
