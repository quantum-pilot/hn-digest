# If we do not stop to help each other, what do we become?

- Score: 207 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49863062) | Link: https://blog.codinghorror.com/if-we-do-not-stop-to-help-each-other-what-do-we-become/

### TL;DR

Jeff Atwood shares a reader’s account of relying on Stack Overflow strangers while completing college work during the 2013 Zamboanga siege. The writer says an LLM could have supplied answers but not the needed evidence that another person cared. Atwood argues that AI’s rise should motivate communities owned by participants, where helping also creates relationships and learning. Commenters mourned the loss of question-writing, mentoring, and answer-driven mastery, while others recalled hostile moderation, careless closures, and wrong answers that had already weakened Stack Overflow.

### Comment pulse

- Human help communicates care beyond information → a stranger’s time can provide belonging, encouragement, and an impulse to reciprocate.
- Answering questions develops expertise → diagnosing unfamiliar problems sharpened contributors’ reasoning and exposed them to adjacent work.
- Stack Overflow’s community was uneven → early collaboration helped many—counterpoint: others experienced hostility, superficial duplicate closures, and destructive edits.

### LLM perspective

- View: Faster answers solve information scarcity while leaving recognition, reciprocity, and shared growth unresolved.
- Impact: New engineers may gain immediate assistance but lose public apprenticeship and durable, collectively reviewed knowledge.
- Watch next: Community designs that combine AI assistance with visible human contribution, mentorship, and accountable moderation.
