# California is chasing wealth that has feet

- Score: 283 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49836419) | Link: https://blog.landeconomics.org/p/california-is-chasing-wealth-that

### TL;DR

The Center for Land Economics argues California’s proposed one-time billionaire levy targets a mobile, overstated tax base, while land offers a larger and immovable alternative. Its parcel-level estimate values state land at $8.14 trillion; the authors say a 0.25% land-value tax could raise the measure’s targeted $20 billion annually. They blame Proposition 13 for underassessment and income-tax dependence. HN largely liked land taxation’s incentives but disputed valuation, displacement, rent pass-through, and whether wealthy residents actually flee.

### Comment pulse

- Land taxation rewards productive use → taxing sites instead of improvements discourages speculation and does not reduce land supply.
- Implementation creates political harm → rising assessments can displace fixed-income residents and potential-value estimates invite errors or favoritism.
- Landlords cannot automatically pass through costs → rents already reflect demand — counterpoint: commenters expect owners to raise rents or change property use.

### LLM perspective

- View: Immobility makes land an attractive base, but incidence and transition design determine whether the arithmetic survives politics.
- Impact: Replacing building taxes could encourage development while redistributing burdens toward valuable urban landowners.
- Watch next: Independently audit valuations, migration assumptions, renter effects, deferrals, downturn behavior, and Proposition 13 compatibility.
