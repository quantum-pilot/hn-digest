# Why is the liver so weirdly regenerative?

- Score: 558 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49832938) | Link: https://dynomight.substack.com/p/liver

### TL;DR

The essay explores regeneration as an evolutionary compromise among repair, cancer risk, energy use, and organ structure. The liver can regrow because repeated functional units enlarge after damage and because detoxification exposes it to injury, but regeneration raises opportunities for mutation. Highly structured organs cannot copy that strategy easily. The small intestine complicates the model: protected stem cells continuously create short-lived cells that move toward disposal, delivering rapid renewal with low cancer incidence at high energetic cost. HN commenters add biological corrections and challenge reducing wound repair.

### Comment pulse

- Regeneration depends on architecture, not merely selection pressure → homogeneous repeated units are easier to replace than precisely connected hearts, kidneys, or neural circuits.
- High turnover can suppress mutations → intestinal cells travel toward programmed shedding before abnormal clones can persist.
- Human repair may not be excessive → impaired wound healing causes severe surgical complications — counterpoint: regenerative pathways can still be hijacked by cancer.
