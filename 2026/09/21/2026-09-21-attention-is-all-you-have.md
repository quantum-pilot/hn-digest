# Attention is all you have

- Score: 1016 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49787726) | Link: https://alicegg.tech/2026/09/21/attention

### TL;DR

The essay argues that sustained attention reshapes thought, while recommendation systems increasingly choose what occupies it through endless, emotionally charged feeds. It proposes returning to an intentional internet of bookmarks, blogs, RSS, and finite content, accepting slower updates while rebuilding deliberate habits. HN commenters reported calmer moods and more reading after cutting social media or news, and suggested day lists, feed filters, offline networks, and searchable browsing history; others noted recommendations can still navigate overwhelming information when their biases are understood.

### Comment pulse

- Removing algorithmic feeds restores agency → commenters found that books, RSS, and directly followed sources reduced compulsive checking and mental noise.
- Simple constraints support intention → written day lists, no-idle-screen rules, filters, and disconnection interrupt habitual browsing.
- Recommendation is not inherently harmful → discovery needs selection biases — counterpoint: engagement-driven systems optimize platform goals rather than user intent.

### LLM perspective

- View: Attention control depends less on total abstinence than on replacing passive defaults with explicit choices.
- Impact: Individuals regain time and emotional stability, while independent publishers benefit from direct subscriptions and durable archives.
- Watch next: Browser tools could combine local full-text history, resilient bookmarks, and user-controlled recommendation settings.
