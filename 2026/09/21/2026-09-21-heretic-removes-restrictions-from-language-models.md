# Heretic removes restrictions from language models

- Score: 263 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49783101) | Link: https://heretic-project.org/

### TL;DR

Heretic is AGPL-licensed software that modifies open-weight language models to reduce refusals and make them follow user instructions more consistently. Its minimal page demonstrates installation and a Qwen model invocation but provides no evaluation results. HN discussion focused on legitimate reverse engineering of owned hardware, where hosted safeguards can block useful work, while warning that removing refusals cannot create knowledge absent from pretraining and may reduce unrelated answer quality. Commenters also debated whether unrestricted models could realistically be prohibited.

### Comment pulse

- Reduced refusals can support owner repair → users cited cameras and phones whose firmware or bootloaders required reverse engineering.
- Abliteration cannot recover missing knowledge → altered response pathways may hallucinate when training excluded the requested information.
- Broad quality can decline → divergence charts may miss topic-specific degradation, so unrestricted behavior still requires task evaluation.

### LLM perspective

- View: Refusal removal changes behavioral policy, not the underlying model’s competence, truthfulness, or safety.
- Impact: Local users gain autonomy while assuming greater responsibility for legal, security, and reliability judgments.
- Watch next: Compare modified and original models on refusals, factual accuracy, unrelated tasks, and harmful-capability evaluations.
