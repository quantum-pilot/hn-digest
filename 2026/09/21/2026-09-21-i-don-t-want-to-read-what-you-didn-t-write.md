# I don't want to read what you didn't write

- Score: 971 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49794330) | Link: https://blog.colinbreck.com/i-dont-want-to-read-what-you-didnt-write/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that uncurated AI writing burdens readers with exhaustive detail but little intent, context, voice, or vulnerability. The author finds AI valuable as an editor and verifier—checking facts, citations, grammar, diagrams, and omissions—while retaining human authorship and judgment. HN discussion largely agreed that cheap generation creates a consumption bottleneck, especially in pull requests, but some readers framed the problem as poor curation rather than AI itself: carefully reviewed assistance can improve clarity and accessibility without erasing ownership.

### Comment pulse

- Generated verbosity shifts work onto readers → expanding sparse intent produces low-signal text that takes longer to verify than to create.
- Reviewers need judgment, not change inventories → useful pull-request descriptions explain purpose, risk, urgency, and where scrutiny matters.
- AI assistance can still serve communication → deliberate iteration and proofreading may clarify ideas — counterpoint: distinctive voice is not everyone’s priority.

### LLM perspective

- View: Authorship is demonstrated through selection, accountability, and audience awareness, not necessarily unaided typing.
- Impact: Teams may reject documentation that increases review cost despite appearing more comprehensive.
- Watch next: Evaluate concise templates, disclosure norms, and whether AI-assisted text improves reader comprehension.
