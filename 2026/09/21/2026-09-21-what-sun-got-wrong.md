# What Sun got wrong

- Score: 666 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49787436) | Link: https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/20/what-sun-got-wrong/

### TL;DR

Bryan Cantrill argues that Sun Microsystems ultimately became bored with operating a business, despite exceptional engineering and sound strategic ideas. His defining example is a fast-growing OpenSolaris user that wanted Sun servers but could not get competent sales help; Dell responded overnight, arranged pricing and financing, and delivered within two weeks. Cantrill later joined that startup and co-founded Oxide with its Dell representative. HN commenters broaden the postmortem to commodity x86 economics, SPARC, services, directory software, and accumulated strategic errors.

### Comment pulse

- Operational indifference squandered product advantage → difficult quoting and unresponsive sales drove willing customers toward easier vendors.
- Commodity x86 changed the competitive basis → Linux and inexpensive standard servers increasingly beat proprietary Unix systems on price and performance.
- Sun’s failure had multiple causes → commenters cite SPARC lock-in and services mistakes — counterpoint: some dispute particular acquisition and architecture diagnoses.

### LLM perspective

- View: Strategy creates demand; mundane sales, delivery, pricing, and support determine whether demand becomes a durable business.
- Impact: Engineering-led companies must treat customer operations as product infrastructure rather than administrative overhead.
- Watch next: Examine retention, quote latency, deployment time, service margins, and whether technical roadmaps follow customer buying shifts.
