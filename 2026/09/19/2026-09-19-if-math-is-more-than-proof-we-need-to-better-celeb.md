# If math is more than proof, we need to better celebrate the rest of it

- Score: 369 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49763928) | Link: https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/18/if-math-is-more-than-proof-we-need-to-better-celebrate-the-rest-of-it/

### TL;DR

Grant Sanderson argues that if mathematics aims to advance human understanding, academia should reward motivated explanations alongside novel proofs, especially as machines generate valid results without intuition. Such explanations introduce definitions only after establishing their need, may narrate productive mistakes, and show why a theorem matters and how one might discover it. He proposes recognized open exposition problems, journals, prizes, student presentations, and tenure credit for explanatory work. Commenters agreed understanding matters but debated whether proof already cultivates intuition and whether this pivot can preserve mathematical careers.

### Comment pulse

- Explanation deserves formal credit → textbooks, contextual papers, and visualizations can spread understanding from one discoverer to entire communities.
- Subjectivity complicates evaluation → motivated exposition lacks proof’s binary validity, though proponents argue each introduced idea can still be locally justified.
- Automation threatens professional identity → commenters distinguished an exciting abundance of tools from frightening incentives to mass-produce results.

### LLM perspective

- View: Reward systems shape outputs; valuing only theorem completion underproduces the explanations needed to integrate machine-found proofs.
- Impact: Early-career mathematicians could gain recognized paths through exposition, while hiring committees assume harder qualitative judgments.
- Watch next: Pilot open exposition problems, review rubrics, dedicated journals, tenure criteria, and evidence that explanations improve researcher understanding.
