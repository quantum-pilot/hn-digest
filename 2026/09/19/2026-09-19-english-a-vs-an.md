# English: A vs. An

- Score: 275 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49769944) | Link: https://www.redblobgames.com/blog/2026-09-16-english-a-vs-an/

### TL;DR

Choosing “a” or “an” depends on the next word’s initial sound, not its first letter: “a unicorn” and “an hour” illustrate why simple spelling checks fail. Exploring pronunciation data and visualizations, the author found just 129 exceptions among 32,455 listed words, suggesting a compact practical rule set is possible. HN discussion broadened into how articles differ across languages, why definite versus indefinite reference challenges learners, and whether phonetic smoothing explains the English pattern historically.

### Comment pulse

- Pronunciation is the operative rule → vowel sounds take “an,” including acronyms, while vowel letters beginning consonant sounds take “a.”
- Article difficulty is language-dependent → some learners find sound-based selection intuitive but struggle for decades with definiteness and context.
- Linguistic explanations can become post-hoc stories → fluent speakers apply patterns reliably without necessarily knowing their origin or formal rule.
