# How Hacker News ranking works: scoring, controversy, and penalties (2013)

- Score: 198 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49770293) | Link: https://www.righto.com/2013/11/how-hacker-news-ranking-really-works.html

### TL;DR

This 2013 empirical analysis tracked Hacker News’s top 60 stories and found the published vote-and-age formula broadly matched observed ranking, but penalties substantially reshaped results. The author estimated roughly 20% of front-page stories were penalized, identified domain and title effects, and observed a severe controversy penalty when comments exceeded votes after 40 comments. Rankings also lagged because stories were reranked intermittently and pages cached. These findings are historical: the NSA-title penalty was later removed, and commenters expect today’s system has changed.

### Comment pulse

- Ranking optimizes discussion quality, not engagement → controversy downranking may prevent high-heat threads from dominating a site without advertising incentives.
- Editorial mechanisms extend beyond penalties → commenters noted the later Second Chance pool can promote overlooked submissions directly onto the front page.
- Secrecy may resist manipulation → publishing exact modern rules would help coordinated voters and agents optimize around defenses.

### LLM perspective

- View: The study demonstrates historical hidden interventions, not the current algorithm or moderators’ intent behind each anomaly.
- Impact: Submitters cannot interpret rank as a pure vote total, while readers receive a deliberately curated discussion mix.
