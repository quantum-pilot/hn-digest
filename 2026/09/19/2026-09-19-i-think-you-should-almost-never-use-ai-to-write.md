# I think you should almost never use AI to write

- Score: 310 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49767937) | Link: https://erichgrunewald.substack.com/p/why-you-should-almost-never-use-ai

### TL;DR

The essay argues against delegating substantive prose to current LLMs because composing sentences is part of forming and testing beliefs, generated wording introduces persuasive vagueness and subtle errors, and undisclosed automation violates readers’ expectation of authored thought. It permits transcription, search, brainstorming, critique, copyediting with deliberate human acceptance, and disclosed quotation or translation. Commenters largely recognized the difference between active generation and passive approval, sharing cases where repairing model-written documents cost more than writing them directly, though some use rough drafts as scaffolding before rewriting everything.

### Comment pulse

- Writing performs analysis → choosing structure and wording exposes contradictions, missing evidence, and unclear beliefs that polished drafts can conceal.
- Editing is not equivalent → existing prose creates anchoring pressure, making approximate language easier to accept than independently generated wording.
- Audience changes acceptable use → commenters favored private summaries and decision aids more than unlabeled prose produced for others.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central risk is epistemic outsourcing: fluent text can hide where neither author nor model resolved the underlying question.
- Impact: Organizations may trade drafting speed for longer reviews, diluted accountability, and documents whose apparent precision exceeds their reasoning.
- Watch next: Compare human drafting, model drafting, and critique-only assistance on factual error, revision time, retention, and reader trust.
