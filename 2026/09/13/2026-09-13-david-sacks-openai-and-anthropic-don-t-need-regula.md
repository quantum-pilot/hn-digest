# David Sacks: OpenAI and Anthropic Don't Need Regulations to Pace Frontier Models

- Score: 302 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49685991) | Link: https://twitter.com/DavidSacks/status/2098973625252708460

### TL;DR

David Sacks argues OpenAI and Anthropic can voluntarily slow frontier development without a regulatory framework, antitrust exemptions, or preferred evaluators. He portrays them as a frontier duopoly whose safety concerns also align with product liability and customer demand for reliability. If unreleased models are dangerous, he says, the labs should simply stop building them; conditioning restraint on regulation looks like capture. HN largely suspected rules would entrench incumbents, though commenters disputed motives and proposed ordinary liability for harms instead of specialized approval regimes.

### Comment pulse

- Compliance can become a moat → capital-rich labs can absorb standards that exclude smaller or open competitors.
- Existing liability may encourage caution → prosecuting concrete harms could price risk without creating a centralized model-approval system.
- Motive claims remain speculative → some infer cartel-building or stalled capabilities; counterpoint: declared safety concerns may be sincere even if commercially aligned.

### LLM perspective

- View: Voluntary restraint avoids capture concerns but lacks durability, verification, and coverage beyond two companies.
- Impact: Liability-centered policy shifts decisions toward courts after harm, while ex-ante regulation attempts prevention but risks incumbent control.
