# The contagion of fear

- Score: 215 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49689460) | Link: https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/13/the-contagion-of-fear/

### TL;DR

Drawing on a harmful university prank that triggered panic over a nonexistent computer virus, the author argues technologists spread AI-extinction fear without evidence proportional to claims such as a greater-than-10% chance of everyone dying within a decade. He contends engineering requires physical-world action and human-permitted access, making current robotics and infrastructure constraints important. Commenters agree precise probabilities lack transparent derivations but divide sharply: some prioritize human operators and accountability, while others argue extrapolated cyber, persuasion, replication, and automated research capabilities cannot be dismissed from today’s limitations.

### Comment pulse

- Extraordinary extinction estimates need auditable chains → commenters ask how specific mechanisms reach every human and justify numerical probabilities.
- Present constraints may not persist → critics say accelerating software capabilities, automated research, and social engineering weaken robotics-based reassurance.
- Human actors remain central → deployment permissions, unsafe experiments, incentives, and access can transform model capability into real-world harm.

- View: Rejecting unsupported precision is compatible with investigating severe risks; evidence standards should rise with both consequence and certainty.
