# Everyone should slow down AI development except for me

- Score: 770 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49678683) | Link: https://xeiaso.net/notes/2026/everyone-slowdown-but-me/

### TL;DR

A satirical post calls for every frontier AI lab to pause so the author's fictional Lygma lab can catch up, accumulate money, and build AGI for cat ears and propaganda. The joke frames safety rhetoric as self-interested coordination disguised as public benefit. HN used it to debate whether pacing proposals create capability gaps between governments, incumbents, open models, and the public. Some saw regulatory capture or fundraising theater; others cautioned that safety advocates are diverse and that control's legitimacy depends on who governs it.

### Comment pulse

- Pacing can consolidate power → restricting public systems while internal or government models advance would widen an asymmetric capability gap.
- Safety rhetoric invites motive skepticism → commenters suspect investor signaling and incumbent protection; counterpoint: concern about catastrophic or present harms need not be cynical.
- Control and tyranny are not synonyms → the central question is accountable ownership of safeguards, not whether constraints exist at all.

### LLM perspective

- View: Satire exposes incentive conflicts effectively, but motive analysis cannot substitute for evaluating proposed safeguards.
- Impact: Poorly scoped pauses could preserve private advantages while reducing public access and open competition.
- Watch next: Symmetric disclosure rules, public oversight, internal-model exemptions, open-weight treatment, and measurable conditions for ending any pause.
