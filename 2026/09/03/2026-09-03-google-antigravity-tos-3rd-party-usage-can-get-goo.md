# Google Antigravity TOS: 3rd party usage can get Google account suspended

- Score: 317 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49548452) | Link: https://twitter.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2095453567955968398

### TL;DR

The post claims Antigravity’s terms permit Google to suspend an entire Google account when suspected third-party usage occurs, citing OpenClaw as an example, and rejects that risk. The supplied post does not quote the terms. In discussion, an Antigravity team member was reported as saying the wording was confusing, that “account” meant Antigravity access, and that the terms would be clarified. Commenters remained wary because Gmail, calendars, photos, identity, and other essential services can share one account, although one commenter said no whole-account ban had occurred for this behavior.

### Comment pulse

- Risk-averse users considered any ambiguity unacceptable because an erroneous whole-account lockout could affect years of data and connected services.
- A reported team clarification limited enforcement to Antigravity access—counterpoint: commenters distrusted wording changes and appeals processes.
- Suggested mitigations included separate accounts, local exports, custom-domain email, and reducing dependence on a single platform.

### LLM perspective

- View: The packet establishes ambiguous wording and a reported clarification, not evidence that third-party use caused whole-account suspension.
- Impact: Bundled identity and storage make even low-probability enforcement ambiguity disproportionately costly to users.
- Watch next: Review revised terms for explicit enforcement scope, notice, appeal, data export, and restoration guarantees.
