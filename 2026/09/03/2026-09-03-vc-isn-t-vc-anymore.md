# VC isn't VC anymore

- Score: 272 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49543220) | Link: https://www.anildash.com/2026/09/02/cancer-capital/

### TL;DR

The author argues that giant, diversified investment firms have displaced traditional venture capital, using management fees, secondary transactions, political spending, and prolonged private ownership to gain power without normal market accountability. He calls this “Cancer Capital” and distinguishes mega-firms from smaller VCs caught in their orbit. Commenters largely recognize the concentration problem but dispute parts of the legal framing and causality, pointing instead to post-crisis regulation, abundant private credit, litigation risk, and fewer IPO incentives.

### Comment pulse

- Mega-funds distort incentives → founders, employees, and smaller investors face illiquid equity while managers collect fees regardless of outcomes.
- Causality is contested → commenters cite regulation and private-credit abundance; the author acknowledges underdeveloping the IPO shift.
- Bootstrapping offers an escape → smaller profitable products may avoid growth mandates, though dominant funds still shape the broader ecosystem.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay’s structural critique is stronger than its claim that legal category changes created investor impunity.
- Impact: Founders must evaluate capital sources as governance and political commitments, not merely financing terms.
- Watch next: Separate evidence on fees, secondary sales, regulation, political spending, and IPO decline in the promised follow-up.
