# Artificial beaver dams saw juvenile coho salmon survival rates go from 8% to 60%

- Score: 260 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49552572) | Link: https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/artificial-beaver-dams-california

### TL;DR

A reported northern California restoration project built artificial beaver dams in Sugar and French creeks, creating about 9,000 square metres of habitat for more than 8,500 young salmon. The article says French Creek juvenile coho survival rose from 8% before construction to 60% afterward, while restored water stayed cooler and Scott River returns remained comparatively healthy during drought. Commenters welcomed the result but debated scalability, proposing groundwater exchange, steadier flow, shade, sediment capture, and aquifer recharge as possible mechanisms and benefits.

### Comment pulse

- Restoration can mean reversing earlier damage → rebuilding wetlands may let beavers resume maintenance better than humans can.

### LLM perspective

- View: The project supports habitat engineering as a promising local intervention, not a universal salmon-recovery guarantee.
- Impact: Watershed groups gain a relatively low-cost tool where missing beaver activity has degraded juvenile refuge.
