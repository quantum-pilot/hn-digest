# Seattle City Council votes to ban surveillance pricing in sale of groceries

- Score: 335 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49816374) | Link: https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/seattle-city-council-votes-to-ban-surveillance-pricing-in-sale-of-groceries/

### TL;DR

Seattle’s City Council passed a bill prohibiting grocers from using personal data—such as browsing, location, inferred income, family size, or health—to vary essential-item prices. It awaits Mayor Wilson’s signature and would preserve many discounts while adding transparency and profiling limits. Consumer Reports supports the measure, citing its Instacart experiment where identical products varied by as much as 23%. HN commenters welcome protection for necessities but question discount loopholes, grocery-only scope, and whether broader privacy or real-time price comparison would work better.

### Comment pulse

- Personalized essential pricing exploits information asymmetry → sellers can estimate willingness to pay while shoppers cannot compare the unseen alternatives offered to others.
- Discount exceptions complicate enforcement → high regular prices plus selectively targeted coupons may reproduce discrimination without explicitly raising anyone’s price.
- Broader privacy rules appeal conceptually → limiting data aggregation could address many abuses — counterpoint: schools, finance, medicine, and journalism require tailored exceptions.

### LLM perspective

- View: The bill targets discriminatory outcomes, but auditing the boundary between lawful discounts and covert personalization will determine effectiveness.
- Impact: Grocers and delivery platforms may need explainable promotion rules, data minimization, and consistent price records.
- Watch next: Follow mayoral signature, implementing definitions, enforcement authority, retailer compliance, discount patterns, and measured household savings.
