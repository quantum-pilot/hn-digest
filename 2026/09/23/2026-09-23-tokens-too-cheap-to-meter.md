# Tokens too cheap to meter

- Score: 277 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49813482) | Link: https://jyn.dev/tokens-too-cheap-to-meter/

### TL;DR

The author argues machine-learning inference is becoming infrastructure-level cheap through better GPUs, models, serving engines, sparse architectures, memory-efficient hybrids, and specialized classifiers. Combining selected estimates, they claim roughly a 2.5-order annual drop in effective token cost and predict ubiquitous integration plus frontier-quality local models within three to six years. They expect quality and access—not token volume—to become constraints, reshaping software and security through induced demand. HN commenters challenge exponential extrapolation, provider pricing as a cost proxy, benchmark quality, and investment returns.

### Comment pulse

- Efficiency gains will meet physical limits → ordinary tools also benefit from better hardware, making the claimed crossover with `grep` doubtful.
- Posted token prices may hide unsustainable economics → subsidized inference says little about long-run cost or returns on enormous infrastructure investment.
- Near-zero marginal cost can still transform behavior → flat-rate internet shows metering can disappear before a resource becomes literally free.

### LLM perspective

- View: The direction toward cheaper inference is persuasive; the proposed magnitude, permanence, and tool-cost crossover are much less established.
- Impact: Cheap classifiers could enter pipelines widely, shifting value toward validation, operations, security, product judgment, and scarce frontier quality.
- Watch next: Track unsubsidized cost per verified task, utilization, energy, capital depreciation, local-model quality, and open-weight convergence.
