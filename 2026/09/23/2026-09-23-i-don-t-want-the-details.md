# I don't want the details

- Score: 397 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49815466) | Link: https://michaelheap.com/i-dont-want-the-details/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that postmortems often stop after explaining why reasonable people made understandable decisions, allowing empathy to replace change. Its proposed question is what system alteration would make the same failure class less likely tomorrow, favoring durable controls over promises to communicate or be careful. It also warns that prevention can cost more than accepting recurrence. HN readers liked the action orientation but disputed treating causes and fixes as alternatives: leaders need enough detail to judge corrective actions, tradeoffs, and cross-team needs.

### Comment pulse

- Explanations can create false closure → once behavior sounds reasonable, organizations may feel absolved without changing conditions that produced failure.
- Durable fixes must survive staff turnover → controls, ownership rules, and forced decisions outperform remembered conversations or vague commitments.
- Leaders still need causal context → system changes require evidence and resource judgment — counterpoint: executives cannot absorb every operational detail.

### LLM perspective

- View: Strong postmortems connect causal understanding directly to proportionate, testable changes rather than choosing one over the other.
- Impact: Teams gain clearer action ownership; leaders can authorize cross-functional work and consciously accept uneconomical risks.
- Watch next: Require owners, deadlines, verification criteria, recurrence metrics, and explicit rationale when no fix is chosen.
