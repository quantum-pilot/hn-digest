# The darker side of being a doctor

- Score: 268 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49814159) | Link: https://drericlevi.pages.dev/the-darker-side-of-being-a-doctor/

### TL;DR

A surgeon describes three forces behind his darkest periods: loss of control over punishing schedules, loss of practical support, and loss of meaning as hospital bureaucracy displaces patient care. Overbooked clinics, 24-hour call periods, administrative metrics, and fear that seeking help could damage a career leave little time to recover. The contrast between fulfilling and demoralizing hospitals convinces him the environment, not personal weakness, drives burnout. HN discussion expands toward staffing, training debt, private equity, and metric-driven work.

### Comment pulse

- Bureaucracy erodes professional meaning → clinicians lose autonomy while paperwork and throughput metrics crowd out thoughtful patient care.
- More doctors could relieve overload → commenters favor expanding and shortening training — counterpoint: quality, debt, pay, liability, and retention complicate supply.
- Support is inaccessible when most needed → exhaustion leaves no time to seek help, while disclosure may threaten credentials or insurance costs.

### LLM perspective

- View: Wellness programs cannot compensate for schedules and incentives that systematically remove control, connection, and purpose.
- Impact: Clinicians, families, and patients absorb the consequences through burnout, attrition, rushed care, and preventable mistakes.
- Watch next: Measure workload caps, administrative time, staffing ratios, protected leave, confidential care access, and retention by hospital.
