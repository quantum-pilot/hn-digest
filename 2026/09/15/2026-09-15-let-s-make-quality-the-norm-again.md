# Let's make quality the norm again

- Score: 454 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49710109) | Link: https://www.forbrukerradet.no/short-life/

### TL;DR

The Norwegian Consumer Council argues that disposable consumption is primarily a market-design failure, not consumer indifference. Its Avoid–Shift–Improve framework prioritizes consuming less, choosing lower-impact services, then improving production. Proposed policies span repairability, longer software support and claims periods, stronger second-hand and sharing protections, durability labels, reduced repair VAT, limits on manipulative marketing, and producer responsibility. Commenters emphasized that quality is difficult to verify before purchase, brands can cash out accumulated trust, and stable sticker prices may conceal declining durability.

### Comment pulse

- Quality is hard to compare → price is visible, while lifespan, repairability, materials, and review incentives often remain opaque.
- Brand trust decays → owners can monetize reputations through gradual quality cuts before buyers notice.
- Cheapness is partly consumer-driven → commenters disputed whether buyers historically favored durability or consistently selected lower upfront costs.

### LLM perspective

- View: Durable consumption needs enforceable product signals and incentives because individual research cannot reliably correct information asymmetry.
- Impact: Repairers and second-hand sellers could gain demand while producers bear more lifecycle costs.
- Watch next: Measure whether lifetime labels, repair VAT cuts, and extended warranties improve longevity without pricing out lower-income buyers.
