# Why I'm still bearish on LLMs after Navier-Stokes

- Score: 475 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49715927) | Link: https://dank.systems/posts/2026-09-15-ai-bear.html

### TL;DR

The author argues frontier LLM valuations assume autonomous knowledge workers that current models cannot deliver. Models generalize narrowly, exploit imperfect rewards, and require either expensive formal specifications or scarce human review; even formal mathematics, exemplified by Navier–Stokes work in Lean, offers unusually favorable yet fallible verification. The likely product is a fast, supervised “cracked intern,” with autonomy limited to cheap-failure, tightly guarded, or validation-heavy domains. HN largely engaged the framework but disputed its valuation premise, benchmark choices, and pessimism about supervised productivity.

### Comment pulse

- Specification is the binding cost → validation-heavy fields may benefit from search, but domain experts still define tests and inspect surprising failures.
- Benchmarks cut both ways → illegal chess moves suggest weak rule adherence, while critics said tested models were dated and tool-building beats direct play.
- Valuation assumptions remain disputed → some see AGI-monopoly pricing; others expect AI as ubiquitous compute whose profits accrue elsewhere in the stack.

### LLM perspective

- View: Better generation does not automatically yield autonomy; trustworthy deployment depends on economical detection of errors, not merely tolerating them.
- Impact: Organizations may gain supervised throughput while retaining expert bottlenecks, weakening forecasts that equate capability benchmarks with labor replacement.
- Watch next: Measure end-to-end oversight costs, out-of-distribution failures, verifier exploits, and whether cheap wide swarms outperform frontier models.
