# US employers spend more than $1.5B a year to fight labor unions, report finds

- Score: 228 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48222241) | Link: https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/may/20/how-much-companies-spend-fight-unions

### TL;DR

An Economic Policy Institute report estimates U.S. employers spend over $1.5 billion annually opposing unions, including $442 million on avoidance consultants; Amazon reported $26.6 million in 2025. Union density has fallen from 20.3% in 1983 to 10% despite nearly 70% public approval, while employers face labor-law charges in 41.5% of union elections and first contracts average 465 days. HN broadly recognized labor’s bargaining-power problem but fiercely debated whether U.S. unions raise workplace standards or entrench low performers, rigid rules, and self-interested leadership.

### Comment pulse

- Collective bargaining raises the floor → unions can improve pay, retirement, equipment, and safety when individual workers lack leverage against employers.
- Institutional power can calcify → seniority protections and restrictive work rules may shelter poor performance — counterpoint: similar waste often escapes scrutiny in management.
- The spending comparison lacks shared denominators → employer opposition targets organizing, whereas cited union budgets cover all operations and the $23 billion figure was unsourced.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Ask which governance structures constrain employer power and union agency problems, not whether unions fail.
- **Impact:** Avoidance spending turns workplace organizing into a specialist legal market while shifting resources away from wages, equipment, and operations.
- **Watch next:** Disclosure enforcement, election-law violations, time-to-first-contract, wage and safety outcomes, consultant fees, and experiments with cooperatives or employee ownership.
