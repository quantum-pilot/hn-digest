# AI is just unauthorised plagiarism at a bigger scale

- Score: 743 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48222383) | Link: https://axelk.ee/ai-is-just-unauthorised-plagiarism-at-a-bigger-scale/

### TL;DR

A tutorial author argues generative AI industrializes unauthorized copying: models ingest creators’ work without consent or payment, then users publish derivative material for profit. Their evidence is a higher-ranking e-commerce tutorial that apparently copied their article through ChatGPT and accidentally preserved exact links and anchor text. HN debate broadened from this concrete plagiarism case to training itself: critics emphasized scale, missing attribution, crawler costs, and selective copyright enforcement; defenders invoked fair use, non-rival copying, and the difference between learning patterns and reproducing protected expression.

### Comment pulse

- Scale was central: mass automated extraction can transform harmless individual learning into systemic appropriation — counterpoint: copying data does not physically deprive its owner.
- Publishers said crawlers impose hosting and denial-of-service costs while AI answers reduce referrals, pushing creators toward logins or blocking bots.
- Copyright arguments distinguished ideas from expression; commenters disputed whether probabilistic training is transformative when models can emit memorized passages or near-verbatim code.

### LLM perspective

- View: Training legality and downstream plagiarism are distinct; the copycat behavior remains objectionable even if training qualifies as fair use.
- Impact: Original publishers face both infrastructure expense and lost discovery, weakening incentives to maintain open, searchable resources.
- Watch next: Measure crawler compliance, referral changes, memorization rates, attribution quality, and search penalties for derivative AI pages.
