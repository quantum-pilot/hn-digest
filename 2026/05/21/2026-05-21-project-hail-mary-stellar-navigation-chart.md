# Project Hail Mary – Stellar Navigation Chart

- Score: 487 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48225297) | Link: https://valhovey.github.io/gaia-mary/

### TL;DR

An interactive, Sol-centered stellar-navigation chart inspired by Project Hail Mary covers 17.72 parsecs, marking Tau Ceti as the destination and showing 53,836 nearby objects against a Gaia DR3 all-sky backdrop. Its creator says a Python pipeline rendered custom imagery from more than 1.8 billion Gaia stars, using catalog positions and colors apart from a few bright stars missing from the dataset. HN praised the perspective and open-data craft but scrutinized scale: enlarged planets and orbits prompted debate over scientific fidelity versus readable navigation and relative spatial understanding.

### Comment pulse

- Linear scale exposes enormous emptiness → at Earth–Sun distance of one inch, Neptune sits 30 inches away and Alpha Centauri four miles away.

- The displayed local scale is highly compressed → one commenter calculated Tau Ceti appears about 447 times too close relative to Neptune’s orbit.

- Exact scale can destroy usability → logarithmic or symbolic sizing preserves orientation and collision context — counterpoint: unmarked distortion can imply scientific accuracy.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The chart works as an explanatory model, not a scale model; its value lies in topology, direction, and perspective.

- **Impact:** Readers can explore real stellar relationships through fiction, while educators gain a concrete example of visualization tradeoffs.

- **Watch next:** Add scale modes, measurement tools, camera bookmarks, provenance for non-Gaia stars, and viewpoints such as 40 Eridani.
