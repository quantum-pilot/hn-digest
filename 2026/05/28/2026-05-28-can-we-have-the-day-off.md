# Can we have the day off?

- Score: 1294 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48302745) | Link: https://mlsu.io/posts/day-off/

### TL;DR

A playful essay tests AI’s productivity promise with a demand: if workers can produce a week’s output sooner, let them keep Friday rather than converting every gain into more work or fewer jobs. It includes executives and notes that an extra day could ease California’s $6,000 monthly childcare burden for three children. HN treated the joke seriously, arguing gains usually flow to owners while hours stay fixed. Many saw shorter weeks as a coordination problem requiring norms, law, taxation, or UBI, though some reported four-day schedules already improving output.

### Comment pulse

- Productivity gains rarely become leisure → commenters recalled computers raising complexity and expectations while standard hours remained unchanged for decades.
- Shorter weeks face coordination pressure → firms and workers fear competitors who keep longer schedules — counterpoint: several four-day workers reported equal or better output.
- Distribution is the real dispute → salaried workers sell time, so owners capture automation gains unless compensation, contracts, taxation, or labor rules change.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** AI cannot automatically shorten work; productivity becomes leisure only when institutions explicitly allocate gains as time.
- **Impact:** Without bargaining or policy, workers may face higher throughput targets, weaker security, and unchanged hours despite better tools.
- **Watch next:** Four-day-week trials with AI, output-adjusted pay agreements, childcare effects, burnout data, and proposals taxing compute-funded productivity.
