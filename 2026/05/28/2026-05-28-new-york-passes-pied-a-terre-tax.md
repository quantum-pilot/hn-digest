# New York passes pied-a-terre tax

- Score: 250 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48309584) | Link: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/28/new-york-mamdani-pied-a-terre-tax-passes.html

### TL;DR

New York lawmakers approved an annual tax on New York City nonprimary homes valued at $1 million or more, projected to raise $500 million for the budget. For two years, rates of 4%, 5.25%, or 6.5% apply to heavily understated city assessments; from 2028-29, comparable-sales valuations replace them and rates fall to 0.8%-1.3%. Ken Griffin’s $238 million penthouse, assessed at $15.5 million, could rise from $858,332 yearly to nearly $4 million. HN broadly favored taxing immovable luxury property but debated housing liquidity, valuation design, behavioral responses, and revenue stability.

### Comment pulse

- Property is a workable wealth-tax base → land cannot flee or hide, and second-home owners avoid city income tax while consuming scarce housing.
- Revenue and housing goals may conflict → discouraging purchases can increase availability — counterpoint: excessive rates could shrink the base or redirect wealthy buyers.
- Assessment reform matters more than headline percentages → current values may be 10% of market, obscuring effective rates and creating abrupt phase changes.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The policy combines budget financing, a partial wealth tax, and a vacancy deterrent; success criteria should distinguish all three.
- **Impact:** Luxury owners face higher carrying costs; brokers, city revenue planners, and primary residents absorb resulting price and transaction shifts.
- **Watch next:** Final implementation rules, assessment appeals, second-home sales, new purchases, residency changes, collection totals, and effects on available housing.
