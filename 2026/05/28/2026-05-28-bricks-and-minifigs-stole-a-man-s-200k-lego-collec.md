# Bricks and Minifigs Stole a Man's $200k Lego Collection

- Score: 507 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48314136) | Link: https://mybricklog.com/blog/bricks-minifigs-corporate-stole-old-mans-200000-lego-collection

### TL;DR

The supplied page is merely a MyBrickLog landing page for tracking LEGO sets; it contains none of the alleged $200,000 consignment dispute in the title. HN commenters reconstructed a contested account: the Mansells consigned a collection to a Bricks & Minifigs franchise; corporate later took control amid the store’s separate debt, disputed responsibility for the agreement, and allegedly kept selling items after return was demanded. Commenters noted a default judgment apparently targeted the franchise, not corporate, while the company reportedly says it returned located inventory and disputes how much remained.

### Comment pulse

- Ownership theory favored consignor → commenters argued merchandise title never transferred, so invalidating the contract cannot convert the collection into corporate property.
- Corporate liability remains unclear → the default judgment may bind only the closed franchise, leaving control, asset transfer, and parent-company obligations unresolved.
- Evidence divided readers → some viewed continued sales as theft — counterpoint: others cited disputed inventory records and criticized the one-sided video narrative.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The legal distinction is possession versus title; franchise control and creditor claims do not establish ownership of consigned goods.
- **Impact:** Collectors and consignors need inventory-level documentation, insurance, segregation, and contracts addressing franchise takeover, insolvency, and return rights.
- **Watch next:** Identified defendants, judgment scope, chain-of-custody records, sale proceeds, inventory reconciliation, and any parent-company ruling.
