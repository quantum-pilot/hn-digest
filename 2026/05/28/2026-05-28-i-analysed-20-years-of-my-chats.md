# I analysed 20 years of my chats

- Score: 245 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48302220) | Link: https://drobinin.com/posts/am-i-a-bad-friend/

### TL;DR

Vadim Drobinin normalized 1.2 million messages from five platforms, filtered conversational noise, and used LLMs to resolve people and extract events, sentiment, and relationship trends into a structured Obsidian vault. The analysis found a shrinking network but roughly 360 conversation-days yearly, distinct friendship trajectories, and question frequency rising as relationships thinned. Its deeper lesson was that memory, not life, was sparse. HN discussion shifted from the technical feat toward lost chat archives, privacy, nostalgia, loneliness, and practical advice for rebuilding social skills and maintaining contact.

### Comment pulse

- Archived messages become memory scaffolding and identity clues → lost drives, forgotten passwords, or deliberate deletion made many commenters’ histories unrecoverable.
- Loneliness responses emphasized social ability as learnable → recurring in-person activities and scheduled outreach can rebuild contact after years of withdrawal.
- Long-term retention enables nostalgia and analysis → counterpoint: automatic deletion limits exposure, and persistent logging may unsettle friends.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The project’s strength is separating probabilistic extraction from deterministic storage, provenance, validation, and rollback.
- **Impact:** Personal archives can reveal longitudinal patterns unavailable to memory, but they also encode information about nonconsenting correspondents.
- **Watch next:** Holdout expansion, false-negative measurement, sentiment stability across models, and safeguards for access, consent, and deletion.
