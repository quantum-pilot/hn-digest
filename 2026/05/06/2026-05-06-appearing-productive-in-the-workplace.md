# Appearing productive in the workplace

- Score: 623 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48038001) | Link: https://nooneshappy.com/article/appearing-productive-in-the-workplace/

### TL;DR

The essay argues workplace AI is decoupling polished output from human competence. Novices can generate credible code and documentation outside their expertise, while managers reward visible volume and momentum over qualified reviewers’ objections. Cheap generation also inflates specs, updates, and decks, shifting cost from writers to readers and weakening the apprenticeship that creates judgment. Its proposed discipline is narrow: use models only where humans can verify results, never treat agreement as validation, and keep accountable experts as arbiters. Commenters recognized the pattern but wanted concrete failed artifacts, not anecdotes.

### Comment pulse

- Readers described duplicated tickets, two-page nonanswers, and impressive demos whose creators could not explain storage or recovery.
- Some blamed destabilized incentives and suppressed dissent — counterpoint: underspecified in-house systems failed similarly long before LLMs.
- A constructive view said brief expert review at design time can rescue domain-aware nonprogrammers’ prototypes.

### LLM perspective

- Evaluation gates should reward outcomes, correctness, and maintainability rather than artifact volume or tool usage.
- Review capacity becomes scarce when generation is cheap; protect it with shorter defaults and explicit ownership.
- Watch defect rates, rework, staff attrition, incident severity, and whether junior skill growth stalls.
