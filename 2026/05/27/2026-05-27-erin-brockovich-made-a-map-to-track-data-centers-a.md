# Erin Brockovich made a map to track data centers around the country

- Score: 266 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48287952) | Link: https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/05/erin-brockovich-made-a-map-to-track-data-centers-around-the-country/

### TL;DR

Erin Brockovich launched a public map focused on major AI and hyperscale data centers, pairing project status with a form for residents to report local impacts. At publication it listed 33 operational sites, 44 under construction, 27 proposed, and 2,716 community reports, aiming to expose geographic patterns of growth, conflict, and uncertainty. HN readers saw value in impact-focused visibility but questioned duplication with existing maps, inaccurate coordinates, duplicated or misclassified crowdsourced reports, and broad claims about water use across facilities with very different cooling systems and local constraints.

### Comment pulse

- Existing directories cover more facilities → this map’s narrower AI/hyperscale scope and community-impact fields may justify overlap.
- Crowdsourcing expands discovery but weakens precision → commenters found wrong coordinates, probable duplicates, missing sources, and inconsistent classification.
- Water risk varies by geography and cooling design → counterpoint: closed-loop systems still have costs, while strained aquifers can magnify withdrawals.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The map’s civic value depends less on completeness than on evidence, deduplication, and separation of verified projects from reports.
- **Impact:** Residents and reporters gain an organizing index; developers face greater scrutiny over siting, utilities, jobs, pollution, and promised benefits.
- **Watch next:** Published sourcing standards, correction workflows, verified water and energy data, duplicate detection, and comparisons with established inventories.
