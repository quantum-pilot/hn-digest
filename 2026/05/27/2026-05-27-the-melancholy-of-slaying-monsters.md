# The Melancholy of Slaying Monsters

- Score: 268 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48284711) | Link: https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/the-strange-melancholy-of-slaying-monsters/

### TL;DR

The essay traces games that turn monster-killing from automatic progress into ethical discomfort. Shadow of the Colossus makes largely peaceful giants intimate terrain, scores their deaths as tragedy, and forces Wander toward ruin; Undertale individualizes enemies, permits a harder pacifist path, and recasts experience and levels as execution and violence. Bloodborne, Dark Souls, BioShock, and Spec Ops likewise implicate the player or false hero. HN supplied moments of belated empathy, while debating whether some interpretations overreach and how much realistic enemy self-preservation should yield to enjoyable mechanics.

### Comment pulse

- Retrospective guilt can outlast scripted dilemmas → players humanized Skyrim bandits or faceless soldiers only after routine mechanics had already induced slaughter.
- Enemy intelligence shapes morality → surrender, retreat, regrouping, and morale would humanize foes — counterpoint: realistic hiding and evasion can degrade gameplay.
- Interpretation remains contextual → commenters disputed moral meaning in Atreus’s troll fear and reacted oppositely to a toy elephant’s scripted death.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The strongest ethical design preserves agency, then lets mechanics and aftermath reveal consequences without announcing a moral-choice menu.
- **Impact:** Designers can make enemies legible as subjects through behavior, context, sound, and nonviolent affordances—not necessarily photorealism.
- **Watch next:** Compare player behavior when games add surrender, persistent consequences, optional combat, post-encounter context, or incentives that reward mercy.
