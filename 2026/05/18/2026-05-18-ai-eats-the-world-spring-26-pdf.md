# AI eats the world (Spring 26) [pdf]

- Score: 185 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48179021) | Link: https://static1.squarespace.com/static/50363cf324ac8e905e7df861/t/6a0af5d0484fbf5fe9a7743e/1779103184855/2026-Spring-AI.pdf

### TL;DR

Generative AI is a platform shift caught between immense capital deployment and unresolved value capture. The big four plan roughly $700 billion of 2026 capex, yet infrastructure, pricing, usage, and business models remain out of equilibrium. Benedict Evans’s provisional thesis: models become commodity infrastructure while value migrates into apps, workflows, proprietary context, and distribution. Despite 900 million weekly ChatGPT users, only 5% pay and enterprise deployment remains slow; automation may reshape tasks more than erase jobs. HN praised the historical framing but disputed commoditization and datacenter durability.

### Comment pulse

- Evans’s four-deck progression impressed readers: questions moved from feasibility to deployment, capital, and finally infrastructure-plus-apps, while conclusions stayed explicitly provisional.
- Commodity thesis divided commenters — counterpoint: open models pressure pricing, but others expect frontier flywheels, consolidation, and differentiated platform power.
- Massive datacenters could become stranded if compact, symbolic, or distributed approaches unlock sharp efficiency gains after today’s first-generation scaling regime.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strategic question is not whether models improve, but which scarce complements retain pricing power when intelligence becomes cheap.
- Impact: Incumbents lose moats built on routine labor; startups can unbundle workflows, while integrators absorb costly organizational change.
- Watch next: Track paid conversion, daily workplace use, inference margins, capex financing, model switching, and pilots reaching production.
