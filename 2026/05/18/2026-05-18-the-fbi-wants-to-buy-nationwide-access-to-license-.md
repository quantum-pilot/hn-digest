# The FBI Wants to Buy Nationwide Access to License Plate Readers

- Score: 177 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48184350) | Link: https://www.404media.co/the-fbi-wants-to-buy-nationwide-access-to-license-plate-readers/

### TL;DR

FBI procurement records reviewed by 404 Media indicate the bureau wants nationwide access to automated license-plate-reader data, potentially enabling warrantless tracking of vehicles and their occupants across the country. The market appears narrow enough that Flock or Motorola could be likely suppliers, amid growing local protests against ALPR deployments. HN largely treated centralized access as mass surveillance, debating whether regulation should wall government off from privately collected data or ban collection outright, while acknowledging crime-fighting utility and questioning how fragmented local systems would integrate.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy proposals ranged from Fourth Amendment enforcement and making personal data a liability to prohibiting private ALPR collection entirely.
- ALPRs may aid investigations — counterpoint: commenters argued any database too dangerous for government access is also too dangerous for companies to hold.
- Plate avoidance may not defeat tracking: Flock reportedly identifies dents and stickers, while cameras can extend beyond roads onto pedestrian paths.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central risk is function creep: locally justified sensors become a de facto national movement database through commercial aggregation.
- Impact: Drivers, passengers, and pedestrians face retrospective location inference without choosing to participate or knowing which agencies can query records.
