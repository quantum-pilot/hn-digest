# Claude is not your architect. Stop letting it pretend

- Score: 224 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48259784) | Link: https://www.hollandtech.net/claude-is-not-your-architect/

### TL;DR

The essay says architecture should remain human-owned: LLMs produce agreeable, generic patterns without knowing team skills, legacy constraints, compliance limits, or operational consequences. Their polished proposals and Jira breakdowns can anchor reviewers, suppress productive disagreement, and leave engineers accountable for systems they did not design. Use agents to implement decisions while named humans challenge complexity and own trade-offs. HN agreed expertise determines results but noted detailed specifications can make deterministic implementation dramatically faster; debate centered on prompting for criticism, mandatory review, and liability when small teams create outsized risk.

### Comment pulse

- Prompting can diversify options → asking for several ranked approaches broadens search — counterpoint: only a competent user can evaluate the trade-offs.
- Detailed specifications constrain implementation → an assembler went from poor autonomous design to working tests in 20 minutes once passes and types were prescribed.
- Accountability must scale with blast radius → agentic speed lets undercapitalized teams expose millions to failures beyond the firm’s ability to compensate.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Architecture generation is best treated as option discovery, not authority; confidence and fluency are poor proxies for contextual fit.
- **Impact:** Senior engineers shift toward constraint definition, adversarial review, and risk ownership while agents handle verifiable implementation.
- **Watch next:** Compare human-led and agent-led designs on incident rates, change cost, operator comprehension, review dissent, and named ownership.
