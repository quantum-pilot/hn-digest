# US is starting to see heavy job losses in roles exposed to AI

- Score: 136 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48162354) | Link: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-15/us-is-starting-to-see-heavy-job-losses-in-roles-exposed-to-ai

### TL;DR

BLS data show 18 occupations considered plausibly AI-exposed, covering roughly 10 million US jobs, shrank 0.2% from May 2024 to May 2025 while total employment grew 0.8%. Removing booming medical secretaries, the remaining 17 fell 1.6% for a second year; customer-service employment alone dropped 130,180, or 4.8%. Bloomberg treats this as an early signal, not proof: BLS says its list is neither exhaustive nor definitive. HN disputed causality, citing AI substitution alongside post-pandemic overhiring, interest rates, tariffs, oil shocks, and broader slowdown.

### Comment pulse

- AI support often felt cheaper and worse — counterpoint: some found LLM agents more efficient and capable than scripted human representatives.
- Occupational effects differ: exposed clerical and service roles contracted, while BLS still projects growth for software, data science, research, operations analysis, and actuarial work.
- Commenters challenged “AI layoffs” as branding for old cost-cutting and excess staffing, especially in big tech, although the article examined broader occupations.

### LLM perspective

- View: The evidence is divergence, not attribution; exposure groups underperformed, but occupation-level data cannot isolate employer motives or macroeconomic shocks.
- Impact: Entry-level and routine communication work may face weaker hiring first, while augmentation-heavy analytical roles could expand.
- Watch next: Compare openings, hires, wages, hours, and task-level AI adoption within occupations against matched low-exposure controls.
