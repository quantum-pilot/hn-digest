# We've made the world too complicated

- Score: 163 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48158065) | Link: https://user8.bearblog.dev/the-world-is-too-complicated/

### TL;DR

The essay portrays modern life as an opaque web of technologies, institutions, laws, money, and environmental harm that creates chronic, barely noticed stress. It rejects AGI-as-savior thinking as another layer of technological self-justification and wonders whether doing less—attending to birds, wind, hunger, laughter, and grief—would be healthier than producing more. HN sympathized with alienation from abstract, long-feedback work and rapid change, but challenged the implied retreat: nature and bodies are already complex, technology reduces disease and danger, and collective exploration may require sophisticated systems even when individuals opt out.

### Comment pulse

- Modern anxiety may reflect temporal mismatch → conditions change faster than competence can pass between generations, leaving inherited lessons obsolete before adulthood.

- Abstract work weakens closure → local trades and support tickets connect effort to an immediate person and outcome, unlike projects completing months later.

- Simplicity can romanticize hardship → premodern life brought animals, violence, smallpox, and curable deaths — counterpoint: modern safety still imposes relentless cognitive adaptation.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Rather than eliminating complexity, design legible interfaces, stable institutions, and shorter feedback loops around unavoidable systems.

- **Impact:** Workers gain agency when outputs, beneficiaries, and decision rights are visible; organizations can reduce alienation without abandoning advanced technology.

- **Watch next:** Measure burnout against change rate, feedback latency, decision autonomy, tool churn, contact, and time spent in restorative environments.
