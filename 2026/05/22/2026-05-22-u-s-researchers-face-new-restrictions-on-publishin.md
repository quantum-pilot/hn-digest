# U.S. researchers face new restrictions on publishing with foreign collaborators

- Score: 319 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48238025) | Link: https://www.science.org/content/article/u-s-researchers-face-new-restrictions-publishing-foreign-collaborators

### TL;DR

NIH grant managers are reportedly treating papers co-authored with foreign-affiliated scientists as containing a foreign component, even when all work occurred in the United States; affected grantees have removed papers from progress reports or sought advance permission. NASA is likewise scrutinizing China-affiliated co-authors under the Wolf Amendment and warning of possible False Claims Act exposure. Neither agency has published a new rule, and both deny changing policy. HN largely saw the ambiguity as a deliberate or negligent chilling mechanism, while some defended scrutiny of asymmetric foreign collaborations.

### Comment pulse

- Informal enforcement is the core objection → case-by-case flagging leaves compliant researchers unable to know boundaries and encourages preemptive self-censorship.
- Affiliation is a poor security proxy → visiting researchers and work performed entirely domestically can trigger restrictions despite eventual public publication.
- Stronger review may correct unequal access → commenters questioned whether China reciprocates U.S. openness — counterpoint: cited programs suggest collaboration exists.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Funding controls should target money, restricted data, and sensitive work; co-authorship or affiliation is too coarse for risk assessment.
- **Impact:** Universities face heavier compliance reviews, incomplete productivity reporting, and pressure to exclude legitimate contributors, risking both ethics and recruitment.
- **Watch next:** Formal NIH and NASA guidance, appeal procedures, enforcement cases, and congressional clarification of the Wolf Amendment.
