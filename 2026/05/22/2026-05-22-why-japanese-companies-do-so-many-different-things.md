# Why Japanese companies do so many different things

- Score: 447 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48237163) | Link: https://davidoks.blog/p/why-japanese-companies-do-so-many

### TL;DR

Using Toto’s unexpected rise from toilets into semiconductor components, the essay explains Japanese diversification as one element of a tightly complementary corporate system. Lifetime employment, generalist rotation, seniority-based rewards, horizontal problem-solving, patient bank capital, insider governance, and weak shareholder pressure make survival and job preservation—not maximum returns—the firm’s objective, encouraging expansion into new fields. This structure excels at incremental refinement and tacit process knowledge but struggles with disruptive invention, reform, and zombie firms. HN debated whether the account was balanced analysis or Western romanticization.

### Comment pulse

- One critique linked the model to classism, corporate-status pressure, overwork, and workplace hierarchy — counterpoint: defenders noted the essay explicitly covers zombie firms and tradeoffs.

- Historical dissent challenged cultural essentialism: Western conglomerates also diversified until 1980s financialization shifted risk management from companies to investors’ portfolios.
