# College students drown out AI-praising commencement speeches with boos

- Score: 353 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48206241) | Link: https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/college-students-drown-out-ai-praising-commencement-speeches-with-boos-deal-with-it-one-speaker-fires-back-as-students-heckle-positive-pitches-for-ais-role

### TL;DR

Students at three U.S. universities booed commencement speakers promoting AI. Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt acknowledged fears that graduates’ futures were already written but still urged them to shape an inevitable technology; music executive Scott Borchetta answered jeers by telling students to adapt. HN read the backlash mainly as distrust of wealthy executives preaching disruption to entrants facing a weak job market. Commenters also noted Arizona’s protest was partly organized around separate allegations against Schmidt and challenged claims of a simple young-versus-old divide.

### Comment pulse

- Tone amplified resistance → rocket-ship inevitability and dismissive replies framed graduates as subjects of executives’ choices rather than participants shaping deployment.
- The divide is economic, not simply generational → workers with devalued skills and limited opt-outs bear risks; owners capture prospective productivity gains.
- Technological change can reward adaptation → careers have survived prior tool shifts — counterpoint: current entrants face debt, hiring cuts, and concentrated upside.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The boos reject governance and distribution claims more clearly than the technology itself; inevitability rhetoric mistakes adoption for consent.
- **Impact:** Universities and executives need audience-specific candor about job displacement, ownership, safeguards, and benefits before pitching AI as empowerment.
- **Watch next:** Graduate hiring data, wage trends, productivity evidence, campus speakers, and whether firms share AI gains with affected workers.
