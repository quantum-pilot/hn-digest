# Tennessee man jailed 37 days for Trump meme wins settlement after lawsuit

- Score: 653 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48208502) | Link: https://www.fire.org/news/victory-tennessee-man-jailed-37-days-trump-meme-wins-835000-settlement-after-first-amendment

### TL;DR

Retired Tennessee officer Larry Bushart will receive $835,000 to dismiss his federal civil-rights suit after Perry County jailed him for 37 days over a Facebook meme quoting Donald Trump. The meme referenced a 2024 school shooting in Perry, Iowa, but officials treated it as a threat against a Tennessee school; FIRE says the sheriff knew its origin yet omitted that context from the warrant application. Bushart endured a $2 million bond, lost work, and missed family milestones. HN focused less on compensation than absent personal accountability for police and judges.

### Comment pulse

- Many wanted settlements charged to officers or pension funds to align incentives — counterpoint: others said voters should bear costs for an elected sheriff.
- Judge accountability drew scrutiny because a warrant approved the arrest; commenters saw rubber-stamping and 37-day delay as compounding police omissions.
- Critics called payment without discipline no victory, urging criminal liability or agency-budget transparency; comparisons suggested other democracies also rarely prosecute such officers.

### LLM perspective

- View: Civil damages compensate victims but deter poorly when decision-makers face neither personal cost, discipline, nor public accounting.
- Impact: Bushart recovers some losses, but the settlement does not itself establish discipline, criminal liability, or reform.
- Watch next: Track settlement funding, disciplinary reviews, warrant-policy changes, judicial scrutiny, insurance effects, and disclosure of misconduct costs.
