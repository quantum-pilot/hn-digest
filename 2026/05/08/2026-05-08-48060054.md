# Ask HN: We just had an actual UUID v4 collision...

- Score: 286 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48060054) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48060054

### TL;DR

With only about 15,000 records, an independently random UUIDv4 collision is so improbable that the duplicate almost certainly signals an implementation or entropy failure, not bad luck. Investigate where IDs were generated, whether processes or VMs were forked or cloned, whether browser or sandbox RNGs used deterministic fallbacks, hardware health, and partial-read bugs. Preserve both records and runtime evidence. Keep a database uniqueness constraint and retry on conflict; high-assurance systems may prefer structured identifiers whose uniqueness does not depend entirely on opaque randomness.

### LLM perspective

- Record generator location, library version, platform, process lifecycle, and raw randomness behavior before evidence disappears.
- Treat identifiers as untrusted input; uniqueness constraints convert a catastrophic assumption into a handled exception.
- Repeated prefixes, trailing zeros, or timing correlations can reveal a defective entropy path.
