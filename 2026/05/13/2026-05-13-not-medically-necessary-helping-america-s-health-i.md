# "Not Medically Necessary": Helping America's Health Insurers Deny Coverage

- Score: 155 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48126000) | Link: https://www.propublica.org/article/evicore-health-insurance-denials-cigna-unitedhealthcare-aetna-prior-authorizations

### TL;DR

ProPublica reports EviCore, a Cigna-owned prior-authorization contractor covering more than 100 million people, markets a 3-to-1 insurer return and sometimes profits directly from reduced claims. Its algorithm cannot deny care, but managers can adjust thresholds so more requests reach human review, where denials become likelier; Arkansas data showed nearly 20% rejected fully or partly versus 7% in Medicare Advantage. Medical groups cite rigid, outdated guidelines and deterrence of legitimate requests. EviCore says evidence-based review prevents unsafe, wasteful care. Commenters’ clinicians described appeals against reviewers outside the relevant specialty.

### Comment pulse

- Clinicians said “peer” reviews often begin with nurses, therapists, or unrelated specialists, making persistence and credential checks essential to overturning denials.
- Prior authorization can deter fraud and unnecessary treatment — counterpoint: savings-linked contracts reward friction even when patients need legitimate care.
- Medicare commenters favored traditional coverage plus Medigap over Medicare Advantage, accepting higher premiums to avoid private networks and discretionary authorizations.

### LLM perspective

- View: Human sign-off cannot neutralize an algorithm controlling how many cases enter a denial-prone process.
- Impact: Burden shifts to clinicians and patients, while abandoned requests appear as reduced utilization rather than explicit denial.
- Watch next: Contract disclosure, specialty-matched reviews, appeal outcomes, guideline audits, denial rates, and stronger penalties.
