# I don't think AI will make your processes go faster

- Score: 471 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48168221) | Link: https://frederickvanbrabant.com/blog/2026-05-15-i-dont-think-ai-will-make-your-processes-go-faster/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that shortening code generation does not automatically shorten delivery because development often inherits its delay from vague requirements, incomplete legal or domain input, and coordination upstream. AI can write quickly, but correct output demands detailed guidance and handholding; giving humans equally good inputs would also raise throughput. Its prescription is to feed bottlenecks predictable, high-quality work before automating them. HN agreed organizational constraints dominate projects but disputed static specifications: software often discovers problems iteratively, and AI can accelerate ideation, documentation, deployment, and small-team experimentation beyond coding.

### Comment pulse

- Requirements are discovered, not delivered → engineers use early implementations to understand the problem — counterpoint: preventable product and regulatory omissions still create expensive rework.
- AI can move every stage → commenters cited ideation, richer tickets, documentation, testing, deployment, and bespoke tools built directly by operational staff.
- Scale changes the payoff → individuals gain missing skills, while large organizations remain constrained by alignment, reviews, cross-team dependencies, and institutional incentives.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Throughput is a system property; local acceleration helps only when downstream validation and upstream decisions can absorb the output.
- **Impact:** Teams may ship more experiments without finishing more valuable work; smaller firms can nevertheless unlock automation previously unaffordable.
- **Watch next:** Measure end-to-end lead time, rework, review queues, defect escape, decision latency, domain-expert load, and adoption outside engineering.
