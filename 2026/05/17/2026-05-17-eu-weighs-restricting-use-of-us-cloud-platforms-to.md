# EU weighs restricting use of US cloud platforms to process government data

- Score: 137 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48171015) | Link: https://www.osnews.com/story/144943/eu-weighs-restricting-use-of-us-cloud-platforms-to-process-sensitive-government-data/

### TL;DR

The EU is considering limiting member governments’ use of U.S. cloud providers for sensitive data. OSNews argues geopolitical trust has collapsed and dependence on Google, Microsoft, and Amazon creates unacceptable exposure, while predicting member-state resistance will dilute any rules. Discussion favored digital sovereignty and named European providers or government on-premises systems as viable alternatives. Skeptics stressed that EU data residency does not settle U.S. jurisdiction or vendor control, but complete separation is technically and politically difficult because identity, email, CDNs, DNS, third-party processors, and cloud-specific architectures remain entangled.

### Comment pulse

- European capacity exists → commenters named UpCloud, Scaleway, Hetzner, and OVH, arguing governments can combine regional compute, open source, and on-premises systems.

- Migration extends beyond hosting → customer data traverses identity platforms, email, CDNs, DNS, and opaque subprocessors, making sovereignty mapping difficult.

- EU coordination is the bottleneck → many member states must implement restrictions — counterpoint: China built domestic capacity by simply excluding U.S. technology.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Data location is insufficient sovereignty; ownership, administrative control, legal compulsion, update channels, and dependency portability all matter.

- **Impact:** Restrictions could create demand for European infrastructure while imposing costly migrations on agencies built around proprietary U.S. managed services.
