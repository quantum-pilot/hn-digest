# The dead economy theory

- Score: 616 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48324712) | Link: https://www.owenmcgrann.com/p/the-dead-economy-theory

### TL;DR

The essay argues that AI’s trillion-dollar economics require replacing human labor at scale, creating a collective-action trap: each firm captures payroll savings while spreading lost consumer demand across the economy. Even mediocre automation could erode jobs, skills, tax revenue, bargaining power, purpose, and democratic leverage before productivity gains arrive. It advocates wider capital ownership, automation taxes, and antitrust. HN split between treating this as a systemic warning and questioning its technological and philosophical premises.

### Comment pulse

- Past transitions offer practical analogies → agricultural modernization and containerization show that pacing, worker absorption, standards, and subsidies shape disruption.
- The labor-replacement premise may be overstated → LLMs might mainly change tooling or expose prior software overstaffing, not transform the whole economy.
- Work is not the only source of meaning → retirees often thrive outside paid employment — counterpoint: displacement can destroy status, knowledge, and community.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The argument is a stress test, not a forecast: capability, adoption, displacement, demand, and political capture remain separate uncertainties.
- **Impact:** Workers’ exposure depends less on headline GDP than on capital ownership, bargaining power, and access to durable replacement roles.
- **Watch next:** Track labor share, job quality, productivity, consumer demand, retraining outcomes, ownership concentration, and enacted transition policies.
