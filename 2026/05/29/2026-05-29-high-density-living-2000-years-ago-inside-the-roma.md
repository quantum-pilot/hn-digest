# High Density Living, 2000 Years Ago: Inside the Roman Apartment Building

- Score: 144 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48322241) | Link: https://commonedge.org/high-density-living-2000-years-ago-inside-the-roman-apartment-building/

### TL;DR

Ancient Rome’s insulae mixed shops below with rooms around light wells above, sometimes reaching eight stories. They pioneered shared stairs, balconies, arcades, and dense walkable living; Roman concrete and Nero’s postfire codes improved resilience. Yet fires, collapses, pests, absent plumbing, falling waste, weak tenant protections, and speculation made poorer upper floors dangerous. Ostia’s surviving brick-faced blocks show some apartments were spacious and prestigious, not merely tenements. HN debated whether this history supports modern density over car sprawl, while emphasizing ancient city life’s severe class and health costs.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Insulae demonstrate that successful density depends on mixed uses and proximity, but also construction quality, sanitation, and tenant safeguards.
- **Impact:** Ancient failures warn that simply permitting height can reproduce risk and inequality unless infrastructure and enforcement rise with occupancy.
- **Watch next:** Comparative evidence on Roman rents, occupancy, building failures, and how accurately Ostia’s unusually durable remains represent ordinary housing.
