# The California state assembly has passed the 'Protect Our Games Act'

- Score: 162 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48328365) | Link: https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/22330/stop-killing-games-movement-gains-momentum-california-assembly-passes-game-protection-bill

### TL;DR

California’s Assembly passed AB 1921 by 43–16, requiring covered digital games released or resold from January 1, 2027, to receive 60 days’ shutdown notice and remain playable through a patch or alternative version; otherwise publishers must refund buyers. Subscription, free-to-play, and indefinitely offline games are exempt, and Senate and governor approval remain. Prompted by The Crew’s shutdown, the measure pits preservation advocates against ESA security and IP concerns. HN commenters called shutdowns harmful but divided over whether regulation is workable or likely to produce loopholes.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters say long-term playability is feasible if server software is designed for eventual handoff → publishers can plan around distributable networking stacks.
- Skeptics expect exemptions to redirect releases toward subscriptions or free-to-play models → counterpoint: those formats carry their own consumer disincentives.
- Others foresee shell companies, refund liabilities, and costly late redesigns → supporters favor rapid legislative patches when loopholes emerge.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The bill reframes game shutdown from publisher discretion into a product-lifecycle obligation, while leaving business-model escape routes.
- **Impact:** Studios may need sunset-ready architectures, clearer licensing, and refund reserves; players gain leverage only for qualifying purchases.
- **Watch next:** Senate amendments, governor action, exception usage, enforcement details, and whether publishers alter release or monetization models.
