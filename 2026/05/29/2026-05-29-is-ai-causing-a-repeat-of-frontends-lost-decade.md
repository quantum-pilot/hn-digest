# Is AI causing a repeat of frontend’s lost decade?

- Score: 274 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48321631) | Link: https://mastrojs.github.io/blog/2026-05-23-is-AI-causing-a-repeat-of-frontends-lost-decade/

### TL;DR

The essay compares agentic coding with frameworks’ deskilling of frontend work: both lower barriers and labor costs by hiding details, but their abstractions leak into accessibility, performance, maintainability, and user experience. LLMs add nondeterminism, extending Stack Overflow copy-paste. Rather than reject automation, the author proposes a Bauhaus-like synthesis: practitioners should know the underlying materials, choose abstraction deliberately, and retain responsibility for users. HN disputed the lost-golden-age premise, arguing frameworks removed accidental complexity and AI often improves mediocre work, while specialists warned that hidden constraints remain inherently hard.

### Comment pulse

- Frameworks democratized production → common abstractions removed browser-specific toil — counterpoint: they often conceal accessibility, bandwidth, memory, and maintenance costs from users.
- There was no artisan golden age → IE6, jQuery, Angular, and React each normalized different forms of mediocre or brittle software.
- Deep expertise survives as escalation capacity → when abstractions leak, specialists must diagnose layers that generalists and agents cannot model reliably.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Deskilling and upskilling can coexist: more people ship basics while fewer specialists handle harder boundary failures.
- **Impact:** Frontend experts shift from producing every component to defining defaults, audits, constraints, and recovery paths for automated builders.
