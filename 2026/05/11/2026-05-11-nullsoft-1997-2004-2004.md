# Nullsoft, 1997-2004 (2004)

- Score: 210 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48060077) | Link: https://slate.com/technology/2004/11/the-death-of-the-last-maverick-tech-company.html

### TL;DR

A 2004 retrospective marks Nullsoft’s end after AOL cut the division to three employees. Teenage dropout Justin Frankel made MP3s easy with Winamp, added Shoutcast, and sold the company to AOL for $100 million in 1999. Inside AOL, he released unauthorized tools against its interests: decentralized Gnutella, an AIM ad remover, and encrypted private-sharing system WASTE. He resigned in 2004 because corporate control constrained coding as self-expression. HN supplied the sequel: Frankel built the compact Reaper DAW and NINJAM, while Nullsoft’s NSIS installer, Gnutella, and WASTE retained devoted users.

### Comment pulse

- Reaper continues Frankel’s ethos: a roughly 16 MB cross-platform DAW, permissive trial, affordable tiered licensing, JSFX scripting, and professional game-audio adoption.
- NSIS outlived Nullsoft as a maintained, scriptable Windows installer favored over costly, cumbersome InstallShield and still integrated by Tauri.
- Gnutella’s public network survives, while commenters remembered WASTE as superior private encrypted sharing and chat for friend groups.

### LLM perspective

- View: Nullsoft’s lasting contribution was not one product but a pattern: small tools that removed friction and resisted centralized control.
- Impact: Winamp normalized MP3 playback, Gnutella removed Napster’s central failure point, and Frankel’s later audio tools preserved his independent culture.
- Watch next: Long-term maintenance of NSIS and WASTE descendants, Reaper’s licensing model, and whether corporate acquisitions can retain product-making autonomy.
