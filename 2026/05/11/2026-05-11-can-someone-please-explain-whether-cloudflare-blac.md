# Can someone please explain whether Cloudflare blackmailed Canonical?

- Score: 231 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48098537) | Link: https://www.flyingpenguin.com/can-someone-please-explain-whether-cloudflare-blackmailed-canonical/

### TL;DR

During an April 30 DDoS outage lasting roughly 20 hours, Canonical moved its security and archive repository endpoints behind Cloudflare, where they stabilized. The claimed attackers said they rented Beamed, a DDoS-for-hire service whose marketing site uses Cloudflare and advertises methods for bypassing its protection. The author characterizes Cloudflare’s simultaneous protection of that site and sale of mitigation to Canonical as racket-like blackmail, while acknowledging no ransom demand or visible payment beyond a subscription. Commenters rejected the conclusion: hosting Beamed’s front end does not show Cloudflare infrastructure launched the attack.

### Comment pulse

- Neutrality advocates favored removal only under lawful orders, avoiding arbitrary gatekeeping and identity requirements for ordinary hosting.
- Abuse responders said Cloudflare’s proxying blocks IP-level defenses and obscures origins, making reports slow, risky, or operationally futile.
- Beamed’s advertised bypass appears to violate technical-abuse terms — counterpoint: removing its site would not necessarily disable the attack service.

### LLM perspective

- View: The timeline supports emergency procurement, not coercion; blackmail requires evidence of coordination, knowledge, or materially enabled attacks.
- Impact: Cloudflare’s scale makes abuse-policy choices consequential for defenders, customers, and lawful publishers sharing the same infrastructure.
- Watch next: Canonical’s incident report, traffic attribution, Cloudflare’s response, Beamed enforcement, contract timing, and verification of the 3.5-Tbps claim.
