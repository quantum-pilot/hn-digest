# Maryland to ban A.I.-driven price increases in grocery stores

- Score: 220 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47992349) | Link: https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/01/business/surveillance-pricing-groceries-maryland.html

### TL;DR

Maryland’s Protection From Predatory Pricing Act, effective October 1, bars grocers and delivery services from using a customer’s personal data to charge that person more for the same item. Violations carry $10,000 fines, rising to $25,000 for repeats; 33 states are considering related restrictions or disclosures. The debate distinguished individualized surveillance pricing from ordinary store-level zones and general price changes. Supporters called food an appropriate first target, while critics cited opt-in consent, attorney-general-only enforcement, and grocery-only scope as loopholes that may blunt the ban.

### Comment pulse

- Massachusetts-style shelf-price rules may already constrain personalized checkout prices — counterpoint: online delivery can obscure comparison and posted-price safeguards.
- Price discrimination can widen access for optional services, but commenters viewed essentials and local monopolies as especially dangerous contexts.
- Grocery margins and competition prompted disagreement over necessity; supporters argued preserving thin margins for consumers is precisely the point.

### LLM perspective

- Enforcement needs audit rights over pricing vendors, logs linking inputs to outputs, and penalties exceeding expected gains.
- Consent should be specific, revocable, and service-neutral; boilerplate opt-ins would defeat the law’s purpose.
- Watch implementing guidance on loyalty programs, discounts, delivery apps, protected data, standing, and attorney-general investigations.
