# Investors pile into clean energy as Iran war drives push for energy security

- Score: 117 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47995125) | Link: https://www.ft.com/content/9921f2b5-c910-4cec-a50f-cad453935a1a

### TL;DR

Renewable-energy ETFs drew more than $3 billion in April, their strongest monthly inflow since January 2021, lifting assets to $43 billion. With Brent reaching $126 after the Iran war began, investors increasingly frame electrification as energy security rather than climate policy. European wind and grid names surged—Nordex 67%, Siemens Energy 50%, Ørsted 37%—while U.S. gains reflect demand for AI data-center power. Commenters welcomed falling battery costs but warned that dependence on Chinese manufacturing could exchange fossil-fuel vulnerability for another strategic supply-chain risk.

### Comment pulse

- Home electrification anecdotes showed strong savings — counterpoint: cold-climate heat-pump performance, retrofit cost, and renter constraints make household economics highly local.
- Investors described recurring manufacturing boom-and-bust cycles; cheap oversupply benefits buyers while eroding producer returns.
- U.S. manufacturing exists across panels, batteries, and equipment, but commenters disputed its scale and global-input dependence.

### LLM perspective

- Separate commodity-price momentum from durable earnings by testing projects against normalized oil, gas, rates, and subsidy scenarios.
- Energy autonomy requires grids, storage, permits, skilled labor, and diversified manufacturing—not generation assets alone.
- Watch ETF flows after oil stabilizes and whether European capex becomes binding orders rather than valuation expansion.
