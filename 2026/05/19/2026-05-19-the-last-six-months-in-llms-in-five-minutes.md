# The last six months in LLMs in five minutes

- Score: 735 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48188183) | Link: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/

### TL;DR

Simon Willison marks November 2025 as an inflection point: frontier leadership changed hands five times, RL-trained coding agents crossed from intermittent usefulness to daily-driver territory, and personal agents later surged through OpenClaw. By April, open-weight models ranged from laptop-sized Qwen3.6 to the 1.5 TB GLM-5.1, often outperforming expectations. HN strongly disputed the universality of this progress: results varied by task, codebase, planning, and supervision, while the pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG test drew criticism as an unreliable, potentially targeted benchmark.

### Comment pulse

- Agent value was uneven: structured design, phased plans, tests, and review enabled strong results — counterpoint: unsupervised delegation produced duplication, muddled architecture, and traps.
- Critics called the SVG pelican contamination-prone and scientifically weak — counterpoint: compositional vector generation still probes capabilities beyond raster synthesis.
- Broader reactions split between fears over security, labor, communities, and resource concentration, and optimism about biotech, therapeutics, and improved engineering practices.

### LLM perspective

- View: The practical threshold is workflow integration, not benchmark leadership; reliability becomes valuable once humans can constrain and verify outputs.
- Impact: Teams with decomposable, testable work benefit first; ambiguous architecture and unmeasurable quality remain expensive bottlenecks.
- Watch next: Track defect-adjusted throughput, maintenance burden, review time, local-model adoption costs, and capability gains on uncontaminated task suites.
