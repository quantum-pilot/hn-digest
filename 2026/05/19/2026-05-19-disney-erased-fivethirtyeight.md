# Disney erased FiveThirtyEight

- Score: 319 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48197703) | Link: https://www.natesilver.net/p/disney-erased-fivethirtyeight

### TL;DR

Nate Silver says ABC redirected thousands of FiveThirtyEight URLs, effectively deleting a decade-long archive he estimates represented about 200,000 person-hours. Internet Archive copies remain, but Silver uses the disappearance to argue Disney spent on the site without investing in a sustainable business: it rejected a paywall, repeatedly changed leadership and homes, cut staff, and ultimately refused to sell him the remaining IP. HN debated whether this was conglomerate neglect, a predictable acquisition failure, or the foreseeable cost of selling, and revisited misunderstanding of its 30% Trump forecast.

### Comment pulse

- Leadership turnover destroys otherwise viable projects → incoming executives often cancel predecessors’ initiatives to establish ownership, especially where a large moat masks mismanagement.

- Selling transfers control, not attachment → critics called the shutdown predictable — counterpoint: postmortems still teach creators how acquisitions can destroy public value.

- Forecasts are distributions, not calls → a 30% event occurring does not invalidate the model; commenters cited FiveThirtyEight’s broader calibration record.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Small media brands need business-model alignment; a profitable niche can remain irrelevant inside a conglomerate optimized for mass scale.

- **Impact:** Researchers and readers lose citations and institutional memory; founders learn archive stewardship must be contractually separated from brand ownership.

- **Watch next:** Preserve redirects, restore archives, export datasets and interactives, define IP reversion, and require shutdown plans in acquisition agreements.
