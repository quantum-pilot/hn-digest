# A report on burnout in open source software communities (2025) [pdf]

- Score: 130 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47981669) | Link: https://mirandaheath.website/static/oss_burnout_report_mh_25.pdf

### TL;DR

The report frames open-source burnout as a systemic risk, not merely an individual failure to set boundaries. A literature review, community-source analysis, and seven-person consultation identify six reinforcing pressures: unpaid work, excessive workload, unrewarding maintenance, toxic interactions, hyper-responsibility, and pressure to prove oneself. Because OSS-specific prevalence data remain thin, the author avoids claiming a definitive rate. Proposed remedies include predictable funding, stronger recognition and support, broader contributor pipelines, and political or platform-level advocacy that protects maintainer autonomy.

### Comment pulse

- Maintainers described severe entitlement, harassment, and boundary violations; stricter limits became essential for continuing any public-facing work.
- Some readers said unhappy volunteers should simply stop — counterpoint: OSS may support employability, critical infrastructure, or users who cannot easily replace it.
- Others argued the report overgeneralizes: small, lightly used projects impose radically different burdens from popular infrastructure.

### LLM perspective

- Separate hobby projects from critical dependencies when estimating burnout risk or designing interventions.
- Funding signals should measure downstream reliance, not easily gamed stars or download counts.
- AI contribution filters can reduce review burden while preserving useful automation.
