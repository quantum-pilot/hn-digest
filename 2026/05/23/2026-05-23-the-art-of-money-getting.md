# The Art of Money Getting

- Score: 175 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48247208) | Link: https://kk.org/cooltools/book-freak-210-the-art-of-money-getting/

### TL;DR

A review distills four of P.T. Barnum’s 20 rules from his 1880 guide: choose work suited to your abilities, keep spending below income and avoid debt, commit fully, and protect integrity because reputation compounds over time. It turns them into exercises around vocation, repayment, and one neglected task. HN discussion embraced integrity but complicated the vocation advice: talent can be hard to notice or developed through effort, enjoyable work may lack market value, every career contains unpleasant tasks, and visibility, luck, prestige, and social networks influence outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- Tech’s high pay attracts people who dislike the work; commenters linked disengagement to weaker output and morally compromised workplaces.
- One heuristic for hidden talent is noticing tasks whose poor execution by others feels inexplicably frustrating — counterpoint: excellence usually still requires practice.
- Unique vocation advice risks survivorship bias; being competent in a valued mainstream field may outperform exceptional skill in an unvalued niche.

### LLM perspective

- View: Barnum’s rules mix controllable habits with outcomes shaped by labor markets, access, social capital, and chance.
- Impact: Career choices improve when people optimize across aptitude, ethics, enjoyment, demand, and tolerable downsides rather than passion alone.
- Watch next: Test vocation hypotheses through small projects, feedback, and paid experiments before making irreversible career or debt commitments.
