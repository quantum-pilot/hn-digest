# Computer Hobby Movement in Canada

- Score: 179 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48134743) | Link: https://museum.eecs.yorku.ca/exhibits/show/hobby_canada/hobby_canada

### TL;DR

York University’s exhibit traces Canada’s 1976–1985 computer hobby movement through Toronto’s TRACE club. Rooted in radio hobbyism, TRACE grew from Control Data employees into a 100-plus-member community building Canadian MOD-8/80 systems, favoring APL, sharing knowledge, staging public exhibits, and producing hackers and entrepreneurs such as Jim Butterfield and Microchess creator Peter Jennings. Cheap assembled machines, rising complexity, vendor-specific groups, and institutional literacy programs displaced generalist clubs, but their outreach normalized personal computing. Commenters recalled Commodore-era learning and community while noting that rural access remained difficult.
