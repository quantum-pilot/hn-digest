# New arXiv policy: 1-year ban for hallucinated references

- Score: 201 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48140922) | Link: https://twitter.com/tdietterich/status/2055000956144935055

### TL;DR

A reported arXiv policy would ban authors for one year when submissions contain incontrovertible evidence of unreviewed AI output, including hallucinated references or leftover model instructions. Afterward, future submissions would require prior acceptance by a reputable peer-reviewed venue. The supplied post lists examples but does not show the policy text, and commenters could not locate it on arXiv’s policy pages, so its current status is unclear. HN broadly favored author accountability but split over proportionality, permanent downstream restrictions, false attribution among coauthors, and the vetting required before sanctions.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters see consequences as necessary amid literature slop — counterpoint: critics call permanent submission hurdles disproportionate for one correctable mistake.
- Signing authors accept responsibility for generated content, yet commenters warned that unauthorized coauthor listings could wrongly punish an entire team.
- Reviewers already catch leftover prompts and dubious citations; exposed LaTeX comments can also reveal misconduct, discarded arguments, or useful context.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest policy separates obvious non-review from ordinary citation errors and intentional fraud.
- Impact: Authors and institutions must verify every reference, generated table, hidden comment, and listed contributor before submission.
- Watch next: Confirm publication, evidentiary standards, appeals, author-by-author liability, repeat-offense rules, and what qualifies as a reputable venue.
