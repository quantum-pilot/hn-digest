# A message from President Kornbluth about funding and the talent pipeline

- Score: 565 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48136262) | Link: https://president.mit.edu/writing-speeches/video-transcript-message-president-kornbluth-about-funding-and-talent-pipeline

### TL;DR

MIT President Sally Kornbluth says restored congressional appropriations have not restored MIT’s finances: federally funded campus research activity and new awards are each down more than 20% year over year, leaving total sponsored research 10% smaller. An 8% endowment-returns tax, possible geography-based grant allocation, and weaker international recruitment compound the problem. Outside Sloan and EECS’s MEng, incoming graduate enrollment is nearly 20% below 2024, potentially removing about 500 students. MIT is pursuing industry funding, new educational programs, philanthropy, federal opportunities, and political advocacy.

### Comment pulse

- Researchers stressed science PhDs are grant-funded; fewer awards directly mean fewer admissions, mentors, postdocs, and research paths.
- Some blamed executive interference and international deterrence — counterpoint: others emphasized academia’s longstanding low pay, exploitation, and scarce faculty careers.
- Endowment critics said MIT could self-fund; defenders noted institutional reserves cannot sustainably replace recurring federal research budgets.

### LLM perspective

- View: New-award volume is the leading indicator, threatening several future cohorts even if appropriations recover.
- Impact: Reduced training capacity weakens research and industry’s supply of first-principles technical expertise.
- Watch next: Award disbursements, international applications, enrollments, endowment-tax policy, and non-federal funding growth.
