# U.S. DOJ demands Apple and Google unmask over 100k users of car-tinkering app

- Score: 334 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48151383) | Link: https://macdailynews.com/2026/05/15/u-s-doj-demands-apple-and-google-unmask-over-100000-users-of-popular-car-tinkering-app-in-emissions-crackdown/

### TL;DR

In its Clean Air Act case against EZ Lynk, DOJ subpoenaed Apple and Google for download data on every Auto Agent installer, plus Amazon and Walmart purchase records for the paired OBD hardware—potentially identifying more than 100,000 people. Prosecutors say usage evidence and witnesses could show whether the product primarily enabled emissions-control defeat; EZ Lynk says it supports lawful diagnostics, monitoring, updates, and modifications. Privacy critics call the demand speculative and overbroad. HN condemned rolling coal yet favored targeted enforcement, roadside citations, or inspections over bulk identification of lawful users.

### Comment pulse

- DOJ may seek a representative usage pattern to undermine EZ Lynk’s lawful-purpose defense — counterpoint: app installation alone supplies no individualized suspicion.
- Commenters proposed enforceable alternatives: ticket visible emissions violations or require periodic inspections, avoiding mass disclosure while directly identifying noncompliant vehicles.
- Centralized app stores create a searchable installation ledger; anonymous distribution may reduce purchase records, but platform scanning and device identifiers can preserve traceability.

### LLM perspective

- View: The evidentiary dispute is proportionality; aggregate usage may matter to product liability, while universal identity disclosure burdens lawful users.
- Impact: A ruling could shape how regulators use app-store and retail records to turn product investigations into user-population discovery.
- Watch next: Track subpoena narrowing, platform challenges, judicial reasoning, sampling alternatives, notice to users, and limits on downstream identity use.
