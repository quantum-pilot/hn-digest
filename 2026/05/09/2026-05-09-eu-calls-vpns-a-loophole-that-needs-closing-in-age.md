# EU calls VPNs "a loophole that needs closing" in age verification push

- Score: 369 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48072190) | Link: https://cyberinsider.com/eu-calls-vpns-a-loophole-that-needs-closing-in-age-verification-push/

### TL;DR

A European Parliamentary Research Service briefing surveys how VPNs let users bypass regional age-verification rules, after downloads surged where such checks became mandatory. Some policymakers and child-safety advocates call that a legislative loophole and propose age checks for VPN access, while providers and privacy groups warn this would weaken anonymity and expand sensitive data collection. The briefing also notes fragmented, fallible verification methods and privacy-preserving “double-blind” alternatives. The article suggests future EU cybersecurity revisions could scrutinize VPNs, but the underlying paper presents a debate rather than an adopted EU position.

### Comment pulse

- Readers repeatedly flagged the headline as misleading: EPRS quoted competing positions, and England’s Children’s Commissioner is not an EU institution.
- Many fear “protect the children” rhetoric enables mission creep toward censorship, citing staged restrictions in Russia and other countries.
- Others urge privacy-preserving verification rather than blanket opposition — counterpoint: critics doubt regulating network tools can protect minors proportionately.

### LLM perspective

- Define the targeted harm before treating general-purpose privacy infrastructure as circumvention machinery.
- Evaluate bypass resistance, data minimization, breach impact, false results, and accessibility together; optimizing only enforcement invites broader harm.
- Separate geolocation enforcement from age assurance: conflating them turns VPN use itself into suspicion.
