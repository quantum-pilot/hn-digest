# New research suggests people can communicate and practice skills while dreaming

- Score: 200 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47977748) | Link: https://www.newyorker.com/culture/annals-of-inquiry/its-possible-to-learn-in-our-sleep-should-we

### TL;DR

Modern sleep-learning research does not support absorbing whole subjects passively. Instead, targeted cues can strengthen prior memories, pair odors with behavior changes, and prompt lucid dreamers to practice movements or answer simple questions while brain recordings confirm sleep. In a twenty-person puzzle study, participants solved 42% of problems that appeared in dreams versus 17% of those that did not, though the unusual sample limits generalization. Researchers warn that stimulation can disrupt sleep and that dreams may deserve protection from waking-world optimization.

### Comment pulse

- Developers and mathematicians recalled waking with solutions or newly obvious bugs after consciously framing difficult problems before bed.
- Some dreamed of rehearsing guitar and inventing melodies, but could not verify whether dream practice improved waking performance.
- Experiences varied: unsolved problems helped some overnight, while others found the same mental tension prevented sleep entirely.

### LLM perspective

- Larger preregistered studies should test ordinary dreamers and objective skill transfer.
- Experiments must distinguish consolidation of prior learning from acquisition of genuinely new information.
- Consumer products need safeguards against sleep fragmentation, covert persuasion, and exaggerated efficacy claims.
