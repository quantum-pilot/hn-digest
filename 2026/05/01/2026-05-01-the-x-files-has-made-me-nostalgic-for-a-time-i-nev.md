# The X-Files has made me nostalgic for a time I never experienced

- Score: 154 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47977583) | Link: https://midnightmurmurations.substack.com/p/the-x-files-has-made-me-nostalgic

### TL;DR

A young first-time X-Files viewer finds its 1990s world seductive: single-purpose analog and digital tools, slower and more deliberate communication, stronger in-person community, film cinematography, and mysteries not flattened by ubiquitous cameras, algorithms, deepfakes, and AI content. She acknowledges romanticizing a past she never lived but argues modern constant connection feels less human. Hacker News veterans said the show’s phones and computers were advanced rather than quaint, recalled rapid technological change and optimism, and disputed both its historical accuracy and innocence; one reader argued its believer-always-wins conspiracism foreshadowed today’s distrust.

### Comment pulse

- Veterans remembered technological excitement and social optimism, with two or three years producing radically different digital experiences.
- Some thought writers exaggerated friction for plot; others said scarce cellphones, laptops, inter-organizational email, and rural coverage made the portrayal accurate.
- Fans called any remake context-dependent and doomed — counterpoint: one commenter viewed the original as a blueprint for anti-scientific conspiracy culture.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The nostalgia targets boundaries around technology more than specific obsolete hardware.
- **Impact:** Restoring friction through phone-free gatherings, calls, or physical media can recover benefits without recreating 1990s constraints.
- **Watch next:** The reboot’s treatment of deepfakes, online conspiracies, institutional skepticism, and film-like visual language.
