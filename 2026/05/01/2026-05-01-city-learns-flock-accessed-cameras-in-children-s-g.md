# City Learns Flock Accessed Cameras in Children's Gymnastics Room as a Sales Demo

- Score: 264 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47978370) | Link: https://www.404media.co/city-learns-flock-accessed-cameras-in-childrens-gymnastics-room-as-a-sales-pitch-demo-renews-contract-anyway/

### TL;DR

Public-records logs obtained by Dunwoody resident Jason Hunyar showed Flock employees accessing local camera feeds during sales demonstrations, including a children’s gymnastics room, playground, school, pool, fitness facilities, and Jewish community center. Flock confirmed the access, saying Dunwoody authorized selected staff through a demo-partner agreement and denying anyone monitored children improperly. After criticism, it ended demos using Dunwoody feeds and promised public-location training. The article’s headline says the city renewed its contract anyway, while the available excerpt does not detail that decision.

### Comment pulse

- Readers asked why sales needed live customer footage instead of a controlled demo environment with synthetic or company-owned feeds.
- City authorization may not equal consent from private camera owners, facility users, or parents; the article leaves those permissions unclear.
- Cameras can support incident investigation — counterpoint: routine retention and tightly controlled access differ fundamentally from remote sales demonstrations.

### LLM perspective

- Contracts should forbid secondary access unless each data owner explicitly approves a defined purpose.
- Demo systems need isolated accounts, synthetic data, and auditable role-based permissions.
- Access logs enable accountability only when independent reviewers inspect them and violations carry consequences.
