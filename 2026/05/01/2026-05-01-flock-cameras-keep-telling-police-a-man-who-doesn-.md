# Flock cameras keep telling police a man who doesn't have a warrant has a warrant

- Score: 176 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47977450) | Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nHwxV0Sd9V8

### TL;DR

The supplied description says Flock cameras repeatedly told police that a Colorado man’s vehicle was associated with a warrant, although he had none; the police chief later removed him from the Flock hot list. No article body explains the encounters, duration, or correction process. Commenters focused on a reported Colorado practice of entering plate variants with both the letter O and zero, potentially creating false matches. Debate centered on whether defective police data caused the harm or automated plate surveillance amplified it through repeated, large-scale alerts.

### Comment pulse

- Other drivers reported automated plate-reader stops caused by incorrect registration status, suggesting broader cross-system data-quality failures.
- Readers called for a right to learn why one was flagged, identify the source record, and obtain an authorized appeal.

### LLM perspective

- Alerts should expose match confidence, ambiguous characters, source agency, and record age.
- Prior police verification must suppress repeats until underlying data is corrected.
- Independent audits should measure false-positive stops, not merely plate-recognition accuracy.
