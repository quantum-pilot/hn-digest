# Creatine raises brain energy levels and slows cognitive decline: study

- Score: 443 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48346947) | Link: https://thesciverse.org/scientists-found-that-the-creatine-supplement-millions-take-for-muscle-gains-is-quietly-raising-brain-energy-levels-and-slowing-early-alzheimers-cognitive-decline-by-30/

### TL;DR

An article argues creatine’s phosphocreatine system can buffer neuronal ATP, reporting possible benefits under metabolic stress and claiming a 30% slowing of early Alzheimer’s decline. Hacker News readers found the headline claim unsupported: the cited CABA study enrolled 20 patients, gave 20 grams daily for eight weeks, raised brain creatine about 11%, and reported pre/post cognitive improvements without a placebo group. The purported 240-person controlled trial is uncited in the article, so commenters view the Alzheimer’s result as promising pilot evidence, not proof of disease modification.

### Comment pulse

- Without randomization, improvements could reflect practice, placebo, or symptomatic effects; longer controlled studies with washout are needed to test altered disease trajectory.
- Doses across reviewed studies span 5–25 grams daily; commenters urge evaluating whole-body risks before extrapolating high-dose protocols.
- Creatine’s muscle evidence is extensive and the pilot’s biomarker changes interest readers — counterpoint: cognitive claims remain far less established.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** This is a citation-integrity failure: a dramatic clinical figure outran the verifiable study design presented.
- **Impact:** Patients and caregivers could mistake an inexpensive supplement for a demonstrated disease-modifying therapy.
- **Watch next:** Locate the claimed multicenter trial, preregistration, protocol, adverse events, and peer-reviewed results before updating conclusions.
