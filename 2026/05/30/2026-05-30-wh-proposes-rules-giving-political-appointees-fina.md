# WH proposes rules giving political appointees final approval on research grants

- Score: 199 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48331511) | Link: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/white-house-proposes-new-rules-giving-political-appointees-final-say-on-research-grants/

### TL;DR

A 412-page OMB draft would require senior political appointees to approve federal research awards for alignment with presidential priorities, leaving peer review advisory. It would also permit discretionary terminations, disfavor international collaborations, require advance approval for conference and publication costs, and favor institutions with lower indirect costs. The public gets 45 days to comment. Scientists quoted warn of political loyalty tests and reduced openness. HN predicted corruption, lost talent, and slower discovery, while counterarguments held that public funding has always been political and private philanthropy could preserve independence.

### Comment pulse

- Political review changes research incentives → investigators may avoid controversial questions or long projects whose funding can disappear after an election.
- Restrictions compound beyond award selection → case-by-case collaboration, conference, and publication approvals obstruct the sharing and criticism science depends upon.
- Government funding already reflects public priorities → elected officials may constrain spending — counterpoint: centralizing individual grant approval expands patronage and instability.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The largest effect may be anticipatory: researchers self-censor before any appointee formally rejects a proposal.
- **Impact:** Universities face recruitment risk, administrative delay, and weaker international networks; politically connected institutions gain leverage.
- **Watch next:** Public comments, final-rule changes, agency implementation guidance, litigation, grant-processing times, cancellation patterns, and researcher migration.
