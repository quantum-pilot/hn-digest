# I'm 60 years old. Claude Code killed a passion

- Score: 185 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47386813) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47386813

### TL;DR

An almost-60 programmer says AI has drained coding’s appeal because the pleasure was solving and understanding each puzzle, not merely shipping results. The discussion confirms that divide but complicates it: some engineers feel reduced to supervising an unreliable junior, while others delegate mechanical work and pursue harder system-design problems or previously impossible projects. Workplace mandates and competitive pressure make simply opting out unrealistic. Several commenters propose preserving craftsmanship through deliberate boundaries, small reviewed increments, and personal projects where humans retain design, interfaces, and acceptance criteria.

### Comment pulse

- Craftsmanship advocates warn that skipped implementation hides architectural friction and weakens judgment — counterpoint: structured delegation can preserve human modeling and responsibility.
- AI enthusiasts clear tedious backlogs and climb toward harder problems; journey-first programmers experience the same abstraction as lost mastery and camaraderie.
- Mandated workplace adoption changes a personal tool choice into a livelihood and burnout issue.
