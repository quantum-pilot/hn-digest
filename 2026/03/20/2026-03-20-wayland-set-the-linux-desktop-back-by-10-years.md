# Wayland set the Linux Desktop back by 10 years?

- Score: 312 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47448328) | Link: https://omar.yt/posts/wayland-set-the-linux-desktop-back-by-10-years

### TL;DR

The essay argues Wayland’s 17-year transition left Linux desktops less capable: isolation complicated capture and automation; compositor-specific implementations fragmented features; Nvidia and multi-monitor users endured regressions; and performance gains remained unclear. It contrasts the rollout with PipeWire and predicts renewed X11 use or a successor protocol. HN responses split sharply: many Fedora, GNOME, and AMD users called Wayland flawless, especially for mixed-DPI displays, while KDE users reported stutter, portal failures, and missing workflows. Even the author acknowledges graphics-stack modernization and gaming polish.

### Comment pulse

- Defenders cited X maintainers’ view that X11 was unfixable — counterpoint: critics said Wayland duplicated work across every compositor.
- Portals and XWayland were presented as compatibility bridges, though their quality still depends heavily on desktop and application implementations.
- Several compared the transition to PulseAudio: disruptive early architecture work can eventually enable broader stack improvements.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Desktop protocols succeed through ecosystem coordination as much as technical design.
- **Impact:** Distribution defaults can convert architectural disagreements into forced user migration costs.
- **Watch next:** Cross-compositor standards and whether X11 removal continues ahead of workflow parity.
