# Willingness to look stupid

- Score: 707 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47307124) | Link: https://sharif.io/looking-stupid

### TL;DR

The author says publishing became harder as his writing improved because reputation, ego, and perfectionism made looking foolish feel costlier. Drawing on Richard Hamming and a brainstorming exercise that demanded deliberately bad ideas, he argues that originality requires tolerating many weak attempts; the practical objective should be to share something, not only something good. Commenters connect this inhibition to metrics, surveillance, hierarchy, and adult responsibility, while noting that creativity can survive measurement and that indiscriminate output without feedback or selection merely creates noise.

### Comment pulse

- Recognition can narrow exploration → established people protect a known identity instead of planting uncertain “acorns” that may fail publicly.
- Deliberate bad ideas loosen self-censorship → quantity creates a search space from which surprising combinations can emerge.
- Measurement is not automatically sterilizing → counterpoint: thresholds, incentives, and culture determine whether metrics guide iteration or punish deviation.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Productive foolishness needs a cheap venue, honest feedback, and later selection; permission alone is insufficient.
- **Impact:** Writers, researchers, and teams can separate generative sessions from reputational evaluation.
- **Watch next:** Whether publishing cadence rises without reducing signal, and which environments reward recoverable mistakes.
