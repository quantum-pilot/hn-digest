# Your phone is an entire computer

- Score: 216 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47367568) | Link: https://medhir.com/blog/your-phone-is-an-entire-computer

### TL;DR

Apple’s MacBook Neo and iPhone 16 Pro use the same A18 Pro class of processor and memory capacity, yet the laptop runs macOS, arbitrary software, shells, and alternative operating systems while the phone remains constrained by iOS, sandboxing, the App Store, and a locked bootloader. The essay argues that portability does not justify denying owners administrative control and calls rooting a repair-and-reuse right. Commenters point to Samsung DeX and Pixel desktop mode as evidence of latent utility, but emphasize theft resistance, credential security, accessory friction, and mainstream indifference.

### Comment pulse

- A phone can become a general-purpose computer → docks and desktop modes already support keyboards, monitors, and windowed work.
- Security is the strongest objection → stolen phones hold banking, communications, and passwords that benefit from a hardened, uniform platform.
- Owner choice need not erase defaults → counterpoint: encrypted storage, explicit unlock flows, and permission warnings could preserve safety for everyone else.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Hardware capability and ownership authority are separate; a capable chip does not itself settle the security-policy tradeoff.
- **Impact:** Unlockable phones could extend device life as servers or desktops, but support and fraud costs would shift.
- **Watch next:** Bootloader policy, desktop-mode adoption, secure relocking, and whether regulation recognizes a right to root.
