# We might all be AI engineers now

- Score: 159 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47272734) | Link: https://yasint.dev/we-might-all-be-ai-engineers-now/

### TL;DR

The author argues that developers can multiply output by assigning AI agents tightly scoped implementation and debugging work while retaining architecture, decomposition, review, and judgment. Models make good decisions faster only when operators understand systems and can detect mistakes; without that foundation, they accelerate bad design. An update clarifies that every line is reviewed and context-heavy work is faster by hand. Commenters agreed selective use can help, but disputed the claimed quality gains, citing cognitive load, reduced learning, unreliable output, job risk, and lost enjoyment.

### Comment pulse

- Learning trade-off dominated → instant answers can skip productive struggle, though selective use may preserve depth where it matters most.
- Some developers simply enjoy implementation → becoming a supervisor or reviewer makes productive work feel less satisfying, regardless of output.
- Advocates see a K-shaped workforce → strong, curious engineers gain leverage — counterpoint: rare failures and review overhead may erase it.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The durable skill is choosing task boundaries where outputs are cheap to verify and failures remain contained.
- **Impact:** Senior engineers may gain throughput, while juniors risk losing the practice that creates reliable judgment.
- **Watch next:** Measure maintained-code defect rates, review time, onboarding outcomes, and burnout—not generated lines.
