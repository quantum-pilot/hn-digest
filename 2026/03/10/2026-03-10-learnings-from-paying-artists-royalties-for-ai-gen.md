# Learnings from paying artists royalties for AI-generated art

- Score: 163 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47318421) | Link: https://www.kapwing.com/blog/learnings-from-paying-artists-royalties-for-ai-generated-art/

### TL;DR

Kapwing’s Tess marketplace paid artists 50% whenever subscribers generated images from style-specific models trained on consenting portfolios. After 20 months, 37 artists and 142 customers produced only $12,172.33 in gross revenue; $18,000 in advances meant no artist earned additional usage royalties. Kapwing shut Tess down after unresolved copyright theory blocked enterprise adoption, artist hostility constrained supply and promotion, and split focus hurt its core product. Commenters questioned whether timing was really the problem, citing weak output quality, limited selection, and reliance on a broadly trained Stable Diffusion base.

### Comment pulse

- Ethical supply did not create demand → buyers still prioritized output quality, selection, and workflow ergonomics.
- Artists feared dilution and backlash → even compensation could not overcome lost control over brands using their styles.
- Licensing style remains contested → counterpoint: creators deserve payment, but expanding style rights could chill derivative art.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Consent is necessary for trust, but insufficient for a viable two-sided generative marketplace.
- **Impact:** Creator-licensing startups need stronger controls, competitive outputs, and concentrated distribution before royalty mechanics matter.
- **Watch next:** Copyright rulings, training-data licensing models, enterprise indemnity, and creator-set usage restrictions.
