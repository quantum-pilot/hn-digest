# The Gervais Principle, or the Office According to “The Office” (2009)

- Score: 284 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47286657) | Link: https://www.ribbonfarm.com/2009/10/07/the-gervais-principle-or-the-office-according-to-the-office/

### TL;DR

Venkatesh Rao reads The Office as management theory built around three roles: power-seeking “Sociopaths,” organization-believing “Clueless,” and paycheck-for-autonomy “Losers.” His Gervais Principle says leaders promote overperforming workers into exploitable middle management, fast-track ambitious underperformers toward senior power, and leave self-aware minimum performers below. Michael, Ryan, and Stanley illustrate those paths; a firm’s life cycle leaves believers holding its hollow shell. HN readers find the model recognizable and useful for explaining why KPIs often assign blame rather than reveal power, while warning that its loaded archetypes and later extensions overreach.

### Comment pulse

- Organizational success follows informal value and sponsorship, not KPI compliance → metrics often rationalize blame after decisions.
- “Loser” is economic, not social → disengaged workers may fare best because their identity and security extend beyond the firm.
- The taxonomy is evocative, not predictive science — counterpoint: readers repeatedly map its information asymmetries onto consulting and management failures.
