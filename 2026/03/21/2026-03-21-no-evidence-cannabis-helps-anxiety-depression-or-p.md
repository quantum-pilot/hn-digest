# No evidence cannabis helps anxiety, depression, or PTSD

- Score: 135 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47470750) | Link: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/03/260319044656.htm

### TL;DR

A Lancet Psychiatry systematic review and meta-analysis of 54 randomized trials conducted from 1980–2025 found no significant benefit from medicinal cannabinoids for anxiety, PTSD, or several other disorders, and no randomized-trial evidence for depression. Limited signals appeared for insomnia, autism, tics, and cannabis dependence, but evidence quality was low; cocaine craving worsened. Researchers warn routine use may delay proven care or increase psychosis and dependency risks. Commenters distinguished temporary symptom relief from durable treatment, while disputing whether the headline unfairly collapses those concepts.

### Comment pulse

- Personal reports describe early mood relief fading into dependence and worse discontinuation, echoing past overconfidence around prescription opioids.
- Some users value reduced suffering from pain or better sleep — counterpoint: subjective benefit does not establish efficacy for the diagnosed disorder.
- THC may intensify anxiety or sensory overload, while cannabinoid composition and dose complicate broad conclusions.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The clinical question is whether benefits exceed harms over time, not whether intoxication changes feelings immediately.
- **Impact:** Prescribers and regulators need condition-specific evidence instead of treating medicinal cannabis as one intervention.
- **Watch next:** Larger blinded trials separating THC, CBD, formulation, dose, duration, withdrawal, and functional outcomes.
