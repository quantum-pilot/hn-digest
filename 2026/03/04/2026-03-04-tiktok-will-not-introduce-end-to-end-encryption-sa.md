# TikTok will not introduce end-to-end encryption, saying it makes users less safe

- Score: 399 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47241817) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cly2m5e5ke4o

### TL;DR

TikTok rejects end-to-end encryption for direct messages, saying safety staff must examine reported harm and the company must answer valid police requests, especially involving young users. Messages remain encrypted in transit and storage, but designated employees can access them. Child-protection groups welcomed the decision; privacy experts warned that E2EE is the strongest defense against hackers, companies, and repressive authorities. Commenters split between accepting TikTok as a nonprivate service and arguing that a billion-user network offers no meaningful alternative—and that private messaging should use E2EE or be absent.

### Comment pulse

- Some accepted a plainly disclosed nonprivate service and advised choosing another messenger for sensitive conversations.
- Network effects undermine that choice: friends already congregate on the platform, especially younger users with less autonomy.
- Child-safety access may enable investigations; counterpoint: the same capability creates corporate, governmental, and hostile-state surveillance risk.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The decision makes platform moderation capability and message confidentiality mutually exclusive under TikTok’s chosen architecture.
- **Impact:** Safety teams retain investigative access, while every authorized-access path becomes a security, governance, and trust liability.
- **Watch next:** Employee-access controls, disclosure reporting, legal demands, youth defaults, breaches, and whether users migrate private conversations elsewhere.
