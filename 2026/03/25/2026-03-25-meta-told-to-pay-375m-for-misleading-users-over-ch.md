# Meta told to pay $375M for misleading users over child safety

- Score: 420 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47514916) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cql75dn07n2o

### TL;DR

A New Mexico jury ordered Meta to pay $375 million under the state’s Unfair Practices Act for misleading the public about child safety on Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Seven weeks of evidence included internal documents, former employees, tests showing minors received sexualized material, and research finding 16% of Instagram users reported unwanted nudity or sexual activity in one week. Jurors counted thousands of violations at up to $5,000 each. Meta says it protects teens, cites newer controls, disputes the verdict, and will appeal.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The case turns corporate safety assurances into testable consumer claims rather than treating harm solely as user content.
- **Impact:** State consumer-protection law may offer plaintiffs a route around traditional platform-liability defenses.
- **Watch next:** Appeal arguments, similar verdicts, penalty durability, recommendation-system evidence, and measurable effects of Teen Accounts.
