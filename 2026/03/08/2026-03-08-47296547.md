# Ask HN: How to be alone?

- Score: 218 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47296547) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47296547

### TL;DR

After two decades in a relationship, a 38-year-old remote worker is confronting solo life for the first time. Pets and online chat soften the isolation, but weekends, unshared daily moments, and previously enjoyable games now feel hollow despite psychiatric care. Respondents rejected quick psychological tricks in favor of repeated, scheduled contact: volunteer, join a service or faith group, attend the same class or gym, linger afterward, and let familiarity accumulate. Several emphasized self-compassion and time, warning that making recovery a perfection checklist can deepen shame.

### Comment pulse

- Recurring contact mattered more than novelty: the same class, gym, volunteer shift, or congregation can turn familiar faces into friends.
- Some advocated becoming one’s own friend; others warned that relentless self-improvement can reinforce the belief that worth must be earned.
- Several said grief fades as new memories accumulate, while moving closer to family provided one commenter immediate relief.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Separation removes a shared calendar as well as a partner; predictable obligations can rebuild structure before motivation returns.
- **Impact:** Remote work makes intentional third places more important because casual workplace contact cannot anchor evenings and weekends.
- **Watch next:** Whether sustained routines restore enjoyment, deepen local relationships, or reveal a need for different clinical support.
