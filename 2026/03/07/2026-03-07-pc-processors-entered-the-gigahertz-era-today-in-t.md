# PC processors entered the Gigahertz era today in the year 2000 with AMD's Athlon

- Score: 154 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47287792) | Link: https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/pc-processors-entered-the-gigahertz-era-today-in-the-year-2000-with-amds-athlon-amd-hit-marketing-gold-with-its-1-ghz-athlon-beat-intel-by-a-nose

### TL;DR

On March 7, 2000, AMD began shipping a 1 GHz Athlon, beating Intel’s comparable Pentium III announcement by two days and gaining months while Intel struggled with supply. The Slot A chip used a 180 nm process, packed 22 million transistors, drew 65 watts, and launched at $1,299. More than a specification milestone, it symbolized an era when clock jumps produced conspicuous everyday gains. Commenters remembered those upgrades vividly, while noting that SSDs, many-core systems, and parallel workloads later replaced frequency as the main source of dramatic speedups.

### Comment pulse

- AMD’s lead embarrassed Intel and, some argued, forced engineering discipline that faded whenever marketing regained control.
- Builders recalled moving from 233 MHz systems as transformative; later software bloat often consumed hardware gains.
- Athlon XP’s affordability mattered more to some than the headline clock — counterpoint: winning the race gave AMD lasting credibility.
