# US banks' exposure to private credit hits $300B (2025)

- Score: 212 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47349798) | Link: https://alternativecreditinvestor.com/2025/10/22/us-banks-exposure-to-private-credit-hits-300bn/

### TL;DR

U.S. banks had lent nearly $300 billion to private-credit providers by June 2025, within $1.2 trillion of lending to non-depository financial institutions. That broader category reached 10.4% of bank loans, versus 3.6% a decade earlier, as banks both compete with and finance nonbank lenders. Wells Fargo led at $59.7 billion, followed by Bank of America, PNC, Citi, and JPMorgan. Moody’s says partnerships diversify income but could expose asset-quality problems. Commenters clarify that banks usually fund lenders, not private-equity acquisitions directly, and worry about guaranteed banks socializing losses.

### Comment pulse

- Risk sits behind multiple capital layers → secured lenders recover before unsecured creditors and equity, so hard losses imply broader borrower distress.
- Concentration matters → commenters compare Wells Fargo’s exposure with its core capital and question whether private-credit stress could become systemic.
- “Extend and pretend” may delay recognition → counterpoint: restructurings can preserve viable firms, but opaque marks make losses harder to assess.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The headline number needs borrower quality, collateral, seniority, and capital context before it measures bank fragility.
- **Impact:** Bank supervisors, shareholders, and borrowers share exposure as nonbank lending becomes intertwined with regulated balance sheets.
- **Watch next:** Charge-offs, line restrictions, NDFI classifications, stress tests, and public disclosure by top lenders.
