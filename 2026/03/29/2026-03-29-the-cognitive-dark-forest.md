# The Cognitive Dark Forest

- Score: 190 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47566442) | Link: https://ryelang.org/blog/posts/cognitive-dark-forest/

### TL;DR

The essay proposes a “cognitive dark forest”: as AI lowers implementation costs and centralized platforms observe aggregate prompt demand, openly sharing ideas may let capital-rich incumbents detect, copy, and normalize innovation faster. Rational builders would then retreat to private communities, weakening the public knowledge commons that trained AI systems. The author labels this a future thought experiment, not a demonstrated account, and concedes current models do not learn continuously. Critics argue execution, distribution, customers, integration, and nontechnical moats remain difficult; AI may equally accelerate originators and enable more ambitious projects.

### Comment pulse

- One camp plans private repositories, printed exchanges, and LAN gatherings — counterpoint: others call withdrawal self-defeating because human sharing retains value.
- Builders noted AI shrinks simple projects while also expanding achievable complexity, potentially preserving replication time for multidisciplinary products.
- A publisher losing search traffic offered a concrete harm; others said secrecy in frontier research predates LLMs and carries adoption costs.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The strongest thesis concerns openness incentives; platform clairvoyance and instant absorption remain speculative.
- **Impact:** Even perceived appropriation could reduce public documentation, independent research, and open-source maintenance before copying becomes frictionless.
- **Watch next:** Prompt-retention policies, training refresh cadence, traffic displacement, provenance compensation, and evidence of incumbents exploiting aggregate demand signals.
