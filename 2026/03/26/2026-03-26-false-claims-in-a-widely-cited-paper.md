# False claims in a widely-cited paper

- Score: 329 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47525378) | Link: https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2026/03/24/false-claims-in-a-published-no-corrections-no-consequences-welcome-to-the-business-school/

### TL;DR

A 2014 Management Science paper claiming high-sustainability firms outperform peers has more than 6,000 citations and influenced policy and investment. Andy King says its authors acknowledged a misreport but will not correct it; the journal permits only authors to request corrections, though it allowed King to submit a reviewed comment after deeming them nonresponsive. Institutions offered no effective remedy. The supplied post does not explain the underlying analytical error, limiting independent assessment. HN sees institutional failure, while others argue journals should publish rebuttals rather than adjudicate every dispute.

### Comment pulse

- Author-only corrections create a veto → researchers least willing to admit error can block labels — counterpoint: volunteer editors cannot adjudicate unlimited complaints.
- Independent comments preserve scholarly disagreement → they can be peer-reviewed without the original authors’ consent or forced retraction.
- Citation-driven incentives amplify attractive findings → policy users may repeat a paper without following later rebuttals or field consensus.
