# Kagi Small Web

- Score: 683 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47410542) | Link: https://kagi.com/smallweb/

### TL;DR

Kagi’s discovery tool presents recent posts from a submitted, open-source index of roughly 5,000 human-oriented sites. Visitors can jump randomly or by topic, find similar posts, search websites, switch among liked, video, code, and comic modes, and subscribe through RSS or OPML; freshness is limited to about seven days. HN liked the StumbleUpon-style serendipity but challenged the branding: requiring feeds and recent posts favors blogs, frequent publishing, technology, and AI content while excluding static, rarely updated, single-purpose, fandom, and experimental sites many consider the small web.

### Comment pulse

- Fresh RSS keeps discovery active → it also excludes durable sites and rewards posting frequency over occasional high-quality work.
- Topic diversity looked weak → one reader saw more than 90% LLM or coding posts across 100 random clicks.
- Serendipity still appeals → users compared the flow favorably with StumbleUpon and shared manually curated blog indexes and similar explorers.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The product is better described as a recent independent-publishing ring than a map of the noncommercial web.
- **Impact:** Bloggers gain discovery and feeds; static-site creators remain invisible unless Kagi broadens inclusion and ranking signals.
- **Watch next:** Static-site indexing, recency controls, topic balancing, human-authorship verification, submission curation, and feedback-driven recommendations.
