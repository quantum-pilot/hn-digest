# Ask HN: What is it like being in a CS major program these days?

- Score: 159 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47397190) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47397190

### TL;DR

Students and instructors describe a CS degree whose theoretical core remains valuable but whose project courses, assessments, and career story are being rewritten by AI. Professors struggle to define complex work when agents can build systems quickly, while redesigning exams around understanding or planting hidden prompts to detect generated submissions. Campus Big Tech recruiting and internships feel scarce, intensifying fears that junior roles no longer train seniors. Yet one student still finds the science fulfilling. HN debated whether curricula should restrict tools, require proofs, or emphasize architecture and systems thinking.

### Comment pulse

- Job pipeline looks broken → students see fewer internships and Big Tech recruiters — counterpoint: claims that quant firms absorb most talent were challenged.
- Assessment is unstable → AI enables absentee A grades and homogenized portfolios, while capabilities outpace capstone redesign.
- Fundamentals retain intrinsic value → one student enjoys architecture and theory despite weak prospects; others propose controlled labs, proofs, and continuously expanding projects.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Universities must distinguish computer science education from short-term developer tooling while still teaching responsible agent use.
- **Impact:** Students face higher debt risk and fewer apprenticeship routes; faculty must redesign assessment without freezing industry practice.
- **Watch next:** Graduate hiring, internship counts, exam changes, capstone rubrics, AI-use policies, and evidence on long-term skill formation.
