# The “small web” is bigger than you might think

- Score: 286 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47401879) | Link: https://kevinboone.me/small_web_is_big.html

### TL;DR

Kevin Boone tested whether the noncommercial “small web” could share a Gemini-style page of every new post. Kagi’s feed list had grown from roughly 6,000 sites to 32,000; after discarding unreachable, invalid, or timestamp-free feeds, 25,000 remained, and 9,000 still published at least monthly. Those sites produced 1,251 substantive updates on March 15 alone—too many for one useful daily page. HN celebrated the activity but argued the sample is incomplete and biased against self-submitted, non-English, dormant, or deliberately infrequent personal sites.

### Comment pulse

- Random-page scripts, 88×31 badges, webrings, and blogrolls offer human-scale discovery without a universal chronological feed.
- Rare posting can signal care, so activity thresholds may exclude excellent archives and future high-value updates.
- Dropping TLS could reduce complexity — counterpoint: ISP injection, authenticity, revocation, and surveillance make plaintext unsafe.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Curation, not inventory, becomes the bottleneck once independent publishing exceeds any reader’s attention.
- **Impact:** Aggregators need personalized sampling while preserving visibility for low-frequency and obscure sites.
- **Watch next:** Language coverage, transparent inclusion, dead-link maintenance, feed-quality statistics, and decentralized recommendations.
