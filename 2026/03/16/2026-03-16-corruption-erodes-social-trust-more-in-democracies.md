# Corruption erodes social trust more in democracies than in autocracies

- Score: 628 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47397593) | Link: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2026.1779810/full

### TL;DR

Using World Values Survey responses from more than 85,000 people across 62 countries plus V‑Dem scores, researchers found perceived corruption predicts lower generalized trust almost everywhere, but the association steepens with democratic quality. They propose normative amplification, where corruption violates promised fairness, and representative contagion, where elected misconduct reflects on fellow citizens; autocratic elites instead seem separate and corruption expected. Robustness checks preserved the interaction, but the design remains associational. HN debated whether regime type explains the pattern better than culture, baseline trust, inequality, or corruption’s everyday function.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Democracy may create more trust and make that asset more sensitive to visible institutional betrayal.
- **Impact:** Anti-corruption policy must repair perceived fairness and social cohesion, not only prosecute officials or improve procurement.
- **Watch next:** Longitudinal and experimental replication, objective corruption measures, regime transitions, cultural moderators, and scandal-specific effects.
