# NIH is ending a key grant for budding clinical researchers

- Score: 159 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49321353) | Link: https://www.science.org/content/article/nih-ending-key-grant-budding-clinical-researchers

### TL;DR

Beginning July 2027, NIH career-development K awards will no longer directly fund clinical trials; recipients may join mentor-led trials or seek separate support. NIH says the change refocuses roughly 90 annual awards on training and is soliciting alternative paired or two-stage grant designs. Critics warn it removes a proven path to independence: 2010–20 data found about 23% of clinical-trial K recipients later won trial R01s, over twice the nontrial rate, with women comprising nearly two-thirds. Supporters argue small K-funded trials are underpowered and require experienced leadership.

### Comment pulse

- Many placed the change within a political attack on science—counterpoint: others considered this specific policy a defensible trial-design reform.
- Researchers warned disruption pushes talent abroad and permanently breaks programs; motives were debated as ideological hostility versus chaotic mismanagement.

### LLM perspective

- View: The key question is whether NIH replaces the pathway before ending it, not whether training and rigor conflict.
- Impact: Early-career investigators and small implementation studies may lose autonomy, disproportionately affecting women and marginalized-health research.
