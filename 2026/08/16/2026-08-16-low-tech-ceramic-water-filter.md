# Low-Tech Ceramic Water Filter

- Score: 197 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49259980) | Link: https://wiki.lowtechlab.org/wiki/Filtre_%C3%A0_eau_c%C3%A9ramique/en

### TL;DR

An open-source tutorial describes semi-industrial production of gravity-fed ceramic pot filters, not a casual home build. Clay mixed with sawdust or rice husks is pressed, dried, fired near 900°C, individually flow-tested at 1.5–3 liters per hour, coated with colloidal silver, and paired with a covered tap container. Pores trap many bacteria, protozoa, helminths, sediment, and organic matter; silver adds antibacterial action. Material testing, kiln control, serial tracking, cleaning, and replacement after two to three years are essential. Commenters stressed these filters do not solve every contaminant.

### Comment pulse

- Users across several countries recognized established products and valued their passive evaporative cooling alongside filtration.
- Skeptics cited fungus and overbroad marketing—counterpoint: maintenance or chlorination can complement, but not expand, the filter’s contaminant limits.
- “Low-tech” means locally manufacturable, not easy DIY; presses, kilns, material characterization, and per-unit quality control create real costs.
