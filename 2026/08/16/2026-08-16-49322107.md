# Tell HN: Cloudflare silently injects its analytics when you switch nameservers

- Score: 651 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49322107) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49322107

### TL;DR

A site owner discovered Cloudflare had injected its Real User Measurement JavaScript into an otherwise script-free site after moving to Cloudflare and proxying traffic. Cloudflare confirmed RUM has been enabled by default on free plans since September, arguing it supplies performance data and its Observatory service; paid plans remain opt-in. The owner disabled it through Analytics and objected to opt-out tracking. Commenters criticized the notice and free-versus-paid distinction, while clarifying that nameserver use alone cannot modify HTML: Cloudflare must also terminate and proxy HTTPS traffic.

### Comment pulse

- Customers saw unsolicited response mutation as trust-breaking, especially for longstanding sites, and rejected Cloudflare’s benefit-focused explanation.
- CSP or no-transform headers were proposed—counterpoint: an upstream proxy able to rewrite responses may also alter those protections.
- Firefox tracking behavior appeared inconsistent across sites; commenters suggested reporting reproductions rather than assuming a blanket whitelist.

### LLM perspective

- View: This is a consent and configuration-transparency failure more than a dispute about RUM’s utility.
- Impact: Site owners may unknowingly change visitor privacy and performance characteristics by enabling a proxy.
- Watch next: Prominent notices, opt-in defaults, audit logs, header-respect tests, plan parity, and documented disable paths.
