# A third world engineer responds to “RISC-V: They should have known better”

- Score: 621 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49321717) | Link: https://rvembedded.com/blog_post/12/

### TL;DR

Writing from Trinidad and Tobago, the author accepts RISC-V’s awkward encodings and extension fragmentation but argues the earlier critique undervalued access. For under $100, he explored ten-cent CH32V003 microcontrollers, a faster CH32H417 board, open Baochip silicon, and a Linux SBC using one base ISA, toolchain, and transferable skills. Optional extensions, he says, let vendors span tiny cores through MMU-equipped systems without ARM’s licensing boundaries. Commenters praised openness and distribution but said this answers embedded-to-low-end scaling, not the original concern about laptop/server performance or portable binaries.

### Comment pulse

- Critics questioned shipping logic: architecture does not set postage—counterpoint: Chinese channels offered free shipping and fewer export or identity barriers.
- A unified toolchain benefits chipmakers and learners despite recompilation, although commenters cited some equally cheap ARM parts.
- Historical analogies suggest volume can fund high-end progress; skeptics said volume alone cannot overcome architectural disadvantages.
