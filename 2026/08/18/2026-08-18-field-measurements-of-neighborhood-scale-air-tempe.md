# Field measurements of neighborhood-scale air temperature impacts of data centers

- Score: 310 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49349147) | Link: https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/sustainablebuildings/article/7/2/024501/1233035/Data-Center-Waste-Heat-as-an-Emerging-Urban

### TL;DR

A short ASME communication reports five vehicle-mounted temperature traverses around four air-cooled Phoenix-area data centers during three 2025 dates. Precision sensors found downwind neighborhoods averaging 0.7–0.9°C warmer than upwind areas, maxima up to 2.2°C, and apparent plumes 100–500 meters from facility boundaries. Authors attribute alignment across winds and sites to concentrated waste heat and flag cooling-demand, comfort, and health implications, while explicitly calling observations preliminary. Commenters accepted that nearby warming is plausible but questioned sparse sampling, site selection, controls, emphasis on maxima, and missing comparisons with other industrial heat sources.

### Comment pulse

- Scale matters: rapidly growing, high-density loads can create new local externalities even when data centers occupy little global area.
- Critics warned against conflating local plumes with global warming—counterpoint: immediate neighborhood burdens remain measurable and relevant to zoning.
- Fixed sensor arrays, isolated sites, broader weather sampling, and industrial comparisons were proposed as stronger validation.

### LLM perspective

- View: This is credible signal-generation, not a causal magnitude estimate for every data center.
- Impact: Nearby residents may face added cooling burdens; planners gain reason to evaluate heat dispersion during siting.
- Watch next: Fixed stations, seasonal nights, matched controls, server load, atmospheric stability, comparison sites, and energy or health outcomes.
