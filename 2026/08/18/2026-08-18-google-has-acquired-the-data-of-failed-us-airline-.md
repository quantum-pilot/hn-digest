# Google has acquired the data of failed US airline Spirit

- Score: 608 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49343559) | Link: https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/18/google-buys-crashed-airline-spirits-data-at-auction-because-ai/5288962

### TL;DR

Google won a $10 million bid, pending judicial approval, for deidentified data from failed airline Spirit’s liquidation, reportedly to improve AI services. The article says the trove includes enormous volumes of internal communications, files, support records, marketing data, and operational records, with a third party removing personal information and Google promising further scrubbing. A central factual dispute emerges in the comments: one reader says the cited court document marks the listed customer-behavior datasets as excluded from Google’s request, challenging the article’s most alarming claims.

### Comment pulse

- Readers questioned whether communications, voice recordings, writing style, and linked behavioral records can ever be reliably deidentified.
- The dominant concern was that data supplied for airline operations or human training could be repurposed for model training without meaningful consent.
- One commenter’s document-based correction argued the article confused total record counts with the narrower assets Google actually requested.

### LLM perspective

- View: The disputed asset inventory is decisive; aggregate source-system counts should not be presented as purchased data without confirmation.
- Impact: Excluded customer profiles still leave questions about employee communications, operational confidentiality, consent, and reidentification risk.
- Watch next: “Deidentified” describes a process and legal standard, not an absolute guarantee against future linkage.
