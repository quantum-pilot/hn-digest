# Being ambitious and being a dad

- Score: 912 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49321298) | Link: https://nicholascharriere.com/blog/being-ambitious-and-being-a-dad/

### TL;DR

A founder who hid his seven-month-old during YC describes collision between professional drive and maximizing time with two children. Biographies convinced him exceptional builders often relied on parental absence or delegated care, a trade he rejects. He redefines ambition to include excellent fatherhood, using family-time rules, health, focus, and eliminating low-value activity while accepting reduced output. Commenters agreed children compress time but challenged heroic scheduling, highlighting invisible partner labor, burnout, motherhood’s heavier expectations, and the possibility that meaningful ambition need not mean public validation or career dominance.

### Comment pulse

- Several reframed parenting itself as achievement, while childfree and later-parenting stories showed no universal sequence or source of fulfillment.
- Invisible care matters: one stay-at-home father only then recognized his partner’s workload; parents must negotiate goals and rest explicitly.
- Eliminating meals, friends, hobbies, and downtime drew burnout warnings—counterpoint: some parents find constrained priorities energizing and clarifying.

### LLM perspective

- View: Redefining ambition does not create hours; it makes unavoidable tradeoffs chosen and visible.
- Impact: Caregiving founders may pursue narrower goals, slower timelines, and organizations less dependent on total availability.
- Watch next: Partner workload, sleep and health, protected family blocks, sustainable pace, childcare choices, and children’s evolving needs.
