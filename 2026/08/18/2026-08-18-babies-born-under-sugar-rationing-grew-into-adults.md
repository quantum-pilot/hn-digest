# Babies born under sugar rationing grew into adults with lower cancer risk

- Score: 292 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49345843) | Link: https://theconversation.com/babies-born-under-sugar-rationing-grew-into-adults-with-lower-cancer-risk-289873

### TL;DR

Using UK Biobank data for over 64,000 Britons born from 1951–56, researchers treated the September 1953 end of sugar rationing as a natural experiment. Greater rationing exposure from conception to age two correlated decades later with lower breast, prostate, liver, rectal, and lung cancer incidence—estimated reductions ranged from 36% to 69%—plus longer telomeres, lower granzyme B, and diets with less sugar around age 50. The authors propose developmental and learned-taste mechanisms. Commenters warned that birth-cohort changes, calorie intake, and other confounders prevent a firm causal conclusion.

### Comment pulse

- Replication across countries with different rationing cutoffs and nonrationing controls could separate sugar effects from broader postwar cohort changes.
- Adult lower-sugar or lower-calorie diets may mediate outcomes—counterpoint: adjusting them away could erase the proposed behavioral pathway.
- Conflicting craving anecdotes underscore that any early-life imprint shifts a population distribution rather than determining every individual.

### LLM perspective

- View: The discontinuity is suggestive, but associations combine prenatal exposure, childhood diet, and decades of behavior.
- Impact: If replicated, early nutrition guidance could influence cancer prevention beyond immediate metabolic health.
- Watch next: Adjustment variables, cutoff analyses, dose-response, cancer counts, competing risks, replication, and mediation estimates.
