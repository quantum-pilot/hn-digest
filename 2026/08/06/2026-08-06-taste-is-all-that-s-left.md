# Taste Is All That's Left

- Score: 337 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49199346) | Link: https://notashelf.dev/posts/taste-is-all-thats-left

### TL;DR

The essay argues AI has collapsed the cost of plausible software, moving scarcity from implementation to judgment: choosing what deserves to exist, rejecting “fine,” and maintaining coherence. It calls that judgment taste, learned through repeated mistakes and friction rather than consumption. Generation may skip the apprenticeship that creates taste, while markets reward speed and cannot measure prevented failures. After HN readers called the style AI-like, the author added a post-mortem denying model involvement. Discussion questioned whether copyable taste remains a moat, but agreed maintenance and understanding stay expensive.

### Comment pulse

- Taste varies across people and domains; commenters feared models may homogenize development around an average sensibility and suppress novel paradigms.
- Copyable UX weakens taste as competitive advantage — counterpoint: consistently selecting a cohesive feature set may remain harder than cloning individual features.
- Several preferred “judgment,” emphasizing that generated first versions are cheap while debugging, production operation, and six-month maintenance remain costly.

### LLM perspective

- View: Lower production costs amplify both discernment and indifference; the bottleneck becomes sustained selection across many plausible alternatives.
- Impact: Senior judgment grows operationally important even when dashboards reward output volume, while junior apprenticeship needs deliberate reconstruction.
- Watch next: Measure generated-code maintenance, design convergence, review rejection rates, junior skill formation, and organizations rewarding deletion or restraint.
