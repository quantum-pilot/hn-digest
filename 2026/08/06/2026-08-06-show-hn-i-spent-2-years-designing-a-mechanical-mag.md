# Show HN: I spent 2 years designing a mechanical Magic Keyboard

- Score: 267 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49197137) | Link: https://electronicmaterialsoffice.com/

### TL;DR

Altar II is a $349 75% keyboard claiming a mechanical switch mechanism inside a 4.75mm chassis, with 1.8mm travel and 40gf actuation. Its lateral tension spring, aluminum body, dual dampers, amber-red backlight, haptic system, speaker, detachable programmable dial, onboard settings, ZMK firmware, and native macOS app aim to combine laptop thinness with tactile feedback. The maker advertises 30-day battery life and Windows support, with $100 off for reservations. HN admired the engineering and industrial design but criticized oversized number legends, missing shifted symbols, the protruding dial, and absent Touch ID.

### Comment pulse

- The creator omitted a fingerprint sensor because third-party hardware cannot support Apple Pay, though commenters proposed smart-card or U2F alternatives.
- Several readers loved the overall form but considered the huge numerals and reduced symbol visibility purchase-blocking stylistic quirks.
- Short pre-travel attracted laptop-keyboard users; the 1.8mm total stroke is about 0.8mm longer than a MacBook keyboard.

### LLM perspective

- View: The product’s differentiation comes from mechanical miniaturization and multisensory interaction, but typography can overpower years of engineering.
- Impact: Thin-keyboard users gain a tactile premium option; buyers tied to Touch ID or conventional legends remain excluded.
- Watch next: Validate switch longevity, battery claims, latency, haptic usefulness, companion-app stability, Windows keycaps, final legends, manufacturing yield, and shipping.
