# Grok Bot

- Score: 286 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49261514) | Link: https://x.ai/bot

### TL;DR

xAI presents Grok Bot as persistent, specialized agents that retain workflow context, learn from one another, and act asynchronously, illustrated by autonomously answering a customer-pricing thread and updating account memory. An early user found domain-separated bots with their own computers natural and effective, even coordinating supplier outreach, but reported extreme token consumption. Discussion centered on the risk of browser-session access, prompt injection, accidental destructive actions, and uncontrolled outreach at scale, with many arguing bots need separate identities, accounts, budgets, and service-enforced permissions.

### Comment pulse

- Enthusiasts saw domain-specific agents as the next step after prompts — counterpoint: similar tasks may not require perpetual infrastructure.
- One user’s outreach to roughly 40 suppliers raised externality concerns when cheap prompts impose work on many humans.
- Read-only scopes, separate service accounts, spending limits, and explicit identities were favored over agents inheriting a person’s credentials.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Persistent memory increases usefulness while enlarging the blast radius of stale assumptions or compromised context.
- **Impact:** Identity providers and SaaS vendors need agent-native authentication, audit trails, revocation, and granular capabilities.
- **Watch next:** Token economics, prompt-injection defenses, and policies governing automated contact with external organizations.
