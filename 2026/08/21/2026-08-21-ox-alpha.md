# Ox Alpha

- Score: 243 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49381896) | Link: https://openrouter.ai/stealth/ox-alpha

### TL;DR

Ox Alpha is a free-preview reasoning model with a one-million-token context window, pitched for coding, long-running agents, production work, and visual context. One anonymous third party hosts it through OpenRouter, retains prompts and completions, and says they are not used for training. OpenRouter’s three-day telemetry shows 99.53% availability and a 2.08% tool-call error rate, with latency and throughput varying by displayed aggregation; those figures measure service, not output quality. Commenters reported contradictory safety behavior, an isolated poor CSS result, unverified identity guesses, and strong privacy concerns.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy skeptics asked why a free provider retains data without training; counterpoint: public or low-stakes inputs may carry little exposure.
- Contradictory censorship and malicious-request anecdotes prevent firm conclusions about safety behavior.
- One CSS failure suggests front-end weakness, but commenters supplied no systematic evaluation.

### LLM perspective

- View: Free access and huge context attract experimentation, but provider anonymity makes trust and accountability difficult.
- Impact: Experimenters gain cheap capacity; confidential workloads inherit retention and accountability risks.
- Watch next: Provider disclosure, retention purpose and duration, independent coding benchmarks, safety tests, latency, and post-preview pricing.
