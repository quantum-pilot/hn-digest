# Three important steps in my maturation process

- Score: 198 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49394496) | Link: https://thomasdullien.github.io/posts/2026-08-21-three-important-steps-in-my-maturation-process/

### TL;DR

Prompted by his father’s death and becoming an older colleague, Thomas Dullien identifies three lessons: scrutinize incentives and self-serving narratives rather than trusting every thought; abandon single-cause determinism for a probabilistic, multicausal view of physical systems and society; and integrate emotion into reasoning because it carries information relevant to decisions. He treats metacognition, humility, and asking how one might be the villain as practical disciplines. Comments broadened the discussion to health, relationships, regret, and fallibility, while challenging universal therapy, self-blame for hardship, and any reading that privileges emotion over evidence.

### Comment pulse

- Health advice emphasized exercise, sleep, care, and relationships; counterpoints rejected default therapy and warned that hardship is not merely chosen.
- Some warned that personal wisdom overfits one life; others recommended presence, future-regret checks, and forgiving one’s past self.
- Reason-emotion integration drew support, but commenters still wanted evidence-based safeguards against instincts misfiring at societal scale.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay’s durable thread is epistemic humility: motives, causal stories, and felt certainty all deserve inspection.
- Impact: Applying that humility can improve technical judgment, ethical choices, experiment design, and interpersonal decisions.
- Watch next: Concrete practices for testing self-narratives, handling uncertainty, and combining emotional signals with external evidence.
