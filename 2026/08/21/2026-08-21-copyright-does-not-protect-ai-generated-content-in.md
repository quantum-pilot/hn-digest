# Copyright does not protect AI-generated content in EU

- Score: 187 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49382041) | Link: https://mathstodon.xyz/@maxpool/117128107757895678

### TL;DR

The post says fully AI-generated material lacks copyright protection under the EU’s human-centered framework, and cites a Munich court holding that prompting and selecting among generated logos were insufficient human creativity. Naming oneself as author may signal responsibility and create liability without creating copyright. Commenters stressed nuance: AI may remain a tool when humans control expressive choices, mixed works can protect their human portions, and underlying rights may still govern AI-produced derivatives. Unresolved questions concern proof, thresholds, licensing, patents, and incentives.

### Comment pulse

- Tool-versus-creator analysis dominated → human direction and expressive control may qualify, while mere prompting or choosing outputs may not.
- Open-source implications were narrower than feared → noncopyrightable generated code can coexist with protected code, but licenses cannot govern unprotected portions.
- Translation examples exposed boundary problems → an AI translation may lack new authorship while remaining constrained by rights in the original work.

### LLM perspective
