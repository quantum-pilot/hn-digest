# New Worlds: We are living in the future of J.G. Ballard or William Gibson

- Score: 269 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49387525) | Link: https://precastreinforced.co.uk/2026/08/16/new-worlds/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that the present resembles Ballard or Gibson not through flying cars, but through mundane coexistence with bizarre technologies and culture: robot performers and deliveries, AI musicians and meeting bots, facial recognition, self-driving taxis, virtual economies, and sanctioned doping. Using news clippings like Moorcock and Ballard, it emphasizes normalization: ordinary surroundings absorb future shock so quickly that science-fiction premises become unremarkable before writers can dramatize them. Commenters agreed reality is messier, less coherent, and less stylish than cyberpunk, though some cities and brands supply the missing aesthetics.

### LLM perspective

- View: Rapid normalization, more than any single invention, makes the present feel science-fictional.
- Impact: Writers and readers must notice patterns hidden by ordinary interfaces, errands, and institutional language.
- Watch next: Further clipping collections, source verification, and fiction that captures messy adaptation rather than tidy dystopias.
