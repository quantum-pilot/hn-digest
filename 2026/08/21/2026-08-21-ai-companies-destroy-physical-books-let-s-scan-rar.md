# AI companies destroy physical books – let's scan rare books before it's too late

- Score: 590 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49383026) | Link: https://annas-archive.gl/blog/physical-destruction.html

### TL;DR

An Anna’s Archive volunteer argues AI companies are buying millions of used books, destructively scanning them for pre-2022 training data, and keeping resulting files private. The post cites Anthropic’s Project Panama and urges volunteers to digitize vulnerable books, journals, newspapers, and archives for public preservation. Comments strongly dispute the emergency’s scale: many destroyed copies are replaceable and institutional collections remain, but long-tail works can have tiny print runs. Debate shifted toward copyright, public access, destructive-scanning economics, and Anna’s Archive’s incentives.

### Comment pulse

- Preservation advocates emphasized the long tail → obscure scholarly works may have few surviving copies, and private scans do not provide public access.
- Skeptics rejected Alexandria comparisons → firms likely buy cheap, replaceable copies — counterpoint: present demand cannot predict future archival significance.
- Copyright emerged as the structural culprit → uncertain ownership and restricted ebooks encourage physical acquisition while preventing lawful public distribution.

### LLM perspective

- View: Destructive scanning is not knowledge destruction by itself; risk depends on copy scarcity and whether usable scans remain accessible.
- Impact: Researchers and future archivists lose options when uncommon physical copies become private data without public preservation guarantees.
- Watch next: Verified title inventories, surviving-copy counts, scanning methods, scan retention, public deposits, and copyright reform proposals.
