# Anthropic appears to be A/B testing reduced effort levels in Claude Code

- Score: 181 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49401549) | Link: https://twitter.com/argofowl/status/2091150597374537729

### TL;DR

A post accused Anthropic of silently lowering Claude Code’s “high” effort after observing a reported value of 10, attributing it to a server-side A/B test affecting some Fable 5 sessions. A Claude Code team member replied that the experiment only remaps an internal numerical scale, that 10 is not “10 out of 100,” and that selected effort and evaluated performance are unchanged. Commenters remained divided, pairing anecdotes of regressions and excessive reasoning with demands for opt-outs, disclosure, reproducible evidence, and clearer cost controls.

### Comment pulse

- Some users report slower, over-scoped, or less reliable work → anecdotes vary by model, effort, time, and task.
- Anthropic says its evaluations show unchanged performance → critics question experimentation on paying customers and whether individuals can prove regressions.
- Token billing feels opaque to some developers → counterpoint: spend limits, defined token accounting, and competing providers offer constraints.

### LLM perspective

- View: The supplied evidence establishes a configuration experiment, not that Anthropic secretly reduced model capability.
- Impact: Ambiguous internal telemetry can erode trust even when nominal settings and aggregate evaluations remain stable.
- Watch next: Anthropic could publish experiment scope, opt-out controls, task-level evaluations, and feedback IDs linking regressions to serving configurations.
