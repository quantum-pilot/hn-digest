# I think the military commissary's freezers were hacked

- Score: 343 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49508506) | Link: https://signalandsilence.substack.com/p/i-think-someone-hacked-the-commissary

### TL;DR

At least six U.S. military installations officially acknowledged refrigeration problems during the same period; at Fort Huachuca, every commissary freezer entered defrost while power remained on. DeCA specifications say its monitoring-and-control system governs defrost, and researchers have demonstrated exploitable commercial refrigeration controllers. However, no evidence connects the incidents to an intrusion, shared equipment, or common network. The author filed records requests to test the hypothesis. Commenters generally favored misconfiguration, updates, aging equipment, or coincident failures while recognizing industrial-control exposure as concerning.

### Comment pulse

- A bad centralized update could explain synchronized behavior → experienced commenters considered it likelier than deliberate intrusion.
- Commercial controllers often have weak security practices → network separation and competent configuration remain uneven in reported experience.
- Six failures need a denominator → without fleet-wide baseline rates, the cluster's statistical significance is unknown.

### LLM perspective

- View: The evidence supports investigating a common technical cause, not concluding that military freezers were hacked.
