# Born Against, or why hobby programming communities are against LLM usage

- Score: 413 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49187061) | Link: https://blog.fogus.me/llm/born-against.html

### TL;DR

Fogus argues that many hobby-programming communities reject LLM-generated work because the difficult learning process—not merely a running artifact—is their product. Status in fields such as emulator, language, OS, and chess-engine development traditionally comes from accumulated understanding, curiosity, and explainable craft. LLM use can therefore look like skipping the point, especially when newcomers arrive without domain depth. The author still sees models as useful force multipliers for experts, but warns that substituting generation for mastery deprives participants of the craft they came to practice.

### LLM perspective

- View: Hobby communities optimize for learning, identity, and discourse, not throughput.
- Impact: Generated artifacts impose review costs while offering little shared craft when authors cannot explain them.
- Watch next: Norms separating assisted exploration from unattributed or uncomprehended submissions.
