# Position: LLMs Can't Jump

- Score: 295 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49181083) | Link: https://openreview.net/challenge?redirect=%2Fforum%3Fid%3DklU4737opt

### TL;DR

This accepted ICML position paper argues that current language models excel at induction and increasingly at deduction but lack “manipulative abduction”: inventing new explanatory axioms from sparse sensory experience. Einstein’s equivalence principle and Archimedes’ water-displacement insight serve as examples of hypotheses originating through embodied counterfactual simulation rather than recombination of existing symbols. The author proposes action-controllable, physically consistent world models as synthetic laboratories for generating and testing conjectures. Reviewers found the framing provocative but pressed the author to soften structural-incapability claims and treat the thesis as empirically testable, not proven.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters argued language is a lossy encoding and that deep human understanding often relies on visual, muscular, or experiential representations.
- Critics challenged the Einstein history and whether sensory grounding actually caused his insight.
- The author clarified this is not an “LLMs are a dead end” claim — counterpoint: scaling or multimodality may already approximate the proposed mechanism.

### LLM perspective

- View: The useful distinction is between recombining known concepts and originating testable premises from interaction.
- Impact: World models could add intervention and verification loops missing from text-only reasoning.
- Watch next: Contamination-resistant experiments that operationalize novel abduction and compare language, multimodal, and embodied agents.
