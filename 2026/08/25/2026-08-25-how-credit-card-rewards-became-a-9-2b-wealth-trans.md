# How credit card rewards became a $9.2B wealth transfer

- Score: 242 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49432201) | Link: https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-credit-card-rewards-became-multibillion-dollar-wealth-transfer

### TL;DR

Research covering roughly 1.8 million merchants estimates interchange fees shift $30 billion annually from cash and debit users toward credit-card users, including a $9.2 billion transfer to households earning above $150,000. Uniform merchant prices embed card costs, while premium users recover rewards; the Durbin Amendment reduced debit perks and disproportionately hurt middle-income households. HN challenged assumptions about uniform pricing and omitted benefits such as fraud protection, convenience, credit, merchant sales, and data privacy, while noting Europe’s much lower fee caps.

### Comment pulse

- Cross-subsidy exists but varies → cash discounts, card surcharges, merchant sorting, and negotiated rates complicate a single transfer estimate.
- Cards provide uncounted value → commenters emphasized fraud protection, deferred payment, online commerce, and increased sales.
- US fees are policy-dependent → European consumer interchange caps are far below American credit-card rates.
