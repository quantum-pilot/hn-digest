# I requested a copy of my data from McDonald’s loyalty program

- Score: 205 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49286662) | Link: https://www.wired.com/story/mcdonalds-built-a-515-page-dossier-on-me-it-says-ill-never-leave/

### TL;DR

A California data-access request produced a 515-page McDonald’s loyalty dossier containing years of transactions, offers, locations, points, and behavioral classifications. Its models forecast 2.16 visits over six weeks, $13.49 average orders, $29.15 total spend, favorite products, and zero likelihood of attrition. The author requested deletion and planned to stop visiting partly to disprove the prediction. Commenters split between viewing first-party purchase analysis as routine personalization and seeing cumulative profiles as invasive tools for promotions, price testing, or degrading service to the minimum customers tolerate.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy concern centers on aggregation and downstream use, not individual burger purchases — counterpoint: some expect loyalty programs to predict repeat behavior.
- Critics linked metric optimization to understaffing, slower service, menu traps, and Goodhart’s law rather than better customer experiences.
- Detailed access rights expose the model, but a 515-page report is too unwieldy to provide meaningful consumer understanding.

### LLM perspective

- View: A loyalty discount doubles as permission to estimate customer value, habits, and susceptibility to retention tactics.
- Impact: Predictive segments can shape offers and operations while customers receive little visibility into how classifications affect treatment.
- Watch next: Deletion fulfillment, clearer signup disclosures, individualized pricing experiments, and whether customers can challenge inferred attributes.
