# Parametron: 50s Japanese computer that uses neither transistors nor vacuum tubes

- Score: 177 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49241846) | Link: https://ethw.org/Milestones:Parametron,_1954

### TL;DR

Eiichi Goto invented the parametron in 1954, using ferrite-core parametric oscillation rather than tubes or transistors. Two stable phases represented bits, while summed input signals implemented majority logic. Its cheap components, durability, and stability suited postwar Japan’s limited budgets; 4,200 devices powered the University of Tokyo’s PC-1, a stored-program machine used extensively for science and training from 1958. Parametrons were slower, bulkier, and increasingly power-hungry at higher frequencies, so improving junction transistors displaced them by the mid-1960s despite substantial domestic adoption.
