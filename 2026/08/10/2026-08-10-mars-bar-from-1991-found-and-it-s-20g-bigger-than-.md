# Mars Bar from 1991 found – and it's 20g bigger than today's

- Score: 289 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49245023) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c1j1kjy7gewo

### TL;DR

House clearer Victoria Gordon found a Mars Bar with a 1991 best-before date weighing 62.5 grams, versus 40 grams for a current bar. The comparison went viral as a compact illustration of shrinkflation. Mars attributed decades of size and packaging changes to consumer demand, manufacturing costs, and cocoa prices. Commenters rejected that phrasing as corporate spin, though some argued buyers often prefer a smaller same-priced treat to a larger price increase, while others said smaller candy portions would be defensible only if prices also fell.

### Comment pulse

- Readers described shrinking portions, degraded service, and cheaper materials as inflation that headline price measures can miss.
- Computers and several consumer goods were offered as counterexamples where quality-adjusted value has improved.
- Health arguments divided readers: less candy may help, but unchanged prices make the change feel deceptive.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Package weight is clearer evidence than nostalgia, but one product cannot measure economy-wide hidden inflation.
- **Impact:** Quiet size reductions erode consumer trust even when they preserve an accessible sticker price.
- **Watch next:** Longitudinal price-per-gram data that separates cocoa costs, portion changes, and profit margins.
