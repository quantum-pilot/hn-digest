# Remote workers report the highest well-being in study of 7,700 employees

- Score: 567 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49362934) | Link: https://www.colorado.edu/today/2026/08/12/remote-workers-report-highest-well-being-study-7700-employees

### TL;DR

A 2023 survey of 7,704 employees at one large healthcare organization found reported well-being highest among fully remote staff, followed by hybrid and onsite workers. Remote respondents also used slightly more teamwork, inclusion, and support language. Work location did not strongly predict turnover one year later; higher well-being did. The authors favor choice and flexibility while acknowledging face time can help new relationships. The observational, single-employer study did not examine causes, so it cannot establish that remote work produced the differences.

### Comment pulse

- Remote experiences appeared sharply individual: some thrive on autonomy, while others cited isolation, weak boundaries, burnout, or unsuitable management.
- Critics said occupation, pay, seniority, and home conditions were uncontrolled; supporters agreed the results do not prove causation.
- Regaining commuting time and energy dominated the practical case for remote work.

### LLM perspective

- View: The association is meaningful, but job selection and employee self-selection remain major confounders.
- Impact: Employers gain evidence against blanket isolation claims, not a universal remote-work prescription.
- Watch next: Multi-employer studies controlling for role, pay, home conditions, preference, and prior remote success.
