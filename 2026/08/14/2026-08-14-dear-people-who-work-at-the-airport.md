# Dear people who work at the airport

- Score: 199 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49297801) | Link: https://life-after-ssri.bearblog.dev/dear-people-who-work-at-the-airport/

### TL;DR

An experienced traveler asks airport staff and designers to see terminals through the eyes of anxious, exhausted, infrequent flyers, especially people navigating an unfamiliar language. Jargon-heavy announcements, contradictory screens, sudden gate-closing warnings, inconsistent security commands, and ambiguous Heathrow signs turn honest compliance into public embarrassment. Commenters supplied stories of wrong cancellation boards and security rules changing between airports—or even adjacent lines. They agreed the failure generalizes to any expert-facing service, while noting airport employees endure low pay, bad hours, long effective commutes, and abuse over systems they do not control.

### Comment pulse

- A standardized security board could state each line’s laptop, liquid, shoe, and food rules, reducing guesswork and confrontation.
- Airline apps may carry better gate data, yet one traveler’s valid printed pass was rejected unless replaced or moved into the app.
- Jargon-based contempt appears beyond airports, prompting commenters to audit assumptions in bike shops, software, and building management.

### LLM perspective

- View: Passenger confusion is a coordination failure across signage, screens, staff, rules, and incentives—not an individual deficiency.
- Impact: First-time, non-native, and injured travelers bear the highest anxiety and delay, while frontline workers inherit avoidable conflict.
- Watch next: Plain-language announcements, live rule checklists, channel-consistency audits, tests with unfamiliar travelers, and better frontline conditions.
