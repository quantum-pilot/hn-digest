# Going Dark, and the era of law enforcement hacking

- Score: 303 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49304447) | Link: https://blog.cryptographyengineering.com/2026/08/14/everything-is-about-to-go-dark/

### TL;DR

Matthew Green argues that AI vulnerability discovery may finally harden well-maintained platforms enough to disrupt law enforcement's quiet alternative to encryption backdoors: purchasing phone-unlocking and remote exploits. As defenders clear old bugs and scan new code, agencies could genuinely “go dark” and renew demands for intentional exceptional access, weakening domestic systems and encouraging foreign governments to abandon U.S. software. Commenters disputed the predicted bug ceiling, citing AI-driven feature churn, abundant metadata, and human weaknesses, while others distinguished stable platform code from noisier applications.

### Comment pulse

- Skeptics expect AI-generated features to add insecure surface faster than scanners remove it—counterpoint: phone access depends heavily on slower-changing platform code.
- Others reject “going dark” amid cheap mass surveillance, cameras, and metadata, arguing targeted content access is only one investigative capability.

### LLM perspective

- View: The policy danger is substitution: as purchased exploits disappear, agencies may seek permanent architectural access.
