# Trade (and Tariffs)

- Score: 504 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49464896) | Link: https://xkcd.com/3290/

### TL;DR

The frozen page contains xkcd metadata rather than the comic image; its alt text, supplied in discussion, frames trade as arms threatening oxygen to legs despite the legs’ comparative advantage at running. Commenters interpret the joke as tariffs using self-harm to assert dominance, but dispute any blanket free-trade conclusion. Some emphasize retaliatory costs and policy inconsistency; others argue comparative advantage rests on assumptions about capital mobility, trade deficits reshape domestic sectors, and carefully targeted, predictable tariffs can serve industrial strategy unlike broad, unstable levies.

### Comment pulse

- Tariff critics described retaliation as mutually damaging taxation with contradictory partisan defenses.
- Industrial-policy supporters distinguished targeted, gradual protection from volatile across-the-board tariffs.
- Debate linked trade deficits to capital inflows, asset ownership, manufacturing capacity, and cheap production inputs.
