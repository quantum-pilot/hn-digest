# I were 17, I'd learn how to build LLMs from scratch

- Score: 591 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49412396) | Link: https://twitter.com/paulg/status/2091544343589060625

### TL;DR

A brief recommendation urges teenagers to build and train language models from first principles using whatever hardware they can access. Supporters treat the exercise like building an operating system or early radio: the product matters less than acquiring mathematical intuition, experimental habits, and awareness of model limits. Skeptics note that internal behavior remains opaque even after implementing the matrix operations. Others say weak hardware, costly cloud access, sparse beginner material, and slow feedback make fine-tuning small pretrained models a more practical entry point.

### Comment pulse

- Hacking new technology compounds learning → enthusiasts value curiosity and deep construction skills even when the resulting model lacks practical utility.
- From-scratch work reveals mechanics, not causality → high-dimensional behavior remains difficult to interpret after the architecture and training loop are understood.
- Accessibility shapes who can experiment → tutorials lower conceptual barriers — counterpoint: limited VRAM makes meaningful iteration slow or expensive.
