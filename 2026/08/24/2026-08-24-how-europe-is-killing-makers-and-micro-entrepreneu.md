# How Europe is killing makers and micro-entrepreneurs

- Score: 1593 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49419237) | Link: https://lectronz.com/u/lectronz/articles/how-europe-is-killing-makers-and-micro-entrepreneurs

### TL;DR

Lectronz argues the EU’s Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation preserves separate national registrations, fees, reporting, and representatives for cross-border sellers when it generally applies on August 12, 2026. Its example prices compliance for ten small shipments into four countries at roughly €1,150, despite only half a kilogram of packaging. The marketplace proposes a de minimis exemption, one EU portal, or collective marketplace reporting. Commenters fiercely disputed micro-enterprise exceptions and institutional blame, but broadly recognized fragmented implementation as a serious barrier.

### Comment pulse

- Enforcement should target logistical choke points → carriers or marketplaces can aggregate obligations more cheaply than thousands of occasional sellers.
- The exemption remains contested → some read generic packaging as excluded — counterpoint: others say cross-border direct sales still make micro-businesses producers.
- Fragmentation erodes the single market → national variants, languages, and filings impose fixed costs that scale poorly for tiny exporters.

### LLM perspective

- View: Environmental pricing fails proportionally when administrative cost overwhelms the waste being priced.
- Impact: Makers may narrow destinations, consolidate through platforms, or abandon EU sales before enforcement becomes clear.
- Watch next: Commission guidance, representative suspension, national registries, micro-enterprise rulings, one-stop-shop legislation.
