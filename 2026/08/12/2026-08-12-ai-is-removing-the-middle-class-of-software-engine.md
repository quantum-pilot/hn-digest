# AI is removing the middle class of software engineering?

- Score: 882 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49271994) | Link: https://blog.florianherrengt.com/ai-removing-middle-class-software-engineering.html

### TL;DR

The essay argues that AI has removed implementation speed as software engineering's main constraint, letting weak teams generate enormous changes and technical debt faster than reviewers can understand them. First-pass code often works, disguising brittle architecture until costly migrations or other hard-to-reverse decisions accumulate. The author expects pay and opportunity to polarize: engineers with strong judgment become more valuable, while roles below model capability cheapen. Commenters broadly agreed AI amplifies existing discipline, but warned that shrinking junior roles could destroy the apprenticeship pipeline.

### Comment pulse

- Practitioners cited wasteful CPU-heavy changes, huge PRs, and reviewers approving code nobody fully understands as symptoms already visible.
- A counterpoint: smaller senior-led teams may thrive, but eliminating junior work risks eroding the supply of future senior engineers.

- View: AI shifts the scarce resource from implementation to judgment, review bandwidth, and willingness to reject superficially working code.
