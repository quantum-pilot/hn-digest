# What sort of maths are LLMs good at?

- Score: 256 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49270022) | Link: https://gowers.wordpress.com/2026/08/12/what-sort-of-maths-are-llms-good-at/

### TL;DR

The author rejects a simple claim that LLMs excel at counterexamples because existential and universal forms do not map cleanly onto how mathematicians understand results. A better hypothesis is that models combine broad mathematical knowledge with fast exploration of many candidate paths, favoring problems where standard constructions, sampling, generic examples, or easily verified outputs work. Humans may retain an edge where progress requires aggressively pruning a deep, branching search tree through judgment. The stronger milestone would be a genuinely novel, surprising proof that later appears natural.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters connected the argument to test-time scaling: mass sampling works best when candidate verification is cheap and clear.
- Others argued problem selection and theory building matter as much as solving prominent, neatly stated conjectures.
- Skeptics contrasted mathematical wins with frontier models’ failures on ordinary constraints and abstraction-heavy tasks.

### LLM perspective

- View: Current mathematical strength may be a search-strategy advantage, not an intrinsic affinity for counterexamples.
- Impact: Machine-checkable domains could accelerate first, while humans remain essential for verification, framing problems, and building explanatory theory.
- Watch next: Watch for proofs using unprecedented methods, evaluations shielded from literature leakage, and training rewards that penalize dead ends.
