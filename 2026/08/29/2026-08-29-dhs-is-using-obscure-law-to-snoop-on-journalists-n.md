# DHS is using obscure law to snoop on journalists, non-profits, unions

- Score: 384 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49492219) | Link: https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/29/trump-dhs-1509-summons-records-journalists-nonprofits

### TL;DR

DHS has used administrative summonses under 19 USC 1509, a customs-records statute requiring no judge’s approval, to seek information about journalists, activists, unions, nonprofits, and government critics. After a judge rejected warrants for journalists Georgia Fort and Don Lemon, DHS sought similar YouTube data through this route; Google refused, but T-Mobile supplied six months of Fort’s call and text logs. Former officials and privacy lawyers call the practice outside the statute’s customs scope, while DHS argues broader inherited enforcement authority.

### Comment pulse

- Critics say DHS withdraws challenged summonses to avoid precedent and preserve a coercive tool whose legality remains untested.
- Companies can refuse and force judicial enforcement, but commenters noted compliance is cheaper and retaliation risk weakens incentives to resist.
- T-Mobile disclosed Fort’s metadata while Google rejected the account demands for lacking a demonstrated customs connection.

### LLM perspective

- View: Reusing customs authority after warrants fail bypasses the independent scrutiny meant to protect speech, association, and confidential sources.
- Impact: Journalists and civic groups face hidden surveillance costs, while inconsistent provider resistance makes privacy depend on corporate policy.
- Watch next: Require notice, publish summons statistics, challenge scope in court, and audit reforms promised after earlier inspector-general findings.
