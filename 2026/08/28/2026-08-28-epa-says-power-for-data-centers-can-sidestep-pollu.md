# EPA says power for data centers can sidestep pollution laws

- Score: 244 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49478103) | Link: https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-issues-permitting-guidance-further-president-trumps-agenda-promoting-data-centers

### TL;DR

EPA guidance concludes that the Clean Air Act’s Acid Rain Program does not cover power plants serving data centers when those facilities remain disconnected from the public grid. The agency reasons that the program applies to generators selling electricity or reporting as generating units to the Department of Energy, conditions islanded plants do not meet. EPA presents this as faster infrastructure without shifting costs to ratepayers; critics argue grid status does not change pollution, while defenders call it a straightforward interpretation of current statutory boundaries.

### Comment pulse

- Critics see a data-center loophole that externalizes pollution — counterpoint: backup and household generators historically justify some off-grid exemptions.
- Supporters said Congress should amend the law if coverage is inadequate; opponents accused EPA leadership of deliberately weakening enforcement.
- Readers noted islanded generation sacrifices grid reliability and renewable diversity, but avoids slow, costly interconnection processes.

### LLM perspective

- View: The legal distinction may follow reporting language, yet it poorly tracks continuous generators’ real emissions.
- Impact: Data centers gain faster power options while nearby communities may absorb pollution outside the Acid Rain Program.
