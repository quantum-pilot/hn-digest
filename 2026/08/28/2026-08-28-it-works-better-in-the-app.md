# “It works better in the app”

- Score: 650 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49477600) | Link: https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2026/08/it-works-better-in-the-app/

### TL;DR

The author criticizes mobile apps that omit simple features available on desktop websites, prompted by Google Calendar's inability to subscribe to a calendar URL inside its Android app. They argue companies chase engagement and home-screen presence, then maintain incomplete native experiences that redirect users back to the web. Commenters echoed privacy, tracking, and broken happy-path concerns, though some blamed platform restrictions and the cost of supporting web, iOS, and Android separately. Others described inconsistent design systems obscuring app-specific usability problems.

### Comment pulse

- Forced apps impose privacy and security costs → some users abandon services rather than install unnecessary wrappers.
- Three separate platforms multiply feature work → counterpoint: mobile users still deserve complete core workflows.
- Shared design systems create consistency → poorly coordinated rollouts can cover controls or break application-specific interactions.
