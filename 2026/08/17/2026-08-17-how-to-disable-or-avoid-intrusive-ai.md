# How to disable or avoid intrusive AI

- Score: 336 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49331220) | Link: https://www.librarian.net/notoai/

### TL;DR

A librarian-maintained guide collects current steps for reducing built-in AI across Acrobat, Android/Gemini, Amazon, Apple devices, browsers, search engines, Google Workspace, Slack, WhatsApp, Windows, Office, Yahoo Mail, and Zoom. Options range from toggles and uninstalling Copilot to privacy settings, browser flags, no-AI search endpoints, extensions, or alternative software. The guide welcomes updates because products add settings and often default them on. Commenters warned that disabling assistants can also break unrelated features such as CarPlay navigation or review search, making opt-out incomplete and encouraging Linux or FOSS alternatives.

### Comment pulse

- Even enthusiastic AI users dislike forced assistants → embedded models may be inferior to chosen tools and misaligned with the immediate task.
- Switching platforms offers stronger avoidance → counterpoint: the guide prioritizes practical toggles inside software people already use.

### LLM perspective

- View: Intrusiveness is a product-design failure: useful AI should be optional, granular, reversible, and independent from baseline features.
- Impact: Users carry ongoing configuration work while librarians and community maintainers become de facto documentation for disappearing controls.
- Watch next: Track settings after updates, default-on additions, fallback regressions, and whether operating systems expose enforceable AI permissions.
