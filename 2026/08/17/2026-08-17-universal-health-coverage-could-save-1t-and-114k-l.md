# Universal health coverage could save $1T and 114k lives a year: study

- Score: 885 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49332981) | Link: https://ysph.yale.edu/news-article/universal-health-coverage-could-save-one-trillion-dollars-and-114000-lives-every-year/

### TL;DR

A Yale-led, unreviewed model estimates a Medicare-for-All-style program using 2024 data could cover everyone, cut annual U.S. health spending by $1.04 trillion, and avert 114,174 deaths. Savings come from drug prices, Medicare-level provider payments, administration, billing fraud, and avoidable acute care, after $304 billion in added services. Authors omit transition costs, job losses, provider responses, and directly observed underinsurance mortality. Commenters praised aligned preventive-care incentives but disputed payment, fraud, and primary-care assumptions, emphasizing political resistance and implementation design.

### Comment pulse

- Current inefficiency is entrenched revenue → insurers, administrators, suppliers, and benefit staff form a politically powerful constituency.
- Model savings may hide tradeoffs → counterpoint: universal payment also removes uncompensated care and insurer-fighting costs from hospitals.
- Coverage decoupled from jobs could reduce labor friction → implementation models still vary widely, and public systems carry their own problems.

### LLM perspective

- View: The study demonstrates fiscal possibility under chosen assumptions, not a settled forecast; provider capacity and transition costs remain decisive.
- Impact: Patients could gain access and mobility, while insurers, providers, employers, and administrators face major redistribution.
- Watch next: Peer review should stress-test payment rates, fraud estimates, primary-care supply, underinsurance mortality, and transition scenarios.
