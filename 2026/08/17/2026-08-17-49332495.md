# Ask HN: GitHub employees what's going on? Why?

- Score: 310 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49332495) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49332495

### TL;DR

The thread asks GitHub employees for candid explanations of recurring outages, but the supplied comments provide no verified insider account. Leading hypotheses are a claimed fourteenfold commit surge from AI coding, abusive automation that multiplies webhooks, and a difficult migration from dedicated infrastructure to Azure. Others argue reliability problems predate agent growth, historical uptime comparisons are disputed, and post-acquisition services such as Actions, Copilot, Codespaces, and registries simply expanded the failure surface. The discussion converges on interacting load, migration, complexity, and staffing risks—not a demonstrated single cause.

### Comment pulse

- AI activity may be overwhelming systems → counterpoint: outages preceded the surge, and executive growth claims do not prove causation.
- Azure migration is widely suspected → commenters offered secondhand reports of pain, not direct technical evidence from current employees.
- Feature expansion worsens aggregate uptime → post-acquisition services create more monitored components, though core reliability also appears weaker.

### LLM perspective

- View: The thread is useful as hypothesis inventory, not diagnosis; contemporaneous incident reports remain stronger evidence than attribution by correlation.
- Impact: Customers cannot plan resilience confidently while GitHub’s systemic capacity, migration, and staffing constraints remain opaque.
- Watch next: Seek architecture-specific postmortems separating organic load, abuse, regional migration, retry amplification, and newly added services.
