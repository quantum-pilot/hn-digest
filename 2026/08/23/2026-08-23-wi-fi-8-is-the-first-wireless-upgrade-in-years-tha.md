# Wi-Fi 8 is the first wireless upgrade in years that isn't chasing speed

- Score: 276 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49406539) | Link: https://www.xda-developers.com/wi-fi-8-first-wireless-upgrade-years-isnt-chasing-speed-home-networks-need-it/

### TL;DR

Wi-Fi 8 keeps Wi-Fi 7’s headline limits—roughly 23Gbit/s per band, 320MHz channels, 4096-QAM, bands, and spatial streams—and instead targets Ultra High Reliability. Draft goals seek 25% better throughput across signal conditions, 25% lower 95th-percentile latency, and 25% less MPDU loss. Proposed mechanisms distribute tones, resist interference, tune modulation per stream, prioritize channel access, use idle non-primary channels, smooth roaming, and coordinate access points. Finalization is expected in May 2028. Commenters welcomed warehouse-grade consistency but warned benefits require compatible clients, mandatory feature support, and gradual replacement of entrenched 2.4GHz devices.

### Comment pulse

- Warehouse operators prioritize dependable 20Mbit/s and roaming over peak rates because old scanners, fixed access points, and interference dominate operations.
- Household samples show few Wi-Fi 7 clients and many 2.4GHz-only devices—counterpoint: compatible 6GHz clients sometimes delivered multi-fold real-world gains.
- Cellular convergence appealed to some, but replies stressed licensed scheduling and unlicensed listen-before-talk networks solve different coordination problems.

### LLM perspective

- View: Reliability-first design addresses the gap between laboratory capacity and crowded, obstructed, heterogeneous deployments.
- Impact: Industrial, smart-home, and multi-AP users benefit only as infrastructure and clients implement the relevant features.
