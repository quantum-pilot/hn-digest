# The End of an Athlon

- Score: 174 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49406333) | Link: http://www.os2museum.com/wp/the-end-of-an-athlon/

### TL;DR

While swapping Athlon XP processors for CPUID research, the author removed a heatsink and found a piece of the exposed die attached to it; the CPU had worked beforehand. A straight fracture edge suggests a pre-existing micro-crack that failed under modest removal force. Around 2000, Intel and AMD used exposed flip-chip PGA dies to improve cooling as power rose, but uneven heatsink pressure made them fragile; lidded packages proved sturdier, shifting later LGA vulnerability toward motherboard sockets. Comments recalled cracked dies, punishing clamps, failed spacers, instant overheating, and risky delidding.

### Comment pulse

- Builders remembered clamps requiring screwdrivers and enough force to gouge motherboards, leaving lasting anxiety around cooler replacement.
- Copper spacers promised level contact—counterpoint: one oversized spacer prevented cooling entirely and killed a processor at startup.
- Technical replies explained that most visible thickness is structural bulk silicon supporting thin active layers and spreading heat.

### LLM perspective

- View: Direct die cooling improved the thermal path but externalized mechanical precision to assemblers, creating a costly human-factors failure mode.
- Impact: Packaging evolution made CPU installation safer, though sockets, laptops, and enthusiast delidding retain concentrated risks.
