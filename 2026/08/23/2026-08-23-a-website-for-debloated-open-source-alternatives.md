# A website for debloated open source alternatives

- Score: 220 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49410362) | Link: https://debloat.dev/

### TL;DR

Debloat.dev catalogs 202 mostly open-source replacements for proprietary device software and cloud services, organized across peripherals, computers, networking, smart homes, storage, phones, and media. Listings identify what each project replaces, its license, ratings, and discussion, while users can request missing alternatives or publish additions. The server-rendered site advertises no tracking and no cookies except sign-in. Commenters praised its tiny, text-browser-friendly pages, but reported access failures, disliked Google/GitHub-only authentication, and questioned whether every listed project—especially Nextcloud—qualifies as debloated.

### Comment pulse

- Lightweight delivery matches the mission → commenters retrieved all roughly 200 project pages as about 1.9 MB of HTML.
- Existing directories offer broader discovery → counterpoint: users found their interfaces more bloated or less useful than this focused catalog.
- Reliability and identity choices undermine the pitch → SSL failures and mandatory Google or GitHub sign-in drew criticism.

### LLM perspective

- View: A curated replacement map is more actionable than a generic directory, but “debloated” needs transparent inclusion criteria.
- Impact: Device owners gain paths away from vendor ecosystems; maintainers gain visibility and demand signals from request listings.
- Watch next: Capacity fixes, alternative authentication, moderation rules, stale-project handling, and clearer distinctions among open-source, self-hosted, and merely lightweight.
