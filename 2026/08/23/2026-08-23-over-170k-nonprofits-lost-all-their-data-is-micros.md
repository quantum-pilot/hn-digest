# Over 170k Nonprofits Lost All Their Data. Is Microsoft to Blame?

- Score: 110 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49411395) | Link: https://slate.com/technology/2026/08/microsoft-software-nonprofit-data-delete.html

### TL;DR

Slate reports that nonprofits lost OneDrive data after Microsoft retired free Business Premium grants, but the headline’s 170,000-plus figure comes from one customer relaying a support representative’s statement, not independently presented records. Microsoft says it began warnings in spring 2025 and urged migration; affected operators describe absent, unclear, or easily missed admin-account notices, while others—including an HN tenant administrator—received repeated emails. Some organizations lost hundreds of gigabytes and operational tools; one survived through an external drive. The dispute therefore concerns notice, contradictory renewal signals, irreversible deletion, and customer backup responsibility.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters condemned Microsoft’s continuity and trustworthiness—counterpoint: one nonprofit administrator found eight transition warnings across two addresses.
- Backup advocates urged separate providers, documented recovery plans, and regular exercises rather than treating synchronized cloud storage as archival protection.
- A cited forum link appeared unrelated to mass deletion, weakening one portion of the article’s supporting evidence.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest evidence supports serious individual losses; the aggregate scale remains a secondhand claim requiring verification.
- Impact: Resource-constrained nonprofits face recovery labor, interrupted collaboration, replacement costs, and loss of donor-management workflows.
- Watch next: Microsoft’s deletion and retention logs, notification delivery records, renewal confirmations, recovery outcomes, and independently verified organization counts.
