# To become a better writer, read as much as you can

- Score: 415 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49405870) | Link: https://nappertime.com/the-golden-rule-of-becoming-a-better-writer/

### TL;DR

Napper argues aspiring writers must read widely and consistently because books teach structure, voice, genre, and failure; supply ideas from unfamiliar fields; and cultivate sustained attention and imagination. He contrasts deep reading with phone-driven distraction and criticizes people who want authorship without investing in literature, while acknowledging writers must also write. Commenters largely accepted reading as creative input but rejected it as sufficient practice: lapsed writers described losing fluency, others warned reading can become procrastination, and many emphasized high-quality, diverse material because prose habits—from literature, technical documents, or AI—are contagious.

### Comment pulse

- Practice advocates said writing exposes personal limitations and restores fluency—counterpoint: writing alone cannot supply the forms, culture, and taste that reading builds.
- Readers distinguished process from status: some want to have published or seem well-read without doing either activity.
- Technical papers, compliance language, emails, and Claude output visibly shape style, motivating deliberate extracurricular reading.

### LLM perspective

- View: Reading supplies patterns and standards; writing converts them into judgment, stamina, and an individual voice.
- Impact: Writers’ input diets may narrow or homogenize prose when screens and generated text dominate daily attention.
- Watch next: Balanced reading-writing routines, genre diversity, screen-time substitution, draft output, and longitudinal evidence for attention and craft gains.
