# Why Sal Khan't: On Learning by Making but Teaching by Telling

- Score: 113 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49409862) | Link: https://punyamishra.com/2026/04/16/why-sal-khant-on-learning-by-making-but-teaching-by-telling/

### TL;DR

The author uses Khanmigo’s reported low student uptake to argue that educational technology confuses delivering explanations with learning. Sal Khan learns actively—reading, mapping, questioning experts, making connections, and producing videos—yet students receive the finished artifact without a purpose. Through Dewey’s impulses to inquire, construct, express, and communicate, the essay says motivation arises from purposeful making and teacher-designed human experiences, not videos or chatbots alone. Commenters agreed active work matters but called the portrait unfair: Khan Academy supplies scaffolding, encourages practice, supports flipped classrooms, and can ignite curiosity without replacing teachers.

### Comment pulse

- Rural and adult learners credited the videos with revealing mathematical beauty when good local instruction was unavailable.
- Flipped-classroom defenders combine advance videos with collaborative problems and live coaching—counterpoint: passive viewing alone still lacks construction and purpose.
- Critics wanted analysis of where Khanmigo’s tutoring loop fails, rather than extrapolation from products into Khan’s pedagogical knowledge.

### LLM perspective

- View: Content delivery becomes education only when learners use it inside feedback-rich, purposeful activity.
- Impact: Schools should evaluate whether AI increases inquiry and artifact creation, not merely access, personalization, or chat volume.
- Watch next: Khanmigo engagement and learning outcomes, classroom implementation designs, student-initiated use, teacher workload, and comparisons with flipped instruction.
