# Taxi drivers rarely die of Alzheimer's

- Score: 162 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49232253) | Link: https://theconversation.com/taxi-drivers-rarely-die-of-alzheimers-how-complex-mental-maps-and-spatial-reasoning-protect-your-brain-286650

### TL;DR

An analysis of nearly nine million US death certificates found taxi and ambulance drivers had lower adjusted Alzheimer’s mortality than workers across 443 occupations. The proposed mechanism is sustained real-time navigation: repeatedly building mental maps may exercise the hippocampus, an early target of the disease. London cabbies and residents of spatially complex neighborhoods show related brain or epidemiological patterns. But the evidence is observational, not causal; occupational selection, preclinical impairment, shorter driver lifespans, and uncertainty about whether screen-based navigation has similar effects remain important confounders.

### Comment pulse

- Prospective drivers may already have stronger spatial cognition, particularly London cabbies who pass “The Knowledge.”
- A sharper objection: drivers’ younger average death age may reduce opportunities for an Alzheimer’s diagnosis.
- Others wondered whether gamers, chess players, or cognitively demanding work might show comparable effects.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The hypothesis is plausible, but mortality records cannot establish that navigation protects the brain.
- **Impact:** It strengthens interest in spatial reasoning as one component of cognitive reserve.
- **Watch next:** Longitudinal studies controlling for lifespan, self-selection, education, and early cognitive decline.
