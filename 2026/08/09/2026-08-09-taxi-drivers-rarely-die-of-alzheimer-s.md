# Taxi drivers rarely die of Alzheimer's

- Score: 162 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49232253) | Link: https://theconversation.com/taxi-drivers-rarely-die-of-alzheimers-how-complex-mental-maps-and-spatial-reasoning-protect-your-brain-286650

### TL;DR
A large US death-certificate study found taxi and ambulance drivers have the lowest recorded Alzheimer’s mortality among 443 occupations (about 1 in 100 vs 1 in 60 overall). The article links this to constant real‑time navigation, which heavily uses the hippocampus—the brain region first hit by Alzheimer’s. Supporting evidence: London cabbies’ hippocampi enlarge with years of “The Knowledge,” and people in spatially complex neighborhoods show both larger navigation regions and lower Alzheimer’s rates. HN commenters, however, strongly question causality and highlight major confounders.

---

### Comment pulse
- Correlation vs causation → People with unusually strong spatial skills may self-select into navigation-heavy jobs, so the job may not “protect” against Alzheimer’s.  
- Survival bias → Taxi drivers die younger on average than the general population, so fewer ever reach the typical Alzheimer’s onset age.  
- Method worries → Adjusting for education might remove a genuine protective factor; death-certificate data and occupational coding add noise to already weak causal inferences.

---

### LLM perspective
- View: Treat navigation-heavy work as one candidate contributor to cognitive reserve, but not a standalone “cure” or strong protective guarantee.  
- Impact: Occupational design and urban planning could explicitly incorporate spatial-complexity tasks, especially for midlife adults at elevated dementia risk.  
- Watch next: Prospective cohort and imaging studies comparing GPS-reliant vs active navigators, and trials of intensive spatial-training programs in at-risk populations.
