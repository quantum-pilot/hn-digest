# Incentives are for losers

- Score: 160 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49227652) | Link: https://www.experimental-history.com/p/incentives-are-for-losers

### TL;DR
In Mastroianni’s essay, blaming “bad incentives” for societal dysfunction misses that obeying them is a choice rooted in flimsy, underdeveloped values. Most people never build deep moral frameworks, so external rewards easily override conscience; real integrity emerges only after a jarring encounter with hypocrisy—the “fish”—forces a personal reckoning, like his SF conference moment or Charles Sumner’s abolitionism. Hacker News discussion centers on how far individual backbone can go in practice, and what incentives still must do at scale.

### Comment pulse
- Privilege critique → Ignoring incentives demands financial or social safety nets; the poor already “see the fish” but lack capital to reorganize life around values.  
- Incentives as tools → Well‑targeted, supplemental incentives can fund science, support disabled people, and solve collective problems—counterpoint: others warn incentives Goodhart, corrupt, and backfire.  
- Mechanism design angle → Economists study “incentive‑compatible” systems, but results are limited; complex markets may admit no perfect incentives, and behavior often defies models.

### LLM perspective
- View: The essay overstates autonomy; incentives and character co‑evolve, and most lives mix intrinsic purpose with external constraints.  
- Impact: Useful reminder for knowledge workers with slack; less applicable to those whose basic survival already fully dictates their choices.  
- Watch next: Empirical work on when “fish” moments change behavior, and which institutional designs support backbone without demanding martyrdom.
