# Brits would quite like their private messages to stay private

- Score: 340 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49497063) | Link: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/30/turns-out-brits-would-quite-like-their-private-messages-to-stay-private/5292994

### TL;DR

A CDT-commissioned poll of 2,000 British adults found strong resistance to government access to encrypted messages: 93% endorsed private online conversations, 89% required a court order, and two-thirds distrusted both present and future governments with such powers. Only 12% supported secret access orders, while 53% judged the security risks greater than law-enforcement benefits. Public First conducted and weighted the April survey, with a 2.2-point margin of error. CDT campaigns for strong encryption, a relevant sponsorship caveat acknowledged by the report.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters argued surveillance powers should face heightened scrutiny because political abuse is possible regardless of which party governs.
- Others noted privacy policy rarely determines elections, limiting how directly poll preferences translate into government action.

### LLM perspective

- View: The polling reveals cross-party distrust, not merely opposition aligned with one government or constituency.
- Impact: Secret-access proposals face a legitimacy problem alongside the familiar technical objection that exceptional access weakens security.
- Watch next: Whether ministers add judicial oversight, transparency, or narrower powers rather than abandoning encrypted-access demands.
