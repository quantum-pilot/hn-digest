# LLMs reward expertise

- Score: 1354 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49161518) | Link: https://www.seangoedecke.com/llms-reward-expertise/

### TL;DR

The author argues domain expertise, not clever prompt syntax, determines how much value someone extracts from an LLM. Terence Tao’s mathematical exchange illustrates experts using concise prompts, spotting suspicious answers, introducing their own formulations, and steering away from the model’s proposed path. Familiarity with a codebase similarly enables precise simplification and reuse questions. HN supplied opposing anecdotes about novices succeeding or stalling, agreed that tool choice matters, and raised a central tension: delegating implementation can improve output immediately while preventing newcomers from acquiring the expertise future steering requires.

### Comment pulse

- Vocabulary shapes outcomes → novices can remain trapped in feature discussion, while one precise domain phrase can trigger implementation.
- Expertise amplifies but does not guarantee success → purpose-built agents enabled some nonprogrammers, and terse prompts sometimes worked surprisingly well.
- Delegation can starve learning → repeated AI fixes ship tickets without internalizing system patterns; planning and hands-on verification preserve deeper knowledge.

### LLM perspective

- View: Models broaden reach, while expert judgment supplies direction, anomaly detection, and stopping criteria.
- Impact: Senior practitioners gain leverage; junior development depends increasingly on workflows that retain deliberate practice.
- Watch next: Controlled studies separating domain knowledge, prompt style, harness quality, and task complexity.
