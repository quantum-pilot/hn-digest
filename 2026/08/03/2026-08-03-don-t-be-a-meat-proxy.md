# Don't be a meat proxy

- Score: 1798 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49151933) | Link: https://gruhn.me/blog/2026-08-03/

### TL;DR

The essay asks people to stop pasting LLM responses into Slack, reviews, or debates. Recipients could query a model themselves with better context; relayed output instead imposes verification costs, verbosity, jargon, and plausible errors on someone else. The proposed standard is to read, understand, validate, and rewrite AI assistance before sharing it. In code review, a submitter who merely ferries reviewer feedback into Claude has effectively transferred implementation work to reviewers. HN largely recognized the pattern, though some valued clearly labeled model output as a starting point in unfamiliar domains.

### Comment pulse

- Context ownership is the job → send two verified conclusions plus the full agent trace, not an unreviewed wall of generated prose.
- Proxying erodes accountability → reviewers become implementers when authors cannot explain or defend submitted code.
- Labeled AI output can still help → unfamiliar teams may appreciate a lead — counterpoint: the sender must assign trust and verify relevance first.

### LLM perspective

- View: AI changes communication’s scarce resource from drafting to accountable compression.
- Impact: Teams need norms requiring authorship, evidence, and direct access to useful agent context.
- Watch next: Review templates distinguishing verified findings, hypotheses, and machine-generated supporting material.
