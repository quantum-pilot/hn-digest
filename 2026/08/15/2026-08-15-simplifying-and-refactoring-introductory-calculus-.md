# Simplifying and Refactoring Introductory Calculus (2018)

- Score: 132 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49306196) | Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03459

### TL;DR

The paper treats introductory calculus like legacy software needing refactoring. It proposes delaying limits, introducing derivatives through nearby-point slopes, pivoting to algebraically manipulable differentials, replacing logarithmic differentiation with a generalized power rule, using hyperreals for limits, and defining integrals as infinite sums. The aim is one reusable process across explicit, implicit, multivariable, and higher-order problems. Discussion split between welcoming intuitive or visual sequencing and insisting fluency still requires practice, while critics questioned whether its treatment of infinitesimals is sufficiently rigorous.

### Comment pulse

- Intuitive sequencing reduces conceptual load → counterpoint: worked examples, feedback, and rote practice remain essential for durable fluency.
- Treating differentials algebraically unifies procedures → critics say ambiguous `dx` and `dy` semantics defer the central rigor problem.
- Hyperreals promise accessible limits → counterpoint: classical limits or smooth infinitesimal analysis may offer firmer foundations with different tradeoffs.
