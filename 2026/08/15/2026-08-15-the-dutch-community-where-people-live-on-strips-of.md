# The Dutch community where people live on strips of land in a lake

- Score: 109 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49312165) | Link: https://www.core77.com/posts/144869

### TL;DR

Loosdrecht’s exceptionally narrow lake islands are remnants of peat mining: dredged peat was unloaded onto thin strips to dry, leaving alternating land and water. Twentieth-century developers later placed houses on the strips, reachable only by boat. Although the article describes hundreds of residents, commenters with local ownership said these are primarily weekend or recreational cottages and official permanent residence is prohibited, though some people stay anyway. Discussion added that electricity arrived recently, water levels are pump-managed, and isolated properties can reach seven-figure prices.

### Comment pulse

- Owners use the islands like cottages → their central location supports weekend escapes and sailboat storage rather than ordinary residency.
- Isolation carries tradeoffs → boat-only access and formerly limited electricity coexist with privacy, nature, and sometimes seven-figure prices.
- Low lake levels are actively managed → pumps maintain a target because the area cannot drain naturally except through evaporation.
