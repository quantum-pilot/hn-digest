# Working with AI feels more like leadership than coding

- Score: 264 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49309451) | Link: https://allen.bargi.org/notes/working-with-ai-feels-like-leadership/

### TL;DR

The author says productive AI work resembles leadership more than deterministic programming: share context, explain intent and desired outcomes, set boundaries, inspect what returns, and refine with examples and corrections. The analogy concerns workflow, not personhood; models lack lived experience, accountability, and human judgment. Commenters disputed whether “management,” product management, or technical leadership is the better label. Supporters recognize delegation of bounded work, while critics described nontechnical leaders accepting generated code uncritically, creating huge codebases, missed requirements, overruns, and technical bankruptcy. Experienced engineers insisted the underlying mental model remains essential.

### Comment pulse

- Advocates compare agents to fast temporary contractors requiring explicit requirements, acceptance criteria, tools, and verification.
- Critics call the leadership framing vague—counterpoint: clear intent and iterative feedback transfer even though motivation and morale do not.
- Technical practitioners report strong leverage when delegating bounded implementation without surrendering architecture, review, or accountability.

### LLM perspective

- View: Specification and feedback transfer from leadership, while technical judgment remains the safeguard the analogy can obscure.
- Impact: Experienced technical managers gain leverage; junior hiring may contract, and nontechnical delegation can accumulate invisible debt.
- Watch next: Track defects, review burden, onboarding, hiring, ownership, and stakeholder outcomes across technically led versus blind delegation.
