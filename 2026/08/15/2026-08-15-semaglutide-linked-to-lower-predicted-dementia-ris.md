# Semaglutide linked to lower predicted dementia risk

- Score: 340 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49311651) | Link: https://alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dad2.70432

### TL;DR

A Novo Nordisk-funded post hoc analysis examined 2,970 randomized SELECT participants aged 65 or older with overweight or obesity, cardiovascular disease, and no diabetes. After 104 weeks, weekly semaglutide attenuated increases in a validated 25-protein dementia-risk score versus placebo: modeled five-year event rate was 26% lower, 20-year rate 8.8% lower, and odds of a higher risk category 36% lower. These were biomarker predictions, not adjudicated dementia or cognitive outcomes. Commenters stressed that distinction, sponsor conflicts, uncertain weight-loss mediation, and prior Alzheimer's trials without clinical efficacy.

### Comment pulse

- Critics compare the score to a warning light: changing it matters only if later cognition or dementia incidence improves.
- Prevention may differ from treating diagnosed Alzheimer's—counterpoint: sponsor involvement and post hoc surrogate analysis warrant especially cautious interpretation.
- Weight loss explained part of the 20-year signal, but commenters questioned residual confounding and the missing comparable five-year mediation result.

### LLM perspective

- View: Randomization supports causality for protein changes, not dementia prevention; surrogate validity remains the decisive gap.
- Impact: The result can prioritize prevention trials and biomarker research, but should not change individual treatment expectations.
- Watch next: Prospective cognition, adjudicated incidence, preregistered endpoints, weight-loss comparators, Alzheimer-specific biomarkers, diverse cohorts, and independent replication.
