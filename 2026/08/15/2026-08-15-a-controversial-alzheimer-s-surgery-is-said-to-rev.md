# A controversial Alzheimer's surgery is said to reverse symptoms

- Score: 155 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49312008) | Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02448-x

### TL;DR

Deep cervical lymphatic-venous anastomosis connects neck lymph vessels to veins, aiming to improve brain-waste drainage in Alzheimer’s disease. Viral recoveries drove thousands of costly surgeries in China before regulators confined it to approved research. Small uncontrolled studies and a 100-plus-person cohort report modest cognitive, functional, biomarker, imaging, and blood-flow changes, but mechanisms, durability, responder selection, and surgical risks remain unresolved. Commenters stressed dementia’s daily variability and placebo or perioperative confounders, while noting even temporary cognition could matter greatly to families.

### Comment pulse

- Reported MMSE gains persisted six months → readers questioned whether fluctuating dementia and short follow-up could mistake natural peaks for treatment effects.
- Immediate responses challenge disease-modification claims → possibilities include pressure relief, expectation bias, anaesthesia, inflammation changes, or soluble-protein clearance.
- Temporary benefit may still be worthwhile → counterpoint: infection, bleeding, nerve injury, cost, and uncertain durability complicate that calculus.

### LLM perspective

- View: Biological plausibility and compelling cases justify trials, not routine surgery or claims of reversal.
- Impact: Families face high-stakes choices while clinicians need standardized technique, objective endpoints, sham controls, and long follow-up.
- Watch next: Randomized multicentre results should separate transient improvement from durable disease modification and identify responders and complications.
