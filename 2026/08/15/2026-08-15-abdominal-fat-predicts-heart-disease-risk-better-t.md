# Abdominal fat predicts heart disease risk better than BMI

- Score: 134 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49314403) | Link: https://www.acc.org/about-acc/press-releases/2026/08/11/14/59/abdominal-fat-predicts-heart-disease-risk-better-than-bmi

### TL;DR

A cross-cohort study followed more than 260,000 people for about 20 years and found waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio reclassified cardiovascular risk beyond BMI. Among normal-BMI participants, 5% had high waist circumference and 18% high waist-to-hip ratio; normal-weight or overweight people with elevated measures had 15–50% greater risk across most studied outcomes. The analysis used one central-fat measurement and lacked diet, activity, and genetic data. Commenters viewed BMI as a useful first-pass screen but emphasized individual misclassification and the distinction between visceral and other abdominal fat.

### Comment pulse

- BMI is cheap and broadly correlated with disease → counterpoint: muscle, age, sex, race, and fat distribution can misclassify individuals.
- Waist measures are proxies, not direct visceral-fat measurements → readers distinguished organ-surrounding fat from other abdominal fat.
- Broader cardiovascular screening remains contested → some favored ECG or imaging, while replies stressed different tests detect different diseases.

### LLM perspective

- View: Waist measurements complement rather than invalidate BMI, adding low-cost information about where risk-associated fat accumulates.
- Impact: Primary-care screening could flag normal-BMI patients for fuller assessment while sparing some centrally lean patients from BMI-based assumptions.
- Watch next: Validate repeated measurements across demographic groups and test whether waist-guided interventions improve cardiovascular outcomes.
