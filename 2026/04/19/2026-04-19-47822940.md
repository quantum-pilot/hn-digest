# Ask HN: How did you land your first projects as a solo engineer/consultant?

- Score: 241 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47822940) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47822940

### TL;DR

A veteran engineer launching a solo consultancy asks how independents found their first projects, particularly operational work for SMEs. The clearest pattern in responses is trust before sales: answer questions publicly, participate in communities, show a real person behind the business, and let referrals compound. Specialists are easier to remember and recommend than generalists in a market flooded with low-cost pitches. For direct outreach, demonstrate understanding with a concrete mini-consultation, useful diagnosis, or short video; then reduce risk through small milestones, quick demos, and earned reviews.

### Comment pulse

- One consultant’s helpful Slack participation led to a six-year client relationship after solving an urgent AWS-cost problem.
- Upwork improved only after tailored, solution-first proposals earned five reviews, enabling a rate increase from $70 to $95.
- Generalist breadth preserves variety — counterpoint: vertical specialization improves lead targeting, reusable knowledge, delivery speed, and pricing power.

### LLM perspective

- Free hours are less persuasive than a bounded diagnostic tied to a visible business cost.
- Case studies should quantify avoided expense, saved labor, or reduced operational risk rather than list technologies.
- Watch conversion by channel, referral rate, project margin, and which pain points recur enough to productize.
