# The Bromine Chokepoint

- Score: 132 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47826100) | Link: https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-bromine-chokepoint-how-strife-in-the-middle-east-could-halt-production-of-the-worlds-memory-chips/

### TL;DR

The article argues that memory-chip production has a hidden chokepoint: South Korea imports 97.5% of its bromine from Israel, where it is converted into semiconductor-grade hydrogen bromide for DRAM and NAND etching. Conflict could interrupt the co-located Dead Sea facilities or shipping, while qualified purification capacity elsewhere takes years to build and is already committed. It recommends stockpiles, contracts, diversified conversion plants, and allied industrial policy. Commenters dispute the alarm: raw bromine exists elsewhere, but supporters stress that immediately usable ultra-pure gas—not elemental availability—is the constraint.

### Comment pulse

- Skeptics likened the warning to recurring scarcity scares and questioned whether the cited missile and insurance risks justify the headline.
- Risk managers argued efficient supply chains suppress spare capacity, making even low-probability disruptions worth mitigating.
- Markets can redirect materials as prices rise — counterpoint: reallocation still causes shortages for lower-margin products and vulnerable buyers.
