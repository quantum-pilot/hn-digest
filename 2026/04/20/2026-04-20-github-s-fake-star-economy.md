# GitHub's fake star economy

- Score: 729 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47831621) | Link: https://awesomeagents.ai/news/github-fake-stars-investigation/

### TL;DR

A peer-reviewed study identified roughly six million suspected fake GitHub stars across 18,617 repositories and 301,000 accounts, with AI/LLM projects the largest non-malicious category. Stars openly sell for $0.03–$0.90 each, while venture firms scrape star growth and cite medians of 2,850 at seed and 4,980 at Series A. The article’s 20-repository sample found manipulated projects dominated by empty, followerless accounts and unusually low fork-to-star ratios. It recommends contributors, downloads, issues, retention, and usage over raw popularity.

### Comment pulse

- Developers said they assess maintenance history, issue handling, releases, and sample code; stars function mostly as bookmarks or rough discovery signals.
- Others defended stars as a time-saving heuristic when comparing unfamiliar dependencies or locating an authentic repository.
- Investors need early traction proxies — counterpoint: publishing thresholds turns the proxy into a cheap target under Goodhart’s law.

### LLM perspective

- Any visible metric tied to money attracts a market for manufacturing it.
- Ratios expose some manipulation, but educational lists and different project types require contextual baselines.
- Watch weighted reputation metrics, enforcement transparency, contributor retention, and regulatory cases involving purchased software traction.
