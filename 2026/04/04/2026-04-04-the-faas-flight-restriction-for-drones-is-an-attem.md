# The FAA’s flight restriction for drones is an attempt to criminalize filming ICE

- Score: 523 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47633947) | Link: https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/04/faas-temporary-flight-restriction-drones-blatant-attempt-criminalize-filming-ice

### TL;DR

EFF says an FAA “temporary flight restriction” is an unconstitutional mechanism for suppressing drone recordings of federal law enforcement. Effective January 16, 2026 through October 29, 2027, it bars unmanned aircraft within 3,000 horizontal feet of facilities or mobile assets—including convoys—belonging to four federal departments, with civil/criminal penalties and possible seizure or destruction. EFF argues unmarked ICE/CBP vehicles make compliance unknowable, violating due process; that protected recording is chilled; and that FAA rules requiring a stated hazard and press-access contact were not satisfied. Its January objection remains unanswered.

### Comment pulse

- Some argued prosecution requires knowing or willful conduct — counterpoint: others said drone destruction and license sanctions could occur without a conviction.
- Mobile, often unmarked vehicles make restricted areas impossible to map; commenters saw that uncertainty itself as a powerful deterrent.
- Several expected courts to reject aggressive enforcement — counterpoint: litigation would impose severe costs only after footage, property, or liberty were lost.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A moving exclusion zone without reliable notice is structurally incompatible with predictable civilian compliance.
- **Impact:** Even unsuccessful prosecutions could chill journalism by making recording contingent on unknowable vehicle locations and costly legal defense.
- **Watch next:** FAA’s response, accredited-media access procedures, enforcement incidents, drone seizures, and constitutional challenges testing notice and recording rights.
