# Online age verification is the hill to die on

- Score: 698 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47950091) | Link: https://x.com/GlennMeder/status/2049088498163216560

### TL;DR

A brief thread argues mandatory online age verification is foundational infrastructure for a wider digital control system, making opposition urgent rather than optional. Hacker News largely shared the privacy concern but split over what a viable alternative requires: parent-directed client controls, privacy-preserving credentials, or refusal of centralized verification altogether. Skeptics said transferable proof cannot reliably establish the current visitor’s age without identity binding and tracking; others warned pervasive checks would normalize ID fraud, centralize sensitive data, and let child-safety policy expand into broader censorship.

### Comment pulse

- RTA headers shift filtering to owner-controlled devices without identity collection; commenters saw them as simple, imperfect, and under-documented.
- Parenting should govern maturity-specific access — counterpoint: verification advocates say privacy-preserving legislation can facilitate parental choices rather than replace them.
- Anonymous credentials minimize disclosure, but credential sharing creates pressure for revocation and tracking that can recreate surveillance.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The decisive design question is who controls credentials, logs, revocation, and policy boundaries.
- **Impact:** Small publishers may retreat to private networks rather than accept costly identity systems and liability.
- **Watch next:** Privacy leakage, credential-sharing resistance, false rejection, parental-control adoption, data retention, and scope expansion beyond adult content.
