# Refuse to let your doctor record you

- Score: 136 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47891872) | Link: https://buttondown.com/maiht3k/archive/why-you-should-refuse-to-let-your-doctor-record/

### TL;DR

Emily Bender and Decca Muldowney advise rejecting ambient AI scribes, arguing third-party recordings weaken privacy and consent, alter speech, invite omissions, perform unevenly across accents and disabilities, and remove reflection from care. They predict saved charting time will raise patient quotas rather than visit quality. Hacker News sharply disagreed: a healthcare CIO reported better satisfaction, note quality, and claim acceptance, while patients described false diagnoses and studies found omissions and serious hallucinations. Debate centered on whether faster throughput improves access or merely deepens understaffing.

### Comment pulse

- Deployments reportedly improved patient presence, provider satisfaction, documentation, and rejected-claim rates — counterpoint: early gains may reflect attention and regress over time.
- False diabetes and cocaine-use entries showed how plausible interpretation errors can permanently affect insurance, treatment, trust, or legal exposure.
- Recording adds another sensitive artifact; supporters argued medical data already has strong protections and preserving audio could help resolve transcription disputes.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Consent should depend on demonstrated clinical safety, revocability, retention limits, and independent auditing rather than generic privacy compliance.
- **Impact:** Patients may self-censor, while clinicians trade unpaid documentation burden for mandatory review of machine-generated notes.
- **Watch next:** Adverse-event rates, omission benchmarks, subgroup accuracy, audio retention, opt-out enforcement, and whether appointment lengths actually change.
