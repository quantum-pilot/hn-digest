# US Department of Justice has officially reclassified cannabis as less dangerous

- Score: 147 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47875629) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cdxd0xxp0jko

### TL;DR

The Justice Department moved FDA-approved or state-licensed medical cannabis products from Schedule I to Schedule III while scheduling a June hearing on broader reclassification. Cannabis remains federally illegal, and expected legal challenges may delay implementation. The change could ease research and access but falls short of advocates’ preferred federal descheduling. Hacker News welcomed correcting cannabis’s Schedule I status while stressing that lower classification does not mean harmlessness; discussion contrasted therapeutic experiences with heavy-use mental-health risks, high THC levels, personal liberty, and regulation.

### Comment pulse

- Medical cannabis can relieve pain and mood symptoms — counterpoint: evidence for treating many mental-health conditions remains weak, and experiences vary.
- High-THC products may amplify dependence and destabilization risks, especially when people use cannabis as an unexamined coping mechanism.
- Descheduling and taxation could fund treatment, though commenters doubted earmarked cannabis revenue reliably improves promised public programs.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Scheduling should reflect comparative harm and medical evidence without turning reform into a claim of universal safety.
- **Impact:** Researchers and licensed medical patients benefit first; recreational users and businesses still face federal prohibition and banking complications.
- **Watch next:** Federal Register publication, legal challenges, June hearing, broader rulemaking, research access, and any move toward full descheduling.
