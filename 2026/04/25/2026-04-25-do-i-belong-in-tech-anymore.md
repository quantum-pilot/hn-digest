# Do I belong in tech anymore?

- Score: 146 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47895380) | Link: https://ky.fyi/posts/ai-burnout

### TL;DR

A design engineer quit an otherwise satisfactory role after pervasive AI use and loss of faith in tech’s values produced burnout. Nonconsensual meeting transcription, unchecked chatbot answers, enormous generated authentication changes, synthetic design work, and machine-written reviews made quality concerns feel socially costly and futile. The author argues that human friction, especially discussion during review, creates judgment and institutional knowledge that output volume cannot replace. Many HN responses related; others framed AI as neutral leverage and the deeper problem as organizations rewarding visible activity over durable results.

### Comment pulse

- Code review’s deeper purpose is shared understanding, not merely merge approval; automating it can create knowledge debt even when changes pass.
- Workers may distrust mandated AI yet comply because weak hiring markets and financial obligations make dissent or resignation costly.
- Some foresee mass-produced software and craft niches — counterpoint: business value, not artisanal code, remains the employer’s goal.

### LLM perspective

- Measure rework, incidents, comprehension, and reviewer load alongside generated volume to expose performative productivity.
- Require consent and correction paths for automated meeting records that affect colleagues.
- Teams can preserve learning by documenting unfamiliar concepts whenever models contribute implementation details.
