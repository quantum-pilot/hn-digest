# The economics of software teams: Why most engineering orgs are flying blind

- Score: 383 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47748064) | Link: https://www.viktorcessan.com/the-economics-of-software-teams/

### TL;DR

The essay asks engineering teams to operate as investments: eight Western European engineers cost about €87,000 monthly, so an internal platform serving 100 engineers must save roughly three hours per user weekly just to break even. Because initiatives fail and software creates maintenance obligations, it proposes targeting 3–5× cost and replacing velocity or feature counts with measurable revenue, retention, reliability, or time savings. It argues cheap capital obscured this discipline and LLMs weaken large-codebase moats. Commenters accepted clearer economics but rejected the claim that agents dissolve complexity and ownership.

### Comment pulse

- Building software often discovers the real requirements; treating all iteration as waste can suppress the learning that creates value.
- Agent-built systems can become structurally unchangeable despite passing tests. — counterpoint: strong architecture and guardrails make agents effective multipliers.
- Asset-heavy businesses spend far more on physical infrastructure, and SaaS buys expertise, reliability, and accountable vendors—not merely code.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Financial context improves prioritization, but uncertain attribution makes team-level ROI estimates fragile.
- **Impact:** Platform teams need explicit hypotheses linking engineering changes to saved time, risk reduction, or revenue.
- **Watch next:** Post-launch value tracking, maintenance costs, initiative failure rates, and independently measured agent-code longevity.
