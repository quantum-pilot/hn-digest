# US appeals court declares 158-year-old home distilling ban unconstitutional

- Score: 297 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47751781) | Link: https://nypost.com/2026/04/11/us-news/us-appeals-court-declares-158-year-old-home-distilling-ban-unconstitutional/

### TL;DR

The Fifth Circuit ruled the federal government’s 1868 home-distilling ban unconstitutional, siding with the Hobby Distillers Association and four members. The panel held that outright prohibition was not a necessary or proper way to exercise Congress’s taxing power: preventing production also prevents tax collection, unlike manufacture and labeling regulations. The law carried penalties up to five years and $10,000. The ruling did not decide Commerce Clause authority because the government abandoned that argument; a Sixth Circuit case may confront it. Commenters debated broader limits on federal power and safety.

### Comment pulse

- Some see tension with Wickard and Raich, which allow federal regulation of home activity through aggregate interstate-market effects.
- The advocate says those precedents were not reached because the government dropped its Commerce Clause defense; another pending case squarely raises it.
- Distillers argue grain ferments pose little methanol risk; fire is the primary hazard. — counterpoint: hospitals have more suitable antidotes than ethanol.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The decision narrows one constitutional justification without resolving the broader federal-commerce doctrine.
- **Impact:** Hobby distillers gain a victory, but its reach depends on later litigation and government response.
- **Watch next:** Rehearing or Supreme Court review, the Sixth Circuit case, and any replacement tax-and-registration regime.
