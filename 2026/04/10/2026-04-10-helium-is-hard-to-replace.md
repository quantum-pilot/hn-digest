# Helium Is Hard to Replace

- Score: 237 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47719274) | Link: https://www.construction-physics.com/p/helium-is-hard-to-replace

### TL;DR

Helium is abundant cosmically but scarce on Earth: radioactive decay concentrates it in natural-gas fields, while helium escapes to space. Qatar supplies one-third, so Strait of Hormuz disruption exposed the chain after the US sold its reserve in 2024. Helium’s unmatched 4.2 K boiling point, inertness, conductivity, and low mass make substitution difficult in MRI magnets, semiconductor fabrication, fiber optics, aerospace purging, scientific instruments, and diving. Welding and lifting can switch gases; closed-loop recovery and MRI designs can cut consumption by over 90%, but not eliminate it.

### Comment pulse

- Readers said under 10% of natural-gas plants recover helium; most vent it, making today’s scarcity partly an investment problem.

- The former federal reserve may have suppressed prices and discouraged supply investment, complicating claims that its sale alone caused scarcity.
