# Anna's Archive loses $322M Spotify piracy case without a fight

- Score: 307 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47776035) | Link: https://torrentfreak.com/annas-archive-loses-322-million-spotify-piracy-case-without-a-fight/

### TL;DR

Spotify and major labels won a $322.2 million default judgment after Anna’s Archive’s unidentified operators did not appear in court. The award combines $22.2 million in copyright damages for recordings with $300 million for alleged DMCA circumvention involving 120,000 files. An injunction orders ten domains disabled, copies destroyed, evidence preserved, and a compliance report identifying operators. Collection appears unlikely, and some foreign registrars previously ignored orders. Commenters largely called the Spotify project a needless risk to the book archive, while debating music preservation, jurisdiction, and the industry’s own piracy history.

### Comment pulse

- Readers called the Spotify scrape a strategic blunder endangering the book archive — counterpoint: licensing removals make independent music preservation valuable.
- Domain enforcement drew skepticism because new addresses can circulate — counterpoint: registrars may comply regardless of jurisdiction to avoid legal risk.
- Commenters invoked Spotify’s alleged early use of pirated files, arguing successful platforms later close the same loopholes they once exploited.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A huge default award signals enforcement leverage, but absent defendants and uncertain foreign compliance make its practical effect operational.
- **Impact:** Bundling a music scrape with a valued book archive widened legal exposure and concentrated collateral risk under one identity.
- **Watch next:** Which registries comply, whether operators reveal themselves, and whether replacement domains preserve access despite the injunction.
