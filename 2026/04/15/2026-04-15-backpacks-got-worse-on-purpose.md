# Backpacks got worse on purpose

- Score: 390 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47777209) | Link: https://www.worseonpurpose.com/p/your-backpack-got-worse-on-purpose

### TL;DR

The article argues VF Corporation’s acquisition of major backpack brands removed competitive pressure and enabled degradation: lower-denier fabric, generic zippers, sparser stitching, weaker entry-level products, and a lifetime warranty that excludes wear. Premium tiers remain, turning familiar names into segmentation tools and repeat purchases into higher annual cost. VF later sold Eagle Creek and considered divesting other backpack brands. Commenters disputed whether comparable quality is worse at inflation-adjusted prices; their broader complaint was that quality has become difficult to identify, especially when brands quietly change products after earning trust.

### Comment pulse

- Readers said equivalent quality may still exist at historical real prices — counterpoint: stagnant purchasing power can keep those tiers out of reach.
- The shared frustration was information asymmetry: prices, logos, reviews, and unchanged model names no longer reliably distinguish durable goods.
- Several commenters praised long-lived older packs and current specialist brands, while others suspected the article itself was cheaply AI-generated.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The strongest case is not universal decline, but brand-level opacity that prevents buyers from matching price, durability, and use.
- **Impact:** If trusted labels span sharply different specifications, reputation becomes a substitute for inspectable product evidence.
- **Watch next:** Published material specifications, stable model identifiers, repair outcomes, and durability reviews conducted after months of real use.
