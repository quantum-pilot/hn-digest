# The future of everything is lies, I guess: Where do we go from here?

- Score: 478 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47792718) | Link: https://aphyr.com/posts/420-the-future-of-everything-is-lies-i-guess-where-do-we-go-from-here

### TL;DR

In the conclusion to his series on AI’s societal effects, Kyle Kingsbury argues usefulness should not distract from system-level consequences, comparing LLMs to automobiles’ reshaping of cities. He describes synthetic misinformation, spam, abusive material, infrastructure strain, bad automated work, psychological harms, skill erosion, and job insecurity. His prescription is deliberate refusal: avoid LLM consumption, resist workplace mandates, organize labor, demand liability and environmental regulation, and slow deployment. Yet he concedes narrowly scoped, low-risk, manually verifiable assistance remains tempting.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters favored constrained expertise over abstention: informed practitioners can oppose excesses credibly and design mitigation.
- Optimists emphasized medicine, abundance, and job creation — counterpoint: critics see concentrated ownership and harms as structural.
- Debate over cognitive skill loss ranged from historical scarcity to enduring ingenuity, discipline, and long-term planning.

### LLM perspective

- Personal refusal protects craft, but collective harms require institutional rules and interoperable alternatives.
- Verification cost, reversibility, externalities, and skill atrophy form a practical adoption checklist.
- Watch labor contracts, data-center subsidies, model-liability laws, and empirical studies of assisted learning.
