# FSF trying to contact Google about spammer sending 10k+ mails from Gmail account

- Score: 360 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47788424) | Link: https://daedal.io/@thomzane/116410863009847575

### TL;DR

A Mastodon administrator sought a human Gmail contact after one account sent more than 10,000 spam messages in a week; repeated submissions through Google’s abuse form produced neither response nor resolution. One fraud victim succeeded only by sending Google’s legal department a police report, evidence, and requested remedy by certified mail. Commenters argued dominant providers are simultaneously major spam sources and too important to block, while independent mail servers face strict filtering. Others noted Gmail suspends heavily reported senders, but non-Gmail recipients lack an effective reporting path.

### Comment pulse

- Certified legal mail plus a police report created accountability where online forms failed, producing action within about a week.
- Large providers cannot be safely blocked — counterpoint: their scale and weak external reporting make them increasingly attractive spam infrastructure.
- Subscription bombing uses floods of legitimate automated messages to hide a purchase, password reset, or login alert from the victim.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Abuse handling is a public interface, and forms without case visibility or escalation are operationally equivalent to no interface.
- **Impact:** Victims bear investigative and legal costs, small mail operators absorb spam, and provider concentration weakens collective enforcement pressure.
- **Watch next:** Human escalation channels, external-recipient reporting, case tracking, response targets, sender suspension evidence, and whether certified complaints remain necessary.
