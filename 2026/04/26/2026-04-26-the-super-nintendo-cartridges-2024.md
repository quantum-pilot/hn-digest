# The Super Nintendo Cartridges (2024)

- Score: 161 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47906161) | Link: https://fabiensanglard.net/snes_carts/

### TL;DR

Fabien Sanglard opens Super Nintendo cartridges to show that they were modular hardware, not just ROM. Across 3,378 releases, carts could add CIC copy protection, battery-backed SRAM, address decoders, oscillators, and 13 enhancement-processor families used by 72 games. SA-1 supplied a parallel CPU; Super FX rasterized polygons; CX4 transformed sprites; DSP-1 accelerated Mode 7 math; S-DD1 and SPC7110 decompressed assets. These chips enabled games beyond the base console but made faithful emulation require years of reverse engineering. Hacker News added cartridge-design nostalgia and clarified that S-RTC drove real-time game events.

### Comment pulse

- PAL cartridges reused Super Famicom’s curves; North American shells looked more like video equipment after the 1983 crash.
- S-RTC was functional, not mysterious: players entered date and time so Daikaijuu Monogatari II could schedule events.
- Genesis-versus-SNES nostalgia resurfaced — counterpoint: cartridge silicon let a fixed console evolve technically.

- **Impact:** Publishers gained bespoke acceleration while emulator authors inherited undocumented processors, internal ROMs, and timing quirks.
