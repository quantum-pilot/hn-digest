# Why has there been so little progress on Alzheimer's disease?

- Score: 406 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47905984) | Link: https://freakonomics.com/podcast/why-has-there-been-so-little-progress-on-alzheimers-disease/

### TL;DR

Freakonomics attributes Alzheimer’s stagnation to an incomplete amyloid-cascade model reinforced by funding lock-in, weak oversight, and manipulated images alleged in studies. Whistleblower Matthew Schrag and journalist Charles Piller describe retractions and investigations involving Cassava Sciences, a landmark 2006 Nature paper, and NIH research. Anti-amyloid drugs may slightly slow decline but do not restore cognition and can cause brain swelling or bleeding. Hacker News distinguished fraud from honest model failure, defended genetic evidence for amyloid in some cases, and emphasized that Alzheimer’s is likely heterogeneous, multicausal, and slow to test.

### Comment pulse

- Amyloid may be causal for a subset, but clearing plaques late can fail even when the underlying mechanism matters.
- Groupthink need not be conspiratorial: careers, grants, publication filters, and sunk costs can crowd out vascular and other competing hypotheses.
- Fraud and incorrect science require different remedies — counterpoint: manipulated evidence can entrench an already incomplete model for decades.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Treat Alzheimer’s as a syndrome with interacting protein, vascular, metabolic, environmental, and clearance mechanisms.
- **Impact:** Diversified funding and independent integrity review could reopen neglected targets while preserving valid amyloid work.
- **Watch next:** Earlier blood-test cohorts, combination trials, vascular interventions, clinically meaningful cognition outcomes, retractions, and oversight reform.
