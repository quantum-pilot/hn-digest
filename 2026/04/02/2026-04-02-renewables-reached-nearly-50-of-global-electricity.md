# Renewables reached nearly 50% of global electricity capacity last year

- Score: 238 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47615756) | Link: https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/01/renewables_generated_nearly_half_global_power/

### TL;DR

Renewables added a record 692 GW in 2025, growing 15.5% and reaching 49.4% of global installed power capacity; solar supplied nearly three-quarters of renewable additions. Yet renewables’ share of new capacity slipped from roughly 92% in 2024 to 85.6%, as non-renewable additions nearly doubled, including 100 GW led by mostly coal in China and more US gas tied partly to AI demand. At 5.15 TW, the world remains far below COP28’s 11-TW-plus 2030 goal. HN stressed that capacity differs from actual generation and examined coal as flexible backup.

### Comment pulse

- During 2025’s first three quarters, solar and wind generation grew 635 TWh, exceeding the 603-TWh increase in global electricity demand.
- China may use modern coal plants as paid standby and replace inefficient units — counterpoint: new capacity still sustains fossil dependence.
- Better solar forecasting helped grid operators safely absorb more variable supply once uncertainty became a practical bottleneck.
