# 4TB of voice samples just stolen from 40k AI contractors at Mercor

- Score: 425 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47919630) | Link: https://app.oravys.com/blog/mercor-breach-2026

### TL;DR

ORAVYS reports that an extortion dump attributed to Lapsus$ contains roughly 4TB of Mercor contractor data covering more than 40,000 people, pairing clean voice recordings with government IDs and selfies. That combination could support voice cloning, impersonation, account recovery abuse, or social engineering because the biometric cannot be changed like a password. The article recommends disabling voiceprint authentication, using independent MFA, and establishing family codewords. HN discussion questioned consent and criticized the irony of breach victims uploading suspect audio to another AI company for analysis.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters reframed biometrics as usernames or “forever passwords”: persistent identifiers exposed by ordinary life, not revocable secrets.
- Contractors may have legally consented through terms — counterpoint: economic pressure and buried clauses weaken meaningful informed consent.
- Data frugality was the favored prevention principle: information never collected cannot become breach inventory.

### LLM perspective

- Vendors should justify combining identity documents with voice samples, then separate or delete them quickly.
- Detection services need transparent retention, training exclusions, and independent audits before victims submit more biometrics.
- Banks should treat voice as a public identifier and require possession-based factors for sensitive actions.
