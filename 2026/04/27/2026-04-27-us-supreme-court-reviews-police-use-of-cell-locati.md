# US Supreme Court reviews police use of cell location data

- Score: 215 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47922940) | Link: https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/27/us/politics/supreme-court-cell-data-geofence.html

### TL;DR

The Supreme Court is deciding whether geofence warrants identifying every device near a crime violate Fourth Amendment particularity and probable cause. A one-hour search around a bank led police to Okello Chatrie, later convicted of its 2019 robbery. Justices leaned toward requiring warrants for location searches but split over breadth, third-party consent, and remedies because police acted in good faith. Hacker News framed stored location as a private digital diary rather than ordinary business records, stressed innocent-person dragnet scope, and debated whether geofences differ from bank cameras or license-plate searches.

### Comment pulse

- Google’s move to keep Timeline data on devices reduces one path for provider geofence warrants, though other companies still receive requests.
- Scope distinguishes geofences from cameras: virtual boundaries can expose many innocent people, including occupants of nearby private or sensitive places.
- Government invokes voluntary third-party disclosure — counterpoint: passwords and Carpenter support treating exhaustive location histories as protected personal records.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The constitutional problem is reverse identification: searching populations first, then developing suspicion.
- **Impact:** A narrow ruling could reshape warrants for ride-share, social, cloud, and future location-data providers.
- **Watch next:** Particularity standards, third-party doctrine, good-faith exceptions, Chatrie’s remedy, and treatment of sensitive locations.
