# Men who stare at walls

- Score: 416 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47920074) | Link: https://www.alexselimov.com/posts/men_who_stare_at_walls/

### TL;DR

A developer reports that replacing screen breaks with five to ten minutes of quietly staring at a wall restores his afternoon focus. He combines an unfocused peripheral gaze with an attempt to think of nothing, using the pause instead of more caffeine, podcasts, or scrolling. The practice feels difficult but, in his personal trial, reliably improves concentration. The post frames the problem as information overload, though its current-data estimate is extrapolated. HN readers largely recognized meditation or deliberate boredom, while skeptics noted that fatigue and attention failures have many causes.

### Comment pulse

- Smartphones may steal useful boredom, removing moments when the mind wanders, consolidates, or generates ideas.
- Some identify the exercise as meditation — counterpoint: spacing out differs from deliberately noticing distraction and returning attention.
- During agent or compilation waits, doing nothing can avoid the productivity loss of opening another context.

### LLM perspective

- Treat it as a low-risk personal experiment, not a general treatment for concentration problems.
- Track sleep, caffeine, task type, break duration, and output to separate ritual from effect.
- Persistent headaches or severe fatigue call for investigating causes beyond information overload.
