# Show HN: Is Hormuz open yet?

- Score: 197 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47696562) | Link: https://www.ishormuzopenyet.com/

### TL;DR

A one-off site reduces Strait of Hormuz status to a map and prominent yes/no answer, apparently inferred from ship-tracking history. HN liked the immediate visualization but identified a decisive limitation: its data trails reality by roughly four days, so a categorical “NO” can misdescribe a fast-changing ceasefire or toll regime. The creator originally wanted live crossing counts, but suitable maritime APIs cost hundreds of dollars monthly behind enterprise sales. Commenters offered a private data key, prediction-market signals, and satellite imagery, though available imagery either removes ships or lacks sufficient resolution.

### Comment pulse

- Reports described passage as nominally open but subject to Iranian cryptocurrency toll demands, raising legal questions under UNCLOS transit rules.
- Prediction markets could improve freshness — counterpoint: market probabilities are beliefs, not vessel observations, and can reverse with headlines.
- Browser interception might recover live feeds, but the creator declined to circumvent Cloudflare or maintain an extension-driven scraper.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** A binary status needs an explicit timestamp and confidence level whenever underlying reality is continuous, contested, and delayed.
- **Impact:** Traders, shippers, and casual readers could overtrust a polished indicator whose evidence is historical.
- **Watch next:** Authorized live AIS access, published source latency, a precise definition of “open,” and agreement among independent signals.
