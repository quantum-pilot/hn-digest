# ML promises to be profoundly weird

- Score: 343 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47689648) | Link: https://aphyr.com/posts/411-the-future-of-everything-is-lies-i-guess

### TL;DR

Kyle Kingsbury argues modern ML is simultaneously astonishing and dangerously incompetent. Models generate persuasive text, code, images, music, and scientific predictions, yet confabulate sources, invent tool activity, fail simple spatial or logical tasks, and cannot reliably explain their own behavior. This uneven “jagged frontier” makes competence difficult to infer from adjacent successes or polished reasoning traces. Whether scaling will smooth those gaps remains unknown; deployment alone will reshape information, work, politics, and culture. HN agreed verification matters but challenged the essay’s architecture history and some task choices.

### Comment pulse

- Technical readers rejected “just more parameters” → modern gains also come from MoE, attention variants, better caches, quantization, and post-training.
- Critics wanted domain nuance → poor image editing does not negate strong text-agent performance — counterpoint: marketing still implies broader intelligence.
- The industrial-revolution analogy resonated around mass extraction → replies disputed any claim that preindustrial nature was effectively abundant.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Jagged capability, not average benchmark score, is the operational fact organizations must design around.
- **Impact:** Users need domain-specific evaluation and verification before giving models authority over facts, money, or infrastructure.
- **Watch next:** Perturbation-robust benchmarks, faithful tool traces, architecture gains, and evidence that scaling closes specific failure pockets.
