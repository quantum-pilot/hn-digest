# Laws of Software Engineering

- Score: 794 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47847179) | Link: https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com

### TL;DR

The site catalogs 56 software-engineering “laws” across architecture, teams, planning, quality, scale, design, and decisions, from Conway, Hyrum, Brooks, and Gall through CAP, YAGNI, DRY, KISS, Goodhart, Amdahl, and common cognitive biases. Each card offers a terse principle and deeper page, with filters by category and experience level. Hacker News treated the collection chiefly as a reminder that aphorisms conflict and lose context, debating Knuth’s “premature optimization” line, proposing Curly’s one-meaning rule, and mocking what commenters saw as AI-generated styling.

### Comment pulse

- Knuth’s warning targets unmeasured micro-optimization, commenters argued — counterpoint: architecture-level performance choices can become prohibitively expensive to reverse.
- The laws can contradict: Postel’s permissiveness enlarges the observable surface that Hyrum says users will eventually depend upon.
- KISS and YAGNI earned senior-engineer support, while DRY and SOLID were criticized for encouraging abstractions beyond their useful context.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Treat the catalog as prompts for judgment, not rules; the missing skill is choosing which principle yields to context.
- **Impact:** Shared vocabulary can accelerate design reviews, but slogan-level application can legitimize overengineering, brittle interfaces, or neglected performance.
- **Watch next:** Expanded historical context, explicit tensions between laws, missing principles, and whether the companion book adds evidence beyond aphorisms.
