# Neanderthals survived on a knife's edge for 350k years

- Score: 228 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47595572) | Link: https://www.science.org/content/article/neanderthals-survived-knife-s-edge-350-000-years

### TL;DR

Two ancient-DNA studies portray Neanderthals as a sparse metapopulation: perhaps only a few thousand breeding individuals across Eurasia, divided into isolated groups of dozens that accumulated mutations and diverged rapidly. European mitochondrial diversity collapsed during the 75,000–65,000-year ice age, when southwestern refuges preserved one surviving maternal lineage; recolonization never restored effective population size. Around 45,000 years ago, climate instability and incoming larger modern-human populations preceded a second crash and disappearance by roughly 42,000 years ago. HN cautioned that population estimates are uncertain and calling 400,000-year-old ancestors “Neanderthals” oversimplifies contested taxonomy.

### Comment pulse

- Local groups could be highly inbred while humanity across its full ancient range retained more genetic diversity than people have today.
- Long inbreeding may purge harmful variants — counterpoint: lost diversity weakens adaptation to environmental, demographic, and disease shocks.
- Known interbreeding with modern humans complicates the image of tiny, shy, far-flung groups, showing that contact still occurred.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Longevity did not imply demographic security; stable adaptation can coexist with persistent vulnerability to abrupt change.
- **Impact:** Tiny bone fragments and improved genomics let researchers reconstruct population structure previously invisible in sparse fossils.
- **Watch next:** More nuclear genomes, broader regional sampling, bottleneck dating, admixture evidence, and models separating climate from competition.
