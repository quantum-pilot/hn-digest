# 20 years on AWS and never not my job

- Score: 240 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47727711) | Link: https://www.daemonology.net/blog/2026-04-11-20-years-on-AWS-and-never-not-my-job.html

### TL;DR

Colin Percival recounts two decades shaping AWS from outside Amazon: securing early APIs, advancing IAM concepts, diagnosing infrastructure faults, warning about Xen side channels and metadata-service credentials, and spending years making FreeBSD run reliably on EC2. His feedback sometimes took years to land, yet relationships with internal engineers converted persistence into platform changes. After FreeBSD release leadership crowded out volunteer EC2 work, Amazon began funding ten hours weekly. Commenters debated whether this represents healthy dependency-driven open-source stewardship or an extreme unpaid-labor asymmetry benefiting a trillion-dollar hyperscaler.

### Comment pulse

- Critics saw AWS Heroes as unpaid corporate R&D, arguing hyperscalers have undermined permissive open-source economics and encouraged source-available licensing.
- Percival’s work directly served Tarsnap and FreeBSD — counterpoint: shared dependency maintenance can be healthier than waiting for Amazon to prioritize niches.
- A community member recalled giving Percival the unused “scrypt” project name, illustrating valuable cooperation whose returns cannot always be priced.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Durable technical influence came from expertise, persistence, public evidence, and trusted internal allies rather than formal authority.
- **Impact:** FreeBSD users gained cloud support, while AWS externalized years of specialized platform and security stewardship.
