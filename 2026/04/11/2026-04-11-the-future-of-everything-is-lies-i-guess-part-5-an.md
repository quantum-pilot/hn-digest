# The future of everything is lies, I guess – Part 5: Annoyances

- Score: 198 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47730981) | Link: https://aphyr.com/posts/415-the-future-of-everything-is-lies-i-guess-annoyances

### TL;DR

Aphyr predicts LLMs will make bureaucracy cheaper for institutions and more exhausting for everyone else. Support bots will feign empathy but lack authority, forcing customers to argue with systems optimized to reduce tickets; high-value customers may retain humans. Models in insurance, policing, pricing, and employment will cloak bias while diffusing responsibility across vendors and operators. In “agentic commerce,” buyer and seller bots invite attacks, dark patterns, unintended purchases, fraud, and disputes. Competitive pressure could force adoption, leaving people paying AI firms to manage complexity those firms created.

### Comment pulse

- Readers expect automated support but reject autonomous purchasing; approving every transaction preserves a cheap safety barrier against injection.
- Simple mainstream questions already benefit from AI. — counterpoint: a plausible answer becomes dangerous when professionals skip verification in high-stakes settings.
- Suppressing tickets cuts costs but destroys customer-service early warning, especially where monopolies eliminate exit as feedback.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The harm comes less from conversation quality than asymmetrical authority: bots can deny, price, and delay without accountability.
- **Impact:** Consumers face more procedural labor; frontline staff lose discretion; firms lose unfiltered evidence of systemic failure.
- **Watch next:** Human-escalation rights, explanation duties, purchase authorization standards, appeal logs, liability rules, and chatbot outcome audits.
