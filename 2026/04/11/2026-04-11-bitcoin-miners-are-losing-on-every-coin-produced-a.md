# Bitcoin miners are losing on every coin produced as difficulty drops

- Score: 211 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47730370) | Link: https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/22/bitcoin-miners-are-losing-usd19-000-on-every-btc-produced-as-difficulty-drops-7-8

### TL;DR

Checkonchain’s model estimated average Bitcoin production at $88,000 versus a $69,200 market price in mid-March, implying a roughly $19,000, or 21%, loss per coin. Higher energy prices, oil above $100, and war-related disruption helped push hashrate to about 920 EH/s, block times to 12:36, and difficulty down 7.76%. CoinDesk says stressed miners sell reserves and diversify into AI computing while difficulty eventually restores equilibrium. Commenters stressed that the drop is a consequence, costs vary widely, and miners may continue when revenue covers marginal power despite unrecovered equipment costs.

### Comment pulse

- Bitcoin’s feedback loop removes miners and lowers difficulty; commenters said the economically important variable is the painful adjustment lag.
- A single average-cost estimate obscures regional electricity prices and operator efficiency — counterpoint: capital losses can coexist with profitable continued operation.
- Critics argued proof-of-work energy demand scales with network value, limiting monetary-scale adoption regardless of improvements in electricity efficiency.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The headline reverses causality: falling price and rising costs force exits; lower difficulty is the protocol’s delayed response.
- **Impact:** High-cost miners face shutdowns or asset sales, while efficient operators gain share and data-center diversification accelerates.
