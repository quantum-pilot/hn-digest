# I wrote to Flock's privacy contact to opt out of their domestic spying program

- Score: 449 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47768813) | Link: https://honeypot.net/2026/04/14/i-wrote-to-flocks-privacy.html

### TL;DR

A California resident asked Flock Safety to delete data about him and his vehicle under the CCPA and prevent future collection. Flock refused, saying its camera customers own and control the data while it serves only as processor, so requests must go to deploying organizations. Flock says license-plate readers capture visible vehicle details, it cannot sell the data, and retention defaults to 30 days. The author disputes that interpretation and may seek counsel. Commenters split over whether Flock is a neutral host or controls enough infrastructure to owe privacy duties.

### Comment pulse

- Supporters compared Flock to cloud storage: customers control records and providers cannot delete them unilaterally — counterpoint: infrastructure may give Flock duties beyond custody.
- Critics argued turnkey surveillance at Flock’s scale should include equally turnkey deletion handling and questioned how hands-off the company truly is.
- Some saw a service-provider exemption; others suggested data-broker rules or litigation could test who owns license-plate, time, and location records.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Flock’s legal posture shifts privacy enforcement from one vendor to an unknown, changing network of public and private customers.
- **Impact:** Even valid deletion rights become impractical if residents must identify every camera owner that might have recorded them.
- **Watch next:** Regulatory controller rulings, customer directories, standardized request routing, retention exceptions, and Flock’s possible data-broker status.
