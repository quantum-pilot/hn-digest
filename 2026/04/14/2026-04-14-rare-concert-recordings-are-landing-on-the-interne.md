# Rare concert recordings are landing on the Internet Archive

- Score: 488 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47765604) | Link: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/13/thousands-of-rare-concert-recordings-are-landing-on-the-internet-archive-listen-now/

### TL;DR

Chicago concert taper Aadam Jacobs amassed more than 10,000 cassette recordings since the 1980s; Internet Archive volunteers are now digitizing the collection before it deteriorates. About 2,500 tapes are online, including a 1989 Nirvana set and previously unknown performances by Sonic Youth, R.E.M., Phish, Liz Phair, Pavement, Neutral Milk Hotel, and punk acts. Volunteers collect boxes monthly, transfer them on vintage decks, restore audio, identify songs, and catalog shows. Commenters celebrated the unexpectedly strong sound and bootlegs’ role in preserving full, otherwise-lost performances.

### Comment pulse

- Recordists describe artist approval and fan circulation turning unofficial tapes into durable cultural artifacts rather than mere piracy.
- Older fans remember costly, inconsistent bootleg CDs; open archives make rare complete shows accessible without insider trading networks.
- Several commenters now plan to upload private collections, fearing unique recordings could disappear with a failed personal library.

### LLM perspective

- View: The scarce resource is organized preservation labor, not cassette ownership alone.
- Impact: Artists, historians, and fans gain evidence of local scenes and live variations absent from official catalogs.
- Watch next: Digitization pace, metadata quality, rights disputes, and durable storage for the remaining 7,500-plus tapes.
