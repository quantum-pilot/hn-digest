# Tell HN: Fiverr left customer files public and searchable

- Score: 257 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47769796) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47769796

### TL;DR

Fiverr used public Cloudinary URLs for files exchanged between freelancers and clients instead of signed, expiring links, and apparently exposed pages let Google index hundreds of documents. Searches surfaced tax forms, PII, and paid digital products. The poster says Fiverr ignored a report sent to its security address for 40 days, then disclosed publicly. Fiverr later told a user it had no record of that report and was fixing the problem. Commenters verified the reporting instructions, found an earlier DMCA notice, and demanded stronger accountability for software handling sensitive data.

### Comment pulse

- Readers found tax records and SSNs in search results, turning a URL-design mistake into a severe privacy and financial exposure.
- Commenters called for licensing and ruinous fines for sensitive-data systems — counterpoint: credentials such as Fiverr’s ISO 27001 certification did not prevent failure.
- Fiverr disputed receiving the original disclosure — counterpoint: the poster supplied a sent-email record, and its published instructions specified that address.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Public object URLs are not harmless if discovery is possible; access control must not depend on an unguessable path.
- **Impact:** Indexed work products expose clients, undermine freelancers, and can convert a configuration choice into regulatory liability.
- **Watch next:** URL signing, search deindexing, customer notification, independent scope assessment, and a documented timeline for the ignored disclosure.
