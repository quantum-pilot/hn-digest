# Wit, unker, Git: The lost medieval pronouns of English intimacy

- Score: 176 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47701572) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20260408-the-extinct-english-words-for-just-the-two-of-us

### TL;DR

Old English distinguished two people with dual pronouns: `wit` (“we two”), `git` (“you two”), and forms such as `uncer` or `unker` (“our, for two”). Poetry used them for intimacy and Beowulf for comradeship; the category survived into Middle English before disappearing around the 13th Century, likely because ordinary plurals were sufficient. The broader history shows pronouns changing through contact and social convention: Norse supplied `they`, Norman-influenced politeness helped `you` displace `thou`, and singular `they` appears by the 14th Century. HN readers debated whether losing the dual also lost emotional precision.

### Comment pulse

- `Wit` the pronoun is unrelated to mental ability, so `halfwit` does not descend from the dual form.
- Commenters noted English also shed case marking and a singular/plural second-person split; `thou` was originally informal, despite sounding formal now.
- Some felt `uncer giedd` carries intimacy that “song of the two of us” loses — counterpoint: modern “we two” remains concise and familiar.
