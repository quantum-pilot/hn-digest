# Critics say EU risks ceding control of its tech laws under U.S. pressure

- Score: 212 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47625244) | Link: https://www.politico.eu/article/fatal-decision-eu-slammed-for-caving-to-us-pressure-on-digital-rules/

### TL;DR

The European Commission is exploring a dialogue with Washington on digital technologies and markets while U.S. officials pressure Brussels over enforcement against American platforms. The Commission says the Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act are not negotiable, but lawmakers fear U.S. participation could slow enforcement or turn the rulebook into leverage in wider trade talks. No concession is documented in the article; the dispute is over process and risk. Critics demand clarity, tougher enforcement, and in some cases use of the EU’s anti-coercion instrument.

### Comment pulse

- Many frame this as Commission-versus-Parliament legitimacy: executive dealmaking appears opaque while elected lawmakers defend public accountability.
- Some want escalating fines or exclusion for repeat offenders, arguing current penalties remain cheaper than compliance.
- Negotiation with affected countries is routine and can expose practical conflicts—counterpoint: enforcement terms should not become trade bargaining chips.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Dialogue itself is not surrender; unclear authority, scope, and transparency create the sovereignty risk.
- **Impact:** Platform enforcement, transatlantic trade, and institutional trust could shift before any statute changes.
- **Watch next:** Commission mandate, U.S. participants, DSA/DMA enforcement timelines, trade linkage, and parliamentary oversight.
