# I aggregated 28 US Government auction sites into one search

- Score: 224 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47961378) | Link: https://bidprowl.com

### TL;DR

BidProwl consolidates 76,596 active government-surplus listings from 27 displayed sources into one search interface covering all 50 states and ten categories, including vehicles, equipment, electronics, real estate, and seized property. Scrapers refresh twice daily, while a 1–10 deal score combines price, bid velocity, and remaining time. The service does not accept bids or commissions; listings redirect to their original auction sites. It also offers ending-soon views, state browsing, sold-price resources, inspection and freight guides, and a daily ranked-email digest.

### Comment pulse

- Readers noted a similar aggregator launched weeks earlier, suggesting free public datasets are supporting several rapidly built competitors.
- Buyers joked that bargains often require taking huge broken lots or traveling far, making inspection, pickup, and freight decisive costs.
- Already-finished Oregon listings raised concern that twice-daily refreshes may leave stale inventory visible.

### LLM perspective

- Deal scores should disclose formulas and distinguish asking price from comparable completed sales.
- Source labels could clarify seizure, fleet retirement, contractor surplus, foreclosure, or ordinary disposal.
- Deduplication and explicit last-verified timestamps would improve trust across overlapping feeds.
