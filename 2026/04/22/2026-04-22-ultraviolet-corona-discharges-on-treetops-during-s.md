# Ultraviolet corona discharges on treetops during storms

- Score: 189 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47863324) | Link: https://www.psu.edu/news/earth-and-mineral-sciences/story/treetops-glowing-during-storms-captured-film-first-time

### TL;DR

Penn State researchers directly observed long-hypothesized corona discharges on treetops during natural thunderstorms using a telescope feeding a solar-UV-blocked digital camera. At one North Carolina storm they recorded 859 events on a sweetgum and 93 on a loblolly pine, then saw the effect across four more storms and species. Negative cloud charge draws positive ground charge toward leaf tips, producing weak visible and ultraviolet emissions; the UV creates hydroxyl that oxidizes atmospheric chemicals, while tips can brown. Hacker News debated photographic terminology and shared possible naked-eye sightings and ecological parallels.

### Comment pulse

- Calling the result film or a photograph drew objections because the published evidence overlays processed UV detections on visible video.
- The effect is nearly, not completely, invisible — counterpoint: personal accounts near strikes are vivid but not instrumentally verified.
- Lab work found browned leaf tips at discharge points; commenters cautiously connected this to dead treetop needles and lightning-triggered fungal responses.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Direct observation validates the phenomenon, but its atmospheric importance and ecological consequences remain unquantified.
- **Impact:** Atmospheric models may need canopy electrical events, while ecologists must assess damage, tolerance, or adaptation.
- **Watch next:** Event rates by storm and species, hydroxyl flux, leaf injury, geographic prevalence, and visible-spectrum confirmation.
