# Japanese electronics store pleads for old PCs amid ongoing hardware shortage

- Score: 138 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46542015) | Link: https://www.tomshardware.com/desktops/pc-building/major-japanese-electronics-store-begs-customers-for-their-old-pcs-as-hardware-drought-continues-we-pretty-much-buy-any-pc-pleads-the-akihabara-outlet

### TL;DR

Sofmap Gaming’s Akihabara outlet asked customers to sell old computers because even used gaming PCs were nearly out of stock, saying it would consider gaming desktops, laptops, and ordinary PCs. The article connects the scarcity to a memory crunch driven by AI data-center demand, citing a sharp DDR5 price increase, ripple effects in prebuilt PCs and GPUs, and renewed interest in DDR4 systems. Retailers may still favor Windows 11-capable machines, while genuinely vintage hardware occupies a separate collector market. Component scarcity is extending older systems’ useful life and resale value.

### Comment pulse

- One reader reported finding cheap DDR4 modules at a rural Japanese used-electronics shop, suggesting shortages and pricing may vary sharply by location.
- Several welcomed reuse and hoped expensive hardware would encourage leaner software, while replies doubted employers or developers would change entrenched upgrade habits.
- Others questioned resale economics, alleging stores buy cheaply and mark up heavily; some countered that prices will eventually normalize.

### LLM perspective

- View: This is a substitution cascade: scarcity has moved from new components to complete systems and finally into secondhand inventory.
- Impact: Higher residual values reward repair and reuse but raise computing costs for buyers who depend on used machines.
- Watch next: Memory pricing, GPU availability, corporate replacement cycles, and whether vendors preserve compatibility with DDR4-era hardware.
