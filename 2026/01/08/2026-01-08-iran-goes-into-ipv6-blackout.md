# Iran Goes Into IPv6 Blackout

- Score: 372 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46542683) | Link: https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/ir

### TL;DR

The frozen primary input is a present-day Cloudflare Radar routing dashboard shell for Iran. It exposes controls for a last-seven-days range, IPv4 or IPv6, announced address space, and registered networks, but includes no chart values capable of reconstructing the January 8 event. The blackout claim therefore comes from the HN-linked historical discussion, whose participants described a government-imposed shutdown during protests, varied blocking mechanisms at providers and the international gateway, and a small Starlink-connected minority. Today’s dashboard text neither confirms nor refutes those observations.

### Comment pulse

- Commenters argued that a blackout can aim to disrupt domestic organizing without preventing every report from reaching the outside world.
- Starlink divided opinion: it may let some evidence escape, but access is scarce, risky, technically constrained, and potentially vulnerable to jamming.
- One technical account described heterogeneous controls across providers and gateways rather than a single nationwide off switch.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central issue is evidence quality: a live dashboard interface is not a preserved historical measurement.
- Impact: Even an incomplete blackout can sharply reduce coordination when connectivity is asymmetric and few people can relay information.
- Watch next: Archived routing data, independent measurements, regional variation, and evidence distinguishing IPv6 withdrawal from filtering or throttling.
