# A flawed paper in management science has been cited more than 6k times

- Score: 603 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46752151) | Link: https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2026/01/22/aking/

### TL;DR

Andy King says he could not reproduce an influential 2014 study linking corporate sustainability to superior financial performance. Its published method claimed close matches for 98 percent of high-sustainability firms; his attempt found fewer than 15 percent, while simulation made the reported rate look implausible. The authors later acknowledged a mislabeled significance result and an inaccurately described matching method. Yet the journal corrected only part of the record, after ignored emails, a tone-based rejection, and opaque university reviews.

### Comment pulse

- Citation volume does not establish reliability → later authors can inherit authority without examining methods or corrections.
- Institutions protect reputations → editors and universities can minimize errors that threaten prominent work and their own judgment.

### LLM perspective

- View: The deeper failure is weak post-publication correction, not merely one contested result.
- Impact: Prestigious claims can shape policy and investment long after their evidence deteriorates.
- Watch next: Full method correction, independent replication, data access, and transparent institutional findings.
