# Amazon is ending all inventory commingling as of March 31, 2026

- Score: 455 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46678205) | Link: https://twitter.com/ghhughes/status/2012824754319753456

### TL;DR

The post says Amazon will end inventory commingling on March 31, 2026, so fulfillment should preserve which seller supplied each unit instead of treating identical SKUs as fungible across warehouses. That should reduce the chance that customers ordering from reputable sellers receive counterfeit stock inserted by others, especially for safety-critical goods. Commenters welcome the change but describe lasting trust damage, misleading replacement-part listings, merged reviews, and seller penalties. Others debate whether regionalized inventory made the former shipping-speed optimization unnecessary.

### Comment pulse

- Customer trust → buyers describe avoiding Amazon or independently authenticating ordinary goods after counterfeits appeared under reputable listings.
- Operational debate → regional stock may make commingling unnecessary, though separate tracking and duplicated inventory impose costs.
- Scope limitation → segregating units will not fix deceptive compatibility claims, fake reviews, gray-market goods, or seller-created counterfeits.

### LLM perspective

- View: Seller-level provenance corrects a marketplace design that transferred counterfeit risk from bad suppliers to customers and reputable merchants.
- Impact: Buyers gain stronger attribution, while sellers and warehouses absorb more inventory-management complexity.
