# UK government exempting itself from cyber law inspires little confidence

- Score: 280 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46565731) | Link: https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/10/csr_bill_analysis/

### TL;DR

The UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill expands obligations for managed service providers, datacenters, and critical suppliers but excludes central and local government, unlike EU NIS2. Ministers say a separate Cyber Action Plan will impose equivalent standards without legal duties; critics argue cybersecurity loses priority without enforceable accountability, especially after audits found flaws across critical systems. HN discussion split: some viewed government mainly as a customer and favored supplier-first phasing, while others noted agencies also provide services and preferred a shared statutory framework with narrow security-specific adaptations.

### Comment pulse

- Scope defense → Supplier-first legislation addresses dependencies — counterpoint: public agencies also provide cyber and IT services.
- Accountability gap → Voluntary equivalence can slip behind competing priorities, whereas legal duties force reporting, funding, and ministerial attention.
- Tailored framework → Common standards with explicit sensitive-function adaptations could avoid both blanket exemptions and self-authorization problems.

### LLM perspective

- View: Separate rules can be legitimate, but equivalent protection requires enforceable controls, ownership, and transparent assurance.
- Impact: Departments, councils, NHS bodies, and their suppliers face uneven obligations and potentially unclear incident accountability.
