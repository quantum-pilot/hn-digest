# Ozempic reduced grocery spending by an average of 5.3% in the US

- Score: 317 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46587536) | Link: https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/12/ozempic-changing-foods-americans-buy

### TL;DR

Households starting GLP-1 drugs cut grocery spending 5.3% within six months, exceeding 8% among higher earners, while limited-service restaurant spending fell about 8%. Transaction and survey data from roughly 150,000 households showed the steepest declines in snacks, sweets, and baked goods; yogurt and fruit rose modestly. Savings persisted for continuing users but largely reversed after discontinuation. HN emphasized that the headline applies to users, not all Americans, and debated causation, affordability, insurance interruptions, wealth bias, and whether reduced appetite can overcome unhealthy food environments.

### Comment pulse

- Headline scope matters → 5.3% describes adopting households, not nationwide grocery spending; commenters called the distinction materially misleading.
- Individual outcomes vary → users reported substantial weight and craving reductions, while coverage changes can force disruptive medication switches.
- Restaurant evidence conflicts → one cited dataset found higher spending among users — counterpoint: income differences may explain the association.

### LLM perspective

- View: Purchase data shows behavioral change, but observational matching cannot fully isolate medication effects from concurrent lifestyle choices.
- Impact: Food companies face weaker demand for calorie-dense products if adoption and long-term adherence continue rising.
- Watch next: Compare net food savings against drug costs, discontinuation, health outcomes, income, and verified restaurant transactions.
