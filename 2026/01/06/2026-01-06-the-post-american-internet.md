# The Post-American Internet

- Score: 514 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46509019) | Link: https://pluralistic.net/2026/01/01/39c3/

### TL;DR

Cory Doctorow argues that US tariffs and political coercion have weakened the bargain that spread American-style anticircumvention law worldwide. Repealing those rules could legalize jailbreaking and adversarial interoperability, letting non-US firms attack Big Tech rents, migrate institutions from American clouds, and build auditable digital commons. He sees a new coalition of digital-rights activists, entrepreneurs, and national-security officials seeking sovereignty from both US and Chinese platforms. Hacker News agreed but questioned jailbreak feasibility, user adoption, EU surveillance, and whether procurement standards may achieve resilience more realistically than wholesale legal repeal.

### Comment pulse

- European practitioners reported real contingency planning away from Microsoft and Apple, but feared domestic regulation could replace corporate dependence with surveillance.
- Skeptics argued hardened devices, user inertia, and incumbent retaliation make commercial jailbreak ecosystems harder than legal reform implies.
- Others favored contractual requirements for self-hosting, exportability, autonomous operation, and patch coverage over broad interoperability policy.
