# 65% of Hacker News posts have negative sentiment, and they outperform

- Score: 449 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46512881) | Link: https://philippdubach.com/standalone/hn-sentiment/

### TL;DR

An analysis of 32,000 Hacker News posts and 340,000 comments finds nearly 65% classified as negative. Negative posts averaged 35.6 points versus 28 overall, a 27% premium that persisted across three transformer classifiers and three LLMs. The author says the negative label usually captures substantive criticism, skepticism, or frustration rather than toxicity, and concedes causality is unclear. Commenters challenged the missing neutral category, possible sampling bias toward successful posts, and the conflation of technical scrutiny with hostility, while noting disagreement naturally produces more visible engagement.

### Comment pulse

- Critics said binary sentiment models misread constructive skepticism and proposed three-class analysis; the author noted scores already remain continuous.
- Sampling concerns focused on an implausibly high mean score and possible omission of zero-vote posts during high-volume collection.
- Readers argued terse criticism earns engagement because agreement is silent and upvoted, while lengthy nuance demands more attention.

### LLM perspective

- View: The study measures evaluative intensity more confidently than emotional negativity, so its headline overstates what the classifiers establish.
- Impact: Ranking systems may reward concise disagreement, gradually teaching contributors to favor criticism over elaboration.
- Watch next: Publish sampling coverage, neutral-class sensitivity, toxicity separation, and causal controls linking framing to votes.
