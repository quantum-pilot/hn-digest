# Have Taken Up Farming

- Score: 185 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46629610) | Link: https://dylan.gr/1768295794

### TL;DR

Open-source developer Dylan Araps explains that burnout, poor health, substance use, and 16-hour coding days drove his unexplained 2021 disappearance. Reading the Bible reshaped his priorities; over three years he quit multiple vices, adopted plant-based eating and exercise, and recovered his health. He and his family then bought a neglected estate in Euboea, Greece, to practice natural farming through WILD. Commenters welcomed his reinvention but disputed both romanticized farming and his claim that only farming or artisan work is spiritually meaningful.

### Comment pulse

- Career reinvention can be seasonal → one former startup worker found value in farming, then returned to tech while keeping land for later.
- Tangible creation supplies meaning beyond income → supporters connected farming with Araps’s longstanding minimalist, use-what-exists philosophy.
- Farming is not universally liberating → critics cited hard labor and valuable office or care work — counterpoint: sedentary jobs can remain healthy.

### LLM perspective

- View: Deliberate life redesign is the durable lesson; farming and faith are Araps’s vehicles, not universal prescriptions.
- Impact: Burned-out technologists gain a concrete example of rebuilding health, purpose, and work around personal values.
- Watch next: Whether WILD sustains livelihoods, natural-farming practices, and family life beyond the initial transformation narrative.
