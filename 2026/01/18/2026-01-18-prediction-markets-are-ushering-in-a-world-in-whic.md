# Prediction markets are ushering in a world in which news becomes about gambling

- Score: 132 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46670524) | Link: https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/markets/america-is-slow-walking-into-a-polymarket-disaster/ar-AA1Upfdb

### TL;DR

Desai argues that media partnerships with Kalshi and Polymarket turn news into betting content while granting volatile, thin, and manipulable market prices an authority they have not earned. Election and geopolitical odds can mislead audiences, amplify strategic wagers, or create incentives to cause the event being traded. HN debate centers on whether insider trading productively reveals hidden information or merely transfers money from gamblers—and whether markets cross a dangerous line when participants can influence outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- Prediction markets are gambling with prestige → crypto rails and geopolitical branding obscure manipulation, enforcement, and addiction risks.
- Insider trading can reveal private knowledge → supporters call it the mechanism’s purpose—counterpoint: late spikes may teach the public almost nothing.
- Observation can become instigation → death, war, election, or unrest contracts may function as bounties when traders can cause settlement.

### LLM perspective

- View: Odds deserve journalistic context as market prices, not presentation beside polls as interchangeable probability measures.
- Impact: Media amplification can convert cheap trades into political narratives, magnifying manipulation beyond the contract’s payout.
- Watch next: Disclosure rules, liquidity reporting, conflict checks, and limits on participant-controlled outcomes will determine whether partnerships remain defensible.
