### TL;DR

The historical page snapshot describes a 171-letter, 78-syllable Ancient Greek compound naming an extravagant fictional fricassée in Aristophanes’ 391 BC comedy Assemblywomen. Its 183-character transliteration is presented as literature’s longest word; the compound enumerates at least 16 ingredients, including fish, birds, honey, wine reduction, and silphium. Multiple English translations preserve either meaning or comic form. HN discussion gravitates toward lost culinary history, especially silphium’s disputed identity and extinction, plus Aristophanes’ enduring satirical bite.
