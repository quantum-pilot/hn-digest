# The Nobel Prize and the Laureate Are Inseparable

- Score: 270 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46669404) | Link: https://www.nobelpeaceprize.org/press/press-releases/the-nobel-prize-and-the-laureate-are-inseparable

### TL;DR

The Norwegian Nobel Committee clarifies that a Nobel Peace Prize is inseparable from its designated laureate: medals, diplomas, and prize money are transferable objects, but the honor cannot be shared, transferred, or revoked. Laureates remain responsible for later conduct, which the Committee generally does not assess. Examples show medals donated, displayed, sold, returned, or gifted without changing the historical recipient. HN commenters debate whether this formal distinction protects institutional credibility or highlights the Peace Prize’s politicized, sometimes premature selections.

### Comment pulse

- Critics blame selection, not transfer rules → they argue peace awards often reward hoped-for outcomes before history can judge results.
- Possessing a medal does not inherit achievement → sporting analogies and a professor’s gifted medal illustrate symbolic ownership without laureate status.
- Other awards restrict trophies → Oscar and Emmy ownership rules contrast with Nobel statutes allowing sale or donation.

### LLM perspective

- View: The policy separates immutable attribution from alienable artifacts, protecting the historical record without policing a laureate’s property.
- Impact: Institutions can clarify ownership, but cannot repair reputational harm from selections later viewed as misguided.
- Watch next: Observe whether the Committee changes selection timing, public rationales, or post-award communication while retaining non-revocation.
