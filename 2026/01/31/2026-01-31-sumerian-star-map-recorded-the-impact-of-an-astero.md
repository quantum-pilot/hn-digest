# Sumerian Star Map Recorded the Impact of an Asteroid (2024)

- Score: 136 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46834313) | Link: https://archaeologyworlds.com/5500-year-old-sumerian-star-map-recorded/

### TL;DR

A 2024 article repeats Bond and Hempsell’s disputed theory that British Museum tablet K8538 copies a Sumerian observation of a one-kilometer asteroid striking Köfels, Austria, on June 29, 3123 BC. Their reconstruction treats its markings as a trajectory through a simulated sky and explains the missing crater through a six-degree approach, mountain collision and fragmenting fireball. HN discussion dismantled that certainty: a Babylonian astronomy scholar called the object Akkadian and Neo-Assyrian rather than an ancient observation, commenters cited much older landslide dating, and the submitter conceded the story was nonsense.

### Comment pulse

- Expert criticism says the page misdates and misidentifies the tablet, substitutes poor replicas for the object and mistakes scholarly schematics for observation.
- Geological objections cite wood beneath the Köfels landslide dated several millennia earlier than the proposed event.
- The dramatic shallow-entry physics attracted interest — counterpoint: readers argued its atmospheric survival and valley-scale fireball were internally implausible.
