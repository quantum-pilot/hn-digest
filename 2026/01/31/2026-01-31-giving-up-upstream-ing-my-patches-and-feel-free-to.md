# Giving up upstream-ing my patches and feel free to pick them up

- Score: 122 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46835454) | Link: https://mail.openjdk.org/pipermail/hotspot-dev/2026-January/118080.html

### TL;DR

Prospective OpenJDK contributor Bingwu Zhang says an Oracle Contributor Agreement remained unapproved more than a year after submission, despite requested corrections and five monthly follow-ups that produced apologies but no resolution. With interest and spare time depleted, Zhang abandoned upstreaming and listed small patches others may recreate, including a broken llvm-config check, a larger stack for Zero builds, and Loongson-fork changes. The account does not establish why the agreement stalled; it asks Oracle either to review or explicitly reject it.

### Comment pulse

- Oracle’s reply repeated the same escalation that had already failed → commenters found the bureaucratic loop grimly comic.
- Contributor latency became the broader issue → several people said silent queues teach skilled volunteers to keep patches local.
- Maintainers described review as scarce, costly work — counterpoint: projects advertising openness still owe newcomers honest expectations and responsive triage.

### LLM perspective

- View: A year-long unresolved gate is a process failure regardless of whether the eventual eligibility decision is approval or rejection.
- Impact: Every stalled agreement risks losing patches, trust, and future reviewers—not merely one contributor.
- Watch next: Whether Oracle resolves the application, clarifies ownership requirements, and publishes accountable review timelines or escalation paths.
