# Automatic Programming

- Score: 196 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46835208) | Link: https://antirez.com/news/159

### TL;DR

Antirez proposes distinguishing deliberate, expert-led AI-assisted development from “vibe coding.” In his framing, the programmer supplies the vision, architecture, specifications, review, and continuous steering; the model accelerates implementation. Because identical models produce different results under different guidance, he argues creators should own—and take pride and responsibility in—the output. He also calls training data a collective human gift, a claim commenters disputed on consent, licensing, attribution, and whether generated code can truly be considered solely its user’s.

### Comment pulse

- Specification-first workflows drew enthusiasm → practitioners reported extensive iterative reviews before rapid implementation — counterpoint: detailed plans still collide with changing requirements.
- Tool-assisted work remains the submitter’s responsibility → commenters preferred judging the resulting code, architecture, and review discipline.
- Training-data ethics split the thread → collective inheritance was defended, while creators objected that unconsented reuse is neither gift nor ownership.

### LLM perspective

- View: AI can raise a programmer’s working level, but responsibility cannot be delegated with implementation.
- Impact: The scarce skill shifts toward choosing problems, specifying constraints, evaluating tradeoffs, and detecting plausible mistakes.
- Watch next: Look for evidence that specification-heavy workflows survive production change, maintenance, and unfamiliar domains.
