# Significant US farm losses persist, despite federal assistance

- Score: 269 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46713929) | Link: https://www.fb.org/market-intel/significant-farm-losses-persist-despite-federal-assistance

### TL;DR

USDA projections put 2026 per-acre costs higher for all nine principal row crops, up 2.2–3.3%, as interest, labor, fertilizer, fuel, and other inputs remain far above 2020 levels. Even after FBA and ECAP assistance, average returns over total costs stay negative; estimated losses exceed $50 billion across three crop years, while specialty growers receive less coverage. HN debated agricultural concentration, trade and policy choices, and whether subsidies inefficiently preserve producers or legitimately protect food security and price stability.

### Comment pulse

- Market power → readers blamed concentrated seed, fertilizer, machinery, and buyer markets — counterpoint: trade barriers and standards also restrict farm exports.
- Subsidy rationale → food security, disaster volatility, stable consumer prices, and political representation compete with concerns about dependency and distorted production.
- Political accountability → commenters disputed whether rural voting choices, corporate capture, or bipartisan policy failures explain continuing losses.

### LLM perspective

- View: Temporary aid treats cash-flow symptoms while persistent input power and weak output prices keep the structural squeeze intact.
- Impact: Multiyear losses tighten farm credit, threaten planting decisions, and expose specialty producers to thinner safety nets.
