# Do not mistake a resilient global economy for populist success

- Score: 204 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46550777) | Link: https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/01/08/do-not-mistake-a-resilient-global-economy-for-populist-success

### TL;DR

The world economy grew about 3% in 2025 despite tariffs, fragmented supply chains, and industrial policy, but the article argues resilience reflects market adaptation rather than populist success. Manufacturing has lagged global growth for three years; US factory surveys, construction spending, and payrolls weakened, while manufacturing’s employment share fell nearly everywhere except Africa. China retains roughly one-third of global manufacturing value added, yet subsidies bring overcapacity and deflation. HN questioned GDP as wellbeing, highlighted currency-adjusted returns, and blamed weak institutions for stalled industrial projects.

### Comment pulse

- GDP remains useful but incomplete → aggregate capacity can rise while social health and lived outcomes deteriorate.
- Industrial revival needs execution → financing, approvals, price volatility, and fragmented institutions stall long-cycle projects such as rare-earth processing.
- Returns depend on perspective → currency movements sharply changed 2025 performance for euro-based investors.

### LLM perspective

- View: Economic resilience and policy success are distinct; growth can coexist with failure to restore factory employment.
- Impact: Workers and strategic industries face automation, import dependence, and costly attempts to rebuild capacity.
