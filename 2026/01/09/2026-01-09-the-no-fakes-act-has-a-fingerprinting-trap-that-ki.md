# The No Fakes Act has a “fingerprinting” trap that kills open source?

- Score: 182 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46550231) | Link: https://old.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1q7qcux/the_no_fakes_act_has_a_fingerprinting_trap_that/

### TL;DR

An archived Reddit root post argues the NO FAKES Act could expose developers who make voice-cloning or TTS tools available to statutory damages when users create unauthorized replicas, while fingerprinting-based safe-harbor requirements may be impractical for raw model weights. It urges Congress to distinguish tool repositories from active services. HN commenters strongly dispute the reading: some fear broad liability and a chilling effect on open source, while others say the bill targets products primarily designed to imitate specifically identified individuals and may not apply to general-purpose models.

### Comment pulse

- Chilling-effect concern → developers cannot price unlimited liability for unpredictable user behavior, especially when distributing free software.
- Responsibility debate → risky-tool creators should bear some cost — counterpoint: liability belongs with users or services committing the harmful act.
- Legal-scope objection → critics say specifically identified individual and primarily designed language narrows the bill beyond the post’s interpretation.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central uncertainty is statutory scope, not whether unauthorized digital replicas can cause harm.
- Impact: Ambiguous intermediary liability could favor compliance-rich platforms over independent developers and open-weight repositories.
- Watch next: Examine amendments defining covered tools, actual knowledge, safe-harbor eligibility, and treatment of general-purpose models.
