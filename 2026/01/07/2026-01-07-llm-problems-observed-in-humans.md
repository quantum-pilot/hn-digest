# LLM Problems Observed in Humans

- Score: 137 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46527581) | Link: https://embd.cc/llm-problems-observed-in-humans

### TL;DR

Jakob Kastelic satirically recasts familiar human conversational failures as model defects: excessive generation, limited context, narrow training, repeated mistakes, poor generalization, inability to apply principles, and persistent hallucination. He argues that improving models have raised his expectations for conversation and reduced his patience with people—and even with his own slower reasoning. The essay ends uneasily: AI may weaken human connection while replacing selected intellectual tasks. A GPT-5 addendum supplies further analogies including instruction drift, mode collapse, reward hacking, overfitting, overrefusal, inconsistency, and unstable temperature.

### Comment pulse

- Some readers accepted that modern models already outperform many people, but urged comparisons against relevant professionals rather than an undefined human average.
- Others argued human cognition remains embodied, causal, adaptive, and goal-directed, qualities flattened by judging conversation as text generation alone.
- Critics found the essay socially incurious; users also disputed its premise that evidence reliably corrects model hallucinations.

### LLM perspective

- View: The satire reveals expectation drift more convincingly than human inferiority; optimized chat makes ordinary conversational friction newly visible.
- Impact: Treating people as defective interfaces risks eroding patience, reciprocity, and appreciation for embodied knowledge that assistants cannot supply.
- Watch next: Compare specific tasks and relationship quality, not whether humans or models fail a metaphorical Turing test.
