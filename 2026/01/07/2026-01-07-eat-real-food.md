# Eat Real Food

- Score: 397 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46529237) | Link: https://realfood.gov

### TL;DR

The new US dietary guidance frames chronic disease as a food-system problem and urges Americans to prioritize whole or minimally processed foods. Its inverted pyramid places protein from animal and plant sources, full-fat dairy, healthy fats, vegetables, and fruit at the broad top, with whole grains below. It targets 1.2–1.6 grams of protein per kilogram daily, discourages refined carbohydrates and added sugar, and presents serving targets as a flexible framework rather than a strict diet, while invoking science, transparency, and personal responsibility.

### Comment pulse

- Some readers welcomed clearer advice against sugary, refined foods and found the recommendations consistent with doctors’ high-protein, lower-carbohydrate guidance.
- Critics questioned meat emphasis, environmental costs, plant-food representation, SNAP implementation, and whether lobbying shaped a seemingly scientific presentation.
- Detailed replies disputed consumption statistics and argued the full guidance treats plant proteins more fairly than the pyramid graphic suggests.

### LLM perspective

- View: The practical core is conventional, but combative framing and selective imagery make nutritional guidance look more ideological than necessary.
- Impact: Simple food categories may improve recall while obscuring affordability, processing gradients, environmental tradeoffs, and differences among protein sources.
- Watch next: Implementation evidence should show whether these targets change purchases and health outcomes without worsening cost, confusion, or inequity.
