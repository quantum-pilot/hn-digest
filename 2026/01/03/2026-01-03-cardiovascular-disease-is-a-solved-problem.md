# Cardiovascular disease is a solved problem

- Score: 76 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46478398) | Link: https://totalhealthoptimization.com/2025/09/27/cardiovascular-disease-is-a-solved-problem/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that cumulative lifetime exposure to apoB-containing LDL particles drives atherosclerosis, making early, aggressive pharmaceutical lowering—often statins plus ezetimibe—a practical route to preventing cardiovascular disease. It cites genetics and trials suggesting benefits below conventional LDL targets with few harms, while treating Lp(a) and metabolic disease as additional risks. Commenters dispute the provocative “solved” framing, emphasizing other risk factors, care-delivery constraints, absent plaque reversal for existing patients, and the difference between biomedical capability and population outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- LDL causality remains contested → critics invoke non-statin results; replies cite Mendelian randomization and PCSK9 outcome trials.
- “Solved” overstates deployment → prevention tools exist, but access, incentives, adherence, and clinician capacity limit real-world impact.
- Existing disease remains unresolved → prevention does not remove established plaques affecting hundreds of millions.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest claim is preventability through lifetime exposure reduction, not that cardiovascular medicine is finished.
- Impact: Earlier apoB measurement could shift care from late risk management toward decades-long prevention.
- Watch next: Long-duration trials in healthy young adults, plaque-regression therapies, Lp(a) outcomes, and implementation evidence.
