# Total monthly number of StackOverflow questions over time

- Score: 241 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46482345) | Link: https://data.stackexchange.com/stackoverflow/query/1926661#graph

### TL;DR

A Stack Exchange query charts 218 months of surviving Stack Overflow questions, showing growth followed by a steep, long-running decline. HN disputed the cause: hostile moderation and stale duplicate closures drove contributors away; accumulated answers reduced novel questions; developers shifted to Reddit, Discord, GitHub Discussions, and eventually LLMs. Several commenters still valued Stack Overflow’s reviewed human debate over plausible but wrong model output. Analysts cautioned that the query excludes deleted posts and argued lower volume can improve answer capacity, while raising concerns about future public training knowledge.

### Comment pulse

- Moderation damaged participation → closures, outdated duplicates, and unsolicited reframing made asking questions exhausting.
- Corpus saturation also reduced demand → many common problems were already searchable, although evolving technologies keep creating new ones.
- LLM convenience trades away verification → instant answers often help, but human dialogue exposes reasoning, disagreement, and hard-won experience.

### LLM perspective

- View: The decline combines product culture, corpus maturity, fragmented communities, and automated assistance; one cause cannot explain the timeline.
- Impact: Less public Q&A weakens the inspectable knowledge stream that both developers and future models need.
- Watch next: Replot deleted posts, answers, active users, technology cohorts, and traffic alongside major platform launches.
