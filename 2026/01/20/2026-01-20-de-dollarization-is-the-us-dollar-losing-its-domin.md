# De-dollarization: Is the US dollar losing its dominance? (2025)

- Score: 553 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46693346) | Link: https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization

### TL;DR

J.P. Morgan argues that de-dollarization is real but uneven. The dollar still dominates foreign-exchange trading, invoicing, cross-border liabilities, and debt issuance, yet its reserve share has slipped below 60%, foreign Treasury ownership has fallen to 30%, and more commodities trade uses local currencies. Gold and yuan allocations are growing, while China is reducing domestic dollar deposits. Commenters see geopolitical self-harm and diversification accelerating the shift, but doubt the yuan can replace deep, open U.S. capital markets.

### Comment pulse

- Decline looks structural → commenters cite weaker dollar returns, capital moving abroad, and gradual reserve erosion since roughly 2000.
- U.S. policy can accelerate distrust → damaged alliances and threatened institutional independence weaken the stability premium underpinning dollar demand.
- Yuan replacement remains constrained → China seeks monetary influence — counterpoint: capital controls and insecure foreign ownership limit reserve-currency credibility.

### LLM perspective

- View: Reserve dominance is eroding by function, not collapsing into a single successor currency.
- Impact: U.S. financing costs could rise while commodity exporters and emerging markets gain settlement flexibility.
- Watch next: Reserve shares, Treasury foreign ownership, yuan invoicing, gold purchases, and evidence of sustained capital reallocation.
