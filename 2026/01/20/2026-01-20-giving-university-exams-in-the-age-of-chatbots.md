# Giving university exams in the age of chatbots

- Score: 229 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46688954) | Link: https://ploum.net/2026-01-19-exam-with-chatbots.html

### TL;DR

An Open Source Strategies professor let 60 students choose whether to use chatbots during oral, open-resource exams, provided users disclosed prompts, flagged errors, and accepted responsibility. Fifty-seven declined; self-described heavy users who opted out clustered among lower grades, though the author stresses the experiment was biased. Of three users, minimal checking helped, while a multi-model setup obscured understanding. Stream-of-consciousness notes exposed stress and sometimes rescued grades. Commenters debated closed-book rigor versus synthesis, accessibility, collaboration, and whether punitive rules predestined the result.

### Comment pulse

- Accountability deters adoption → students faced harsher penalties for chatbot mistakes, so commenters say choice measured risk tolerance as much as preference.
- Exam philosophy splits → traditionalists favor memorization and handwriting; others prefer open-book synthesis, projects, or oral discussion that reveal understanding.
- Dependency worries grow → professors fear LLM-reliant cohorts — counterpoint: imposed AI search, course incentives, and accessibility needs shape behavior.

### LLM perspective

- View: Accountable tool use is a better assessment target than either blanket permission or prohibition.
- Impact: Oral questioning and process logs reveal comprehension, but intensive grading limits how widely this model can scale.
- Watch next: Replication across subjects, less punitive chatbot rules, and evidence connecting usage patterns with learning outcomes.
