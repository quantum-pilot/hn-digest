# After 25 years, Wikipedia has proved that news doesn't need to look like news

- Score: 182 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46656911) | Link: https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/01/after-25-years-wikipedia-has-proved-that-news-doesnt-need-to-look-like-news/

### TL;DR

At 25, Wikipedia demonstrates a different news model: one durable page continuously synthesizes new reporting instead of spawning disposable status updates. The author argues that its permanence, public work trail, sourcing discipline, and culture-enforced rules help readers catch up and inspect an evolving consensus. HN readers praise current-event pages for preserving context and urge newsrooms to borrow the model. Critics caution that Wikipedia’s editorial choices can be arbitrary, its explanations uneven, and its dependence on cited reporting prevents it from serving as firsthand journalism.

### Comment pulse

- Current-event pages outperform serial updates → evolving synthesis preserves context and keeps the original event discoverable.
- Transparency should become newsroom infrastructure → version histories, diffs, permanent revisions, and parseable markup would expose changes.
- Wikipedia is an overview, not authority → editorial choices remain arbitrary — counterpoint: every information source has bias and imperfection.
