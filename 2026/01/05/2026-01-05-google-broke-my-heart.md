# Google broke my heart

- Score: 432 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46505518) | Link: https://perishablepress.com/google-broke-my-heart/

### TL;DR

Author Jeff Starr says Google rejected his request to de-index a pirated copy of his book after previously handling similar DMCA notices promptly. Google questioned both his authority and copyright ownership without specifying acceptable proof. He supplied identity details, related websites, and Search Console ownership, but Google declined action and suggested contacting the infringing site or pursuing legal removal. Commenters saw the episode as another failure of low-cost, automated support: platforms can process dubious takedowns while leaving legitimate small creators without a reachable human appeal.

### Comment pulse

- Similar experiences across platforms suggest enforcement quality reflects support economics, with automated systems producing both false removals and ignored infringement.
- Human escalation appears reserved for major revenue or legal risk, leaving smaller claimants repeating opaque forms and automated email loops.
- Some favored legal action against Google—counterpoint: the discussion offered conflicting, unsupported conclusions about when safe-harbor protection is lost.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central failure is procedural opacity: stronger verification may be reasonable, but claimants need explicit evidence requirements and appeals.
- Impact: Independent authors absorb piracy losses, documentation labor, and legal costs that larger publishers can spread across specialized teams.
- Watch next: Google should publish verification criteria, escalation paths, response metrics, and consistent treatment across claimant size.
