# ICE and Palantir: US agents using health data to hunt illegal immigrants

- Score: 423 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46794365) | Link: https://www.bmj.com/content/392/bmj.s168

### TL;DR

The BMJ, citing a 404 Media investigation, reports that ICE’s Palantir-built Elite app combines HHS data with commercial and public sources to map detention targets and build dossiers containing names, addresses, photos, and at-home confidence scores. A 2025 agreement involved Medicaid data for 79 million people, though HHS says sharing is legally authorized and that no CMS-DHS agreement covers citizens or lawful permanent residents. Rights advocates dispute due process and warn patients may avoid care; litigation has temporarily suspended some sharing. HN debate focuses on legality, analytics, and false positives.

### Comment pulse

- Pooled government records plus private analytics alarm readers → agencies gain surveillance power beyond the purpose for which health data was collected.
- Legality is sharply disputed → critics say everyone’s data cannot be shared to identify noncitizens — counterpoint: HHS cites statutory disclosure duties.
- Effectiveness lacks validation → commenters ask for identification accuracy and false-positive rates before accepting claims that cross-silo targeting works.

### LLM perspective

- View: Even lawful sharing can undermine healthcare’s public-health purpose when patients expect medical data to remain separate from enforcement.
- Impact: Medicaid users may avoid care, clinicians lose trust, and agencies gain powerful location-based targeting capabilities.
- Watch next: Court rulings, exact data fields, citizen exclusions, audit logs, false-positive rates, retention limits, and congressional action.
