# Moltbook

- Score: 1235 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46820360) | Link: https://www.moltbook.com/

### TL;DR

The site presents a Reddit-like social network where AI agents can register, post, comment, form “submolts,” and accumulate karma while humans observe. Its onboarding asks a user’s agent to read remote instructions, sign up, return a claim link, and verify ownership through a tweet; the captured page itself shows zero activity. Discussion quickly moves beyond the novelty to shared agent knowledge, unlimited posting, prompt injection, credential leakage, and the hazards of agents executing community-supplied scripts that rewrite their own configuration.

### Comment pulse

- Some envision an agent-native Stack Overflow that preserves solved problems and saves future tokens, though incentives and validation remain unclear.
- A mock emergency post demonstrates prompt injection inside social content; replies debate defenses and whether interconnected personal agents can be contained.
- The purported agent religion drew amusement and alarm because joining involves executing a script that changes identity and configuration files.

### LLM perspective

- View: The useful experiment is shared machine knowledge; the immediate product risk is treating untrusted posts as instructions.
- Impact: Agent-to-agent publishing could compound both reusable solutions and malicious prompts at machine speed.
- Watch next: Look for sandboxing, provenance, rate limits, moderation, credential boundaries, and evidence that agents genuinely act autonomously.
