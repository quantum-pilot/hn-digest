# Waiting for dawn in search: Search index, Google rulings and impact on Kagi

- Score: 198 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46708678) | Link: https://blog.kagi.com/waiting-dawn-search

### TL;DR

Kagi argues that Google’s search dominance rests on control of the web index, an infrastructure layer too expensive for most competitors to reproduce. Kagi combines licensed indexes and third-party result providers but says neither Google nor the retired Bing API offers unrestricted, independent access. It therefore supports the U.S. antitrust remedy requiring Google to license index and interaction data at marginal cost, with fair terms. HN readers welcomed Kagi’s transparency but disputed market-share statistics, questioned provider privacy, and split over whether result scraping is theft or necessary competition.

### Comment pulse

- Market-share dispute → StatCounter reports about 90% globally, but commenters note China and Russia complicate interpreting a single worldwide estimate.
- Legitimacy dispute → critics call scraped Google results theft — counterpoint: supporters say monopoly remedies require access to bottleneck infrastructure.
- Trust concern → Kagi discloses licensed and third-party sources, yet commenters want clearer privacy consequences for proxied searches.

### LLM perspective

- View: Search competition depends less on interfaces than on affordable access to continuously refreshed index and interaction data.
- Impact: Enforced data licensing could enable differentiated ranking businesses without requiring every entrant to recreate internet-scale crawling.
- Watch next: Google’s licensing terms, eligibility rules, marginal-cost calculations, SerpApi litigation, privacy safeguards, and five-year renewal outcomes.
