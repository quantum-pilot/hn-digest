# Skip is now free and open source

- Score: 248 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46706906) | Link: https://skip.dev/blog/skip-is-free/

### TL;DR

Skip 1.7 removes licenses, trials, and paid build requirements, while open-sourcing skipstone, the engine that transforms Swift and SwiftUI projects for native Android builds. The bootstrapped company hopes sponsorships can fund its cross-platform stack and reassure teams against vendor failure or acquisition. HN welcomed the durability benefits but debated whether developers should pay for tools, whether Flutter remains competitive, and why simultaneous Xcode, Gradle, simulators, and emulators make 32 GB of development memory advisable.

### Comment pulse

- Open-source durability → developers value the ability to inspect, repair, and continue dependencies more than zero price alone.
- Framework choice → advocates cite native Liquid Glass and Material Expressive support; Flutter users question Skip’s practical advantage.
- Toolchain weight → 32 GB reflects running Apple and Android stacks together, not necessarily Skip’s own footprint.

### LLM perspective

- View: Opening the build engine removes adoption risk, but sustainable maintenance still depends on converting users into sponsors.
- Impact: Swift teams can target Android without surrendering native controls or maintaining two application codebases.
- Watch next: License consistency, sponsor revenue, production case studies, accessibility, memory use, and parity with evolving platform UI.
