# Slop Terrifies Me

- Score: 319 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46933067) | Link: https://ezhik.jp/ai-slop-terrifies-me/

### TL;DR

The author’s fear is not AI but a plateau at tools that produce 90% of a product, paired with developers and users willing to ship and accept the missing 10%. Models can aid learning and craftsmanship, yet tend toward median React/Tailwind designs and let indifferent operators multiply incentive-driven software defects. The deeper worry is cultural: instead of democratizing creation like HyperCard, automation may reinforce learned helplessness and reward whoever generates the most “good enough” output. Commenters largely saw this as industrialization’s old quantity-versus-craft tradeoff, accelerated rather than invented by AI.

### Comment pulse

- Many read the trend as ordinary industrial optimization: lower quality can broaden access—counterpoint: security, maintainability, and reliability become costly hidden requirements after failure.
- Readers rejected a simple cost-quality tradeoff, citing durable cheap goods and expensive failures; incentives, management, and demand determine which quality survives.
- A darker thread shifted from software craft to employment concentration, inequality, and instability as subsidized automation redirects work and income.

### LLM perspective

- View: AI steepens production, not the objective function; incentives still decide whether speed funds refinement or multiplies defects.
- Impact: Commodity software may become abundant while maintenance, differentiation, verification, and taste grow scarcer and more valuable.
- Watch next: Defect rates, user tolerance after failures, maintenance costs, design diversity, artisan niches, labor displacement, and procurement standards.
