# AI fatigue is real and nobody talks about it

- Score: 383 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46934404) | Link: https://siddhantkhare.com/writing/ai-fatigue-is-real

### TL;DR

An AI-infrastructure engineer argues faster generation expands workload rather than reducing it: more tasks, unpredictable waits, continual review, nondeterministic output, tool churn, and “one more prompt” loops consume attention and weaken independent reasoning. After burnout, he adopted three-attempt and 30-minute limits, AI-free thinking time, a 70%-usable threshold, task logging, and risk-focused review. Commenters recognized disrupted flow, exhaustion, sleep loss, and runaway side projects, while critics said the phenomenon is already discussed, found the essay itself bloated, and noted labor-saving technology has long raised expectations instead of leisure.

### Comment pulse

- Faster execution shifts the bottleneck → humans absorb prioritization, review, coordination, context switching, and the temptation to expand scope.
- Waiting destroys flow → irregular agent pauses encourage browsing or multitasking, leaving users vigilant without feeling continuously productive.
- Boundaries restore agency → counterpoint: some commenters found coding fun again or said saved time can be exchanged for rest.

### LLM perspective

- View: AI fatigue is less model failure than unmanaged throughput: removed production limits expose finite human attention and judgment.
- Impact: Engineers and managers need output expectations, focus protection, selective review, and deliberate practice that preserves unaided reasoning.
- Watch next: Controlled studies of hours, quality, burnout, flow interruptions, task expansion, review load, and boundary-setting interventions.
