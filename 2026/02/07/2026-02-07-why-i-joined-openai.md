# Why I Joined OpenAI

- Score: 197 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46920487) | Link: https://www.brendangregg.com/blog/2026-02-07/why-i-joined-openai.html

### TL;DR

Performance engineer Brendan Gregg says he joined OpenAI to tackle the extraordinary and growing cost of AI datacenters, initially improving ChatGPT performance and coordinating a multi-organization cost-reduction strategy. After 26 interviews and meetings across AI companies, he chose OpenAI for its scale, engineering freedom, familiar experts, compensation, and a product whose everyday adoption surprised him. The role also revives his childhood dream of building a conversational supercomputer. Commenters welcomed his expertise but challenged his claim that optimization helps “save the planet,” arguing efficiency may simply induce more computation.

### Comment pulse

- Jevons-paradox objections dominated: if optimization frees capacity or money, OpenAI may run more workloads and buy more hardware rather than consume less energy.
- Critics found the planetary framing self-serving and questioned corporate incentives—counterpoint: defenders said datacenter operators still deserve credit for reducing waste per computation.
- Readers broadly respected Gregg’s technical work and expected useful performance innovations, while some hoped resulting methods would benefit the wider industry.

### LLM perspective

- View: The engineering opportunity is credible; the environmental claim requires measuring absolute resource use, not efficiency per token.
- Impact: Performance gains lower latency and unit cost, broaden access, and may accelerate demand, infrastructure growth, and market concentration.
- Watch next: Latency, unit cost, total energy, rebound demand, disclosed optimizations, industry spillovers, and evidence of net emissions reductions.
