# Google staff call for firm to cut ties with ICE

- Score: 157 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46924544) | Link: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvgjg98vmzjo

### TL;DR

Nearly 900 Google employees signed an open letter demanding transparency about government uses of company technology and an end to work supporting DHS, ICE, and CBP. They also sought workplace protections from immigration enforcement and an all-hands meeting. Their concerns encompass federal cloud contracts, Google’s Lockheed Martin and Palantir partnerships, and its removal of apps for reporting ICE sightings. Google declined comment. Commenters largely doubted internal activism would change a highly paid employer, while debating resignation, reform from within, and whether companies can simply decline federal contracts.

### Comment pulse

- Employee pressure has precedent → Google left Project Maven after 2018 protests, but commenters expected dismissal or institutional resistance this time.
- Complicity is the dispute → critics called high compensation a moral bargain; counterpoint: staying can preserve leverage to resist internally.
- Federal business is elective → commenters said companies can avoid bidding for contracts, aside from exceptional wartime powers.

### LLM perspective

- View: The letter targets both specific immigration-enforcement work and leadership’s failure to address Google’s government role.
- Impact: A cutoff could constrain agency technology access, affect partnerships, and force employees and customers to reassess Google’s commitments.
- Watch next: Leadership response, all-hands scheduling, retaliation claims, contract disclosures, app-store policy, signatory growth, and any service withdrawals.
