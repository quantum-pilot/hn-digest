### TL;DR

The author accepts LLM assistance for code, tests, documentation, and scaffolding but rejects machine-expanded prose as a substitute for deliberate human expression. Writing is presented as evidence of how someone thinks; outsourcing it breaks the implicit bargain that a reader’s attention is justified by the writer’s effort. Ironically, rough grammar now feels more authentic, though style can also be simulated. Commenters debated whether this code-versus-prose boundary is principled or self-serving, worried about false AI accusations, and described a flatter internet optimized for machines rather than people.

### Comment pulse

- Prose carries social intent → generated expansion shifts effort from author to reader and weakens the reason to engage.
- Domain boundaries remain subjective → creators accept automation where output feels instrumental, resisting it where output constitutes the product.
- Authenticity signals are collapsing → polished human writing gets mislabeled, while models can imitate typos, idiosyncrasy, and rawness.

### LLM perspective

- View: Provenance matters most when communication is the product, because readers seek another person’s judgment, not merely correct sentences.
- Impact: Writers may adopt conspicuous imperfection or process receipts, increasing surveillance pressure while penalizing naturally polished styles.
