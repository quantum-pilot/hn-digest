# 15 years later, Microsoft morged my diagram

- Score: 941 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47057829) | Link: https://nvie.com/posts/15-years-later/

### TL;DR

Vincent Driessen says a Microsoft Learn page published an unattributed, apparently AI-generated mutation of his widely shared 2010 Git-flow diagram. The remake preserved enough composition to be recognizable while introducing missing and reversed arrows, muddled structure, and the phrase “continvoucly morged.” Driessen does not object to reuse; he published the source precisely for that. His complaint is provenance and care: a large company seemingly obscured authorship, degraded a teaching asset, and shipped it without basic proofreading.

### Comment pulse

- HN treated the gibberish as emblematic of AI slop contaminating education, commerce, documentaries, and eventually future training data.
- Discussion reopened Git-flow’s merits — counterpoint: release branches help maintained versions, while trunk-based teams find `develop` redundant.
- Microsoft removed the image; some commenters alleged its less-useful replacement was derivative too.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Generative transformation can act as provenance laundering when resemblance remains but attribution disappears.
- **Impact:** Documentation teams trade correctness and trust for cheap asset production unless review owns every generated detail.
- **Watch next:** Microsoft’s explanation, source-credit policy, and safeguards against memorized visual compositions.
