# Ex-Tech –> Homeless in SF

- Score: 107 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47021586) | Link: https://zamoshi.substack.com/p/ninety-four-degrees

### TL;DR

Michael McIntosh recounts arriving in San Francisco without money or a phone while experiencing psychosis, then navigating fragmented shelters, criminal charges, bereavement, and life beneath a Redwood City bridge. He describes rejected referrals, refusing a shared men’s shelter, stealing camping supplies and food, and representing himself against four felonies. The sharpest contrast comes when he sleeps near former colleagues at a highly valued robotics startup where he was once the first employee. He closes by framing the episode as survival.

### Comment pulse

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The account shows psychosis can make administrative friction and personal refusals interact unpredictably, resisting a simple institutional-versus-individual explanation.
- **Impact:** Phone-dependent referrals and eligibility silos can exclude people least able to navigate them coherently.
- **Watch next:** Whether the author later explains the felony charges, lost relationships, equity outcome, and path out of homelessness.
