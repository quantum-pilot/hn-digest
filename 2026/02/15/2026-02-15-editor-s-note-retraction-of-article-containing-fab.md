# Editor's Note: Retraction of article containing fabricated quotations

- Score: 119 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47026071) | Link: https://arstechnica.com/staff/2026/02/editors-note-retraction-of-article-containing-fabricated-quotations/

### TL;DR

Ars Technica retracted an article after discovering that it contained fabricated quotations attributed to researcher Scott Shambaugh. The publication says the material violated its policy against unlabeled AI-generated content, apologized to Shambaugh and readers, and described the incident as isolated after reviewing the author’s recent work. Commenters focused less on the apology than on process: the quoted source, not Ars, caught the fabrication, and the note offered little detail about new safeguards or editorial accountability.

### Comment pulse

- Readers wanted concrete prevention measures, including quote verification, disclosure rules, and audits, rather than assurances that the case was isolated.
- The author accepted responsibility while mentioning illness and lost sleep; commenters largely rejected those circumstances as an explanation.
- Several treated the episode as another demonstration that fluent model output cannot be presumed factual.

- **View:** Generated quotations are a provenance failure, not merely a prose-quality defect.
