# Personal Statement of a CIA Analyst

- Score: 120 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47102975) | Link: https://antipolygraph.org/statements/statement-038.shtml

### TL;DR

A former CIA analyst recounts repeated polygraph screenings across CIA, NRO, FBI, and DIA work, describing arbitrary accusations, painful retests, and results disconnected from later clearance decisions. She concluded that sensors measure bodily reactions but examiners cannot reliably infer deception, while interrogation pressure can elicit admissions and lasting psychological harm. After failing successive tests yet receiving strong reviews and additional access, she refused a third DIA polygraph, lost project badges, faced termination by her contractor, and resigned. Commenters viewed polygraphs mainly as coercive props, though experiences varied.

### Comment pulse

- Some argued the apparatus can prompt useful admissions without measuring truth — counterpoint: sincere subjects may be punished while practiced liars pass.
- Other cleared workers reported both uneventful tests and abusive interrogations, reinforcing the examiner-dependent nature of the experience.
- Some criticized the author’s one-time countermeasure experiment, despite her account that it occurred during calibration and was never repeated.
