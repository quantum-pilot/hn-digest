# Study: Self-generated Agent Skills are useless

- Score: 161 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47040430) | Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670

### TL;DR

SkillsBench evaluated seven agent-model configurations over 7,308 trajectories spanning 86 tasks in 11 domains. Human-curated procedural skill packages raised average pass rates 16.2 percentage points, ranging from 4.5 points in software engineering to 51.9 in healthcare; focused two- or three-module skills beat comprehensive documentation. Skills generated by the agent before attempting a task produced no average benefit, and 16 of 84 measured tasks worsened even with curated skills. Commenters warned this does not test skills distilled after research, experimentation, or human collaboration.

### Comment pulse

- Pre-task generation mostly restates latent knowledge → the benchmark allowed neither web research nor prior codebase exploration before skill writing.
- Post-task skills are a different intervention → lessons captured after struggle can preserve context-specific discoveries for future sessions.
- Eval-driven curation earns trust → contributors favored small, evidence-backed instructions over broad repositories lacking measurable gains.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Skills function best as external procedural memory, not extra deliberation from the same untouched context.
- **Impact:** Smaller models may match larger unassisted ones where domain experts supply focused, tested guidance.
- **Watch next:** Human-AI coauthored skills, fresh-session reuse, real codebases, longitudinal learning, and per-domain ablations.
