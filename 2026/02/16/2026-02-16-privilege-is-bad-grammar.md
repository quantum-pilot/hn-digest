# Privilege is bad grammar

- Score: 172 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47038125) | Link: https://tadaima.bearblog.dev/privilege-is-bad-grammar/

### TL;DR

After noticing executives’ typo-filled, brusque emails, the author proposes grammar privilege: junior workers labor over polished messages to signal competence, while powerful people can ignore professional conventions without penalty. Leaked correspondence from prominent executives reinforced the pattern. Commenters identify this as countersignalling, but offer other explanations: senior leaders process more messages, communicate briefly downward and formally upward, or simply prioritize comfort and speed. Critics answer that brevity need not mean carelessness, and that clear grammar remains a form of respect rather than merely a status performance.

### Comment pulse

- Sloppiness can countersignal secure status → people already recognized as important no longer need conventional markers of competence.
- Workload explains shorter replies → counterpoint: correct spelling and respectful tone often add negligible time.
- AI-perfect prose may invert the signal → human imperfections can now suggest authenticity rather than education.
