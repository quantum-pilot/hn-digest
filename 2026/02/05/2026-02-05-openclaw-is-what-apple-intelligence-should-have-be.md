# OpenClaw is what Apple intelligence should have been

- Score: 513 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46893970) | Link: https://www.jakequist.com/thoughts/openclaw-is-what-apple-intelligence-should-have-been

### TL;DR

Jake Quist argues that people buying Mac Minis to run OpenClaw reveal demand for computer-controlling agents that Apple Intelligence should have supplied. Apple could combine trusted hardware, personal data, and cross-device integration into an agent platform, then make services integrate on its terms. He suggests Apple prioritized liability and platform conflict over a long-term moat. Commenters strongly countered that OpenClaw ignores prompt-injection, malware, and reliability risks unsuitable for mass deployment, while debating whether Apple’s delay is prudent, whether such agents are wanted, and whether Apple can still catch up.

### Comment pulse

- Apple can wait for proven models and security, then integrate deeply—counterpoint: Siri’s long stagnation weakens confidence that this strategy still works.
- OpenClaw was characterized as a safety-skipping demonstration, with malicious skills, prompt injection, incoherent behavior, and unpredictable actions making broad deployment unacceptable.
- Some expect agents to become computing’s dominant interface; others compare the forecast to abandoned metaverse hype or simply prefer direct software use.

### LLM perspective

- View: The product vision is plausible, but Apple-scale trust demands bounded capabilities, inspectable actions, recovery, and adversarially robust permissioning.
- Impact: Safe agents could shift power toward operating-system vendors; unsafe ones could automate fraud, data loss, and irreversible mistakes.
- Watch next: Apple releases, permissions, prompt-injection defenses, skill provenance, audit logs, rollback, OpenClaw incidents, and platform resistance.
