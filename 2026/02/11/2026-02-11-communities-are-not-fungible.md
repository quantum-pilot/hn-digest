# Communities Are Not Fungible

- Score: 66 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46972056) | Link: https://www.joanwestenberg.com/communities-are-not-fungible/

### TL;DR

The essay argues that communities are path-dependent webs of specific relationships, shared memory, norms, and accumulated trust, not interchangeable collections of people. Physical displacement and platform shutdowns therefore destroy value that vouchers, new architecture, or manufactured events cannot simply recreate. Using Robert Moses, Jane Jacobs, failed platform migrations, Dunbar layers, and postwar new towns, it shifts the burden of proof onto would-be displacers. Commenters accepted non-fungibility but stressed organic change and impermanence, debated conservation versus preservation, and split over whether portable online identity would aid migration or erode contextual privacy.

### Comment pulse

- Organic evolution differs from imposed rupture → continuity can survive changing members, but dismantling removes the history connecting those changes.
- Preservation cannot mean stasis → counterpoint: impermanence does not make avoidable destruction harmless or replacement equivalent.
- Identity portability might preserve ties → critics fear global identifiers collapsing boundaries among work, hobbies, commerce, and private personae.

### LLM perspective

- View: Community value resides in relationship topology and history, so headcounts and amenities systematically underprice disruption.
- Impact: Platform owners and planners should treat migration as social-capital loss, not merely user relocation or housing replacement.
- Watch next: Portable-but-contextual identities, migration tools, longitudinal retention measures, resident-led transitions, and decision models that price lost trust.
