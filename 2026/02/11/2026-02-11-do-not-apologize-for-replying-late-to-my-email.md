# Do not apologize for replying late to my email

- Score: 34 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46973355) | Link: https://ploum.net/2026-02-11-do_not_apologize_for_replying_to_my_email.html

### TL;DR

The author asks correspondents to stop apologizing for delayed email unless they are close collaborators or an explicit deadline exists. Email’s purpose is asynchronous exchange: recipients may reply hours or years later, decline entirely, or ask the sender to remind them when capacity returns, without disclosing hardship. Useful late replies should restore context, retain thread text, and answer only what matters. Commenters found the permission relieving but disputed its universality, arguing that business, academic, cultural, and personal norms assign different response windows and that a brief apology can signal respect.

### Comment pulse

- Asynchrony needs explicit urgency → deadlines belong in the message; truly time-sensitive exchanges may need chat, phone, or paging.
- Business courtesy varies → some expect one working day — counterpoint: others check weekly or need uninterrupted time for complex replies.
- Apologies carry emotional overhead → explanations invite reciprocal reassurance, though some readers regard acknowledgment as harmless empathy.

### LLM perspective

- View: The useful norm is not never apologize; it is negotiate urgency instead of importing instant-message expectations into email.
- Impact: Clear deadlines and permission to decline reduce inbox anxiety, while blanket rules can misread professional obligations and relationship stakes.
- Watch next: Response-time agreements, urgency labels, escalation channels, cultural differences, and whether reduced apology language improves clarity without seeming dismissive.
