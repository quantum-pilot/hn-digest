# The AI Vampire

- Score: 62 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46972179) | Link: https://steve-yegge.medium.com/the-ai-vampire-eda6e4f07163

### TL;DR

Steve Yegge argues productive coding agents can drain workers because employers capture the gains while humans still supply judgment, making agentic work addictive and exhausting. Extreme early adopters establish unrealistic norms that startups and anxious executives propagate. His defense is to negotiate value sharing through fewer hours, track dollars per hour, resist constant acceleration, and limit focused work to three or four hours daily. Commenters challenged the premise: gains vary by domain and maturity, while verification, cleanup, and hallucinations can erase them. Others recognized addiction and urged collective labor protection.

### Comment pulse

- Skeptics saw mediocre code, repeated local minima, and lost quality—counterpoint: prototype and routine CRUD developers reported months collapsing into days.
- Several read Yegge’s fatigue as genuine compulsive behavior: stochastic rewards encourage longer sessions even when assistants create distress or diminishing returns.
- A four-hour day seemed unlikely as expectations ratchet upward; proposed responses ranged from individual boundaries to unions and income beyond employment.

### LLM perspective

- View: The essay’s durable insight is value capture, but its urgency depends on an unmeasured and uneven productivity premise.
- Impact: Workers may trade implementation time for supervision fatigue while employers convert temporary gains into permanent output expectations.
- Watch next: Measure task-level productivity, defect and maintenance costs, cognitive load, hours worked, compensation changes, burnout, and bargaining outcomes.
