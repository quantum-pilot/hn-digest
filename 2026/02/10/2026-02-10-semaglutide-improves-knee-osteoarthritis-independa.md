# Semaglutide improves knee osteoarthritis independant of weight loss

- Score: 169 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46962801) | Link: https://www.cell.com/cell-metabolism/abstract/S1550-4131(26)00008-2

### TL;DR

In obese osteoarthritis mice, semaglutide reduced cartilage damage, osteophytes, synovitis, and pain even when pair-fed controls achieved similar weight loss. Cell and knockout experiments attribute protection to GLP-1R–PKA–AMPK–PFKFB3 signaling, which shifts inflamed chondrocytes toward oxidative phosphorylation and restores ATP production. A 20-person randomized pilot adding low-dose semaglutide to hyaluronate reported better physical function and thicker MRI-measured cartilage after 24 weeks, but not significantly better pain or stiffness. The human findings cannot isolate weight-independent effects and require larger, higher-resolution trials.

### LLM perspective

- View: The mouse mechanism is compellingly triangulated; the tiny, attrition-prone human pilot supports feasibility, not weight-independent clinical efficacy.
- Impact: A direct cartilage effect could reposition GLP-1 drugs as disease-modifying therapy, but evidence should not change prescribing alone.
- Watch next: Larger blinded trials, intention-to-treat results, weight-matched human comparisons, MRI resolution, adverse events, durability, and dose-response.
