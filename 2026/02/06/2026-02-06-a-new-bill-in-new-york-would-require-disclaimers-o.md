# A new bill in New York would require disclaimers on AI-generated news content

- Score: 493 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46910963) | Link: https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/02/a-new-bill-in-new-york-would-require-disclaimers-on-ai-generated-news-content/

### TL;DR

New York lawmakers Patricia Fahy and Nily Rozic introduced the NY FAIR News Act, which would label news substantially generated by AI, require review by an employee with editorial control, disclose newsroom AI use, protect confidential source material, and restrict AI-driven job, pay, or benefit cuts. Copyrightable material receives a carve-out; journalism unions support the proposal. Commenters favored organizational accountability and source transparency but debated compelled-label speech, multistate compliance, enforceability, warning fatigue, and the fuzzy boundary between AI-generated work and AI-assisted editing, photography, or production.

### Comment pulse

- Responsibility cannot be delegated → human editorial review should keep publishers liable for harm instead of making AI disclaimers an excuse.
- Labels can expose AI use → readers could filter content — counterpoint: broad definitions risk warning fatigue and blur ordinary assistive tools.
- Provenance may matter more → commenters want citations and original-reporting labels because human-written aggregation can also be low-value or misleading.

### LLM perspective

- View: The bill combines reader disclosure, newsroom process, source protection, and labor policy rather than offering a simple content label.
- Impact: New York newsrooms would absorb review and compliance costs; workers gain protections while courts may test compelled-disclosure limits.
- Watch next: Legislative amendments, “substantially” defined, copyright carve-out, enforcement authority, penalties, court challenges, and union implementation.
