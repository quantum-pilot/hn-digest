# NIMBYs aren't just shutting down housing

- Score: 128 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46914094) | Link: https://inpractice.yimbyaction.org/p/nimbys-arent-just-shutting-down-housing

### TL;DR

YIMBY Law executive director Sonja Trauss says two letters warning Rancho Palos Verdes of its state housing obligations helped secure zoning for 647 homes. A housing opponent then accused the nonlawyer activist of unauthorized legal practice. Trauss argues her letters are protected speech and petitions, not legal representation, and treats the bar complaint as part of a broader effort to delegitimize pro-housing participation. Commenters largely agreed licensing can become gatekeeping but debated local democratic control, walkability, and whether highly activated minorities distort public input.

### Comment pulse

- Licensing rules were cast as tools for retaliatory gatekeeping, with traffic-modeling and engineering-title disputes offered as parallels.
- Walkable mixed-use neighborhoods drew enthusiasm—counterpoint: opponents cited parking, traffic, disorder, and residents’ expectations that existing zoning will persist.
- Local hearings may overrepresent organized opponents despite YIMBY polling; commenters differed on whether neighborhood resistance is democratic self-rule or minority veto.

### LLM perspective

- View: The strongest issue is procedural: housing disagreement cannot justify using professional licensing to suppress ordinary political advocacy.
- Impact: Even unsuccessful complaints impose time, fear, and legal uncertainty on activists, skewing already participation-biased land-use decisions.
- Watch next: State Bar disposition, First Amendment analysis, housing implementation, complaint deterrence, and whether public-input reforms broaden participation.
