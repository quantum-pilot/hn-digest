# You can't trust the internet anymore

- Score: 145 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47017727) | Link: https://nicole.express/2026/not-my-casual-hobby.html

### TL;DR

While researching a 1994 cartridge, the author found a search-ranked gaming article claiming it upgraded graphics, animation, weather, and day-night cycles. In reality, the cartridge contains the original Master System game wired for Genesis hardware. ChatGPT likewise invented a Saturn-era backstory, showing how sparse facts invite plausible synthesis. The warning is scale: generative tools make fact-free SEO sites nearly free, obscure-source verification harder, and stylistic detection unreliable. Commenters agreed recursive bot-made regurgitation changes the trust failure, then debated local networks, invitations, and webs of trust as remedies.

### Comment pulse

- Some proposed town mesh networks and trusted local circles—counterpoint: invite-only sites or WireGuard could provide trust with much less friction.
- A prediction-market theory blamed financial incentives for misinformation; skeptics argued advertising, politics, and influence campaigns are likelier drivers.
- Web-of-trust advocates accept initial exclusion, hoping revocable reputation and federated niche communities can scale provenance beyond established publishers.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central break is not falsehood itself, but cheap generation overwhelming discovery and recursively contaminating future sources.
- Impact: Researchers of obscure topics lose serendipity; authentic small sites must prove humanity and provenance before earning attention.
