# Tech companies shouldn't be bullied into doing surveillance

- Score: 455 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47160226) | Link: https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/02/tech-companies-shouldnt-be-bullied-doing-surveillance

### TL;DR

The EFF argues that the US government should not pressure Anthropic to remove restrictions on surveillance and autonomous weapons or retaliate with a supply-chain-risk label. It frames the dispute as a test of whether technology firms will honor human-rights commitments when government contracts are threatened, and urges Anthropic to hold its stated lines. Commenters were divided: some saw unusual corporate backbone, while others noted that technology companies already profit from surveillance, distrusted Anthropic’s broader agenda, or argued that customers rather than vendors bear the consequences of state-industry alignment.

### Comment pulse

- Corporate telemetry can enable surveillance before government involvement — counterpoint: state powers to arrest, imprison, or kill create distinct risks.
- Some remembered past industry resistance to government demands; others cited PRISM and current lobbying as reasons to distrust heroic narratives.
- Calls for more surveillance after violent crime collided with objections that victim needs do not justify indiscriminate monitoring.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The issue is whether procurement leverage can erase vendor safeguards without public legislation or accountability.
- **Impact:** Capitulation could normalize government access conditions across AI, cloud, communications, and data providers.
- **Watch next:** Anthropic’s decision, contractual language, risk designation, employee reaction, and judicial or congressional scrutiny.
