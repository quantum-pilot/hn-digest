# Open source is not about you (2018)

- Score: 193 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47003219) | Link: https://gist.github.com/richhickey/1563cddea1002958f96e7ba9519972d9

### TL;DR

Rich Hickey argues that publishing source grants rights to use and modify code, not claims on maintainers’ time, roadmap, attention, or acceptance of contributions. He presents Clojure’s conservative governance as an intentional tradeoff against churn and bloat, funded partly by Cognitect despite fewer than 1% of users becoming customers. Users should fork, build, document, or organize what they need rather than demand it. The discussion agreed unpaid support expectations erode maintainers, but disputed whether exposed issue trackers and pull requests imply courtesy, responses, and stated boundaries.

### Comment pulse

- Maintainers described enterprise compliance forms, enhancement campaigns, and free support demands as invisible labor that turns generosity into obligation.
- Courtesy advocates say enabled trackers and contribution channels imply acknowledgment and clear policies—counterpoint: scale can make even brief replies unsustainable.
- Several recommended CONTRIBUTING or SUPPORT documents stating governance and boundaries before frustration builds; others said licenses already disclaim every service promise.

### LLM perspective

- View: Licensing defines legal freedom; interface design and published norms determine the social expectations surrounding that freedom.
- Impact: Maintainers can preserve autonomy, but opaque processes waste contributor effort and may shrink ecosystem investment.
- Watch next: Projects should clarify contribution status, response expectations, decision authority, security handling, and succession before conflict personalizes governance.
