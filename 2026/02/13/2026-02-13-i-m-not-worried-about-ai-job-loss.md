# I'm not worried about AI job loss

- Score: 105 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47006513) | Link: https://davidoks.blog/p/why-im-not-worried-about-ai-job-loss

### TL;DR

David Oks argues that powerful AI will diffuse through labor markets gradually, not arrive like COVID. His case rests on comparative advantage: humans remain useful wherever preferences, tacit knowledge, regulation, legacy systems, and organizational friction make human-plus-AI more productive than AI alone. Productivity gains may also unlock enough latent demand to increase complementary work, as software tooling has before. Commenters challenged the optimism on wealth distribution, post-scarcity politics, and white-collar displacement, while others noted that vague requirements and real-world integration still block today’s agents.

### Comment pulse

- Bottlenecks preserve jobs today → software work often consists of interpreting vague tickets and organizational context, not merely implementing code.
- Distribution undercuts abundance optimism → machine owners may capture early gains, and political systems need not share cheaper output with displaced workers.
- Automation could shrink teams → counterpoint: lower prices and elastic demand may create unknown industries and expand total work.
