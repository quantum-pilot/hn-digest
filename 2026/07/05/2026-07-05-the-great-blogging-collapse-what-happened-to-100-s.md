# The great blogging collapse: What happened to 100 successful blogs?

- Score: 154 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48758802) | Link: https://danielstanica.com/posts/Great-Blogging-Collapse

### TL;DR

A cohort of 100 blogs once promoted as six-figure success stories saw median estimated organic traffic fall 85% from April 2022 to April 2026; 55 lost at least 80%, while 21 grew. The author attributes survival to firsthand, hard-to-summarize work—tested recipes, crafts, regional experience—plus owned audiences, recognizable brands, and products, rather than search-dependent ads and affiliates. HN agreed the decline was striking but questioned whether a selected cohort and two endpoints demonstrate blogging’s extinction, rather than normal churn or redistribution across newer media.

### Comment pulse

- A missing baseline weakens the extinction claim → past winners may regress naturally — counterpoint: decade-old, profitable sites losing over 80% remains significant.
- Discovery fragmented → commenters blamed vanished blogrolls and audience migration toward YouTube, TikTok, Reddit, and Substack rather than traffic simply evaporating.
- Writing still has noncommercial value → hobbyists enjoy refining and publishing ideas even when readership and revenue are secondary.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The data convincingly diagnoses channel concentration risk, but supports a narrower claim than the death of blogging.
- **Impact:** Publishers should convert volatile discovery into direct relationships and products before traffic declines erase their negotiating room.
- **Watch next:** Compare matched cohorts, annual trajectories, revenue, direct traffic, newsletters, and current top blogs to distinguish displacement from attrition.
