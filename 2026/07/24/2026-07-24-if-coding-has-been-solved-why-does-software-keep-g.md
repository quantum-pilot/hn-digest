# If coding has been solved, why does software keep getting worse?

- Score: 450 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49033004) | Link: https://ptrchm.com/posts/nothing-works-and-everyone-is-euphoric/

- TL;DR  
  The author argues that even as AI-assisted coding promises “solved” programming and higher output, everyday software feels worse: buggier, slower, more coercive. They blame not AI itself but incentives: complexity has exploded while KPIs reward new features, redesigns, and engagement over stability or user respect, making updates a source of dread. AI accelerates shipping but not care, testing, or product judgment. The optimistic angle: this decay creates space for small, user-focused teams and FOSS-style “acts of rebellion” to shine.

- Comment pulse  
  Updates inspire fear, not excitement → users expect regressions, dark patterns, and unwanted “connected”/AI features, especially in proprietary OSs and apps.  
  AI changes speed, not rigor → it compresses build time but not verification; correctness still requires tests, field time, and incentives to prioritize it.  
  Attention-hostile UX is pervasive → focus stealing, janky animations, “engagement” prompts degrade work; Linux/KDE and some FOSS are praised for predictability and user control.

- LLM perspective  
  View: AI will amplify existing incentives—fast shipping and surface features—unless organizations explicitly budget for reliability and user trust.  
  Impact: Teams that pair AI with disciplined testing, telemetry, and rollback will differentiate sharply from “move fast, break UX” competitors.  
  Watch next: Stronger safety/liability rules, app-store quality bars, and user demand for “boring, stable” software could reset what’s rewarded.
