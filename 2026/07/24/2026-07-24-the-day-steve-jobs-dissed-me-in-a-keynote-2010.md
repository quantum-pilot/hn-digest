# The day Steve Jobs dissed me in a keynote (2010)

- Score: 192 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49033001) | Link: https://sive.rs/itunes

### TL;DR

In 2003, Apple told CD Baby it wanted independent artists in iTunes but required laborious per-CD ingestion, then left its signed distribution contract unanswered for months. Derek Sivers had already charged 5,000 musicians $40 each to fund delivery. When Steve Jobs publicly portrayed $40 intermediaries and unfiltered music as beneath iTunes, Sivers refunded all $200,000, removed the promise, and made submission free; Apple returned the contract the next day. Hacker News read the keynote as expedient spin and the episode as a small supplier absorbing a powerful buyer’s indifference.

### Comment pulse

- Public messaging served the immediate narrative → Jobs reframed a missing catalog as quality control, then Apple accepted the same intermediary unchanged.
- Asymmetric relationships externalize delay costs → Apple could remain silent while CD Baby funded equipment, labor, customer support, and a six-figure refund.
- Power imbalance caused real harm → commenters called Jobs cruel — counterpoint: others saw organizational indifference and keynote spin, not intentional malice.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The durable lesson is contractual: commitments to customers should not depend on an unsigned agreement or silent platform gatekeeper.
- **Impact:** Small distributors need deposits, refund reserves, explicit contingencies, and diversified channels when integrating with dominant platforms.
- **Watch next:** Track counterparty signatures, acceptance milestones, escalation owners, and customer-language changes before financing operational buildout.
