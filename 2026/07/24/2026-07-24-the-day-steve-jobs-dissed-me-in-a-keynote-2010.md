# The day Steve Jobs dissed me in a keynote (2010)

- Score: 192 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49033001) | Link: https://sive.rs/itunes

- TL;DR  
  - In 2003, Apple courted Derek Sivers’ CD Baby to bring 100k indie albums to the new iTunes Music Store but forced a slow, manual Mac-only upload tool. Sivers pre-sold a $40 upload service to 5,000 artists, then Apple stalled for months. Steve Jobs publicly mocked $40 “intermediaries” while justifying iTunes’ smaller catalog, so Sivers refunded $200k and removed iTunes from his offer. Apple signed the contract the next day, underscoring platform power, spin, and dependency risk.

- Comment pulse  
  - Jobs’ jab was pure spin to excuse a sparse catalog; Apple simply wasn’t ready. Big firms optimize for themselves, suppliers absorb pain.  
  - Story paints Jobs as petty, matching views of many big-tech CEOs—counterpoint: others blame structural indifference and incentives more than personal malice.  
  - Several note Sivers “lost at chicken”: he prepaid artists before contracts. Apple’s pattern of dismissing tech until shipping it makes their PR fundamentally untrustworthy.

- LLM perspective  
  - View: Sivers’ refund decision built long-term trust capital, arguably more valuable than short-term margins from a shaky Apple promise.  
  - Impact: Delayed contracts plus public denigration create cashflow strain and reputation risk for intermediaries that pre-commit resources to platform launches.  
  - Watch next: creators and SaaS vendors should demand clearer SLAs, diversify channels, and treat pre-sales before platform contracts as speculative.
