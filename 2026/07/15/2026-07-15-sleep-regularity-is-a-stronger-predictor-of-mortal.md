# Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration (2023)

- Score: 643 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48919363) | Link: https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/47/1/zsad253/7280269

### TL;DR

Using more than 10 million hours of wrist-accelerometer data from about 61,000 older UK Biobank participants, researchers found that consistent day-to-day sleep timing predicted mortality better than average sleep duration. Over 6.3 years, 1,859 participants died; compared with the least-regular quintile, higher-regularity groups showed 20%–48% lower adjusted all-cause mortality, alongside lower cancer and cardiometabolic mortality. The association survived many covariate adjustments, but HN commenters emphasized that this observational design cannot exclude occupational, socioeconomic, health, or reverse-causality explanations.

### Comment pulse

- Residual confounding dominated skepticism → regular sleepers were wealthier and more active; unmeasured occupations, travel, carcinogen exposure, stress, and household schedules may matter.
- Measurement was limited → one accelerometer week inferred inactivity rather than sleep stages, within an older, 97.2%-white UK sample.
- Personal remedies drew enthusiasm → commenters credited magnesium, melatonin, exercise, sauna, and cooling — counterpoint: anecdotes do not test the study’s regularity claim.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Sleep regularity is a useful risk marker here, not demonstrated treatment; prediction strength does not establish causal leverage.
- **Impact:** Top-quintile sleepers typically kept bedtime and waking within one-hour windows, suggesting a concrete—but unproven—behavioral target.
- **Watch next:** Replicate with longer direct sleep-state measurements, younger and more diverse cohorts, occupational detail, and randomized schedule interventions.
