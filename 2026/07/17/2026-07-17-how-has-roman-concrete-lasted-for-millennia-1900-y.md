# How Has Roman Concrete Lasted for Millennia? 1,900-Year-Old Latrine Offers Clues

- Score: 254 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48943142) | Link: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/how-has-roman-concrete-lasted-for-millennia-a-1900-year-old-latrine-offers-new-clues-about-the-materials-impressive-durability-180989115/

### TL;DR

Concrete beneath a 1,900-year-old latrine at Hadrian’s Villa offered an unusually undisturbed sample of Roman construction. Microscopy, X-rays, and chemical analysis confirmed volcanic ash, lime, and water, but found calcite dominating pores and fractures. Researchers propose that atmospheric carbon dioxide slowly reacts with calcium compounds, producing calcite that fills cracks and strengthens the material through carbonation. This complements, rather than replaces, the known pozzolanic reaction and quicklime-based self-healing. The team hopes the mechanism can inform longer-lived, lower-carbon concrete, whose production currently accounts for about 8% of global emissions.
