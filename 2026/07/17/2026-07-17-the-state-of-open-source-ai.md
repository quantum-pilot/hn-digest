# The state of open source AI

- Score: 353 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48947825) | Link: https://stateofopensource.ai/

### TL;DR

Mozilla argues open-weight models are viable defaults: near closed-model parity for coding, dominant OpenRouter token volume, lower prices, and vendor exit rights. Yet its survey finds only 51% of open-model teams reach production versus 63% using closed systems, reflecting gaps in infrastructure, maintenance, security, compliance, integration, and support. Value is moving into agent harnesses, where proprietary labs retain integration and usage-data advantages. Open AI’s decisive weaknesses are operational standardization, enterprise accountability, long-context fidelity, sustainable funding, and portable permission controls for consequential actions.

### Comment pulse

- Usage evidence may be selection-biased → OpenRouter misses direct provider APIs and subscriptions, so its rapid open-model growth cannot establish total market share.
- Open economics remain unsettled → cheap inference pressures closed vendors — counterpoint: training, hardware, and strategic generosity may make free weight releases unsustainable.
- Presentation undercut credibility → readers found the charts and synthetic-sounding prose overwhelming; proposed automatic authorship detectors drew accuracy objections.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Model weights commoditize faster than operations; leverage shifts to orchestration, permissions, observability, compliance, and model portability.
- **Impact:** Teams can gain cost and sovereignty benefits only by accepting responsibility for deployment, security, maintenance, evaluation, and liability.
- **Watch next:** Measure production conversion, neutral-harness benchmarks, direct-API share, permission standards, open-lab revenue, sovereign funding, and major misuse events.
