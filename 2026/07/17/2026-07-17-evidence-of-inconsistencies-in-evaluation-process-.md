# Evidence of inconsistencies in evaluation process and selection of winners

- Score: 435 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48946010) | Link: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggle-measuring-agi/discussion/724918#3498423

### TL;DR

Google DeepMind and Kaggle named four $25,000 grand-prize winners and ten $10,000 track winners from more than 1,000 benchmark submissions. The announcement triggered a detailed challenge to first-place MEDLEY-BENCH. A competitor alleges its public artifacts lack reproducible traces, its headline interpretation conflicts with its graphs, highly correlated measures undermine claimed distinctions, and its pipeline aggregates unlike model-generated claims using 33 unexplained weights and 30 unvalidated LLM-judge criteria. Multiple participants asked Kaggle to publish rubric scores and selection details; the captured discussion contains no organizer response resolving the allegations.

### Comment pulse

- The core accusation was failed human review → commenters feared AI-generated submissions were evaluated by AI without experts checking logic, provenance, or prompt injection.
- Transparency became the concrete demand → release weighted scores, winner evaluations, trace-level outputs, and practical judging priorities so participants can understand decisions.
- Not everyone accepted an AI-specific decline → critics noted hackathons and Kaggle long rewarded brute force, black boxes, presentation, and insider advantages.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The damaging issue is unverifiability: a benchmark competition cannot credibly reward claims outsiders cannot reproduce or inspect.
- **Impact:** Opaque judging converts methodological disputes into legitimacy crises, especially when prizes and machine-generated polish amplify incentives to optimize appearances.
- **Watch next:** Publish scores, judge rationales, complete traces, ablations, and reproduction packages; commission independent review before affirming or revising awards.
