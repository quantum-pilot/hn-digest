# The human-in-the-loop is tired

- Score: 296 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48942000) | Link: https://pydantic.dev/articles/the-human-in-the-loop-is-tired

### TL;DR

Pydantic’s Laura Summers argues that LLM coding is simultaneously productive and destabilizing. Models generate plausible code faster than humans can finish, review, and preserve coherent intent, turning developers into supervisors of high-volume output while stripping rewards from implementation. Parallel sessions intensify work, invite late-night prompting, fragment attention, and replace collaboration with solitary machine interaction. The durable skills are judgment, taste, architecture, and knowing a domain deeply enough to detect impressionistic output. Better workflows distill expertise into pre-mortems, review rules, and explicit constraints—but human attention remains the scarce bottleneck.

### Comment pulse

- The reward-loss thesis resonated → some feared hating the job more than losing it, while others found fulfillment in shipped products and polished features.
- A calmer workflow emerged → use one session, plan deeply, inspect each step, refactor immediately, and manually code enjoyable or already-clear work.
- Supervision was not universally worse → managers valued blunt technical iteration without ego management, though downstream reviewers still inherit a large unfamiliar change.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** AI moves the bottleneck from production to judgment, but throughput metrics conceal whether completion, understanding, satisfaction, and learning improve.
- **Impact:** Organizations may gain output while exhausting their quality gate, weakening ownership, mentorship, and accountability until the human becomes blameable.
- **Watch next:** Limit concurrent work, measure review burden, preserve manual practice and collaboration, and reward completed outcomes rather than volume.
