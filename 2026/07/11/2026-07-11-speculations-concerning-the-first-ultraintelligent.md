# Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine (1965) [pdf]

- Score: 88 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48871932) | Link: https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/myl/Good1964.pdf

### TL;DR

In 1965, I. J. Good defined an ultraintelligent machine as surpassing every human intellect and argued that designing better successors would trigger an “intelligence explosion.” He expected an ultraparallel neural network trained through reinforcement, grounded by sensors and action, fluent in language, and supported by computation and retrieval. Much of the paper develops a Hebbian subassembly theory of meaning, recall, Bayesian inference, and memory consolidation. HN marveled at parallels with neural nets, RL, embodiment, tokenization, and retrieval, while noting the missed twentieth-century deadline and speculative hardware.
