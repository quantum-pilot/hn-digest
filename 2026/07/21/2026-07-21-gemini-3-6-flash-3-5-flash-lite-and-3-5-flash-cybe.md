# Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and 3.5 Flash Cyber

- Score: 588 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48993414) | Link: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-6-flash-3-5-flash-lite-3-5-flash-cyber/

### TL;DR

Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and restricted 3.5 Flash Cyber. The workhorse 3.6 costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 output, reportedly using 17% fewer output tokens than 3.5 Flash while improving coding, knowledge work, and computer use. Flash-Lite targets 350 output tokens per second at $0.30/$2.50; Cyber powers CodeMender for approved defenders. Hacker News valued speed and effective task cost but criticized missing cross-vendor comparisons, delayed Pro access, and fragmented consumer, Workspace, enterprise, and IDE packaging.

### Comment pulse

- Speed may be Google’s strategic edge → commenters saw fast, cheap inference as better suited to Search-scale deployment and iterative agent workflows.
- Benchmark framing weakened confidence → Google mainly compared prior Gemini generations — counterpoint: token efficiency can reverse apparent price differences against verbose rivals.
- Product operations repel willing customers → subscription removals, consumer-enterprise splits, billing constraints, and difficult setup drove advocates toward competing APIs.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** For production agents, dollars and seconds per completed task matter more than leaderboard rank or sticker-price tokens.
- **Impact:** High-volume teams gain cheaper orchestration options; security researchers receive a capable model only through a controlled service boundary.
- **Watch next:** Evaluate independent quality, tool-call counts, task completion cost, enterprise provisioning, Cyber access controls, and 3.5 Pro availability.
