# New US homeownership measure puts people first

- Score: 205 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48991139) | Link: https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/new-homeownership-measure-puts-people-first

### TL;DR

The Minneapolis Fed proposes HPOP, the share of all adults who own their residence, as a complement to the familiar owner-occupancy rate, which counts homes rather than people. By this measure, U.S. homeownership is 53%, not 65%; 13.9% of adults live in owner-occupied housing without owning it. The gap is especially revealing for adults under 35—22% HPOP versus 37% owner occupancy—and widens where housing costs are higher. HN welcomed clearer person-level measurement but debated cultural assumptions, metric purpose, and whether supply, taxation, or investment incentives drive affordability.

### Comment pulse

- No metric is universally better → HPOP answers who owns; owner occupancy answers how units are occupied, so policy questions should determine the denominator.
- Multigenerational living complicates interpretation → counting parents or adult children as nonowners may reflect culture — counterpoint: widening gaps in costly states suggest economic pressure.
- Affordability arguments moved beyond measurement → commenters split among supply liberalization, land taxation, social housing, and restrictions on multiple-property ownership.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** HPOP exposes co-residence hidden by unit statistics, but measures legal ownership rather than housing security, autonomy, or household wealth.
- **Impact:** Young adults and expensive states look substantially worse, potentially redirecting housing policy from unit occupancy toward individual wealth access.
- **Watch next:** Compare HPOP with tenure security, household contributions, wealth, age, and later ownership across cultures and housing markets.
