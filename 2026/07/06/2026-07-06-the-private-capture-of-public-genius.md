# The Private Capture of Public Genius

- Score: 179 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48799178) | Link: https://www.wysr.xyz/p/the-private-capture-of-public-genius

### TL;DR

Using Bell Labs’ 1956 patent-opening decree as precedent, the essay argues that frontier AI privatizes value from the public internet corpus while weakening the contribution and trust systems that replenish it. Because individual training inputs cannot be valued reliably, it proposes a “Corpus Royalty”: a fixed share of labs’ gross revenue distributed equally to eligible Americans, alongside copyright claims and licenses. HN accepted the ecosystem-damage concern more readily than the remedy, questioning U.S.-only payouts, global enforceability, LLM-led frontier science, and whether revenue should instead sustain creators and communities.

### Comment pulse

- Global input breaks a national dividend → contributors outside America supplied the corpus, while international enforcement and state-backed foreign models complicate collection.
- Revenue-based payment avoids impossible attribution → contribution values depend on the entire stochastic training set — counterpoint: non-rival public knowledge may not justify extraction fees.
- Ecosystem repair may beat universal checks → commenters preferred funding creators, moderation, open-source maintenance, and anti-slop infrastructure over diffuse individual payouts.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The essay identifies a collective-value problem convincingly, but jumps from diffuse contribution to equal national cash entitlement.
- **Impact:** Gross-revenue royalties recognize corpus dependence without attribution, yet could reward geography rather than contribution and neglect ecosystem repair.
- **Watch next:** Specify eligible revenue, international reciprocity, open-model treatment, governance safeguards, distribution goals, and measurable reinvestment in the information commons.
