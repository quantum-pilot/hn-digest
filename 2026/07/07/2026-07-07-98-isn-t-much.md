# 98% isn't much

- Score: 460 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48816959) | Link: https://whynothugo.nl/journal/2026/07/03/98-isnt-very-much/

### TL;DR

The essay argues 98% reliability or browser support can mislead: a 2% failure rate means one in fifty users, potentially 150 million globally, while aggregate compatibility may badly represent a site’s audience. Robust systems should degrade gracefully instead of excluding the tail. HN’s dominant response was contextual: supporting every configuration has engineering cost, and many businesses need only a viable segment. Counterarguments stressed critical services, network effects, rotating failure cohorts, and reputational damage. Commenters recommended expressing edge probabilities as failure odds—98% to 99% halves failures from one-in-fifty to one-in-a-hundred.

### Comment pulse

- Coverage is an economic choice → long-tail compatibility consumes time and complexity — counterpoint: excluding users without measuring actual audience or fallback cost is careless.
- Failures can compound beyond two percent → different users may fail each release, and network effects amplify one person’s exclusion across groups.
- Failure odds expose edge improvements → moving from 98% to 99% sounds incremental but halves failure frequency from one-in-fifty to one-in-a-hundred.
