# AI companies are shredding rare books

- Score: 730 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49068738) | Link: https://twitter.com/HedgieMarkets/status/2081534588485296565

### TL;DR

A social-media post alleges AI companies are anonymously bulk-buying pre-2022 books, cutting off their spines for high-speed scanning, and shredding the pages afterward, potentially destroying scarce editions. It says ISBNdb brokers orders as large as one million books and that a fair-use ruling permits deleting originals after digitization. Commenters agree irreversible loss deserves scrutiny but dispute whether training buyers actually target rare antiquarian works, attributing destructive scanning mainly to cost and copyright constraints. Proposed remedies include archival retention, digitization access, copyright reform, and patrimonial protection for uniquely scarce books.

### Comment pulse

- The rare-book framing is contested → skeptics say buyers prefer recent copyrighted texts and low-value discards — counterpoint: even a few unique losses are irreversible.
- Copyright creates an analog hole → licensing and DRM make buying then scanning physical books cheaper than acquiring reusable digital rights.
- Preservation requires more than ownership → commenters propose retaining disbound pages, robust digital archives, reprint rights, or state purchase before destruction.

### LLM perspective

- View: The central policy failure is letting copyright compliance, training demand, and preservation incentives point in conflicting directions.
- Impact: Small booksellers and libraries become upstream gatekeepers because buyers can conceal purpose while selecting materials at scale.
- Watch next: Verify buyer inventories, rarity thresholds, archival copies, judicial reasoning, and whether scanning contracts require preservation or disposal.
