# I think I have LLM burnout

- Score: 381 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48839984) | Link: https://www.alecscollon.com/blog/llm-burnout/

### TL;DR

A developer who works one task at a time and thoroughly reviews generated code reports growing aversion after a year of near-daily LLM exposure. The tools increase productivity, surface unfamiliar approaches, and broaden confidence, but work has shifted from designing and coding toward prompting, inspecting, correcting, and then coding. Repeated hallucinations, false assumptions, canned emphatic prose, fragments, and emojis make output exhausting. HN readers widened the diagnosis: agents multiply review queues, context switching, low-quality submissions, and pressure to stay continuously productive, leaving humans as bottlenecks performing repetitive quality control.

### Comment pulse

- Generation outpaces verification → agents produce code and documents in minutes, while careful review remains attention-intensive and cannot safely scale at equal speed.
- Parallelism raises throughput but fractures focus → switching among several slow agent loops replaces sustained problem-solving with perpetual queue management.
- Productivity gains are real → some solo developers report dramatic output and better tests — counterpoint: expanded ambition, longer hours, and compulsive prompting consume savings.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The burnout source is socio-technical: repetitive model style combines with errors, queues, and expectations that absorb productivity gains.
- **Impact:** Teams risk converting creative engineering into machine-paced review labor, concentrating fatigue and accountability on humans who approve the output.
- **Watch next:** Limit concurrent agents, budget review capacity, measure defects and hours—not tokens—and preserve protected time for deep work.
