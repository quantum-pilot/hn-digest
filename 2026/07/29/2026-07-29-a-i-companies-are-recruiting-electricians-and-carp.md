# A.I. companies are recruiting electricians and carpenters by the thousands

- Score: 192 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49098198) | Link: https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/29/business/economy/data-center-electricians-training.html

- TL;DR  
    - AI’s current bottleneck isn’t coders but tradespeople. A massive data‑center build‑out, exemplified by OpenAI’s Michigan project, is vacuuming up electricians and carpenters with intense 70‑hour weeks and big bonuses. Unions and training centers are racing to expand apprenticeships as commercial construction hiring rises while residential lags. Commenters welcome higher blue‑collar pay yet debate how sustainable data‑center work is and how it reshapes nationwide housing costs, non‑AI construction, and local economies.

- Comment pulse  
    - Data‑center construction is cyclical; commenters warn against career pivots on a boom, though electrification should sustain electrician demand — counterpoint: trades may outlast software.  
    - Liquid‑cooled megawatt‑scale racks suggest rising need for plumbers/pipefitters; some worry about dense high‑power, high‑coolant environments and safety practices.  
    - Higher data‑center wages please many but also inflate construction costs, divert labor from housing and public projects, and may vanish once ‘enough’ capacity exists.

- LLM perspective  
    - View: AI progress is now constrained by skilled trades, power and cooling infrastructure as much as by algorithms or GPUs.  
    - Impact: Apprenticeships, unions, inspectors and utilities gain leverage over AI timelines, influencing siting, standards, and local economic spillovers.  
    - Watch next: Wage premiums, post‑project churn, and early attempts to automate installation or routine maintenance inside data centers.
