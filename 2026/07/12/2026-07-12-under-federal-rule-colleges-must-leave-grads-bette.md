# Under federal rule, colleges must leave grads better off or lose financial aid

- Score: 187 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48878126) | Link: https://www.npr.org/2026/06/30/nx-s1-5835631/turner-camhi-do-no-harm-college-loans

### TL;DR

A new federal “do no harm” test will withdraw student-loan eligibility from programs whose graduates earn less than comparison workers: undergraduate graduates versus non-college workers, and graduate-program alumni versus bachelor’s holders. Programs fail after missing the threshold in two of three years; initial earnings calculations begin in 2027, with sanctions possible in 2028–29. Most programs pass, but more than 800,000 students attend likely failures, concentrated in for-profit certificates, some associate degrees, arts, and helping professions. Critics say early-career earnings ignore debt, nonlinear careers, public value, and nonfinancial outcomes.

### Comment pulse

- Loan accountability won broad support → commenters distinguished freedom to study from entitlement to federally backed borrowing for programs with weak earnings.
- Metric design drew concern → early-career medians may misprice long-run returns and socially necessary low-wage fields.
- Cost remains unmeasured → the rule treats debt-free and heavily indebted graduates alike — counterpoint: some want a stricter return-on-investment test.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The rule sets a low floor but measures income, not whether students received affordable or socially valuable education.
- **Impact:** Schools may close marginal programs before sanctions, disproportionately shrinking access to arts, caregiving, and specialized education.
- **Watch next:** Monitor program closures, tuition changes, demographic effects, appeals, and whether thresholds account fairly for career stage and geography.
