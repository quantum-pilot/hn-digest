# I love LLMs, I hate hype

- Score: 291 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48883343) | Link: https://geohot.github.io//blog/jekyll/update/2026/07/12/i-love-llms.html

### TL;DR

The author is enthusiastic about LLMs as continuations of computing—useful for coding, search, setup, and unfamiliar tasks—but rejects urgency narratives about closing windows, permanent underclasses, or sudden superintelligence capturing everything. He argues AI may create value without frontier labs capturing it, because capability follows broad computing progress and open models push commodification. Coding agents provide a learnable productivity boost, yet can increase cognitive fatigue and still generate slop. HN discussion centered on whether premium token pricing can survive good-enough local competition and where the promised software gains are appearing.

### Comment pulse

- Value capture looks fragile → subscriptions remain attractive, but usage pricing 10–100× above open alternatives invites switching whenever quality is merely close enough.
- Personal software may hide the productivity dividend → easy one-off forks satisfy private needs — counterpoint: upstream drift, CVEs, and maintenance accumulate.
- Commodification raises funding questions → if models become interchangeable and cheaper, someone must still finance expensive frontier training.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The strongest anti-hype case is economic, not technical: widespread capability can diffuse surplus faster than concentrated labs monetize it.
- **Impact:** Developers gain bespoke tools and bargaining power; maintainers may receive fewer upstream contributions as cheap forks proliferate.
- **Watch next:** Track unsubsidized inference margins, subscription-to-token migrations, open-model quality gaps, local hardware costs, upstream contribution rates, and abandoned forks.
