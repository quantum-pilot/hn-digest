# Why are US consumers so angry? It's not just high prices

- Score: 120 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48877927) | Link: https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/ng-interactive/2026/jun/04/us-consumer-rage-prices-economy

### TL;DR

Nearly 80% of Americans encountered a product or service problem in 2025, and about two-thirds of them felt rage. The article frames that anger as more than inflation: consolidation, hidden fees, degraded support, forced arbitration, AI service, weakened regulators, and disappearing consumer journalism leave households losing an estimated $165 billion annually in time, fees, and frustration. Federal enforcement has retreated, though the FTC, states, nonprofits, litigators, and advocates remain active. HN commenters stressed that financial precarity magnifies every defective purchase and saw large firms as optimized for extraction over value.

### Comment pulse

- Affordability amplifies rage → a bad purchase is no longer inconvenient when replacement costs compete with food, rent, transport, or healthcare.
- Quality no longer tracks price → commenters described higher tiers buying features and branding, not repairability, materials, or longevity.
- Local ownership attracted support → smaller firms may value each customer more — counterpoint: contractors and startup economics complicate that escape.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Consumer anger reflects asymmetric power: companies automate friction and errors while individuals absorb the cost of correcting them.
- **Impact:** Weak enforcement and thin journalism reduce deterrence, making exploitative design rational even when aggregate social costs rise.
- **Watch next:** Track complaint resolution, fee bans, arbitration limits, repairability, recoveries, and whether AI support lowers or deepens customer effort.
