# Autopsy Study Finds Replicating SARS-CoV-2 in the Hearts of Long Covid

- Score: 125 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48877214) | Link: https://my.uscap.org/uscap/program/S0tc675/index.cfm?pgid=5167&sid=14770&abid=51228

### TL;DR

A RECOVER autopsy-cohort poster examined left-ventricular tissue from 74 people who died at least 60 days after SARS-CoV-2 infection. Eleven had viral reverse-strand RNA, treated as evidence of replication; 9 of those 11 had cardiac long-COVID symptoms versus 23 of 63 without it. Positive hearts more often showed ventricular dilation, increased heart-weight-to-wall-thickness ratios, pericardial fluid, viral signal in myocytes, and 44 altered inflammatory or host-response genes. The findings support a persistence mechanism in a subset, but the observational decedent sample cannot establish causation or treatment benefit.

### Comment pulse

- Case definition needs precision → the inclusion threshold was 60 days, while time since last positive test differed markedly between viral-positive and negative groups.
- Persistence does not guarantee antiviral responsiveness → commenters cited 15- and 25-day Paxlovid trials with null primary outcomes, without replication-selected enrollment.
- Broader infection caution resonated → readers favored vaccination over deliberate exposure — counterpoint: anecdotes included vaccine-associated myocarditis and cannot quantify comparative risk.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Reverse-strand detection narrows the persistence hypothesis from leftover antigen toward replication, but only within sampled fatal cases.
- **Impact:** Biomarker-defined long-COVID subgroups may need different trials; treating an undifferentiated syndrome could conceal benefit in a small viral-positive population.
- **Watch next:** Replicate in living cohorts, quantify viral load and viability, test cardiac imaging, and enroll replication-positive patients in trials.
