# Don't you mean extinct?

- Score: 179 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48881830) | Link: https://fabiensanglard.net/extinct/index.html

### TL;DR

Fabien Sanglard uses stop-motion expert Phil Tippett’s transition on Jurassic Park—from fearing CGI extinction to shaping digital dinosaur movement—as a model for programmers facing LLMs. His prescription is adaptation without surrender: learn how models work, use agents to accelerate exploration and routine work, but still understand architecture, review output, enforce personal style rules, demand smaller PRs, and strengthen tests. He expects smaller teams and higher quality bars; HN commenters questioned whether volume equals value, whether reviewing generated code erases speed gains, and whether automated tests verify intended behavior.

### Comment pulse

- Output divided readers → supporters cited faster debugging of costly issues — counterpoint: critics said governance, maintainability, and user value—not code volume—set the pace.
- Assistance beats authorship for some → agents excel at codebase search, flow reconstruction, bug triage, tests, and review without replacing implementation understanding.
- Adaptation can preserve employment but not joy → new tools may eliminate paid craft even when practitioners can continue it as a hobby.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Tippett’s success came from transferring domain expertise into new tooling; tool adoption alone would not have created convincing motion.
- **Impact:** Experienced engineers can encode standards and judge tradeoffs; juniors need protected practice that agents cannot silently complete for them.
- **Watch next:** Measure end-to-end lead time, escaped defects, review comprehension, test behavior coverage, developer fatigue, and team size—not generated lines.
