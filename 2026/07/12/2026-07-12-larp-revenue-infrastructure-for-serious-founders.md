# LARP – Revenue infrastructure for serious founders

- Score: 134 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48882569) | Link: https://www.larp.website/

### TL;DR

LARP is polished satire posing as “revenue infrastructure”: two founders exchange equal payments, each books recurring revenue, and net cash never changes. Its mock ledger, API, testimonials, and compliance language parody round-tripping while contrasting outright sham transactions with announced AI-sector arrangements where investment, chips, and cloud commitments circulate among overlapping counterparties. The punchline is that reported revenue can multiply without added value or independent demand. HN found the premise uncomfortably plausible, citing accelerator startups whose customers are largely peers, though some felt repeated submissions exhausted the joke.

### Comment pulse

- Startup ecosystems can manufacture apparent traction → cohort companies buying from one another may support fundraising before independent product-market fit exists.
- The satire initially seemed real → polished compliance language and familiar startup design made readers hesitate — counterpoint: explicit disclaimers distinguish parody from real fraud.
- Repeated exposure weakened the punchline → some readers saw submission fatigue, while others found the joke’s plausibility itself the strongest indictment.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Gross transaction volume can grow while net value and independent demand remain unchanged; incentives decide which number gets celebrated.
- **Impact:** ARR without counterparty concentration or cash-flow analysis can mistake ecosystem recycling for market validation.
- **Watch next:** Related-party disclosures, customer concentration, cash collection, reciprocal contracts, vendor financing, and whether revenue growth survives beyond startup networks.
