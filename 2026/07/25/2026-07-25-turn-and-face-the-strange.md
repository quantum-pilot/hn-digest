# Turn And Face The Strange

- Score: 126 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49051369) | Link: https://fly.io/blog/kurt-scott-money-sprites/

- TL;DR  
  - Fly.io’s founder explains a strategic pivot: betting that AI coding agents will dominate software creation, so the company is refocusing on “Sprites,” semi-disposable cloud computers tailored for agents, with durable storage, cloning, and secure connectors. Fly has raised new funding and hired ex‑Docker CEO Scott Johnston, while founder Kurt Mackey moves to an advisory role. Hacker News readers like the vision but question Sprites’ severe reliability issues, Fly’s historical ops problems, and whether AI sandboxes are too commoditized to sustain a venture-backed business.

- Comment pulse  
  - Fly.io / Sprites are unreliable → users report frequent outages, data loss, zombie instances, and slow or absent paid support, forcing migrations despite liking the vision.  
  - Agent pivot looks risky → many see AI sandboxes as commodity atop any VM; doubt Sprites’ moat or AWS disruption—counterpoint: some report recent reliability improvements.  
  - AI triggers identity crises → commenters note people and companies questioning building products agents can one-shot, urging shifts toward high-impact domains like energy.

- LLM perspective  
  - View: Sprites’ thesis—agents need cheap, durable, resumable “personal computers,” not stateless sandboxes—makes sense but demands ruthless execution and stability.  
  - Impact: If Fly.io nails reliability and DX, it could become backend for agent workflows; otherwise incumbents or self-hosting win.  
  - Watch next: independent benchmarks of Sprite reliability, cost vs. DIY GPUs, and whether non-developers touch Sprites indirectly through higher-level tools.
