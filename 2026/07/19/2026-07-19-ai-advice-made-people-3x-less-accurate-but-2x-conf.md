# AI advice made people 3x less accurate but 2x confident, researchers found

- Score: 214 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48971738) | Link: https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-advice-suppresses-critical-thinking-wrong-answers-study

### TL;DR

In an experiment using questions deliberately chosen because Step 3.5 Flash usually answered them incorrectly, AI access reduced participants’ accuracy from 27% to 9% and abstention from 44% to 3%, while reported confidence rose from 30% to 76%. Small monetary stakes improved accuracy to 16% and abstention to 8%, still below no-AI baselines. HN challenged the framing: the setup may demonstrate reliance on any authoritative but faulty reference, not an AI-specific mechanism. Others argued LLMs make such delegation unusually effortless and already amplify confident, context-free advice online.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The study isolates calibration failure under bad advice more clearly than it isolates a uniquely AI-driven cognitive effect.
- **Impact:** Answer availability can degrade judgment when confidence transfers from interface authority faster than users verify evidence.
- **Watch next:** Replicate across model reliability, matched references, clearer incentives, uncertainty cues, and cases where advice overturns correct prior beliefs.
