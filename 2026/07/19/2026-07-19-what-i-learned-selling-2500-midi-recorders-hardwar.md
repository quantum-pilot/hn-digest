# What I learned selling 2,500 MIDI recorders: Hardware is not so hard

- Score: 385 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48966713) | Link: https://chipweinberger.com/articles/20260719-hardware-is-not-so-hard

### TL;DR

Jamcorder, an automatic MIDI recorder for pianos, has sold 2,500 units and become a self-sustaining business. Its solo founder says hardware was easier than expected: he assembled the first 500 devices in four days, while roughly 200,000 lines of firmware, app, and manufacturing software consumed three years. That result was deliberately engineered through a 25-component board, one-screw assembly, simple molding, off-the-shelf parts, removed features, strong margins, and local QA. HN admired the product but rejected broad generalization, citing certification, inventory cash flow, scale, reliability, supply chains, and complex electronics.

### Comment pulse

- Simple design made hardware manageable → certified modules and one PCB avoided complexity — counterpoint: scale, certification, supply shocks, and cash exposure remain structurally unforgiving.
- Software dominated this product → three years and 200,000 lines outweighed assembly, but commenters noted software’s cheap iteration masks hardware’s irreversible mistakes.
- Customers valued graceful independence → storing ordinary MIDI files on a card reduced fears that a discontinued companion app would strand recordings.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The transferable lesson is product scoping, not hardware ease: remove features until manufacturing, testing, margins, and support become tractable.
- **Impact:** A narrow, value-dense device can let one founder reach sustainable medium volume without recreating the complexity of mass-market electronics.
- **Watch next:** Seek the promised follow-ups on injection molding, certification, anti-counterfeiting, supplier selection, failure rates, returns, margins, and inventory financing.
