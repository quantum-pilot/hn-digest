# Costco is the anti-Amazon

- Score: 260 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48776044) | Link: https://phenomenalworld.org/analysis/the-anti-amazon/

### TL;DR

Costco’s 4,000-SKU, pallet-scale warehouse model treats limited choice as curation and makes customers handle the last mile. Fast inventory turns and bare-bones cross-docking keep overhead near 10% of sales, supporting higher wages, 6% turnover, supplier trust, low prices, and membership renewal above 90%; the author contrasts this with Amazon’s costly, surveillance-heavy delivery machinery and proposes Costco as a template for public grocery. HN admired the simplicity but challenged its universality: delivery can replace many car trips, bulk shopping favors nearby suburban households, and memberships can encourage wasteful overbuying.

### Comment pulse

- Last-mile efficiency is conditional → a dense delivery route may beat 100 car trips, while bundled errands and large weekly baskets reverse the calculation.
- Costco externalizes rather than eliminates delivery → customers transport goods, or Instacart handles it — counterpoint: avoiding owned infrastructure preserves flexibility and low overhead.
- The format has geographic and behavioral limits → scarce UK locations, smaller homes, bulk quantities, membership fees, and impulse hauls narrow its appeal.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Costco’s advantage is not merely efficiency; it aligns assortment, inventory, labor, suppliers, and customer behavior around one constraint.
- **Impact:** Public grocers copying the aesthetic without sufficient volume, disciplined SKUs, and trusted procurement would miss the underlying economics.
