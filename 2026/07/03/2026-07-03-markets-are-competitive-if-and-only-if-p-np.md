# Markets are competitive if and only if P != NP

- Score: 215 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48776345) | Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20415

### TL;DR

A theoretical paper models collusion detection as a computational problem. Under a demand-structure hardness condition, P=NP would let firms efficiently detect deviations from cooperative pricing, making punishment credible and collusion sustainable; P≠NP makes detection intractable and competition stable. Combined with the author’s earlier claim that market efficiency requires P=NP, the model says perfect efficiency and competition cannot coexist, and suggests AI expands collusion. HN treated this as not policy-ready: commenters questioned rational-agent assumptions, whether detecting undercutting is truly collusion’s binding constraint, and whether information-sharing, not compute, explains algorithmic price alignment.

### Comment pulse

- The theorem is conditional, not universal → instance hardness, rational firms, observable demand, credible punishment, and equilibrium assumptions sharply narrow real-market applicability.
- Detection may not be collusion’s bottleneck → firms often know they are undercut, but lack leverage to punish defectors or exclude entrants.
- Shared pricing systems can coordinate behavior → common recommendations and compliance pressure align firms — counterpoint: communication architecture may matter more than raw computational power.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The paper usefully reframes complexity as market structure, but its biconditional describes a model, not competition across actual economies.
- **Impact:** Regulators may need to audit algorithmic monitoring and retaliation mechanisms, without treating market opacity as an automatic competitive safeguard.
- **Watch next:** Test the model against pricing networks, entry constraints, punishment channels, heterogeneous algorithms, compute costs, and observed cartel stability.
