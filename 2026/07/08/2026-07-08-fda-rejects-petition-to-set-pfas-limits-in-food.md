# FDA rejects petition to set PFAS limits in food

- Score: 200 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48831785) | Link: https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/08/us-food-and-drug-administration-rejects-petition-to-set-pfas-limits-in-food

### TL;DR

The FDA rejected a group’s petition for binding PFOA and PFOS limits in seafood and milk, saying evidence was insufficient while promising nonbinding action levels. The article argues food is a major PFAS exposure route, citing contamination tests, limited monitoring, entry paths including pesticides, packaging, polluted water, and sewage-sludge fertilizer, plus differences between enforceable tolerances and advisory thresholds. HN emphasized that the EPA’s 4-ppt rule covers drinking water, not food; commenters focused on contaminated biosolids and political inconsistency, while a thread discussed blood donation as a possible exposure-reduction mechanism.

### Comment pulse

- Water rules leave a food gap → the EPA’s 4-ppt drinking-water limit does not answer whether contaminated seafood or milk should be saleable.
- Biosolids can spread contamination into agriculture → historical sludge fertilizer use may render farms unsafe, making systematic soil and food testing essential.
- Blood donation may lower donors’ measured PFAS → immediate transfusion benefits matter — counterpoint: commenters questioned transferring contaminants and warned against self-treatment.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The dispute is evidentiary and legal: whether current data justify enforceable sale limits now, not whether PFAS exposure exists.
- **Impact:** Nonbinding action levels preserve agency flexibility but offer weaker removal obligations and less predictable protection than legally enforceable tolerances.
- **Watch next:** Lawsuit outcome, FDA action-level scope, assay sensitivity, representative food surveillance, source attribution, biosolids testing, and cumulative-exposure modeling.
