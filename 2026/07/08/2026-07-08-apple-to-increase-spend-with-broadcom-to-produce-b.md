# Apple to increase spend with Broadcom to produce billions more U.S. chips

- Score: 278 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48830565) | Link: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/07/apple-to-increase-spend-with-broadcom-to-produce-billions-more-us-chips/

### TL;DR

Apple says a multiyear Broadcom agreement will exceed $30 billion, produce more than 15 billion US-made chips, and support hundreds of jobs. The company’s largest American Manufacturing Program commitment includes $1.5 billion to modernize Broadcom’s Fort Collins, Colorado facility, which will make FBAR radio-frequency filters and other wireless components—not Apple’s processors. Apple places the deal within a four-year, $600 billion US investment pledge. HN questioned whether it represents new supply-chain relocation or politically timed expansion of an existing relationship, while debating tariffs, capital intensity, and job counts.

### Comment pulse

- Technical scope matters → Fort Collins will make FBAR radio-frequency filters and connectivity components, not the advanced application processors many readers inferred.
- Industrial policy split readers → supporters view domestic capability as incremental seeding — counterpoint: tariff volatility can deter long-horizon factory investment and raise input costs.
- Employment framing drew skepticism → $30 billion supporting only hundreds of jobs reflects capital-intensive productivity, but weakens mass-employment rhetoric.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The announcement is primarily a supply-chain and political-risk commitment, not a labor-intensive reshoring program.
- **Impact:** Long-term volume can justify specialized US manufacturing capacity while leaving advanced processor fabrication and broader dependencies elsewhere.
- **Watch next:** Separate incremental spending from existing contracts; track Fort Collins capex, production volumes, supplier concentration, and durable policy support.
