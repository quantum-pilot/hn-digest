### TL;DR

Twenty 1986 lectures by Hal Abelson and Gerald Jay Sussman present the Hewlett-Packard course behind the first edition of Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs. The sequence moves from Lisp, procedures, higher-order functions, and compound data through symbolic manipulation, generic operators, state, objects, streams, interpreters, logic programming, register machines, compilation, storage, and garbage collection. Licensed under commercially compatible Creative Commons terms, they remain useful with the second edition because themes and ordering survived code revisions. HN recommended lectures first, the book as reference, and MIT Scheme or Racket for exercises.
