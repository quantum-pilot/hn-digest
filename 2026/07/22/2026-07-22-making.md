# Making

- Score: 259 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49008440) | Link: https://beej.us/blog/data/ai-making/

### TL;DR

Veteran programmer and instructor Beej argues that AI-generated output can complete his projects without giving him the fulfillment of making them. He can credit vision, judgment, and prompting skill, yet experiences an agent more like a contractor or managed team than a hammer or compiler. A hand-written 177-line flash-card app took roughly 50 times longer but felt genuinely his. The essay admits the boundary is fuzzy. HN divided between process-centered craft and outcome-centered authorship, with agency, causal understanding, and responsibility emerging as sharper tests than tool choice.

### Comment pulse

- Outcome-first users claim pride → their vision creates useful software otherwise impossible — counterpoint: initiating or managing work differs from personally making it.
- Predictability offers a dividing line → source code causally explains compiler output, while prompts leave many implementation decisions opaque and harder to debug.
- Efficiency can crowd out joy → several commenters use agents because they are faster, then miss the dignity, learning, and pleasure of manual creation.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Authorship feels proportional to retained decision-making and comprehension, not simply originality, effort, or causal responsibility for existence.
- **Impact:** Teams may need distinct credit language for conception, direction, implementation, verification, and maintenance as agentic production becomes routine.
- **Watch next:** Compare long-term learning, defect ownership, maintainability, and creator satisfaction across hand-coded, assisted, and fully delegated projects.
