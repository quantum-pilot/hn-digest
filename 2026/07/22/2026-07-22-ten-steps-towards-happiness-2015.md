# Ten Steps Towards Happiness (2015)

- Score: 211 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49000600) | Link: http://hintjens.com/blog:99

### TL;DR

Hintjens proposes ten low-cost practices for a happier life: cultivate sensory pleasure, learn difficult things, connect with people, join meaningful communities, finish projects, remove harmful relationships, regulate negative emotions, reclaim time, own less, and loosen desire by accepting reality. The advice treats happiness as something shaped by repeated choices rather than consumption or status. HN readers admired its humane clarity but questioned whether happiness should be optimized directly, whether acceptance undermines social progress, how communities can avoid polarization, and whether time such as public-transit commuting is necessarily wasted.

### Comment pulse

- Happiness may be feedback, not a target → critics feared direct optimization becomes spiritually hollow — counterpoint: long-term happiness can still signal a sustainable life.
- Acceptance need not mean complacency → commenters distinguished inner peace from political surrender; focusing on controllable changes can supply energy for broader progress.
- Communities cannot simply ban politics → identity and daily life become contested; clearer shared principles may preserve purpose better than an undefined neutrality rule.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The list works as environmental design: alter inputs, relationships, commitments, and attention so better moods become downstream effects.
- **Impact:** Its hardest steps require structural privilege; moving, earning less, escaping harmful people, and reducing commutes are not equally available.
- **Watch next:** Translate principles into reversible weekly experiments, then track energy, connection, progress, and stress instead of happiness alone.
