# It's a shame what's happened to radio

- Score: 212 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48999825) | Link: https://blog.jimgrey.net/2026/07/21/its-a-shame-whats-happened-to-radio-3/

### TL;DR

A former college and commercial DJ argues radio lost its defining value: a live local personality making listeners feel connected, not merely delivering songs. He blames advertiser-driven demographics, post-deregulation ownership consolidation, voicetracking and satellite formats, plus granular ratings that punish talk and reward shorter, predictable blocks. The result is an anonymous appliance that cannot beat personal streaming on convenience. HN largely mourned lost companionship, serendipity, and cultural guidance, while pointing to public, campus, community, global internet radio, and livestreams as surviving or replacement forms.

### Comment pulse

- Shared simultaneity still matters → listeners valued knowing a nearby DJ and audience heard the same selection, making lonely work or travel communal.
- Human curation teaches taste → DJs expose young listeners to unfamiliar culture beyond search terms and engagement algorithms — counterpoint: streaming recommendations can broaden discovery.
- Noncommercial radio remains the refuge → commenters praised college, public, and volunteer stations for eclectic programming, local knowledge, and surprising conversation.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Commercial radio optimized away the only feature streaming cannot cheaply copy: accountable, place-specific human presence.
- **Impact:** Local artists, civic information, and emerging listeners lose a shared discovery layer when programming becomes centralized and demographic-driven.
