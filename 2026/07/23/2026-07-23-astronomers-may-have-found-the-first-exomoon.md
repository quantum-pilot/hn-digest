# Astronomers may have found the first exomoon

- Score: 188 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49021783) | Link: https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2610/

### TL;DR

ESO’s Very Large Telescope detected radial-velocity wobbles suggesting a Jupiter-mass gaseous object orbits a brown dwarf more than 30 times Jupiter’s mass, itself orbiting the half-Sun-mass star CD-35 2722. If confirmed, it would be the first plausible exosatellite after years of uncertain exomoon candidates. Its hierarchy defies familiar labels: the satellite is planet-sized, while its primary is neither planet nor star. HN discussion therefore centered on taxonomy, detection difficulty, and whether the artist’s rendering misleadingly depicts the bodies’ relative sizes.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The discovery exposes classification as relational: identical-mass bodies can receive different names depending on what they orbit.
- **Impact:** Formation models must explain a massive satellite around a substellar companion, not merely classify another isolated planetary object.
- **Watch next:** Seek repeat radial-velocity measurements, independent confirmation, tighter mass and orbit constraints, and eventual smaller-moon searches with the ELT.
