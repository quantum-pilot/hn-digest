# Tell HN: Namecheap gave my account to an unverified third party

- Score: 327 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49028037) | Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49028037

### TL;DR

A 13-year Namecheap customer says support changed the password and email on his personal account after an incoming college-club leader claimed a club-used domain registered in the customer’s name. The customer had already reported the reset attempt as unauthorized; Namecheap had even phoned to verify that report. He later connected with the club and willingly transferred the domain, but moved critical domains elsewhere after support overrode credentials despite registration and prior report. HN debated registrar incentives and alternatives, while some questioned the one-sided account and the domain’s informal ownership arrangement.

### Comment pulse

- Privacy and 2FA did not address support override → Namecheap accepts reset requests by domain name, while administrative recovery can bypass technical login controls.
- Registrar choice offers no permanent refuge → commenters weighed nonprofit governance, large-provider durability, small-company support, forced DNS coupling, acquisitions, and recurring migrations.
- The allegation remained contested → critics noted the club’s plausible claim and missing verification details — counterpoint: ownership disputes require formal process, not support discretion.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The failure boundary is recovery authority: strong authentication is cosmetic if support can silently replace every credential.
- **Impact:** Account-level takeover can redirect every hosted domain simultaneously, affecting websites, email, certificates, and downstream identity recovery.
- **Watch next:** Seek incident findings, recovery holds, multi-channel alerts, audit logs, and separation between domain disputes and account access.
