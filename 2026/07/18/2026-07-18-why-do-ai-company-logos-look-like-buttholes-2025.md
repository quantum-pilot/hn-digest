# Why do AI company logos look like buttholes? (2025)

- Score: 411 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48956924) | Link: https://velvetshark.com/ai-company-logos-that-look-like-buttholes

### TL;DR

A satirical survey says many AI brands converge on circular marks with central openings, radiating elements, soft curves, and gradients, creating an unfortunate anatomical resemblance. Beneath the joke is a critique of conformity: circles signal friendliness, wholeness, and legitimacy; pareidolia supplies the resemblance; copying and committee risk aversion erase distinctiveness. The author links this wave to earlier design fashions and recommends sharper geometry, inventive negative space, asymmetry, flat color, and diverse audience testing. The case is observational rather than quantitative and includes an acknowledged mistake about OpenAI’s supposed gradient.

### Comment pulse

- Details weaken the case → OpenAI has no gradient, and Claude’s click animation was inconsistent — counterpoint: the animation strengthened the joke for others.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The stronger diagnosis is category sameness, not anatomy: safe symbolism helps legitimacy while sacrificing recognition and meaning.
- **Impact:** Interchangeable marks increase acquisition costs and misattribution as crowded AI products struggle to build durable visual memory.
- **Watch next:** Test brand recall, mistaken identity, motif frequency, audience associations, and whether newer entrants abandon radial forms.
