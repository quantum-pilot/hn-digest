# If You Build It, They Will Come

- Score: 230 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48959090) | Link: https://www.benlandautaylor.com/p/if-you-build-it-they-will-come

### TL;DR

The fastest way to enter a community, the author argues, is to organize gatherings around its core activity or help existing organizers. Events seem like natural products of a social scene, but they exist because a few people handle invitations, logistics, and follow-through; those contributors notice one another and often become leaders. Modern alienation may therefore reflect too many social consumers and too few producers. The practical remedy is local: stop waiting for social fabric to appear, create something useful, and invite people you want to know.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Communities are coordination systems, not audiences; their scarcest input is someone willing to convert interest into recurring structure.
- **Impact:** Hosting accelerates belonging, reputation, and leadership, but sustained success requires shared ownership before the organizer burns out.
- **Watch next:** Measure repeat attendance, volunteer conversion, co-host emergence, organizer workload, inclusion, and whether commercialization weakens community identity.
