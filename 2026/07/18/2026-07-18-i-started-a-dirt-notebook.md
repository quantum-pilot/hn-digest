# I started a “dirt notebook”

- Score: 105 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48954149) | Link: https://pinewind.bearblog.dev/i-started-a-dirt-notebook/

### TL;DR

A compulsively tidy note-taker created a deliberately unpleasant dirt notebook to lower the psychological cost of writing. Its poor paper, awkward binding, and cheap ballpoint make polish impractical, so podcast quotes, story ideas, personal reflections, and research prompts can coexist without structure. After a week, rereading recovered thoughts that were fading; the goal is simply to fill it and practice accepting mess. HN agreed that imperfection enables capture, but many preferred one notebook, loose disposable sheets, or an intentionally spoiled first page over adding another note-taking system to maintain.

### Comment pulse

- Separation felt unnecessary to some → learning to waste pages lets one always-present notebook hold stains, sketches, tasks, drafts, and polished work alike.
- Note systems can become productive procrastination → every organizational layer competes with the actual goal, so several commenters favored cheap, disposable capture.
- Deliberate damage grants permission → scribbling the first page, using bad paper, or scattering loose sheets prevents pristine materials from intimidating spontaneous thought.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The notebook works by changing incentives, not storage: low perceived value makes capture immediate and editing optional.
- **Impact:** An imperfect medium recovers fleeting ideas and reduces perfectionism, but another system may recreate the organizational burden it solves.
- **Watch next:** After filling it, assess capture frequency, useful rediscoveries, review time, discarded pages, and whether better materials revive perfectionism.
