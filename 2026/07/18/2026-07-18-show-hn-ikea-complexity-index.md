# Show HN: IKEA Complexity Index

- Score: 141 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48955042) | Link: https://ikea.greg.technology/

### TL;DR

An unofficial index ranks roughly 20,000 IKEA products by expected assembly burden. Its complexity score multiplies instruction steps by total parts, then converts that score into estimated time using a factor derived from self-reported build times. A separate one-to-five ease rating averages IKEA customer reviews, including approximations for combination products; users can also filter for products with one instruction PDF. The tool exposes hidden labor before purchase, but its linear proxy cannot capture confusing instructions, awkward sequencing, teamwork, skill, disassembly, or products that look deceptively simple.

### Comment pulse

- Assembly time changes effective price → one commenter added labor at $30 per hour, finding roughly 24–51% premiums — counterpoint: some enjoy building furniture.
- Assembly scores omit reversibility → owners reported furniture that required destructive tools or became effectively permanent after glued dowels.
- Simple-looking products can still confuse → user failures prompted the creator to add review-derived ease ratings; sentiment analysis could refine the estimate further.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Assembly difficulty is multidimensional; parts and steps estimate workload, while reviews reveal ambiguity, ergonomics, and misleading apparent simplicity.
- **Impact:** Adding time valuation turns a furniture catalog into a total-cost tool, though enjoyment makes labor value user-specific.
- **Watch next:** Add confidence intervals, skill profiles, team size, disassembly, instruction quality, return risk, and comparisons across flat-pack vendors.
