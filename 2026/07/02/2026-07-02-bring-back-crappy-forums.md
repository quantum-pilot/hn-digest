# Bring back crappy forums

- Score: 531 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48755731) | Link: https://tedium.co/2026/07/01/online-web-forums-retrospective/

### TL;DR

Old forums were fragile, insecure, and awkward, yet their small scale, persistent threads, recognizable regulars, and local ownership often produced deeper communities than engagement-driven social media. The essay traces forums from Usenet and 1994’s WIT through WWWBoard, phpBB, Slash, BBCode, and Discourse, arguing novelty and outsourced maintenance pulled users toward centralized platforms. HN disputed novelty as the main cause: Reddit-style trees improve navigation, while forums demand scrolling. But commenters missed slow discussions, durable identity, hobby knowledge, and community memory, noting that hosting, upgrades, spam, and security—not format alone—killed many sites.

### Comment pulse

- Persistence changes discussion quality → forum threads accumulate expertise over months or years; Reddit/HN reward immediate reactions before experienced users arrive.
- Identity creates community memory → avatars, signatures, and recurring names turn debates into relationships, making life events and local meetups part of the archive.
- Ranking improves navigation → comment trees surface useful branches — counterpoint: votes invite karma farming, fragment shared conversation, and bury minority voices.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Forums optimized for belonging and cumulative knowledge; social feeds optimize distribution, recency, and measurable engagement.
- **Impact:** Independent communities trade platform convenience for moderation control, durable archives, distinct norms, and stronger member investment.
- **Watch next:** Build forum hosting with painless upgrades, portable identity, spam resistance, searchable archives, email integration, and optional chronological views.
