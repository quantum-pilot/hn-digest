# The fall of the theorem economy

- Score: 252 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48758048) | Link: https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/the-fall-of-the-theorem-economy

### TL;DR

The essay argues academia’s theorem-centered reward system mistook a measurable proxy for mathematics’ real product: human clarity, concepts, intuition, and reusable theory. AI may soon automate many technical proofs and formalize them correctly, yet produce opaque, noncanonical blobs that neither expand understanding nor integrate cleanly into shared libraries. This could capture prestige, strand essential cleanup work, and erode research and teaching incentives. HN discussion envisioned automated truth mining leaving humans to visualize and interpret, while debating proof standards, superhuman abstractions, open science, and vanishing entry-level work.

### Comment pulse

- Correctness and understanding are distinct → a verified result can still lack the abstractions, explanations, and reusable interfaces that make mathematics cumulative.
- Human comparative advantage may shift → visualization and cross-domain intuition remain valuable — counterpoint: future AI could invent abstractions inaccessible to human cognition.
- Automation may narrow career entry → junior researchers typically learn through small problems that powerful systems could absorb first.
