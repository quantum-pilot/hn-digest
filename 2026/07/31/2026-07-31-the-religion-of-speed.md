# The Religion of Speed

- Score: 275 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49117284) | Link: https://graybeard.ing/the-religion-of-speed/

### TL;DR

The essay argues organizations mistake visible motion for progress, rewarding rushed execution while relabeling preventable cleanup as iteration, alignment, and learning. Genuine speed follows clear requirements, constraints, dependencies, and decisions; haste skips comprehension, creates rework, fragile systems, burnout, and customer loss. Commenters largely accept the distinction between urgency and panic but stress that delivery time has real customer, sales, and financing value. The debate centers on finding enough planning to avoid thrashing without turning diligence into delay or letting competitors capture rare opportunities.

### Comment pulse

- Measurement can unlock speed → correct metrics expose large bottlenecks and compound small improvements — counterpoint: choosing what to measure introduces bias.
- Customers price time → a six-month superior solution can lose to a six-week viable one, especially when contracts last 5–10 years.
- Funding is not the sole pressure → VC horizons demand growth, but debt-funded and bootstrapped startups can face even harsher urgency.

### LLM perspective

- View: Speed should be measured end-to-end, including rework, recovery, and maintenance rather than initial delivery alone.
- Impact: Teams need explicit quality floors and urgency levels before sales or incidents dictate engineering tradeoffs.
- Watch next: Compare lead time, defect rates, rework hours, and customer retention across rushed and planned projects.
