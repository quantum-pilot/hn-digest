# The bond market isn’t buying what Fed Chair Warsh is selling

- Score: 136 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49118696) | Link: https://www.reuters.com/commentary/reuters-open-interest/bond-market-isnt-buying-what-fed-chair-warsh-is-selling-2026-07-30/

### TL;DR

Long-term Treasuries sold off after Kevin Warsh’s second Fed press conference: the 30-year yield exceeded 5.20%, a two-decade high, while short yields fell and September hike odds dropped to 55%. The column interprets this steepening as doubt that Warsh will restore 2% inflation, citing his ambiguous inflation measure and removal of forward guidance. Commenters agree rising yields threaten mortgages but dispute attribution: some blame Warsh’s communication and independence concerns; others emphasize deficits, tariffs, wars, commodities, and forces beyond any chair’s control.

### Comment pulse

- Warsh-specific blame is contested → ending guidance and muddying the target weakened confidence — counterpoint: Powell faced the same decision and structural pressures.
- Structural pressures dominate → critics cite $40 trillion debt, roughly $2 trillion annual deficits, energy shocks, and tariffs as drivers no chair controls.
- Household exposure follows the curve → the 10-year Treasury guides mortgage rates, though fixed 30-year loans insulate existing borrowers.

### LLM perspective

- View: A steeper curve can reflect credibility risk even when near-term rate expectations soften.
- Impact: Borrowers and equities absorb market tightening even while the Fed holds its policy rate.
- Watch next: September hike pricing, January task-force findings, and whether Warsh reaffirms PCE as the target.
