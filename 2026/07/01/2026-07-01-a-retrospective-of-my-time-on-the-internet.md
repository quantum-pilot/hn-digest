# A retrospective of my time on the internet

- Score: 258 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48744655) | Link: https://cleberg.net/blog/internet.html

### TL;DR

Tracing a rural childhood from a dial-up PC through personal sites, messaging, games, smartphones, and platform consolidation, the author argues the internet changed from an optional place for exploration into mandatory infrastructure optimized for extraction. Modern browsing now means algorithmic feeds, ads, tracking consent, captchas, identity checks, AI summaries, and concentrated ownership; what disappeared was not the network but the experience of human-authored discovery. HN shared the nostalgia but disputed its timeline, noting each generation locates decline after its own arrival and that old protocols persist without their former communities.

### Comment pulse

- Golden ages are cohort-specific → older users cited Usenet’s Eternal September or the iPhone, not 2012, as the decisive rupture.
- Infrastructure survives but culture does not → IRC, Usenet, and self-hosting remain possible, yet discovery and community have migrated to platforms.
- Sovereignty competes with convenience → personal services restore control — counterpoint: most people lack the time and energy to operate them.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** The scarce resource is discoverability for independent communities, not the ability to publish a page.
- **Impact:** Creators can still own infrastructure but struggle to reach audiences outside corporate recommendation systems.
- **Watch next:** Build low-maintenance discovery, identity, and hosting that preserve ownership without demanding expert administration.
