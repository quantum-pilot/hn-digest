# Frog-derived gut bacterium eradicates tumors in mice

- Score: 341 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48744232) | Link: https://www.thefocalpoints.com/p/new-study-frog-derived-gut-bacterium

### TL;DR

A peer-reviewed mouse study found that one intravenous dose of a frog-derived Ewingella americana strain eliminated colorectal tumors in all five treated animals, produced immune memory, concentrated roughly 3,000-fold inside hypoxic tumors, and cleared from circulation within 24 hours without detected organ toxicity. The bacterium appears to combine direct tumor damage with broad immune activation. HN found the mechanism promising but rejected “breakthrough” framing: implanted mouse tumors are much easier than human cancers, comparisons with doxorubicin and anti–PD-L1 may be narrow, and most successful mouse therapies never translate.

### Comment pulse

- Tumor hypoxia supplies targeting → facultative anaerobes proliferate inside oxygen-poor masses, exposing them to immune cells while bacteria elsewhere die quickly.
- Mouse success is a screening threshold → injected tumors and short follow-up overstate clinical relevance — counterpoint: 5/5 responses justify larger studies.
- Strain identity matters → efficacy came from the cultivated frog isolate, so random E. americana is neither equivalent nor safe for self-administration.
