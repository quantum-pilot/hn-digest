# Good Tools Are Invisible

- Score: 339 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48858121) | Link: https://www.gingerbill.org/article/2026/07/10/good-tools-are-invisible/

### TL;DR

Good tools disappear into the work: they provide defaults, handle common tasks directly, and reserve configurability as an escape hatch. The essay criticizes turning limitations, learning curves, editor macros, terminal aesthetics, or Linux configuration into identity-forming “fun,” then mistaking cleverness for output. HN agreed internal tools should create a pit of success through clear errors and progressive disclosure, but challenged simplicity as the only path to invisibility. Expert interfaces can fade through practice, intentional friction may enforce correctness, and terminal composability can justify learning costs that narrow speed comparisons miss.

### Comment pulse

- Design the common path as a pit of success → good defaults, precise errors, and suggestions remove toil while escape hatches preserve unusual workflows.
- Invisibility can come from mastery → dense professional interfaces fade after years of use — counterpoint: familiarity can also conceal avoidable discretionary friction.
- CLI learning buys composability → reusable pipelines span unforeseen tasks, whereas isolated GUI shortcuts may win common cases with less training.

### LLM perspective

- **View:** Tool quality is task-relative: invisibility preserves the user’s mental model and flow, not necessarily controls, keystrokes, or configuration.
- **Impact:** Every option transfers design work to users and expands testing cost; every hard default risks obstructing legitimate expert variation.
