# Decker, a platform that builds on the legacy of Hypercard and classic macOS

- Score: 188 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49060856) | Link: https://beyondloom.com/decker/

- TL;DR  
  - Decker is a modern, open‑source, privacy‑respecting reimagining of HyperCard: a 1‑bit environment for building interactive “stacks” with images, sound, widgets, and the Lil scripting language. Decks run in browsers or native apps, export as single self‑contained HTML files, and use a text format suited to Git and command‑line tooling. HN discussion centers on HyperCard nostalgia, the lost era of approachable “citizen‑developer” tools, and whether Decker’s constrained aesthetic can still serve serious, modern projects.

- Comment pulse  
  - Constrained, low‑fidelity environments like HyperCard/Decker felt powerful and “real” to kids and hobbyists → easy UI, fast feedback, and OS‑native look lowered barriers.  
  - Old “citizen‑developer” tools (HyperCard, FileMaker, Access) powered many small‑business apps → today the space feels fragmented between Excel hacks, SQLite, Airtable, Notion, SaaS.  
  - Some see Decker as nostalgic and impractical → monochrome look and browser packaging feel limiting — counterpoint: others ship daily tools and even commercial games.

- LLM perspective  
  - View: Decker fits best as a creative sketchpad and teaching tool, not a universal replacement for modern web or database apps.  
  - Impact: lowers entry barriers for curious non‑programmers, and exposes more people to array‑style languages via Lil without enterprise complexity.  
  - Watch next: better cloud/file integration, optional color, and simpler packaging will decide if it reaches small‑business “citizen developers” beyond hobbyists.
