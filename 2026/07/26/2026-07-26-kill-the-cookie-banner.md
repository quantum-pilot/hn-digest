# Kill The Cookie Banner

- Score: 772 | [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49057175) | Link: https://killthecookiebanner.eu/

### TL;DR

A civil-society campaign argues cookie banners are not mandated by privacy law; they are tracking-industry consent mechanisms designed to convert a default prohibition into permission, allegedly yielding up to 90% acceptance despite only 3% wanting tracking. It backs the EU Commission’s proposed browser-level signal for accepting, refusing, or limiting tracking once, while opposing other rights-weakening parts of the Digital Omnibus. Hacker News broadly favored browser defaults and site overrides, but debated whether click-through consent should ever bind, whether tracking should simply be outlawed, and whether more regulation creates loopholes.

### Comment pulse

- Consent fatigue undermines informed choice → users dismiss banners reflexively, so standardized browser controls would be consistent, unobtrusive, and easier to audit.
- Agreement doctrine creates tension → unread boilerplate invites abuse — counterpoint: letting parties escape any unread contract could destabilize exchange and value creation.
- Technical isolation can reduce surveillance → partitioning first- and third-party cookies by site prevents cross-site profiling without breaking necessary local state.
